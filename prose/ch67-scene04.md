# Chapter 67, Scene 4 — The Deep End

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the residence bath. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2370 words.*

---

He made the proposal on the Saturday morning, over the tray Shizune
had stopped pretending was for one, in the flattest working register
he owned, because the register was the only part of the operation he
trusted.

"I would like to share the bath this evening."

Tsunade looked up from the border digest.

One eyebrow went up, travelled the distance slowly, and stayed there,
taking its time, the way she read an X-ray she was enjoying.

"Would you, now," she said.

"Yes."

"The deep one. Downstairs. The cedar."

"Yes."

"Well then," said Tsunade, and went back to the digest, and turned a
page with a serenity so total, so perfectly administrative, that a
man who had spent three years learning her face would have found —
if his instruments had been functioning, which they were not, having
spent themselves entirely on delivering one sentence — the corner of
a smile being held in escrow for later.

---

He prepared the bath the way he prepared everything, which was the
first failure of the evening and, he would conclude much later, the
most diagnostic.

Water at the temperature the Tarugi pools had run, verified twice.
Lamps at the raking angle she had chosen for the slab, because it
was the best light either of them had stood in all winter. Towels in
two stacks, squared. The cedar lid stacked in the order the staves
wanted. He surveyed the result at a quarter to eight and understood,
looking at it, that he had laid out the room like a theatre before a
procedure, and that somewhere in the small orange literature there
was certainly a sentence about men who did that, and he elected not
to go and find it.

He was in the water at eight, which was the plan — control the
variables, be the fixed point, receive the situation from a position
of thermal and postural stability.

At four minutes past, the door went, and the situation arrived
wearing one towel.

The literature had prepared him for many things. It had a page and a
half on steam. It had the misunderstanding about towels, which he
had annotated for plumbing costs. It had, at no point, prepared him
for the *administration* of it — for a woman crossing her own
bathroom in a towel and nothing else with precisely the unconcern of
a medic of fifty years' standing for whom the human body held no
remaining novelties, hanging her robe on the peg, saying "budge up"
— *budge up* — and stepping down into the far end of the cedar tub
with a sigh of purely professional satisfaction at a correct water
temperature.

His systems went to white noise on the *budge*.

*Row one. Aggregate, yard, twenty-two per cent—*

"Good water," said Tsunade, settling back, arms out along the rim,
entirely at her ease, hair pinned up off her neck with two lacquered
sticks he had never catalogued because until tonight he had never
seen her use them. "You matched Tarugi."

"Yes," he said. It came out at about half its usual size.

"Verified twice, I expect."

"...Yes."

"Mm," she said, and closed her eyes, and let a silence happen, and
the silence was merciful for perhaps forty seconds, which he spent
on rows two through nine and on breathing in the four-count he used
for still water, and then she opened one eye, and he understood, in
the way prey understands weather, that mercy had been a phase.

---

She drove the first half entirely, and afterwards, writing it up in
the privacy of his skull, he could not fault a single decision.

She talked. Ordinary things, tower things — the digest, a council
seat coming vacant, Shizune's campaign against the fourth-floor
filing — requiring of him only the smallest answers, *yes* and *the
ninth* and *she is right about the filing*, each one a handhold,
and he clung from handhold to handhold like a man traversing a cliff
that happened to be the most beautiful problem he had ever failed to
solve.

*Pulse: unavailable. Vocabulary: eleven words, recycling. The four-
count is holding at about sixty per cent, which on any bench in the
world would halt the procedure, and the procedure is declining to be
halted. She is six feet away. The towel is engineering I do not
understand. There is a bead of water on her collarbone that has been
there for a minute and a half and I have surveyed it more thoroughly
than I have ever surveyed a load-bearing wall.*

"You've gone about the colour of the good copper," she observed, at
one point, conversationally. "In patches. It's a very honest
circulation you've got. Whole map of you, right there."

"I am finding the limit," he managed.

"Oh, I know exactly what you're doing," said Tsunade, with the
warmth of a stove door opening, "and I want you to know it's going
beautifully."

And then — because the stove door was open, and because mercy had
been a phase, and because she was, beneath the towels and the titles
and the fifty-three years, the single most competitive creature the
Land of Fire had ever produced — she decided to find the limit for
him.

She came off the far rim without a word. Not swimming, not standing:
*crawling*, slow, down the length of the cedar tub, through the lamp
steam, eyes on his the whole way, with the unhurried patience of
something that has already eaten and is moving again purely on
principle — one hand placed, then the next, the water parting around
her shoulders, the two lacquered sticks riding the whole advance like
masts on a ship coming in to take a harbour that had, to be fair,
been flying every flag of surrender since four minutes past eight.

The white noise did not increase. It *organised*. It became a single
high clean tone, and the edges of the room went soft and bright at
once, and some deep unhurried administrator at the base of his brain
began, without consulting him, to shut down non-essential
departments, and he recognised the procedure from the medical
literature because he had read the medical literature, and he had
just enough remaining budget to be professionally offended by the
diagnosis.

*Syncope. Vasovagal. I am — she is going to — I am going to lose
consciousness in a bath in front of the Hokage of the Land of Fire
and the incident report will have to say why—*

"If you are going to do something that sexy," he said — out loud,
in the camber register, level as a spirit gauge, because the
register was the only part of him still holding, and it held —
"please warn me. I would rather not pass out in front of you."

There was a beat of absolute stillness.

And then the warmth arrived on his upper lip, and he put two fingers
to it, and they came away red.

"Vasodilation," he reported, to nobody, faintly, examining his own
blood with the last of the departmental budget. "Heat, pressure,
capillary — it is not structural—"

Tsunade laughed — the first instalment of it, the short bark she
gave a good hand of cards — and closed the final two feet not as
weather but as a medic, took his chin in one hand, laid two
fingertips of the other against the bridge of his nose, and the
green light came up in the water between them like something
surfacing.

The Mystical Palm. Five decades of the finest medical chakra
control in recorded history. Deployed, at eight-nineteen on a
Saturday evening, against a nosebleed.

"There," she said, checking her work with a thumb, entirely
matter-of-fact, her face six inches from his and the lamp doing
exactly what he had set it up to do, which suddenly seemed like a
decision made by a much younger man. "Cauterised, cleaned, and if
you tell anybody what I just spent it on, I'll deny it under seal."

"Understood," he said.

"Good," said Tsunade.

And then she kissed him.

Not the eighteenth's kiss, which had been an answer, and not any of
the tower's since, which had been punctuation. This one was a
paragraph. It was long, and thorough, and unhurried in the specific
way she had been unhurried all evening — a woman with the whole of
the night in hand and no intention of being anywhere else — one hand
still holding his chin at the angle she had chosen for it, and
whatever the literature said about such moments, whatever the page
and a half of chapter twelve had alleged, the truth of it was
simpler and much larger: he did not conduct one second of it. He
reacted. That was the entire scope of his participation, reaction,
the way the shore participates in the tide, and somewhere in the
middle of it the white noise finally, gently, went out — not
overloaded, *retired* — and what was left underneath, when she drew
back at last and looked at him from four inches with her thumb
moving once along his jaw, was quiet.

Actual quiet. The first since the towel.

"Back with me?" said Tsunade.

"Yes," said Sazare, and was.

---

The second half of the evening was the half he had wanted, and it
arrived only after the first half had burned every plan he'd made
for it, which he filed, floating in the cooling steam, as the single
most consistent finding of his entire life.

They talked. Human again — the equipment returned one department at
a time — and the talk went where the good talk went, nowhere, at
walking pace, and somewhere in the middle of nowhere he told her the
finding, because the finding was the actual gift and he had carried
it up two flights wrapped in a bath.

"It has gone past too fast," he said. "That is the sentence I have
been carrying since the ferry. I want to say it properly, because
said badly it sounds like a complaint, and it is an inventory. Every
large thing arrived on schedule — the schedule was just never ours;
it belonged to incidents. And all the small rows — the walking
without a destination, the eating at stalls, the gifts, the laughing
on purpose — the small rows are empty on my side, and you have been
filling yours unilaterally for eight weeks and saying nothing,
because you decided I was worth the shortfall." He looked at her
across the water. "I do not intend to be worth the shortfall. This
was step one."

Tsunade was quiet for a moment, in the way she was quiet over a
chart that had told her something she had known and not expected to
see written down.

"Step one," she said, "was you, in a bath you spent two hours
setting up like an operating theatre, going copper in patches and
getting a nosebleed from six feet of crawl."

"Yes."

"It was the nicest thing anybody has done for me in years," said
Tsunade, matter-of-fact as the towel, "and I include the holiday,
and I'll thump anyone who tells you otherwise. Right." She stood,
water sheeting off her, entirely unhurried, and reached for the rim.
"I'm cooked. Stay in as long as you like — you've earned the
lease."

She climbed out.

And the towel — the towel had been engineering he did not
understand, and the exit resolved the question in full, and he
looked, because he was three years a surveyor and some structures
do not permit themselves to be unseen: the long line of her back,
and below it, frankly, magnificently, one of the great load-bearing
compositions of the age, and he admired it with the whole of his
attention for a count of one, two, three—

—and reached the limit, and knew it for the limit, felt the high
clean tone spooling back up and the departments starting to file
their shutdown notices, and did the only structurally sound thing
remaining to a man at his limit in four feet of water.

He ducked under.

The world went cedar-dark and warm and pressurised, and through it,
through eighteen inches of water and sixty years of good staves,
came the sound.

She was laughing. Not the bark, not the card-table instalment, not
any denomination he had catalogued in three years of the ledger —
she had gone past all of it into the country beyond, the full
helpless unbanked thing, both hands and no governor, a sound with
her whole fifty-three years in it and none of their weight, and it
came to him filtered through the water of a bath she had made him
free of, and he hung there in the warm dark and listened to it the
way he had once listened to his mother's frames, and knew — with the
same instrument, the one that had never been wrong — that he was
hearing the true note of a structure, the one it gives when nothing
is asked of it.

*There is nothing I have ever held, grown, mended or guarded,* he
thought, in the dark, with great precision, *that I would put ahead
of that sound. The blade, the paper, the stones, the name — all of
it can queue.*

*That is the specification. Not tolerance. Tolerance was the wrong
objective from the first line — tolerance is a wall's property, and
this was never a wall problem.*

*The objective is that sound, at that amplitude, at a frequency of
considerably better than six times baseline, for the rest of my
life. The literature is hereby demoted from syllabus to parts
catalogue.*

*Amend the study. Continue the scenes.*

He surfaced when the oxygen was spent and the limit had receded to a
survivable distance, and she was sitting on the rim in her robe,
wiping her eyes with the heel of one hand, still going in small
aftershocks, and she looked down at him — hair plastered, nose
recently cauterised, a man who had just conducted a tactical
withdrawal to the bottom of his own romantic gesture — and it set
her off again, and he sat in the water and watched her laugh at him
with the reverence other men saved for altars, and counted.

*Ten,* he logged. *Eleven. Twelve.*

*The eighth of the month is no longer the best data I have ever
collected.*
