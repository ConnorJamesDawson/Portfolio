# Chapter 56, Scene 1 — The Box

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, outer office. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1430 words.*

---

He walked down the hill and up the other one and got to the tower at
twenty to nine, and the finding of the walk was that nothing had
happened.

*Fishmonger on the corner: the same. Two clerks on the records stair
going up with the same folders they take up every morning. Guard on the
front: Ōtani, who has been on the front on a Thursday for eleven
months, who said good morning, in the same voice, at the same volume,
with the same nod.*

*The village has not changed by any measurable amount.*

*I have been awake for ninety minutes and everything in my life is
different and I have just walked through eleven hundred people who do
not know and are never going to.*

He went in.

---

Shizune had the box on the desk before he had his coat off.

It was not a box. It was four boxes and a bound stack tied with tape
and a folder that had been sat on, and she put the fourth one down on
top of the third and stood back and folded her arms.

"Correspondence," she said. "Eleven weeks. Dispatch, nine. Standing
returns, four. And that—" she pointed at the tied stack "—is the sixth
month."

He looked at the tied stack.

"That's the sixth month of *what*?"

"Ledgers."

"Whose?"

"Nobody's," said Shizune. "That's rather the point. They came up from
the annexe in the seventh month when the floor was relaid and they have
sat behind that cabinet ever since and nobody in this building has
needed a single figure out of them, and nobody ever will, and you are
going to reconcile the whole of it against the quartermaster's copy."

"That's about nine hours."

"Eleven."

"Nine," said Sazare. "The quartermaster's copy is Kanzaki's and
Kanzaki's is right, so it isn't a reconciliation, it's a transcription
check, and a transcription check runs at about forty a page once you've
got the shape of the hand."

Shizune looked at him for a moment.

"You said day one," she said. "I thought I'd have until Friday."

"You did say you were going to invent some."

"I did say that. I did not expect to be *thanked* for it."

"I'm not thanking you."

"No," she said. "You're pricing it. It's worse."

---

He sat down at the second desk.

It was against the wall on the left of the outer office, under the
window that looked at the side of the records annexe, and it had been
nobody's desk since the eighth month and had a lamp on it that did not
work and four drawers of somebody else's string.

He had never had a desk.

He noted that and did not do anything with it. There had been a bench
in a yard and a low table in a room over a persimmon tree and a place
in the fabrication room that was his because nobody else would use a
room that cold, and there had never been a flat surface in a building
with his name in the ledger next to it.

*Second desk, outer office, from 12/11. Provisional.*

*I am going to have to be careful about how much I like this.*

He got the first box open.

---

It was, on any reasonable measure, the least demanding work he had done
since he was twelve.

That was the first thing and it was not a complaint. Sixty per cent of
correspondence was acknowledgement — a form, a name, a date, a
reference and a line that said the same eleven words in all of them —
and he had the shape of it inside four minutes and was running at about
thirty an hour by the second, and there was nothing in any of it that
required him to think.

*Which means the whole of me is available for something else.*

*And there is nothing else.*

*There is no bench. There is no article. The instrument is four
hundred miles away being looked at by people who do not have to be
polite. The post does not start until the first of the twelfth month
and there is no fabrication room until there is, and the yard is
Kanzaki's and I have not been up it since the eleventh.*

*I have got two hands and a pen and eleven weeks of acknowledgements
and an entirely empty head.*

He worked until about eleven and it went past like nothing at all.

---

At some point in the middle of it Shizune said, "You've reorganised my
out-tray."

"I've reordered it."

"That's the same thing."

"It isn't," said Sazare, not looking up. "Reorganising would be
changing what goes in it. I've reordered what's in it, by date, because
it was in the order things were put down, which is not an order, and
the top four were all from the ninth month and were sitting on three
things from last week."

There was a silence behind him.

"How long did that take you?"

"About forty seconds."

"When?"

"When you went out for the temple thing."

"Ruri," said Shizune, "you have been at that desk for two hours and
twenty minutes and you have done four hundred acknowledgements and
reordered my out-tray, and I want you to tell me, honestly, whether you
have noticed that you have not stood up."

He thought about it.

"No," he said.

"No, you haven't noticed, or no, you haven't stood up?"

"Both."

---

She made him stand up.

She made him stand up and walk to the window and look out of it for two
minutes, and she stood next to him and did not say anything, and he
looked at the side of the records annexe and the corner of the roof of
the hospital behind it and a bird doing something on a gutter.

"This is going to be a problem, isn't it," said Shizune.

"Which part?"

"You. Sitting still. All day. In a building." She had her arms folded
again. "I've had assistants. I had a chūnin for eight months in the
sixth year and he was very good and by the fourth week he was finding
reasons to be in the annexe, and that's normal, that's what a person
does, that's the body objecting. And you have sat there like a bench
vice for two hours and twenty and you'd have done nine if I'd let you."

"Yes."

"Why?"

He looked at the gutter.

*Because there is nowhere I would rather be, and I have never once in
my life been able to say that sentence about a room.*

*And I am not going to say it in this one, because the reason it is
true is two floors up in a meeting about the Fire Temple, and Shizune
already knows, and saying it out loud in front of her would make her
have to do something about it.*

"Because it's quiet," he said, "and there's a stack, and the stack goes
down."

Shizune said nothing for a second.

"That's true," she said, "and it isn't the answer, and I'm going to let
you have it."

---

He went back to the desk.

The sixth-month ledgers came out at about half past two and he had the
shape of the hand by ten to three, and it was a bad hand — a clerk who
had joined his fours and left his sevens open, which meant every
four-seven pair had to be read twice — and he settled into it and found
that he did not mind that either.

*Page eleven. Two errors, both the clerk's, both trivial, both in the
same direction, which means he was tired and not careless and it was
after about the ninth hour when he did them.*

*I could tell somebody that. There is nobody to tell.*

*Kanzaki would want it. Kanzaki is a fifteen-minute walk away and would
be delighted and would then ask me why I am doing the sixth-month
ledgers, and I have got no answer that is both true and short.*

He wrote *cl. err. p.11 ×2 (fatigue, post-9th hr) — not carried* in the
margin in pencil, because leaving it was not available, and went on.

Above him, two floors up, in a room he could have drawn from memory, a
woman he was not going to think about was telling the Fire Temple for
the third time that it had been moved.

He did four hundred and sixty acknowledgements that day.

He had never in his life had a day go past without noticing where it
went.
