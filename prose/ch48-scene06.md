# Chapter 48, Scene 6 — No Report

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2,412 words.*

---

He slept from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon, which
was the longest unbroken sleep of his adult life, and woke on the floor
with his coat still on and the window open and the sixteenth of the
eighth month going gold outside.

The first thing he did was look at his hand.

The second thing he did — before the notebook, before water, before
standing up properly — was reach over to the low table for the tin.

He got out one grain and put it on the felt and put the good fingers of
the bad hand on it and laid a course.

It came up in four seconds. Three deep, fine damping coarse, no
hesitation, no effort, nothing in it that felt like anything other than
every other time since he was nine.

He sat back against the wall with his eyes shut for a bit.

*Unchanged.*

*I want to put down what I was actually afraid of, because I was not
afraid of it in any of the eight hours I was awake for it and I was
afraid of it before I had opened my eyes.*

*I have a trade. It is the only thing I have ever had and it goes
through my hands, and I have spent one night finding out that my hands
are a route rather than a tool, and if that route had closed I do not
know what I would be.*

*Test result: nothing has closed. Course laid normally with an eighty
per cent hand, thumb unaffected, patch not in the load path.*

*Second finding, and I am going to write it down and then leave it
alone.*

He had put the palm on it, afterwards. Not the fingers — the patch
itself, flat on the seat of the rod, because he had a sample of an
unknown material permanently attached to his own hand and there was no
version of him that did not check.

The note had come up.

Four voices, as always, and the two middle ones beating against each
other about twice a second, and under all of it, faint, at the bottom
of what he could hear and possibly below it, a fifth thing that was not
a pitch and had no business being there.

He had taken his hand off after about two seconds.

*Fifth voice, or something in the position where a fifth voice would be.
Two seconds only. Not repeated.*

*I am not going to repeat it.*

*I gave my word at half past seven this morning to a woman who had been
awake for twenty-six hours, and the entire content of the word was that
I do not do this alone, and it is now four in the afternoon on the same
day, and the first thing I did on waking was reach for a tin.*

*The distance between those two facts is the actual finding of this
week and it is not about crystal.*

---

He wrote the rest of it at the low table with the lamp not lit because
it was daylight, which was strange, and which made him write differently
and he did not know why.

*Protocol, fifteenth of the eighth month, twenty-two twenty. Question:
can the operation be reproduced. Reference: rose 3. Method: hold the
reference and match it. Product: whatever I get. Prediction: nothing.
Risk: none identified, underlined.*

*There was no operator line on that protocol.*

*I have written four hundred protocols. Every one of them has a sample
or a reference. About nine of them have a subject, and all nine are
hospital documents, and I wrote them for patients, and I was extremely
thorough about them, and I built a doctrine on top of them that has my
name on it in three files and does not say the word "patient" anywhere.*

*I wrote "product: whatever I get."*

*The product was an inch of stem and a hand. I did not write myself
into my own paper, and there is no version of this where that is a slip
of the pencil.*

He looked at that for a while.

*The doctrine says nobody has a thing done to them without knowing. I
have now failed it twice in four months. Once against four hundred and
eleven people on paper with a tare weight, which Kanzaki stopped and
the Hokage improved. Once against one person in a room at half past
ten at night, which nobody stopped, because I was the only person
there and I did not think there was anybody there.*

*And the third thing, which is not a failure and which I have put off
writing all afternoon because I do not know what to do with it.*

*It worked.*

*Nine inches of stem, two leaf pairs, four thorns, one bud a third
open, out of my own palm, four minutes after I shut my eyes. It is in a
sheet of paper in a box in the tower and one person in the world knows
which drawer.*

*I made a rose.*

*Not because I am gifted and not because there is something in me. I
had a specimen in my hand and I matched it, the way I matched a bezel
at eleven and a retaining figure at seventeen, and the operation
underneath was one I have been performing since I was seven and did not
know had a general form.*

*And I copied it so exactly that his crooked third thorn is on mine.*

*I do not know what a rose is. I could not build one from the idea of a
rose if my life depended on it. I built THAT rose, the one in my other
hand, faults and all, because copying an object is the only way I have
ever been able to acquire anything and I have never once in my life had
the notation.*

*Everything I have written for eleven years about what I am is wrong in
a way I cannot yet state and can state the edge of:*

*I do not have a bloodline. I have a habit.*

---

*The other finding, which I said out loud at twenty to six with one
hand while she held the other one and could not get up.*

*Six days is not decay. It is a battery.*

*A plant is built by drawing — every part of it was made out of
something that came in from outside, which means every part of it is a
way in. The structure IS the intake.*

*Mine is laid. Everything it will ever have goes in at the start, and
then I close it, automatically, without ever knowing I am doing it, and
it spends what it has and the order slumps because there is nothing
left to stand up in.*

*And the closing move is the one that did not fire last night.*

*The thing that protects me is the thing that kills everything I build.
Not two mechanisms. One habit, doing both jobs, since I was seven.*

*So the target is an article that draws. Not a bigger store — an
intake. A stone that does not run out because it is standing in the
thing it is made of, the way every plant that has ever lived stands in
it.*

*And she is right about the other direction and I am writing it here so
that I cannot claim I was not told: an open article is a route both
ways, and I do not have a way to make one one-way, and until I do it
does not go near a ward.*

*Hashirama's forest is seventy years old and makes its own seeds.*

*It is not permanent because he made it well. It is permanent because
he never shut it.*

---

*The second thing and I have been avoiding it for six hours.*

*I did not consider the hospital.*

*I have gone over it eleven times. I did not weigh it and reject it.
There was no moment on that lane in which the hospital was one of the
options and there was no moment in which anything else was either. I
was already walking north and I had been walking for some seconds
before I noticed which way.*

*Eleven minutes to four hundred staff, a night registrar and Yagi, who
has held every important thing I have ever handed her.*

*Twenty-two minutes uphill to one woman who had been back in the
village for about eight hours and whom I had not seen in a month.*

*There is no notebook entry about deciding. I have checked. There is
nothing on that page between "22:41" and getting up.*

*The eleventh month of last year: everything that has ever worked
arrived without a check running.*

*I wrote that down and I was pleased with it and I have been quoting it
to myself for nine months as though it were a piece of self-knowledge,
and it is not. It is a description of a route that was cut a long time
ago and has been open ever since, and last night I walked down it with
a stone hand at eleven o'clock at night without one single conscious
thought, and it took me to the correct door for reasons that had
nothing to do with it being correct.*

*Four per cent.*

*I wrote four pages about that in the fifth month and headed the last
section WHAT I AM GOING TO DO ABOUT IT and wrote "nothing" underneath
it, and gave three reasons, and said the third one was the true one.*

*It is still the true one and I am not revisiting it tonight.*

*But I would like it on this page that the arithmetic has not stayed
where I left it. I put "nothing" in a book in the fifth month and in
the eighth month my feet took me up a hill without asking, and a woman
came down a staircase at a run in her shirt, and neither of those was a
decision by anybody.*

---

*What it cost.*

*Her: a night. Five unloads into a tower wall, the last one bad. A
report she is not writing, which is a thing a Hokage does not do, and
which she costed out loud in front of me on purpose so that I would
carry the accurate version. A guard on a stair and whatever the second
watch makes of it by Thursday, which she says will not be about a hand
and which she is right about.*

*And a question she has not asked a creature she has had for forty-one
years, which she asked at twenty past eleven last night because there
was nothing else left, and which had never had anything preventing it.*

*"You never asked, Tsunade-sama."*

*I wrote that down at the time because I write everything down and I
have looked at it four times today and I am not going to interpret it.
It is not mine.*

*Me: the last fifteen degrees of the left index. A patch on the heel of
the palm about the size of a large coin, going deep, no report, skin
whorls intact across the surface of it. Eighty to eighty-five per cent
of a hand, which is a considerable amount more than I had any right to
at eleven o'clock last night, and which is permanent, and which she
said out loud in front of a witness so that neither of us would spend
four years wondering.*

*The trade is unaffected. I tested it at four this afternoon before I
had stood up.*

---

He stopped, and read the whole of it back, and then wrote the last part
because the ledger got both halves or it got neither, and this one he
had been carrying since about six in the morning.

*What I got.*

*Three facts about a thing nobody in this village can explain, off a
creature who could not explain them and tried for forty minutes and was
sorry about it.*

*It fills. That is her word. A body that cannot take it up does not
turn into stone — it runs out of room to be alive in, and the stone is
what is left when there is no room.*

*It comes out slowly, at a fixed rate, into anything with room, and
capacity is the only variable.*

*And the answer to all of it, the practical one, the one that would
have prevented every single thing that happened last night, is that a
person who is going to touch this must first be able to have room.*

*Which is exercise one.*

*Which is a sphere between the palms with no material in it, held to a
count of thirty, given to eleven-year-olds in the first term, at which I
have had a count of two for thirteen weeks and nine hundred and eighty
attempts.*

*I have been doing it every night because I did not want to be the man
who stopped. I did not think it went anywhere.*

*It goes everywhere. It is the whole road. It has been the whole road
since the twenty-fourth of the fifth month and I have been doing it out
of stubbornness and writing the number at the bottom of the page under
the interesting things.*

He put the pencil down.

There was one more thing and he had been not writing it since about
five in the afternoon, and he got as far as putting the pencil back on
the page for it.

*The second of the eighth month was thirteen days ago.*

He looked at that for some time and then did not finish it, and the
reason he gave himself was that it was not the same object — that a
thing produced by accident in the course of injuring yourself is not a
gift, and that arriving with it thirteen days late would be arriving
with an excuse.

Both of those were true.

*State: refused. Reason known. Not resolved.*

*Same three words as the fifth month and I have written them twice now
and I would like a future reader to notice that they were not more
honest the second time.*

Then he picked it up again and wrote the last line, at the bottom of
the page in the ordinary place, in the ordinary hand, where it had been
every night for thirteen weeks.

*Exercise 1: two.*

*Not tonight. Hand. Tomorrow.*
