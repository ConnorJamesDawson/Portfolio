# Chapter 55, Scene 7 — Angelic

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's house. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1940 words.*

---

She came down in five minutes and forty seconds and she was the Hokage.

Coat. Hair up. The haori over one arm rather than on, which meant she
was going to put it on in the lane, which meant she had thought about
where the lane could see her from.

She did not look at him.

"Shizune," she said, "what have I got."

"Delegation at nine. The Fire Temple thing at eleven, which has moved
twice and will move again. Yagi at two, and he's asked for forty
minutes and he means forty minutes."

"Cancel the temple."

"I can't cancel the temple."

"Move the temple."

"I've moved the temple twice."

"Then move it a third time," said Tsunade, "and tell them it's me and
not you, and let them be annoyed with the right person for once."

And that was it. That was the whole of it — twenty seconds of the
ordinary business of a Thursday, delivered standing, in a kitchen she
did not use, over the head of an eighteen-year-old who had spent the
night in her bed.

*There it is.*

*She has decided what last night was on the way down the stairs and
she has put it into the room, and what she has decided is that it is
Thursday.*

*And Shizune told me she would say it to her and not to me and she has,
word for word, and she has been standing in this house since twenty
past one in the morning knowing it was going to happen.*

He drank the cold tea.

---

Shizune got up and put her cup in the sink, and the noise of that took
about four seconds, and in those four seconds Tsunade looked at him for
the first time.

She did not say anything.

She looked at him the way she looked at a figure that had come in from
somebody she trusted and did not agree with, which was for slightly
too long and with nothing on her face, and he sat there and let her do
it and did not take it apart.

That was the hardest thing he did all morning, and he had been told
twice, by two different people, and he had started counting.

Then she turned round to say something else about the temple.

And he said it.

---

"You look angelic when you sleep."

The kitchen stopped.

He heard it land and did not attempt to retrieve it, because there was
nothing to retrieve it with, and because it was true, and because he
had been sitting on it since about twenty past seven and it had come
out of him the way things came out of him, which was sideways and at
the wrong moment and at whatever velocity it happened to have.

Shizune had gone absolutely still at the sink.

Tsunade turned back round.

"What," she said, "so I don't look angelic anyway?"

She said it fast and she said it flat and it was armour, plainly, put
up in about half a second, and she had already started to turn away
again because the entire function of the sentence was that nobody would
answer it.

He answered it.

---

"No," said Sazare.

Tsunade stopped.

"Awake, you look like somebody working out what a thing is going to
cost."

He was not looking at her while he said it. He was looking at the
table, because that was where he looked when he was getting something
right and did not want to be put off it.

"That's not an insult and I'm not going to dress it up as one. It's the
only face I've ever actually seen you wear. It's on you in a briefing
and it's on you in a corridor and it was on you at eleven o'clock last
night when you were standing at that window, and it's there when you're
reading a supply figure and it's there when you're reading the other
sort and it never changes, and the reason it never changes is that you
put both hands flat on the desk about a second before and then you
*price it*, whatever it is, and then you pay.

"You have got the face of a woman who has been paying for other people
since before I was born.

"And asleep you haven't got it.

"That's all *angelic* meant. It wasn't a compliment about a face. I
don't know anything about faces. It meant that for six hours this
morning there was nothing on you that was working, and I have never
seen that, and I sat and looked at it for a long time and I couldn't
stop."

He looked up.

"So no. You don't look angelic awake. You look like the best thing I
have ever seen and it costs you something every second and I can see
the price on it, and that's not the same word and I'm not going to use
the same word for both."

---

Nobody said anything.

The tap was dripping, at about one every four seconds, into a sink in a
house nobody lived in.

Tsunade crossed the kitchen.

He did not have time to construct anything at all. She came round the
end of the table in three steps and bent down and kissed him on the
cheek, once, about an inch below the bone, and it took perhaps a second
and a half, and one hand came onto his shoulder while she did it and
came off again immediately.

Then she straightened up.

"Right," she said. "Nine o'clock."

---

Shizune left.

She did it quickly and without a word and she got the door on the way
past, and it was not embarrassment and it was not tact; he saw it
clearly and had it whole about two seconds later.

*That is a concession.*

*She has stood outside a door all night and sent a guard home and told
me an hour ago that I could hurt her, and she has just watched the
thing she was warning me about happen in front of her, and she has not
said one word, and she has gone out.*

*She has stood down.*

"Come on, Shizune," Tsunade shouted after her, "I wasn't about to mount
him on the table."

The door shut.

---

He sat there.

*That is cover.*

*She has made it crude on purpose and she has made it loud on purpose
and she has done it in front of a witness on purpose, and the whole of
the purpose is that on Monday, in a corridor, in front of somebody who
matters, it can have been a joke.*

*She has put a henge on it.*

*Same shape. Something that only works while nobody calls it, held up
in the doorway so that the thing behind it does not have to be looked
at.*

*I am not going to call this one.*

*I called the other one and it cost her more than anything I have ever
done to anybody and I got the whole of the rest of the night out of it,
and I am not going to spend that twice in nine hours.*

*Let her have the joke.*

And then his body arrived.

---

It came late.

That was the part he could not account for afterwards, and he tried
several times: that there had been about eleven seconds of clean
thinking between her mouth leaving his face and anything happening at
all, and that when it came it came all at once and from underneath.

*Pulse.*

*I am not going to take it.*

*I am not going to take my own pulse in this kitchen, because I know
what it is, and because taking it is the thing I would do to make it
into a finding, and it is not a finding.*

He sat with his hands flat on the table, which he had got off her, and
went through it anyway because there was nothing else in him that
worked.

*Vasomotor. Face and neck. Visible, probably, and there is nothing I
can do about that.*

*Cutaneous. Left cheek, an area of about four square centimetres, and
there is no receptor arrangement in the human face that justifies what
that is doing, and I have taught that section, and I could stand up in
front of eleven first-years and prove that what I am currently
experiencing is not possible.*

*I know the whole of this. Every part. The gland, the axis, the
timings, the half-lives, chapter four, eleven readings, full marks.*

*And it is doing exactly what it likes.*

*Eighteen years with nothing pointed at it and it has taken one and a
half seconds and an inch below the bone.*

*I am going to be like this now. This is going to be a condition of
being alive and nobody told me and I could have told me.*

He looked up.

She was picking up the haori.

And what came out of him was not any of the four things he had built
while she crossed the room, and it did not go through anything, and it
was the first thing he had ever asked anybody for in his life.

"Can I have another one, Hime?"

---

She did not answer.

He watched her hear it. He was quite sure about that afterwards — that
she heard the whole of it, and that there was about half a second where
something went across her that was not armour and was not the Hokage
and did not get out, and he had four hundred pages on that expression
and no entry for that instance of it.

She put the haori over her arm.

"Nine o'clock," she said.

She went to the door.

And she stopped in it, with her back to him, exactly the way she had
stopped in a gateway in the tenth month with a lamp behind her and
three pieces of stem on a felt and a slug saying nothing at all.

She did not turn round.

She stood there.

Then she went out, and the door did not bang, because it never did.

---

He sat in the kitchen for a while.

There was a cold cup in front of him and a pot that somebody else had
brought into a house that had nothing in it, and a shutter across a
window that had not been opened in three weeks, and upstairs on a shelf
there was a dead flower with a saucer under it.

He got up and washed the two cups, because leaving them was not
available.

And he was at the door with his hand on it when it arrived, and it
stopped him where he stood, and it was the only thing out of the entire
morning that frightened him.

*I didn't count it.*

*She stood in a doorway with her back to me and I did not count it.*

*I have counted every one of them. I counted eleven seconds in a
gateway in the cold and I have carried the eleven for eleven weeks and
got nothing out of it, and I counted nineteen last night while her hand
was up, and I counted her breathing down from twenty-six with my arms
round her because it was the only honest thing I had.*

*I have counted every single thing that has ever happened to me since I
was seven years old.*

*And she stood in that doorway just now and I have no idea how long it
was.*

*None. Not to the nearest second. Not to the nearest five.*

*The instrument didn't come out.*
