# Chapter 42, Scene 7 — Six Rows and a Column

*Act 4, part D. Konoha General, the fourth-floor landing, and the room
over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,752
words.*

---

She handed him the marking sheet on the sixth of the fifth month.

It had been folded three times and then four and it had lived in a
satchel for three weeks, and the creases had gone soft and furred and
one of them had begun to part at the fold, and she gave it to him
across the landing at half past seven the way she gave him a bed state,
which is to say without any ceremony at all.

"That's yours," she said. "I've copied nothing off it. You can burn it
if you like."

He opened it out.

Fourth of the fourth: no entry, because there had been no sheet.
Fifth: no entry, because the sheet had arrived that morning and she had
declined to score a day she had spent ruling it. Sixth: *4*. Seventh:
*2 (harder conditions — Kaburagi walked past)*. Eighth: blank, nights.
Ninth: *6*. Tenth: *6 — he says I carried it. He's right. Not
changing it.* Eleventh: blank, the road accident. Twelfth: *ARGUED.
Best one.* Thirteenth: *7*.

And then nothing.

Fourteenth onward, the boxes were ruled and empty. Twenty-three of
them, all the way down the page and onto the back, in her hand, with
the dates written in and no numbers in any of them.

"You stopped on the eighteenth," said Sazare.

"I stopped on the fourteenth. The eighteenth's when I noticed I'd
stopped."

"Why?"

Sakura hitched the satchel strap over the two old knots.

"Because you stopped practising," she said. "You can't mark somebody
for a thing they're not doing any more. On the eighteenth you asked me
about a staircase and I told you about Ino, and there is no version of
that morning where I sit down afterwards and write a number on it, and
when I got the sheet out to try, the fourteenth was already blank and I
hadn't even noticed doing it."

"That was the best three weeks of my adult life," said Sazare.

He heard it leave and did not chase it, which he had been unable to do
for eighteen years and had managed twice now in a month.

Sakura went completely still.

"That's a compliment," she said.

"It's a measurement."

"No it isn't. There's no scale in it and no comparison in it and you
said *my adult life*, which isn't a unit, it's a—" She stopped, and
went very red, and then apparently decided that the correct response to
being handed something was to hold it. "Thank you. I'm going to say
thank you, because that's what you do, and if you tell me it was a
measurement again I will push you down the east stair."

"Understood."

"Half seven tomorrow," said Sakura, and went up the corridor.

---

He ruled the six rows again that night.

He had made the first version in the ninth month of the previous year,
on a fresh sheet, with a column headed *STATE*, and he had filled it in
in an evening with the flat certainty of a man auditing a stranger. He
got it out and set it beside the new one and did not throw it away,
because a superseded figure is evidence and only a fool destroys
evidence.

*I. Of Presence.* — *Downgraded.*

*Was: adequate, possibly excessive. Now: adequate, and I have found out
why, which is worse. Presence costs nothing to a man who does not have
to say anything. I have been turning up to eleven unrostered
attendances for two years and calling it the one chapter I could do,
and it was the one chapter that asked nothing of me. Revised state:
competent at the free one.*

*II. Of Speech without Object.* — *Six out of ten on a good morning.*

*The book is wrong and a fourteen-year-old corrected it on a staircase:
there is an object and it is the other person, and the work is not
filling a silence, it is putting down somewhere for somebody else to
put a thing.*

*Honest note. I can do this with one person, on one landing, at one
time of day, with a woman who is marking me out of ten. That is not a
capability. It is a habit with a person inside it, and if she is posted
out of this hospital in the ninth month I will be at zero by the
eleventh, and I would rather write that down now than find it out.*

*III. Of Inquiry.* — *Done once. Properly. On the eighteenth.*

*Correction to record: I have had this chapter mis-filed for five
months. I read it as a technique for obtaining information about a
person, which is a thing I have been extremely good at since I was
seven and did not need eleven pages on. It is not that. It is the
opposite of that. The information is a by-product and the actual
transaction is that somebody finds out they are the kind of thing that
gets asked about.*

*She has not been asked in eighteen months. Eight people who love her
stopped, and none of them know they stopped, and there is no name for
the thing that is missing, which is why it has taken her a year and a
half to be able to say it in a stairwell to a man she has had
forty-one conversations with.*

*Forty-three.*

*Note against the sheet: on the sixteenth she said "nine out of ten"
out loud on a landing and never wrote it down, and I did not notice at
the time that she had stopped writing them down two days earlier.*

*V. Of the Hand and the Shoulder.* — *One. Unplanned.*

He wrote five before four, and noticed, and left the order as it stood.

*Handa, the shed, the evening of the sixteenth. On the back of the
shoulder, about as long as a short word, mid-sentence, reaching for
a try square. Six seconds of horror when I attempted it deliberately
forty minutes earlier.*

*The rule, third instance: the check breaks it. The birthday arrived
when I stopped planning. The finding arrived when I stopped writing it
up. The hand arrived while I was reaching for something else. Three is
a rate.*

*Hatake says everything above a thrown knife is a controlled loss of
position. I gave up thirty-one per cent of everything I can make on a
Tuesday without hesitating, because I could see what it bought. I
cannot give up four inches of ground on a promise. That is the whole of
the remaining problem and it is smaller than it was in the third month
and I would not have been able to write this sentence in the third
month.*

*VI. Of Occasions.* — *Eleven, and a bun.*

*Handa, second of the third, marked. Tomoe came and he was rude to her
again and then sat with her twenty minutes and found out that she
remembers four of him and does not think about it at all, and that he
could have had that any time in a decade by walking two streets.*

*Eleven years of a thing going round a man's head, ended by a bun and
two streets. Enter it in full. It is the cheapest repair I have ever
watched anybody make and it is the only one this year that I did not
have to design.*

---

He left chapter four until last, and then sat with the pencil over it
for a considerable time.

*IV. Of Gifts.* — *Unattempted.*

The word had stood in that row since the ninth month. Eleven weeks of
work, three designs, one burned in a stove at two in the morning, and
the row still said *unattempted*, and it was accurate, and he had been
letting it be accurate for five months without asking why an accurate
word could sit that badly.

He crossed it out.

*IV. Of Gifts.* — *Not unattempted. Refused, five times, by me.*

*I can price anything. I have never in eleven years failed to work out
what an object is worth to the person holding it, and I have made four
things for people this year and every one of them landed, and I cannot
make her one, and I have been telling myself for five months that this
is because I cannot establish the requirement.*

*That is not the reason. The requirement is not hard. She is not a hard
person to buy for; she is a person who owns almost nothing and wears
one thing that was given to her.*

*The reason is on page sixty-one of chapter four and I have read it
eleven times and read past it eleven times. "An extravagance is a
demand disguised."*

*Every design I burned, I burned because I could see the demand in it.
Not in the object. In me. I would be making a thing that had to be
worn, or kept, or looked at, and I would know it was being worn or kept
or looked at, and that is not a gift, that is a claim staked in a
material I happen to be able to work.*

*I want something back. I have known that since the eleventh month. I
have not been able to write it down until tonight and I am not going to
do anything about it, and those are two separate facts and both of them
go in.*

*State: refused. Reason known. Not resolved.*

He sat back.

Then he turned to the back of the book, to the page with no unit at the
head of the second column, and wrote the seventh item under the sixth.

*Seventh item. Somebody scored me a two out of ten and I have thought
about it more than about anything else this month, and the reason is
not the two.*

*The reason is that she wrote the two down and then wrote the excuse
for it underneath in the same box, in the same hand, without being
asked, and I have been keeping ledgers for eleven years and I have
never once seen anybody give an honest number and an honest reason in
the same entry.*

*I have been doing that on paper since I was seven and I did not know
it was a thing a person could do to another person.*

He closed the notebook.

Outside, the persimmon was four days into leaf, and there was a shift
at six, and he got about five hours, which was the most he had managed
in a fortnight.
