# Chapter 70, Scene 2 — Clause Four

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the second floor. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1110 words.*

---

The specification session for the lower half of the full set
convened the previous Thursday, at the regular fitting hour, and
he understood inside the first ninety seconds that he had walked
into an ambush that had been in preparation for some days, and
that the terrain had been chosen by a master, and that there was
no version of the next hour in which he was not going to write
down every word of it.

That was the weapon. He identified it early, for all the good
identification did: the lead model of the Hime line, in the
calibration rig, in the flat administrative voice she used for
council budgets, delivering a garment specification — clause by
numbered clause — to a fabricator whose own standing orders
required him to record specifications verbatim, and whose own
working register, the armour of the entire enterprise, obligated
him to remain at grading pitch while doing so.

She had him in a procedural vice of his own manufacture, and she
knew it, and she had brought notes.

"Clause one," said Tsunade. "Coverage. The brief is support, not
modesty — this is a working garment for a working woman, and the
cut stops where function stops. I want the lower line to sit—"
and she indicated, on herself, with one precise fingertip, at
survey grade, exactly, "—here. Not a fraction higher. A fraction
higher reads apologetic, and nothing about this body has ever
once apologised. Read it back."

He read it back. His voice held. It was the last clause for
which this was entirely true.

"Clause two. The waist sits below the navel by two finger-
widths — mine, not yours, yours are calipers — because the
line of this particular body—" the fingertip travelled the
line in question with a clinical detachment that was itself,
he understood dimly, an act of war, "—does its best work
uninterrupted, and I have fifty years of empirical support
and one recent independent assessment. The assessment lasted
three seconds and then its author hid underwater. Cite it in
the file if you need a reference. *Sazare-18-Tarugi-bath.*"

"...Cited," said Sazare, at perhaps forty per cent, writing.

"Clause three. Fabric weight at the seat, heavier by one
grade than the pilot spec. Not for support." She waited until
he had looked up, which was an error he committed against a
standing internal advisory. "For *drape*. Drape is the
difference between a garment that fits a body and a garment
that has opinions about it, and I want this one opinionated,
fabricator. I want it to hold views."

*Row nine. Aggregate, reject, twenty-two per cent. Row ten—*

"Clause four." She settled back against the rig frame,
unhurried, terminal, a woman arriving at the paragraph the
whole document had been drafted to carry. "The seat seam. I
want the line of it to *inform*, not to announce. There is a
difference. Announcement is for women whose figures require
advocacy; mine requires only accurate reporting. You are
going to render that distinction in crystal-graded
tolerances, off the rig numbers you already hold — and I
want my dissatisfaction minuted in advance, dated today, if
it announces. If it merely informs—" the pause was
conducted, roped off, landscaped— "the fabricator will be
notified of the model's satisfaction through the usual
channels, at the usual hour, in the usual detail."

The pen did not stop. He would enter that in the file with
pride and no witnesses: through clauses one to four, at
tolerances his own systems were reporting in copper and
white noise, the pen did not stop once.

"Questions from the fabricator," said Tsunade, magnanimous,
closing her notes.

He had one. He had been assembling it, in the smoking wreck
of the departments, for three clauses, and he asked it in
the working register, level, because the register was the
only dignity left in the room and both of them respected a
last stand:

"Clause four defines *inform* by outcome and not by
measurement. I can build to the rig numbers, but the
acceptance test as specified requires the model's judgement
on the finished article, worn, in motion, under review by
the — " he consulted his own minutes, which was a mistake,
because his own minutes contained her exact words, and
reading them aloud was the ambush's final chamber — "under
review by *the only assessor whose standards on this
subject have never once been met by the garment trade of
five nations.* Which means a wear trial. Which means
fittings. Plural. Of the finished lower half. With the
assessor present and — " the sentence arrived at its
destination and he watched it happen from outside, the way
you watch weather — "and *opining*."

"Well," said the assessor, with the satisfaction of a
sabaki master watching the last stone go down precisely
where it had been headed for forty moves, "I suppose that
is what the acceptance protocol requires. Poor scheduling
for you, fabricator. I hear the model's very demanding.
Same hour next Thursday — bring the article, the minute
book, and whatever remains of your composure. Clause five
we'll do *then*." She stepped down out of the rig, entirely
pleased with the world, and paused at the screen. "There's
a clause five?" said Sazare, to the minutes, to the room,
to nobody.

"There's a clause five," confirmed Tsunade, from behind
the screen, in the voice of a woman folding a winning hand
face-down. "It's my favourite one. I wrote it *last*."

*Entry, Thursday, study file, filed under a heading the
file has needed for some weeks and gets tonight:*

*SHE IS BETTER AT THIS THAN I WILL EVER BE.*

*Sub-entry: the session produced, nominally for a garment,
the most precise anatomical self-survey ever entered into
a Corps-adjacent record, dictated by the subject, at her
own initiative, with citations, one of which is me. The
study's original objective — familiarisation without
system failure — has been achieved in the most literal
sense conceivable: I am now the holder of a complete,
minuted, clause-numbered specification of the exact
territory that shorted me out in November, taken down in
my own hand, at her dictation, while she watched me
survive it.*

*She has turned my format into a delivery mechanism. Again.
The towel, the increments, the metering — and now the
minutes themselves.*

*Conclusion, standing, henceforth to be assumed in all
planning: there is no armour. There is only the pace she
sets, and the pace she sets is a gift, and the gift is
that she never once sets it faster than I can bear and
never once slower than I can grow.*

*Clause five status: unknown. Threat assessment: total.
Looking forward to it is not a professional category, and
is nonetheless the finding.*
