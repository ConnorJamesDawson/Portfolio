# Chapter 27, Scene 1 — What Remained

*Act 4, part B. Four days after the Konoha Crush. Sazare Ruri, age
sixteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,809 words.*

---

Four days after the invasion, Sazare went back into the street with two
crutches, one Hyūga and written permission from Nurse Yagi that named the
hospital gate as the furthest point he was allowed to reach.

The street had been washed.

That was the first thing wrong with it. Blood remained dark in the joints
between paving stones and beneath the pharmacy shutter, but the broad surfaces
were clean. Bodies had been removed. Broken tiles stood in stacks against the
walls. Somebody had swept ordinary rubble into six piles and crystal into
thirty-two chalk circles, each numbered to correspond with Hyūga Natsumi's
report.

The dumpling stall had no front. Its counter remained under the collapsed
awning. A paper sign tied to one upright said *closed for repairs* in
brushwork large enough to suggest the writer was personally offended by the
need.

The hospital window was still shut.

Nothing else on the frontage remained as it had been. The iron gate had been
removed at the hinges and replaced with two ropes tied across the opening. The
left pier carried a diagonal crack from the first barrier. Three admissions
windows were boarded with mismatched timber: cedar packing board, pine
shelving and one painted panel that had previously said *pharmacy*. The
hospital sign leaned six degrees towards the road because its upper bracket
had taken a projectile.

Public works had marked every structural fault in red chalk. Security had
marked every crystal site in white. Medical staff had used blue pencil where
contaminated material had been removed from a casualty. In places the three
systems overlapped until the wall looked annotated rather than damaged.

Natsumi's evidence route followed the white marks. Yagi's permitted route
ended at the red ones. Sazare's attention went to the blue.

At the foot of the steps, a square of paving had been lifted and replaced with
compacted gravel. "What was there?" he asked.

"Two bodies joined to the road," Natsumi said.

"Where is the paving?"

"Evidence storage."

"All of it?"

"The crystal could not be separated without destroying the growth pattern."

The village had taken a piece of its own street indoors to understand what he
had done to it.

"Five minutes," Yagi said from the gate.

"The permission says twenty."

"The permission was written by a medic who had not watched you get down the
stairs."

"You wrote it."

"I have acquired evidence. Five minutes standing, then you sit."

Natsumi unfolded a canvas chair beside chalk circle one. Its feet straddled
the white line without touching the crystal inside.

"She brought a chair," Yagi said.

"I can see that."

"Use it before you cannot."

The repaired knee did not hurt in its old place. It hurt everywhere touched by
the operation and nowhere familiar, which made each step require attention. He
placed both crutches, advanced the left foot until the splint stopped it,
transferred weight through his hands and brought the right foot level.
Thirty-two repetitions from the hospital doors to the first chalk circle. He
had once crossed three countries on the same knee without counting a step.

Inside circle one lay the remains of the gate barrier: nine pieces larger than
a thumb, grey aggregate, two pieces of iron scale from the post and one dark
blue section containing the seed.

Sazare lowered himself into the chair.

Natsumi crouched on the other side. "Active?"

He looked for the retained order. It answered faintly at eight feet.

"The dark piece. Possibly the two beside it."

"Possibly is not a category."

"It is now."

She activated the Byakugan. The veins at her temples rose. "Three. The centre
piece retains the largest concentration. The one north of it has a narrow
thread. The third is touching the chalk."

"Move the chalk."

"Why?"

"It will be carried away on somebody's shoe."

Natsumi looked at the pale dust touching the blue-grey fragment and shifted
the circle outward with the side of a stick.

They had recovered sixty-three pieces large enough to wrap and number. The
street contained hundreds smaller than that. Kanzaki had refused to begin the
material audit until Sazare identified which remained capable of growth, and
Natsumi had refused to allow him near them until the hospital authorised it.
Four days of institutional disagreement had produced the chair.

At circle two, he attempted recall.

The piece lay twelve feet away. He found the return route, began at the
furthest surviving edge and drew. The crystal surface dulled. A blue point no
longer than a grain of rice slumped into pale grit.

The effort pulled heat through both forearms. His right hand tightened on the
crutch handle.

"Stop," Yagi said.

"That was less than a gram."

"Your pulse went from eighty-two to one hundred and six."

"You can't count that from the gate."

"I can see your neck."

Natsumi said, "The pathway leaked at the elbow."

"Both of you are difficult to work with."

"Good," Yagi said. "Sit."

He was already sitting.

They worked without recall after that. Sazare identified; Natsumi confirmed;
an evidence clerk marked the wrapping *ordered*, *partial* or *inert*. At
circle eight they found a fragment inside a sandal sole. At twelve, a thin
piece had grown into the stationer's wooden threshold and could not be removed
without cutting it. At seventeen, the crystal was ordinary mineral except for
one course fine enough that Natsumi could see it and Sazare could not feel it
at contact.

At fourteen, Daichi arrived from the hospital with his burned arm held in a
sling and two fewer fingers inside the bandage. He had come to identify a
blade and stopped when he saw Sazare in the chair.

"You lived," Daichi said.

"Yes."

"Good."

He looked at the chalk circles. The one beside his sandal contained the
remains of the sloped plate that had diverted the paper bombs.

"That yours?"

"Yes."

"Nearly killed me."

"The brace by the gate?"

"Put it where I was stepping."

"Yes."

"Also stopped the second bomb taking the windows."

"Yes."

Daichi appeared to wait for an explanation that reconciled the two. Sazare had
none.

"Mika says you came back," Daichi said.

"I didn't leave."

"She saw you on the northern roofline."

"I went two steps."

"That's leaving in instalments."

Sazare looked at the roof. "Then I came back."

Daichi nodded. "That's what she says."

He continued towards the evidence clerk. Neither thanked the other. The street
did not require it.

"Keep that separately," he said.

The clerk wrote *sub-perceptual retention* and underlined it twice.

At circle nineteen they reached the place where the resonator user had died.

The crystal had been cut from the body in three sections. It lay on waxed
cloth beside the shattered metal apparatus from his arms. Dried blood remained
in the fractures despite washing. Sazare recognised the pressure before he
recognised the pieces: three routes driven towards one another on the roof
because the man had said Guren's name.

"Active?" Natsumi asked.

"No."

She looked through them. "Correct. The resonant discharge destroyed the
ordered centres."

Correct did not mean honest. He had not looked.

Sazare put one crutch down inside the permitted route and moved to the next
circle.

At the bend, O-Shige sat on an upturned crate beside what remained of the
dumpling stall. Her right wrist was wrapped. She watched the evidence party
reach her frontage and said, "Hospital boy."

"Yes."

"You put three holes through my floor."

"One. The other two were in the road."

"Come and look."

Yagi said, "He is not going into a damaged building."

"There isn't enough building left to go into."

This was accurate. Sazare moved to the counter with Natsumi on one side and
the chair carried behind him by the clerk.

The kitchen floor contained three narrow blue-grey plugs. One passed through
two boards and ended in the packed earth below. The other two had entered from
the road at a shallow angle beneath the wall sill. O-Shige had marked each
with a ring of flour.

"One hole through the floor," Sazare said. "Two through the foundation."

"Three holes I didn't have."

"Yes."

"Can you take them out?"

He sat on the chair and put his right hand on the nearest plug. At contact,
the return path was clear. He found the tip beneath the board and drew
backwards. The crystal softened by courses. Pale grit filled the hole. When
the last blue colour disappeared, the boards remained pierced but no longer
forced apart.

The second took longer. The third cracked halfway through and left an ordered
fragment beneath the foundation where he could not reach it.

"That one requires lifting the sill," he said.

"How much?"

"Two men, a jack, half a day if the timber underneath is sound."

"Money."

He costed the jack hire, two labourers, replacement boards, nails and lost
trading time. O-Shige listened with her arms folded.

"You don't have that," she said when he finished.

"No."

"Village does."

"Possibly."

"Then put it on their paper. You were working for them."

"I wasn't."

"You were working for the hospital."

"Yes."

"Village hospital."

He looked towards the admissions window. "Yes."

O-Shige tapped the flour ring with her sandal. "Their paper."

The evidence clerk had already begun a damage form.

At circle thirty-two, Sazare's hands stopped answering the crutch grips. Yagi
ended the inspection. Sixty-three recovered fragments had become eighteen
ordered, twenty-seven partial, sixteen inert and two unresolved. The original
nineteen grains had produced a recoverable crystal mass of two hundred and
eighty-four pounds, excluding material still fixed in structures or removed
from bodies.

The classifications were less stable than the numbers suggested. Two fragments
marked inert at the gate showed visible threads under the Byakugan when
carried nearer to him. One partial piece stopped responding after he attempted
to find it three times. Another became clearer after lying against an ordered
fragment for twenty minutes, though neither Sazare nor Natsumi could say
whether anything had transferred or their first observation had been wrong.

The evidence clerk wanted a fourth category.

"Uncertain already exists," Sazare said.

"Unresolved is for disagreement between observers. This is change over time."

"Then write the time beside the observation."

"For every piece?"

"Yes."

"There are sixty-three."

"Yes."

The clerk looked towards Natsumi for relief. She was adding time columns to
her own sheet.

By the end, every category had become a dated statement rather than a
permanent property. Kanzaki would approve. Everybody required to recopy the
form would not.

Kanzaki would weigh it again.

On the way back through the gate, Sazare stopped beside the place where the
first stretcher had fallen. Washing had not removed the red line beneath the
paving edge. The route north across the roofs remained visible between two
chimneys.

He had taken two steps towards it.

"Ruri," Yagi said.

He moved on.

The repaired window caught the afternoon light and held it in the white star.
