# Chapter 73, Scene 2 — The House

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, Tsunade's house. Tsunade, age fifty-three.
POV: Tsunade. ~1400 words.*

---

She went to the house on the Thursday, two days early, alone,
and stood in the hall for a while with the door shut behind her
and the dust turning slowly in the light from the shutters,
because a woman who intends to invite somebody into a building
ought to know what is in it.

She had bought it eleven years ago. She had paid a caretaker
for nine of those years and dismissed the caretaker two years
back on the reasoning that paying somebody to dust a museum was
worse than the museum. There were four rooms. A kitchen with
nothing in it that could spoil. A room she had never named. A
bathroom. And a bedroom, and in the bedroom a bed she made
every time she left, on the theory — she had never once
examined the theory, and examined it now, in the hall, at
fifty-three — that a made bed is a room's way of saying it
expects somebody back.

*I have not expected myself back in this house since the year
I bought it.*

*I bought it because a Hokage cannot live in an inn, and I
have lived in the tower ever since like a woman on a very long
posting, and this building has been the address I put on
forms.*

The glass was on the nightstand.

Water, three weeks of dust on the surface of it, the meniscus
gone dull. She had put it there on some night she could not
recover and never drunk it, and every time she came back the
glass was one of the four things in the house that proved
somebody had once intended to stay the night, and she had
never emptied it, and she had never let herself ask why she
had never emptied it.

She looked at it for a while.

*Not today,* she thought. *Saturday. If Saturday goes the way
I am about to spend four days pretending I am not planning it
to go, then it gets washed, and it gets filled, and it goes
back where it stands. And if it doesn't — if I get this
wrong, if he — then it stays exactly as it is and I never
look at it again.*

*Gods, woman. You are fifty-three. You have governed a
military power. You are negotiating with a drinking glass.*

---

She cleaned the house herself. It took the afternoon and most
of the evening, and she was well aware of what she was doing,
which was the only reason she permitted herself to do it: a
woman preparing a room does not have to say the thing the room
is being prepared for, and that was the entire architecture of
her nature and always had been, and she had made her peace
with it decades before this particular boy arrived to make it
inconvenient.

She aired the bedding. She washed windows nobody had looked
out of. She put a lamp on the nightstand where there had never
been a lamp, because the light in that room was overhead and
flat and clinical, and something in her — the same something
that had put the raking light on a Tarugi slab, the same
something that had noticed a boy in a bathtub setting lamps at
an angle he thought nobody was reading — was not going to have
flat clinical light in it.

She did not touch the glass.

And at eleven at night, sitting on the edge of a made bed in a
clean house, she got out a sheet of paper and a brush and
faced the actual work.

---

The sealed prize.

*It was a good device when I designed it and it is a monstrous
one now that I have to use it,* she thought, looking at the
blank sheet. *Which is why it is the correct device. Everything
I have ever wanted out loud, I have wanted through a document.
Fine. Consistent. At least I am not a hypocrite.*

*So write it, Tsunade.*

The brush did not move.

*Write what, exactly? "I want you." Adequate. True. And if he
loses, he never sees it, and if he wins, he opens a piece of
paper with a demand on it and the entire thing becomes a
transaction, which is the one shape it must never have,
because that is the shape everything in my life takes and this
is the only thing in fifty-three years that isn't—*

She put the brush down.

She sat in the lamplight with her hands flat on her knees and
made herself do the honest version, the register, the one
nobody read, because she had learned from a boy that you write
the whole of it or the record is worth nothing.

*Item. I am frightened.*

*Not of him. Never of him — I have never once been frightened
of him, which is a sentence I could not write about a single
man I have ever taken to bed, including the good ones,
including Dan, because you are always a little frightened of a
man who could leave.*

*I am frightened of ME. Of the arithmetic. Decades, doctor.
Say the number: it has been decades. I have had nothing since
before this village had a fifth Hokage and I have arranged it
so, deliberately, at every turn, with the same competence I
have arranged everything else — and competence is the whole
problem, because I have been competent at not wanting for so
long that the wanting came back in and found the room
completely refurnished.*

*And I am out of practice in the way a hand is out of
practice. I know precisely what I am doing. I have written
papers on what I am doing. And there is nine hundred miles
between the anatomy and the — the being looked at. He is
going to look at me. Not the way men look. The way HE looks —
the way he looks at a casting, the way he looked at that
sword, the way he goes over a thing entirely, seeing all of
it, and there is no version of that where he does not see
fifty-three years, and I have never once in my life needed
anybody to see fifty-three years and not flinch, because I
have never once let anybody get close enough for it to come
up.*

*Item, and this is the one under the other one: I have buried
everybody I ever wanted. Every single one. And the boy has
already been hurt twice on my account — the petrification, and
whatever the shin cost him — and there is a part of me, and
she is very old and very quiet and she has been running this
house for eleven years, who believes that the day I let myself
have him entirely is the day the universe reads my name on
the docket.*

*Which is superstition. Which is the same weak arithmetic as
the pinwheel. And I said no to it in the dark at Tarugi and I
am saying no to it again here.*

She picked the brush back up.

*So: not a demand. Not a transaction. He is going to open
this piece of paper with his hands shaking — I know how that
boy opens documents — and whatever is written on it is the
last thing that stands between us, and it had better be
true, and it had better be OURS.*

And then she knew what to write, and it took no time at all,
because it had been sitting in the register for two months
waiting for a form to be poured into, and it was three words
long, and it was the phrase they had built between them out
of a bathtub and a ditch and a chain and a wager and every
single time either of them had gone into water over their
heads on purpose because the other one was on the far side of
it.

She wrote it. She sealed it.

Then she sat in the lamplight in a house with a made bed and
a lit lamp and a dusty glass, fifty-three years old, with her
heart going like a girl's, and permitted herself, out loud,
in an empty building, exactly one sentence:

"Please win, you idiot."

Then she snorted at herself, and put out the lamp, and went
back to the tower to be Hokage for two more days.
