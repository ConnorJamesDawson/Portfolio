# Chapter 56, Scene 7 — Four Days

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, outer office. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1690 words.*

---

He put the box on Shizune's desk at ten past four on the fourth day.

"Correspondence to the eleventh. Dispatch to the fourteenth, and the
last three days are in there as well because they came in while I was
doing the eleven weeks and it would have been stupid to leave them.
Standing returns, four weeks, arithmetic checked both ways — there were
nine errors and eight of them were the same error and I've put a note
at the front.

"The sixth month's not done. That's another five hours and I'll have it
Tuesday."

Shizune looked at the box.

Then she looked at the second desk, which was clear, which it had not
been since Thursday.

"Four days," she said.

"Four days."

"You said four days on Thursday morning at about the ninth minute."

"Yes."

"I hoped you were showing off."

"I've never shown off in my life," said Sazare, and it came out flat
and true and entirely without any awareness of what it sounded like,
and Shizune put her hand over her eyes for a second.

---

"Right," she said. "Sit down. I'm going to say a thing and then we're
not going to talk about it again."

He sat down.

"That box was the whole of it. That was eleven weeks and I have been
having it as a physical weight in my chest since about the ninth month,
and I would have got to it in the second month of next year, and I'd
have done it badly and I'd have done it at night and I would have got
some of it wrong.

"You've done it in four days and there isn't an error in it, because
I've checked forty of them at random and there isn't one, and I'm going
to check the rest and there won't be one there either."

"There's one," said Sazare.

"Where?"

"In your coat pocket."

Shizune stopped.

"Yes," she said. "There's one. All right." She put both hands flat on
the desk, which she had off somebody. "Here's the thing, then.

"I haven't got a control any more."

"No."

"I invented three days of work on Thursday and you did all of it and
you knew which half was invented and you did it anyway, and now the
box is empty and the ledgers are Tuesday, and after Tuesday I have got
nothing at all that would keep you at that desk that isn't a lie.

"And I'm not going to lie to you. Not because I'm honest. Because you'd
know, and then I'd have spent it."

"So what happens on Tuesday?"

"On Tuesday," said Shizune, "I've got no idea, and neither has she, and
neither have you, and that's the first honest position anybody in this
building has had about you since the eleventh."

---

Yagi came up at five.

He heard her on the stair before he saw her and he knew it was her from
the tread, because he had heard it come down a ward corridor perhaps
four thousand times, and he had about nine seconds to work out that
there was no medical reason for the head of the ward to be on the
second floor of the tower at five o'clock on a Sunday.

She came in without knocking and stood in the middle of the outer
office and looked at the second desk, and then at him, and then at the
empty box.

"Well," said Yagi.

"Good evening."

"Don't *good evening* me, I've walked up a hill." She did not sit down.
"I have had four people in four days ask me what you did."

"What did you tell them?"

"I told them to mind their business, and then I came up here to find
out, because I have got no business minding."

---

"Nobody's punishing me," said Sazare.

"Ruri."

"That's true."

"I know it's true, everybody says that about you, it's the most —" She
stopped and started again. "It is what it *looks like*. That's what
I've come about and I'm going to say the whole of it because nobody
else will.

"You got a post on the eleventh. Fabrication Research, effective the
first of the twelfth, which is the first post of its kind in this
village and which eight people voted for and seven did not, and the
day after you got it you appeared at a clerk's desk in an outer office
doing correspondence.

"Now. You know what that is, and I know what that is, and Kanzaki knows
what that is because she has been in this village for fifty years, and
it is exactly and precisely what it looks like when somebody has been
quietly sat down.

"It's what they did to Ariake's brother in the sixth year. It's what
they did to a man in supply in the ninth. You don't get told. You get
given a desk and a stack and everybody is very pleasant to you, and in
four months the post you were given goes to somebody else with an
apology attached."

She folded her arms.

"So four people have asked me what you did. And two of them were
sympathetic and one of them was pleased, and the fourth one was Emiko,
who did not ask what you did, she asked whether you were all right, and
she asked it in a corridor with her hand on my arm."

---

He sat there.

*That is the second-order effect and I did not model it.*

*I costed the rumour. I costed the rumour on Wednesday morning at about
eight and I said the word* concealment *and I put a figure on it, and
what I costed was people finding out. It never once occurred to me
that a control designed to stop people finding out would produce, on
its own, a completely different story that everybody would believe,
because it is the more ordinary story, and because ordinary always
wins.*

*Shizune has built me a box, and the box is the correct box, and from
outside the box the man in it has been disgraced.*

*And it is going to hold for as long as it works, and it works by being
exactly the shape of a punishment.*

"I can't tell you," he said.

"I know you can't."

"Yagi—"

"I've known you four years," said Yagi. "You have never once not told
me a thing, including several I would have paid you to keep. So you not
telling me is the answer, and I don't want the rest of it, and I'm not
asking again."

She picked her bag up off the chair.

"I'll go on telling people to mind their business."

"Thank you."

"I'd like it noted that I am extremely annoyed about it."

"Noted."

---

She got as far as the door.

Then, two floors down, a door went, and somebody said something to
somebody in the stairwell, and there were footsteps on the inner stair.

Sazare looked up.

He was not aware of doing it. He became aware of it about a second and
a half later, which was approximately a second too late, because by
then he had turned his head to the door of the inner stair and had it
established beyond any argument that the tread was wrong, that it was
two clerks and a folder and not what he had turned round for, and that
the head of the ward was standing in the doorway watching him do the
entire thing.

Yagi did not say anything for a moment.

Then she put her bag back down on the chair.

"Oh," she said. "*Oh.*"

"Yagi—"

"No, no. No. Sit there." She had her hand up. "I have been standing in
this room for eleven minutes being *angry on your behalf*. I walked up
a hill."

"You don't know what you've—"

"Ruri, I have run a ward for nineteen years and I have watched about
four hundred of you do that exact thing in a corridor and I have never
once been wrong, and I have never in my *life* been so unprepared for
it." She sat down on the chair, on top of her own bag. "You. You did
not look up when the sprinkler failed in theatre two. There is a
recorded instance of you not looking up when a man fell off a ladder
four feet behind you."

"That was because I was holding a—"

"I'm not finished."

He stopped.

"How long?" said Yagi.

"That's not—"

"How *long*, Ruri."

"Four days," said Sazare.

Yagi shut her eyes.

"Four days," she said. "Four days, and you have got a face on you like
a boy who's been hit with a plank, and you have done eleven weeks of
somebody else's filing in four days, and I have spent this entire
conversation being furious with a village that has done nothing to
you."

"I'd rather you didn't—"

"I'm not going to say a word," said Yagi. "I am going to go home. And
at some point in the next month you are going to want to talk to
somebody about it, and it is not going to be Shizune, and when that
happens you know exactly where the ward is."

She picked her bag up for the second time.

"Four *days*," she said, to nobody, on the way out.

---

He sat at the empty desk for a while after she had gone.

There was one thing on it. It had come up from the annexe at about
three and he had found it face down under the temple file, and he had
not moved it since, and he had done no work for eleven minutes when he
found it.

A Corps docket. Four pages, the quartermaster's flimsy still pinned to
the back. Clause four struck through and reissued, the tool schedule
attached, and a determination in the margin in a hand he could have
picked out of a thousand.

And at the bottom of the covering minute, in the ordinary place, where
the originating officer went:

*S. Ruri, Medical Corps (Fabrication), on referral.*

She had not said a word about it.

She was not going to.
