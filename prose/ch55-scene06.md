# Chapter 55, Scene 6 — The Hug Box, Made Real

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's house. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1250 words.*

---

"Right," said Shizune. "My turn."

"Your turn?"

"You've had twelve. I get one thing, and it isn't a question, and
you're going to say yes to it."

"All right."

"Don't say all right before you've heard it. That's a terrible habit
and you've got it off her."

"All right," said Sazare.

Shizune put both hands flat on the table, which he noted, and which he
was going to have to stop noting about people who had spent twenty
years standing next to somebody.

"You're my assistant," she said, "from this morning, until my paperwork
is caught up."

---

He looked at her.

"That's it?"

"That's it."

"That's not a punishment."

"It isn't a punishment," said Shizune. "I want to be clear about that,
because I know exactly how you'll file it if I let you, and you'll go
away and decide you've been let off. You haven't been let off. There's
nothing to be let off *for* — you did not do anything last night that
anybody could put on a charge sheet and I sat outside the door and I'd
say so.

"It's not penance, Ruri. It's a control."

"Say the rest of it."

"You've got one name at the bottom of everything you make. Fifteen
people voted on that and eight of them thought it was adequate and
seven of them thought it was a piece of paper, and they were both
right, and it worked when you were a fabricator." She turned the cup
round. "You are not a fabricator any more. You came back off that hill
in the eleventh month able to do things nobody in this village has
seen, and the box that holds you is a *signature*, and everybody who
matters is going to work that out inside six months.

"So for the next however-long, the control isn't a signature.

"It's me. In a room. All day. With a clipboard."

---

He sat with that for a moment and found, to his considerable surprise,
that the thing he felt was not resentment.

*She is right and she has been right twice in one morning and this is
the better version of a thing I have been carrying since the eleventh.*

*One name at the bottom of a docket is a control that cannot see me.
It cannot tell whether I have done the thing or not. It is a hook you
hang a consequence on afterwards.*

*A person in the room is a control that works in advance.*

*And they gave me the hook in a promotion and everybody said congratula
—*

He stopped himself and did not finish that one.

"How far behind are you?" he said.

"Don't."

"I'm asking as an assistant."

"You are asking so that you can work out the end date," said Shizune,
"and you will have it inside four minutes of seeing the stack, and then
you will come to me at about half past two this afternoon with a
proposal."

"Yes," said Sazare.

"At least you don't lie about it."

"I've never lied about anything."

"I know," she said. "It's your single most exhausting quality and it's
the reason I'm doing this instead of something worse."

---

He got the stack out of her at about the ninth minute, because she told
him, because there was no version of the morning in which she did not.

*Eleven weeks.*

*Correspondence at eleven weeks, dispatch log at nine, the standing
returns at four, and she has been keeping the diary current the whole
time because the diary is the only one that shows.*

*Right.*

*Correspondence is the bulk and about sixty per cent of it is
acknowledgements, which is a form and a name and a date. Dispatch is
mechanical. The standing returns are four weeks of arithmetic that
somebody else has already done twice and got wrong once.*

*Say twenty-two hours. Say twenty-six, because I do not know her
filing.*

*Four days. Three if she lets me start at the fourth hour, and she will
not, because she gets in at the fourth hour and would have to explain
what I was doing there.*

"Four days," he said.

"Fifteen minutes," said Shizune. "That's a personal best. The last
person who tried it took a fortnight to say a number and the number was
wrong."

"Is mine wrong?"

"Yours is exactly right, which is why you're not getting four days."

---

"You're going to invent more."

"I'm going to invent more," Shizune agreed, entirely without shame.
"I'm going to find a great deal of extremely important work, Ruri, and
some of it is going to be genuinely important and some of it is going
to be a box of ledgers from the sixth month that nobody has ever needed
in their lives, and you are going to do all of it and you are not going
to be able to tell which is which."

"I'll be able to tell."

"Yes, you will. And you'll do it anyway."

"Yes."

"Why?"

He thought about it properly, because she had asked properly.

"Because the box is the price of the thing," he said, "and I would
rather be in a box that's a person than one that's a piece of paper,
and because if the alternative is somebody else designing one for me
then this is the best offer I am ever going to get, and because—"

He stopped.

"Say it," said Shizune.

"Because there's nobody watching me," said Sazare. "There never has
been. Not since I was seven. Katsuyu found eleven years of faults in my
hands in one evening that I could not see and would never have seen,
and every single one of them was there because there was no adult in
the room, ever, at any point, and I have been building the whole of my
life out of a hand with a fault in it and calling it a method.

"You're offering to be in the room."

Shizune did not say anything for a second.

"That is not what I offered," she said.

"It's what it is."

"...Yes. All right. It's what it is."

---

Overhead, in the room at the top of the stairs, a floorboard went.

They both stopped.

It was not a creak; it was the specific noise a person makes standing
up off the edge of a bed, and it was followed by about four seconds of
nothing and then by a drawer.

Shizune looked at the ceiling and then looked at him, and whatever had
been in her face for the last hour went out of it and was replaced by
something considerably more guarded.

"Right," she said quietly. "Here's the last thing and then I'm done.

"She is going to come down those stairs in about six minutes and she is
going to be the Hokage. Coat on, hair up, and she'll have decided on
the way down what last night was, and whatever she's decided will be
what she says first, and she will say it to *me* and not to you,
because I'm the safe one to say it to."

"And what do I do?"

"Nothing," said Shizune. "You do nothing at all, Ruri. You sit there and
you drink the tea you haven't touched, and you let her put whatever
she's decided into the room, and you do not take it apart in front of
her."

He looked at the cold cup.

"I've been told that twice in nine hours," he said.

"Yes," said Shizune. "By two different people. Start counting that."
