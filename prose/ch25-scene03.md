# Chapter 25, Scene 3 — The Piece

*Act 4, part B. Konoha, the hospital approach. Sazare Ruri, age
sixteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,808 words.*

---

Sazare put the piece against his forearm.

There was no design in it. No measurement, no time to choose a face or follow
the grain. He gave it the simplest instruction he knew: across, around, hold.

Crystal ran over his sleeve from wrist to elbow in overlapping blue-grey
plates. The last plate was still thin when the blade arrived. It struck above
the wrist, went through cloth, and stopped far enough in to touch skin without
opening it.

The weight drove his arm into the road.

The impact put his left knee against the paving. Pain went through the old
injury in the familiar diagonal, from the outside of the kneecap to the hollow
behind it. For seven years he had known exactly how much weight that leg would
take at each angle. The answer at this angle was none. He turned the fall onto
his hip before the joint moved far enough to lock.

The plate did not move with him properly. It had grown over two folds in the
sleeve and fixed the cloth to itself. When he bent the elbow, the inner edge
bit into the muscle. When he straightened it, the weight pulled through his
wrist. Armour made by somebody who understood armour would have carried
through the shoulder and left the joint clear.

This had been made by somebody lying in a road.

Sazare had once spent three days making a bracer from boiled leather for a man
in Shimonoseki who had wanted both wrists protected and had not understood why
the second one cost as much as the first. This was worse work. It pinched the
tendon at the thumb, left the inside of the elbow open and weighed nearly five
pounds.

It held.

The Sound shinobi pulled at his blade.

Sazare pushed in the other direction.

A short crystal point came out of the plate under the man's jaw. It required
no course and no duration. It needed to travel four inches and exist until it
arrived.

The man's hand opened. His blade stayed caught in the plate when he fell.

Sazare looked at him for no longer than he looked at any other obstruction. He
pulled the blade out, failed to pull the plate off, and stood with the extra
weight dragging his left shoulder down.

Ashida stared once. Then the wounded carrier screamed and the road resumed.

"Lift," Sazare said.

They lifted.

The loaded stretcher weighed perhaps one hundred and forty pounds. Ashida had
the heavier end because Sazare could not close his left hand properly with the
plate on it. They turned sideways through the gate. The broken crystal shifted
under Sazare's sandal and he felt the ordered centre again—not through the
sole, not as touch, but as a remembered line he could have followed with his
eyes shut.

He had felt something similar during recall practice. At contact, his own
crystal was never entirely silent. There was always a direction in it pointing
back towards the chakra that had made it, the way the grain in split timber
pointed towards the tree. He had assumed the sensation belonged to the whole
structure.

It belonged to the ordered part.

A thrown blade struck the road between his feet. Ashida dragged the stretcher
hard enough to pull Sazare's shoulder and the thought went away.

The covering pair gave them twelve seconds. One died buying the seventh. The
other came backwards through the gate with a cut scalp and no weapon, seized
the head of the loaded stretcher and helped carry it up the steps.

The hospital doors closed behind them as a volley struck the outside.

In the admissions hall, Nurse Emiko pointed without asking what was on
Sazare's arm.

"West room. Red tag. The other one against the wall. Ashida-san, doors."

"They'll come through them."

"Then put something in front."

Ashida looked at Sazare's arm.

"Something that belongs to the hospital," Emiko said.

They used the admissions desk.

For four minutes the attack was elsewhere. The building filled with the sounds
of people making use of the interval: feet on stairs, buckles undone, scissors
through clothing, somebody vomiting into a metal bowl. Sazare cut the dead
defender's harness away so they could move him out of the treatment space. He
put the captured blade under the trolley where nobody would tread on it.

The plate on his arm ticked.

It had been under load for more than six seconds. It should already have
failed. Instead it had become still—not held by him, but finished, the way
cured mortar was finished. The original fragment sat under the centre course,
a darker shape he could feel without seeing.

The seed had not extended the time of a technique. It had given the technique
something to become.

He tested the conclusion with the smallest amount of chakra he could spare.
The plate answered at the seed first and travelled outward through the courses
already laid. It was not still being held. It had retained the path. The
distinction was the difference between propping up a wall forever and teaching
the bricks where down was.

Sazare put the heel of his right hand against the outer surface and tried
recall.

For half a second the nearest course softened. Blue-grey crystal lost its face
and became the ordinary mineral aggregate it had been grown from, held
together only by the pressure of the layers around it. The plate sagged
perhaps an eighth of an inch. Chakra returned along his palm thin and warm and
missing nearly half of what he had put out.

Slow. Lossy. At contact.

Nothing about the result had become useful because somebody was trying to kill
him. It was, however, the first time he had recalled from a seeded structure,
and the seed remained when he stopped.

He would have written it down if the windows had not broken.

The thought arrived complete and useless because the front windows broke.

Three Sound shinobi came through them behind a storm of glass. One landed on
the displaced admissions desk. One struck the floor in a crouch and swept a
hooked blade through the porter's calf. The third never reached the tiles:
Ashida hit him sideways in the window frame and both went back into the
forecourt.

The shinobi on the desk wore a metal apparatus over his right forearm, drilled
in rows like the cover of a flute. He brought it across his body.

Air struck Sazare without wind.

The crystal plate screamed.

Every face whitened at once. Cracks ran from the caught blade, crossed the
overlapping courses and reached the seed. Sazare felt the order shake inside
it. The Sound shinobi smiled as though a sum had come out correctly.

Then the cracks turned.

They followed the courses instead of crossing them. The outer plate shed in
flat pieces. The inner course remained around Sazare's arm.

The smile changed.

The resonant strike had a pattern. It entered through the caught blade and
spread in rings, searching for a continuous face. Guren's crystal, if the man
meant Guren, must have presented one coherent structure for the vibration to
travel through. Sazare's plate was bad armour and worse crystal: overlapping
courses laid at different angles around a foreign piece, with cloth trapped
between two of them and a seed that had been grown on a kitchen table over
fifty-one minutes.

The faults saved it.

The Sound shinobi had prepared for quality.

"Again," the man said.

His companion raised a second resonator.

Sazare put his right palm against the largest fallen piece. It was touching
the floor, the desk and the man's leading sandal.

A spike came out through the top of the foot.

The man screamed and discharged into the ceiling. Plaster came down. The first
resonator turned towards the floor, towards the fragment, understanding
arriving one movement behind the thing itself.

"Why doesn't it break like hers?"

Sazare heard the word as a shape before it became a name.

Hers.

Guren.

The shinobi on the desk never said it. The one pinned to the floor did,
shouting through his teeth at somebody who had trained him for a
countermeasure that had just failed.

"Guren's shatters—"

The porter pulled the admissions desk over on top of him.

Nurse Emiko hit the other man behind the ear with an oxygen cylinder. It did
not knock him unconscious. It did make him turn. The surviving Konoha defender
came out of the west room with one hand clamped over his scalp and drove the
captured Sound blade under the man's ribs.

For two seconds nobody moved.

Then Emiko pointed at the broken windows.

"Outside. All of you who can stand. They do not fight in my building."

It was not clear whether she meant the enemy or everybody.

Ashida climbed back through the window instead of moving the desk. Sazare
followed because the doors were blocked and because three patients in the
admissions hall had no cover from another volley.

The forecourt had acquired defenders while they were inside. Two Konoha genin
stood at the gate with a chūnin whose right sleeve was burned away. Another
shinobi was on the hospital wall exchanging projectiles with figures across
the east road. None appeared to be assigned there. They had arrived because
there was fighting and stayed because there was a hospital behind it.

The Sound dead had left six large pieces of crystal on the admissions floor
and four in the road. Sazare could feel each one when he looked for it: not
chakra exactly, but the shape his chakra had persuaded the material to
remember.

The tin in his pocket contained eighteen grains.

The street contained ten more things that might serve.

"Can you do that again?" the burned chūnin asked.

"Yes."

"How many times?"

Sazare looked downhill.

There were shinobi on three roofs, movement in both alleys and more coming
round the bend beyond the dumpling stall. The hospital gate had one hinge
split. The road rose nine feet from the bend to the forecourt. Its surface had
drainage joints every six feet and mineral aggregate throughout. Behind him
were eleven people who could not be carried in under half an hour.

"I don't know," he said.

The chūnin nodded as if that were an answer he could use.

"Find out," he said.

Sazare looked once through the broken admissions windows. Nurse Emiko was on
her knees beside the porter, cutting his trouser leg away from the hooked
wound. Behind her the floor showed every passage in blood and glass. The
repaired stretcher stood beside the west-room door with LIGHT dark across its
canvas and somebody far too heavy lying on it.

"That one has a broken rail," Sazare said.

Emiko did not look up. "Then keep the road open long enough to complain about
it later."

He turned back to the gate.
