# Chapter 35, Scene 2 — Four Lines in My Own Hand

*Act 4, part C. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age seventeen. POV: Sazare. ~1,131 words.*

---

He practised it.

That was the part he could not, afterwards, explain to anybody in terms
that did not make him sound unwell, and it was also the part that made
the whole thing perfectly clear in retrospect, if anybody had been in
the room to see it.

He got a sheet of the hospital's blank requisition paper, which he had
about four hundred of, and he ruled it, and he wrote the four lines out
to see how they sat.

*I, Ruri Sazare, of Konohagakure, being the son of Ruri —*

He stopped there because he did not know how the form wanted the
relationship stated and made a note to ask Kudō, and started again.

*I, Ruri Sazare, claim as surviving —*

He stopped there too.

The third attempt he did in his mother's clerk hand rather than his
ordinary one, upright and evenly spaced, because it was a formal
document and formal documents got the formal hand, and he got as far as
*surviving cl* before the pen came off the paper.

He looked at that for a while.

Then he wrote it out eleven more times over the following four
evenings, in various forms, on various paper, and at no point in any of
those eleven attempts did he complete the word.

---

The problem, he established on the fourth evening, was not emotional.

He was extremely clear about this and wrote it down in order to be
clear about it.

*This is not distress. I have checked. My hands are steady, I have
slept adequately for the period in question, and I completed a full
shift today including two dressings requiring fine control and a
cannulation that Yagi described as tidy.*

*The problem is that I do not know whether the statement is true.*

*The form requires me to declare in good faith that I am the surviving
claimant of the Ruri holding at ward eleven. The register states* Cl.
ext. *Four years of Kirigakure records, compiled independently by
people with no reason to lie, agree that the clan is extinguished.*

*I am aware that I am alive. That is not the question. The question is
whether one living person constitutes a clan for the purpose of a
schedule, and I do not know the answer, and it is not a question I can
answer by observation.*

He read that back and knew, in about four seconds, that it was
horseshit.

He knew it the way he knew a bad join: not by reasoning to it, but
because the whole thing had a wrong sound to it, and he had spent nine
years learning that when his own work had a wrong sound the fault was
always in the part he had been most careful about.

He had been extremely careful about that paragraph.

He wrote underneath it:

*The above is a fabricated technical objection. I have constructed it in
order to have a problem that can be solved by asking a clerk a
procedural question, and I have known that since approximately the
second attempt.*

And then, because there was no point in an audit that stopped at the
convenient place:

*I do not know what the real one is.*

---

The eleventh attempt was on the fourth night and he did it in the small
hours after coming off a late, and it was the closest he got.

He had the pen down and the paper square and the lamp trimmed and he
had already written *I, Ruri Sazare, of Konohagakure*, which he had
written eleven times and which cost him nothing, and he got to the
relationship clause.

And what came out of him — sitting alone in a room over a persimmon
tree at the second hour of the morning, with a bench he had built out
of scrap against one wall and a paulownia box on the shelf and a tin
with six grains in it and an empty picture frame — was not a sentence
at all.

It was a fact, arriving whole, the way the necklace had arrived and the
chip on the roller had arrived, without any of the four rungs that
were supposed to precede it:

*If I write this and it is refused, then a government will have
formally considered the question of whether the Ruri still exist and
will have answered no.*

He put the pen down.

*At the moment nobody has decided. The register says extinct because a
clerk worked off a district roll, which is not a decision, it is an
absence. Nobody has ever looked at the question and ruled on it.*

*If I file, somebody looks at it. And there is a very good chance that
somebody in a section three streets from a hunter-nin office reads four
lines in my handwriting and writes* not established *on the bottom, and
files it, and goes to lunch.*

*And then it will not be an absence any more. It will be a finding.*

*And the last thing that will ever have happened to my family, the
actual last event in four hundred years of them, will be a boy of
seventeen in a foreign country asking a clerk whether they still counted
and being told no.*

He sat with that until the window went grey.

---

He did not write it down.

That was the only part of the entire four evenings that he could not
account for by any of his ordinary rules, and it stood out on the page
afterwards as a gap — four nights of dated entries, in order, each one
audited, and then a night with nothing in it at all.

What he wrote instead, at the sixth hour, before he washed and went to
work, was:

*Eleven attempts, none completed. Cause unestablished.*

*Query for Kudō: how is the relationship clause to be stated? Cannot
proceed without this.*

It was a lie and it was in his own handwriting in a book he had kept
honestly for four years, and he looked at it for a moment before he
closed the notebook, and then he closed the notebook, and went and did
the six o'clock observations.

His hands were steady. He completed the round in the usual time. At bed
four he re-checked a respiration count he had already taken, and did
not notice, and the auxiliary who noticed did not say anything about
it, because he was the ward lead and she was three weeks in and it was
not her place.

She mentioned it to Nurse Emiko at the end of the shift, because Nurse
Emiko had asked her, on her first day, to mention anything on that
floor that was out of pattern, no matter how small, and to let Emiko
decide whether it mattered.
