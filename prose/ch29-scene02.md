# Chapter 29, Scene 2 — The Bench

*Act 4, part C. Konoha General, the quartermaster's shed. Sazare Ruri,
age sixteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,431 words.*

---

They worked in the shed behind the laundry because it had a door that
locked and nobody wanted the smell of what the first three attempts
produced anywhere near a ward.

Sazare kept the log the way he kept every log, in his own hand, dated,
with the flattering half and the honest half entered without
preference for either.

*Ninth day. Attempt one.* A seeded chip, notation copied whole from the
granite test, charged with Yagi's pattern and carried across the yard
in a lined pocket the way a real device would need to travel.

It discharged before it reached the door.

Nobody had jostled it deliberately. Kanzaki reconstructed the fault
from the fracture afterward, turning the ruined chip under her lens
for the better part of an hour: the trigger had been keyed to contact
alone, without the four-count Sazare had used at his own bench, and a
coat pocket delivered several hundred small contacts a minute without
meaning to.

"You built a device that fires if somebody walks briskly," Kanzaki
said. "That is not a medical instrument. That is a landmine with
excellent intentions."

"I know."

"I am saying it anyway, because you need to hear it said before you
fix it, or you'll fix the symptom and keep the habit."

He added the count back in. Attempt two survived the pocket, the
stairs, and a deliberate drop onto the shed's stone floor from waist
height, which Ashida performed with more enthusiasm than the test
strictly required.

*Twelfth day. Attempt two,* survives transport.

---

*Fourteenth day. Attempt three.* Yagi's pattern, loaded properly this
time, tested against a strip of pork shoulder from the kitchens with a
severed length of rubber tubing standing in for a vessel.

It worked exactly as designed and told them nothing useful, because
pork does not bleed, does not have a pulse, and does not carry chakra
of its own for the device to read against. Kanzaki pointed this out
before the test had finished, in the tone of somebody who had known it
would fail and had let them run it anyway because some lessons only
land after the meat is ruined.

"You've built a device that works perfectly on a dead pig," she said.
"Congratulations. The dead pig was never the patient."

"We needed to know the discharge itself was clean."

"You needed to know that a fortnight ago, on the granite. This told
you nothing you didn't already have, and it cost you a pattern Yagi
spent forty minutes loading correctly." She did not raise her voice.
She never did. "Test on the thing that's actually hard before you
convince yourself the easy part is the whole of the problem."

The easy part had been the whole of the problem for exactly one
attempt.

*Eighteenth day. Attempt four,* tested against an anaesthetised
practice subject from the animal wards, with a genuine chakra network
running under the skin for the first time.

It refused to fire.

Yagi's pattern sat in the chip, whole and correctly stored, and did
not answer the living tissue at all. The trigger Sazare had built —
copied from his own bench, keyed to a seed's recognition of the
chakra that grew it — waited for a signature that had never touched
this particular animal, because the chakra that had loaded the pattern
belonged to Yagi and the chakra the chip was meant to answer belonged
to whoever's arm it eventually sat against.

"It doesn't know who it's for," Sazare said, turning the inert chip
over in his hand. "It only knows who made it."

"Which is exactly what Urushibara warned you about," Kanzaki said,
"and exactly what you didn't have an answer for yet."

---

He took the problem to the respiratory ward the next morning, and
Urushibara listened to the whole of it with his eyes half closed and
his hands folded on the lap board, the particular stillness he used
for a question he had been waiting to be asked rather than one that
surprised him.

"You've built a lock that only opens for the locksmith," he said. "I
told you this was coming. I withheld it on purpose, because a student
who understands the fault before he's built one doesn't respect the
fault enough to remember the fix."

"Then tell me now."

"A signature isn't a fingerprint. It isn't fixed to the person who
first supplied it. It's a shape — direction, pressure, the particular
rhythm a given chakra falls into when it's doing a specific job — and
a shape can be copied onto a different key the way a locksmith cuts a
new blank to an old pattern, provided the person doing the copying
understands both shapes well enough to hold them side by side."

"You're saying the trigger can be built to answer the patient instead
of the maker."

"I'm saying it has to be, if this is ever going to be more than a toy
that only you can arm. Take a small sample of the patient's own
chakra at the moment you load the pattern — not enough to matter to
them, less than a diagnostic seal draws — and key the trigger to that
instead of to whoever's hands did the growing. The crystal doesn't
care whose signature it answers to. It only cares that you tell it
clearly which one, and you have not, until this morning, ever had to
tell it anything but your own."

Sazare sat with that for a moment, turning it the way he turned
everything that rearranged a problem rather than solving it outright.

"Then the maker's signature stops mattering at all."

"Not entirely. You still have to be able to reach it to activate it
remotely — that part hasn't changed, and I'd be careful assuming it
ever will. But the store, the *for whom*, the part that decides who
this discharges into — that belongs to the patient from the moment
you load it, not to you, and not to Yagi either, once she's finished
supplying the pattern." Urushibara opened his eyes fully. "Which
means, incidentally, that if you ever lose the thing, or it ends up on
the wrong arm, it will sit there being perfectly correct crystal and
do nothing whatever for the person wearing it. That is not a flaw.
That is the only safety feature this device is ever going to have,
and I'd advise you not to build a second one on top of it, because
every additional safeguard you add is another thing that can fail at
the exact moment it matters."

---

*Twenty-third day. Attempt five,* Yagi's own chakra standing in for a
patient's — she volunteered it herself, on the grounds that a device
built to answer somebody who hadn't agreed to anything yet had no
business existing at all, whoever it was eventually meant for.

It answered.

Not Daichi — Sazare would not draw so much as a trace from him for
this without his answer first, and said so before Kanzaki could ask —
but the practice subject, seeded with Yagi's signature instead of the
maker's, close enough that the discharge fired clean and complete at
the four-count, and did not so much as warm at contact with anyone
else's hand in the room, Sazare's included.

It also lasted four hours before the store leaked itself dry against
nothing at all, which took another nine days to solve and involved,
in the end, nothing cleverer than a second, smaller notation Urushibara
called a *seal on the seal* — a closed structure holding the first one
shut until the trigger arrived, rather than trusting an open store to
sit patiently on its own.

Kanzaki entered every attempt in her own ledger beside his, weight
against weight, chakra against chakra, and said, on the twenty-third
day, closing the book on the fifth attempt with more care than she had
shown any of the first four:

"This one didn't fail."

"No."

"Say what that means before you get ahead of yourself."

"It means the pattern holds, the trigger answers the right signature
and no other, and the store survives long enough to be useful rather
than merely provable."

"It means," Kanzaki said, "that you have built something I would let
near a person I liked. Which is not a sentence I say often, and I'd
ask you not to make me regret having said it."

She weighed the finished chip on the small brass balance, wrote the
figure down, and did not tell him what it was, which he understood,
correctly, as her way of making him ask Yagi instead.
