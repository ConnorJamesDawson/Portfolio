# Chapter 45, Scene 5 — Her Own Lens

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,258 words.*

---

She came back on the twenty-ninth, which was a Tuesday, seven days
after.

He knew she was in the yard before he saw her because of the sound of
a stand being set down on a bench, which was a sound he had heard
perhaps forty times and had apparently learned without any part of him
being consulted about it.

Then he came round the corner of the awning and saw what she had put
down, and stopped.

It was a lens.

It was her own lens. Brass, older than she was, better than Kanzaki's
by an order that was visible from nine feet — a proper triple, in a
collar, on a stand with a rack and a fine screw, and she had it out of
a fitted case with a worn strap and she was setting it up on the far
bench in full sun with a taper going for reasons she had explained
twice.

On the first day she had ever been in that yard she had complained
about Kanzaki's lens.

She had gone on using it for six weeks.

---

"Morning," she said.

"You brought your lens."

"Mm. Should have weeks ago, that one of Kanzaki's is a disgrace." She
did not look up. "The eleven are done, by the way. Went in Friday.
They took four days less than I said, which I'd rather you didn't
mention to anybody who commissions me."

"Congratulations."

"It's eleven seals for a filing office, Sazare, it isn't the war."

She had never called him Sazare. It had been *you*, entirely, for six
weeks — *you're closing the third stroke*, *don't stand in it*, *your
hand's not bad* — and he had never once noticed the absence of a name
until the name arrived.

---

She worked until five and it was all completely correct.

She corrected him twice. The first was a genuine fault in a bezel seat
and she gave it to him with a *sorry, but* on the front of it, and the
second was about the same third stroke it had been about for six weeks
and she said *that's closing again, when you get a minute*, and both of
them were softened, and both of them were softened in a way that a
person who had not spent eleven years reading the wear on objects
would have received as ordinary courtesy.

They talked about the eleven seals. They talked about the sealing
office's new requisition form, which was worse than the old one in four
specific ways she enumerated and which he agreed with in all four. She
asked after Kanzaki's shoulder.

Nothing in it was wrong.

The whole of it was a conversation of exactly the size of the object on
the table, and not one inch larger, and he sat in it for four hours
and understood — with the completeness of a man watching a load path
and knowing where it will go — that she was doing it on purpose, and
doing it well, and that she was better at it than he would ever be.

---

At about half past three he did the sphere.

He had not intended to do it in front of anybody and had got into the
habit of doing it in the gaps, the way he did everything, and he had
his palms eight inches apart at the awning bench with nothing between
them before he remembered she was there.

He got a count of two.

Then nothing, and then he tried again and got nothing at all, twice,
and on the third he did not even get the pressure.

When he looked up she was watching.

"That's the Academy sphere," she said.

"Yes."

"You can't do it."

"No."

Kurihara Saeko put her brush down on the rest, very precisely.

"I couldn't do it until I was fourteen," she said. "Everyone in my year
had it at eleven and I had it at fourteen, and I did it every night for
three years in a room over a laundry, and the reason I got it in the
end was nothing to do with technique. I stopped trying to hold the
outside of it. You can't hold the outside of it. There isn't an
outside."

He was already reaching for the notebook.

"Don't write it down," she said.

He stopped.

"Sorry," she said, immediately. "That was — no. Write it down. That was
me being — " She picked the brush back up and did not use it. "Write it
down. It's good technical content and you should have it and I'd have
been furious if you hadn't."

He wrote it down.

It was, he thought afterwards, about nine seconds long from *don't* to
*write it down*, and it was the only nine seconds in four hours in
which either of them had said a true thing, and he had spent it holding
a pencil.

---

She packed the lens at five. It took a while — a fitted case with a
worn strap takes a while — and he stood at the end of the bench for the
whole of it and did not help, because helping would have been a
decision about her.

"Right," she said.

"Saeko."

She stopped.

He had not used her given name before either. It arrived in the yard
between them and sat there, and he had nothing whatsoever to put after
it, because the thing he had to put after it was *I did something with
what you gave me in that noodle house that you would not have consented
to, and I stopped, and I have recorded it, and I am ashamed* — eleven
seconds, constructed, available, sitting in his mouth exactly as it had
been sitting there on the twenty-second.

Four hours. Two softened corrections. A lens in a fitted case.

"The third stroke," he said. "I'll do the batch again."

Kurihara looked at him for a moment.

"Yes," she said. "All right."

She shouldered the case.

"I'm at the sealing office Tuesdays and Thursdays from next month," she
said, "and Kanzaki's got me down for the second certification in the
seventh, so I'll be in the yard for that. It's four days' work."

"Right."

"Take care, Sazare."

---

He wrote it up that night and it was four lines, which for him was
almost nothing.

*She brought her own lens.*

*Two corrections, both softened. Four hours, no subject larger than the
object on the table. Her given name used once by me at the gate with
nothing after it.*

*Nine seconds in which she said "don't write it down" and then took it
back inside two, which is the only part of today that was real, and I
was already holding the pencil when she said it.*

*Second certification, seventh month. Four days' work. Booked.*

He looked at the four lines.

Then he wrote one more and did not extend it, because extending it
would have been a way of doing something about it on paper instead of
in a yard, and he had four separate entries in this book about that
exact manoeuvre.

*I could have said eleven seconds of true thing at any point in four
hours and I said none of it, and she has arranged the next two months
so that she does not have to be in a room with me except by contract.*

*She did that in seven days. It is extremely good work and I would
admire it enormously if it had been done to somebody else.*
