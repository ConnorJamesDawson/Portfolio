# Chapter 25, Scene 5 — The Bend

*Act 4, part B. Konoha, the bend below the hospital. Sazare Ruri,
age sixteen. POV: Sazare. ~2,177 words.*

---

The bend held for eleven minutes.

The first four were spent discovering what the others could do.

Mika, the genin from the hospital wall, threw wire well and landed badly. Her
teammate Ren could make two convincing clones and one useless third whose left
foot passed through the road. Ashida knew academy forms, had not used them in
a serious fight for several years and compensated by hitting people with
whatever the street supplied. Daichi's burned arm would no longer close fully,
so every two-handed technique ended with his right hand shaking.

They learned Ruri more slowly.

"Blue means don't step there," Mika shouted after the second trap.

"Everything is blue," Ren shouted back.

"Then don't step anywhere."

"Useful."

Sazare grew a narrow ridge across the western gutter. "The smooth pieces are
finished. The cloudy ones may still move."

"May?"

"I noticed this fourteen minutes ago."

Ren made the useless third clone and sent it running through the crystal.
Three attackers threw at it. Daichi killed one while their hands were empty.

"See?" Ren said. "Useful."

It held because Daichi understood people, Ashida understood orders, the two
genin did what they were told while frightened, and Sazare understood the
road.

He put crystal where a mason would have put it if the object had been to make
passage difficult: low, angled, mutually supporting. Nothing impressive. A
shin-high course against the kerb. A handhold that became a blade when used.
Three seeded plates beneath overturned tables from the dumpling stall. Broken
faces presented towards the enemy so the smooth sides remained behind them.

Sound shattered them. The road acquired more pieces.

The defenders changed. A chūnin arrived and left with a casualty. A genin with
an academy scarf stayed for four minutes, killed one man and was carried back
without his sandal. Two civilian men brought buckets, discovered there was
nothing useful to throw water on and began moving bodies instead.

Sazare stopped knowing which deaths belonged to him. A spike he grew through a
calf put a man under Daichi's blade. A fragment under an overturned table went
through two people because the second had been directly behind the first. One
Sound shinobi struck the forearm plate with a resonator at arm's length; when
the surface shed instead of shattering, he stared at it long enough for Sazare
to drive the lower edge into his throat.

The tin became lighter.

Thirteen grains. Eleven. Eight.

Sazare began putting the empties back into the left side of the cloth and the
unused grains in the right so he could count by touch. Twice he reached for
one and stopped because a broken seeded piece lay closer. The fragments were
not equal. Some contained nothing he could find. Some held a narrow ordered
vein. A few answered almost as clearly as the original grains.

He used the clear ones first and hated himself for it because that was the
opposite of how stock should be managed.

The road filled with failed work. A rib came up too late and was cut through
at the base. A waist-high face caught a resonant pulse and divided into seven
plates. A spike grew at the wrong angle, ran along a shin guard and did
nothing except tell the wearer where Sazare was looking. The next spike
entered above the guard.

Every mistake remained physically present. In a workshop he swept failures
into a tin and weighed them on Thursday. Here they changed the ground, caught
sandals, reflected movement and waited for him to understand what else they
could be.

At ten oh-two a paper bomb took the front off the dumpling stall.

The blast lifted Sazare and put him down on the far side of the road. He
landed on his left foot. Something inside the knee separated with a soft,
private movement he felt more clearly than the explosion.

He stood because he had not yet received the part of the pain that would
prevent it.

The part arrived while he was looking for Daichi.

His knee enlarged inside the strap. Heat spread below the kneecap and the
lower leg stopped agreeing with the upper one about direction. He put the foot
down, felt the joint open sideways and took it off the ground again. The old
injury had always been an argument between pain and strength. This was
structural. Something no longer joined.

Mika crawled out from behind the overturned counter with blood across her
academy scarf. She had Ren by the back of his jacket. Ren's eyes were open and
his hands were still trying to make a seal, but his right leg left a track
that ended in nothing below the calf.

"Help me," she said.

Sazare got his shoulder beneath Ren's arm. The plate made the height wrong.
Mika took more of the weight. Together they moved six yards before a Konoha
chūnin coming uphill took Ren from them and shouted for both to fall back.

Mika went with her teammate.

Sazare did not. Daichi was still somewhere in the stall wreckage and Ashida
had begun moving under the roof section.

Then the next Sound line appeared through the dust, twelve abreast where the
road only admitted seven, and staying became indistinguishable from being
killed in the wrong place.

Daichi was down. Ashida was underneath part of the stall roof. One genin was
visible. The other was not. Through the dust came more Sound shinobi than the
bend had room for.

Somebody behind Sazare shouted to fall back.

He did.

He went up the wall because the road was occupied, put one foot on a balcony
rail and caught the eave with his good hand. The crystal plate dragged at the
other arm. His knee failed when he pulled himself onto the tiles, so he
crawled the last yard and got upright against a chimney.

From there the route was plain.

The roof pitched beneath him at thirty degrees, clay tiles wired in pairs over
battens. His good foot had purchase only in the overlap. His bad leg hung
straight because bending it caused the lower half to shift. To the north,
laundry courtyards and workshops formed an irregular ladder towards the wall.
To the south, the roofs descended into smoke and moving chakra flashes. West
was the hospital. East was the road by which the attackers had come.

He marked all four without deciding to. North was escape. West was work. South
and east were somebody else's war.

Roofs ran north and west in connected steps. Beyond three of them the smoke
thinned. He could be out of the hospital quarter in under four minutes, off
the reported route in seven, and at the north wall before anybody had time to
amend box eleven.

Below him, the Sound shinobi passed the bend.

They did not look at the broken crystal. They kicked it aside. One stamped a
standing plate until it cracked and laughed when the pieces went flat.

Sazare moved north.

One step. Then another, badly placed because the left leg did not know where
straight was.

The hospital stood uphill behind him. From the roof he could see its second
floor above the smoke. The men's-end window was shut. The gauze packing made a
pale line along one side of the frame.

Eleven people behind it could not walk.

The wanting did not arrive as a thought. It went through him like fever: hot,
complete and physically unreasonable. He wanted the window there tomorrow. He
wanted Handa to tell him the sill fell by one and a half degrees. He wanted
Nurse Yagi to complain about the lines on his arm and Kaburagi to ask whether
he knew what a roster meant. He wanted his week.

It was more frightening than the road.

Wanting had a cost. His sister had wanted frightened strangers out of a
district and every person in their house had paid it. Haku had wanted one
person to need him and had died able to defend the arithmetic. Sazare had
spent four years replacing wants with tasks because tasks ended when the work
was finished.

The window required new timber. Handa required another six weeks in bed. The
ward would need clean sheets tomorrow and the day after that. There was no
finished state hidden inside any of it.

Below, one of the Sound shinobi pointed at the hospital. The group divided
around the broken stall. They had stopped looking for defenders. There were
none left in the road that they could see.

Sazare put his north foot back beside the other one.

Sazare stopped.

Below, a Sound shinobi drove a heel into one of the seeded fragments. The
piece moved six inches and came to rest against another. For an instant Sazare
felt both of them: separate pieces, the same order, each carrying the faint
route by which he had once drawn chakra home.

The street was full of them.

From ground level they had been debris: blue faces among tile, plaster, bowls
and bodies. From above he could see the distribution. The first barrier had
left three pieces at the gate. The admissions plate had left six inside the
forecourt and two below the window. The staggered blocks had broken downhill,
following the camber. At the bend, both overturned tables were surrounded by
flat fragments. Every resonant strike had spread them further. Sandals and
blasts had carried the smallest pieces into gutters, trouser cuffs and the
seams between paving stones.

He had spent eleven of the nineteen grains. He could account for the locations
of nine. The other two had gone into structures destroyed in the smoke. That
was not the same as gone.

Sazare put his right hand against the crystal on his left forearm.

Ordinary recall began at contact. The nearest course softened under his palm.
Chakra returned warm and thin, carrying the wrong sensation of mineral losing
an instruction. He followed it inward until he found the dark retained order
of the seed.

Beyond it was another direction.

Not a physical line. The fragment at the bottom of the roof was not joined to
the plate. Neither was the broken barrier by the gate. But when he reached for
his own work, each offered the same small recognition. His chakra had passed
through it. The material remembered enough of the passage that he could find
the way back.

Recall took that way towards him.

On the road, the Sound group advanced over the fragments. One kicked a plate
through the hospital gate. Another used a resonator on a standing rib and
broke it into four pieces without slowing. The man beside him laughed and said
something Sazare could not hear.

They had learned the wrong lesson correctly. Guren's crystal broke under their
technique. His broke as well. They had no reason to distinguish between a
broken weapon and a broken piece of material that still knew how it had been
made.

Behind the repaired window, a carry sheet passed again. Four people were
moving one of the eleven. At that rate the Sound group would reach the front
doors before the third patient reached the stairs.

Sazare looked north once more.

He could still go. The calculation had not changed because he wanted it to.
Four minutes to clear smoke, seven to the wall, perhaps twenty before anyone
organised a search. His amnesty prohibited combat. His liaison was under a
roof in the street. Nobody in the hospital had ordered him to remain outside.
Every written instruction he possessed pointed north.

The plate under his hand ticked faintly as the recalled outer course settled.

He closed his fingers over it.

The hand shook. Not fear alone. Chakra expenditure had begun to show in the
small muscles first: thumb, two outer fingers, the tremor along the wrist
where the plate transferred its weight. He had enough for the six grains
remaining in the tin. He did not know whether he had enough for everything
already lying below.

There was no test piece on the roof except the armour keeping his forearm
straight. No ledger, no scale, no second attempt if he guessed wrong. The
sensible method would have been one fragment at contact, then one at ten feet,
then two together, with Kanzaki watching and a clock on the table. The
sensible method would take three weeks.

The hospital doors moved under an impact. The admissions desk shifted visibly
behind the broken glass. A defender inside thrust a spear through the gap and
withdrew it red. The Sound line spread across the forecourt, stepping between
the blue-grey remains because the remains no longer appeared capable of
stopping anyone.

Sazare placed his good foot downslope. The bad leg would not take weight, so
he wedged its heel behind a roof batten and let the tiles hold it. He lowered
himself until one knee and the crystal plate made three points against the
roof.

It was a poor stance. It was stable enough.

Recall was growth backwards.

He had always pulled.

Sazare turned towards the hospital.

Chapter 25 ends.
