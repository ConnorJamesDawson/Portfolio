# Chapter 21, Scene 4 — Not Mine to Give

*Act 3, part C. The causeway head. Sazare Ruri, age sixteen. POV: Sazare.
~3,600 words.*

---

He planned it like a job, because it was one, and because it was the only
way he had of doing anything at all.

**Open ground.** The head of the causeway where the road came off the
bridge, forty yards of bare hardcore with the water on one side and a low
bank on the other, no cover anywhere and nothing behind him.

**Daylight**, at the ninth hour on a bright cold Thursday with the wind
gone round to the west and the whole channel glittering.

**Witnesses.** About sixty of them: half the town had come out to see them
off and was standing along both sides of the causeway head in its good
clothes making a noise.

**Hands out from his body.** No roll, no pack, nothing in either hand and
both sleeves pushed up to the elbow so that the scar was showing, because
he had thought about that for an hour the night before and had concluded
that a man with a hidden scar is a man with something hidden.

**And no approach.** He did not walk up to them. He went out at the eighth
hour and sat down on a bollard at the causeway head in plain view and
waited an hour, so that whoever came off that bridge would see him from
four hundred yards and have the entire length of it to decide what he was.

He had learned that from a boy who had walked up a shingle beach in front
of a house with a handcart, whistling.

---

They came off the bridge at about the twentieth minute past nine with half
the town walking backwards in front of them.

He had a good look at all four of them for the first time in daylight at
under twenty feet, and made himself do it properly and without any
sentiment, and what he saw was four extremely tired people.

The girl had her pack on both shoulders and her hair pushed back with the
red band and a plaster on the side of her neck, and she was walking on the
outside of the group between them and the crowd, still, without being told
to, ten days after there had been anything to guard against.

The dark-haired boy had his arm out of the sling and was not using it, and
had a two-week-old grey-yellow look about the face that Sazare could have
put a name and a blood loss figure to from six feet, and was walking
exactly one pace behind the blond one and slightly to the left, which was
almost certainly not deliberate and was the single most interesting thing
about him.

The blond one was talking. He had been talking since the fourth pier and
was going to go on talking; he had his hands behind his head and his pack
swinging off one elbow and he was doing something with his voice that was
making the woman from the bread stall laugh.

And behind them, thirty feet back, without the crutch, with his hands in
his pockets and the small orange book nowhere in sight, came the tall one
with the silver hair.

He had seen Sazare before he came off the bridge. Sazare knew that within
about two seconds, because the man's pace did not change by a fraction and
his head did not turn and he did not look at him at all, and a man who
does not look at the only stationary person on a causeway head is a man
who has already looked.

Sazare stood up off the bollard, slowly, with his hands out from his
sides.

The three children walked past him.

The fourth man stopped six feet away.

---

Close up, in the sun, without the fog and the crutch and the doorway, he
was younger than Sazare had assumed — thirty at most, and probably less —
and taller than he looked, and thin in the way of somebody who does not
think about food. Silver hair, standing up on the left. The mask over the
lower face, dark blue-black, worn soft at the edges from years of the
same thing being pulled up and down. The protector down over the left eye
with the leaf on it, and a thin white line of scar tissue running out from
under the bottom edge of it down onto the cheekbone.

One dark eye, half-lidded, looking at him with what would have been
boredom on anybody else.

He smelled, very faintly, of dog.

"Morning," said Kakashi.

"Good morning."

"You're the mender."

"Yes."

"You did the thigh on the Miura boy," said Kakashi. "High compression at
the groin, packed, and then you got the leg up on a rope coil, which
nobody thinks of." A pause about a second long. "Field medic?"

"No. Two years with a country doctor in the Land of Hot Water."

"Mm," said Kakashi, in a tone that could have meant anything at all, and
waited.

---

Sazare had rehearsed it and the rehearsal survived about four words, which
he had expected, so he did it the other way, which was to say the true
things in descending order of danger and stop.

"My name is Sazare Ruri," he said. "I'm from Kirigakure, the lower
district off the fish market. I'm sixteen. I've got a kekkei genkai and
there's an entry on me in the current book with a price on it, alive, and
I'd rather you had that from me than found it.

"I have never served any village. I was in the Academy at Kiri for four
years and I never graduated, because the intake was suspended, and I have
no rank, no headband, no papers and no affiliation of any kind and never
have had.

"I'm not asking you to take me anywhere today and I'm not asking for
anything from you personally. I want to come to your village and be
looked at, under whatever conditions your people want to set, and I will
accept all of them, including being watched, and I have three hundred and
sixty thousand ryō in the lining of this coat which I will hand over at
the gate and account for if I'm asked to.

"That's the whole of it. I'll answer anything you ask me and I'm not going
to volunteer any more than that, because most of it isn't relevant and
some of it is other people's."

The wind came across the causeway head. Somebody in the crowd forty feet
away shouted something about the bridge and there was a lot of laughing.

"Well," said Kakashi. "That's the most organised thing anybody's said to
me all month."

---

"What are you wanted for?"

It was the dark-haired boy. He had turned round about nine feet away and
was standing with his hand on his own pack strap, and he had asked it
flatly, in the first four words Sazare had heard out of him, and it was
the only question in the whole of that conversation that went straight to
the middle.

"Sasuke," said Sakura.

"It's a fair question," said Kakashi, without turning round. "He'll get
asked it at the gate by somebody a lot less polite."

Sazare looked at the boy.

"I killed a state officer," he said. "A public executioner, in the
sentencing square at Kiri, in front of about four hundred people. I was
thirteen. He was two-thirds of the way through executing my family at the
time and I did it with the bloodline, and there were four hundred
witnesses, and I'm not disputing any part of the account."

Nobody said anything at all.

The blond one made a small noise. The girl had gone extremely still with
one hand up at the plaster on her neck.

And the dark-haired boy stood in the middle of a causeway in the sun and
looked at Sazare Ruri for about four seconds with an expression that did
not change in any particular, and did not ask a second question, and did
not say one single word about it then or afterwards; and then turned round
and walked back to where he had been standing and put his pack down and
looked out at the water.

Sazare, who read people for a living and had been doing it since he was
eight years old, got precisely nothing off him, and understood only that
something had happened and that it had not been what he expected.

---

"Right," said Kakashi. "My turn, and there's three, and you won't like the
last one.

"One. Why not Kiri? You're a bloodline holder from a village that is short
of them. There is a version of this where you walk into a Kiri border post
and hand yourself in and are extremely well looked after for the rest of
your life."

"I've read my own entry," said Sazare. "It says *alive only*, and
*undamaged*, and there's a line at the bottom that says damage to hands or
forearms voids the premium. Nobody writes that about a soldier. That's how
you write about a *breeding animal*, and it's the only sentence in the
entry that isn't a fact about what I did." He kept his hands where they
were. "So no."

"Mm," said Kakashi. "Two. What do you actually want? And be specific,
because *a better life* is going to get you turned round at the gate."

"To be taught," said Sazare.

"Taught what?"

"Anything. Everything. I'll take whatever's going." He heard his own voice
do something and did not try to stop it. "I've got no jutsu at all. Not
one. I've got a bloodline I worked out on my own on a bench with a hammer,
and I can hold a piece for four seconds under load and I have not moved
that number in eight months, and about a fortnight ago I watched a girl of
twelve walk up a tree and stand on the side of it for forty seconds while
she explained something with her hand off the bark.

"And I have thought about that every day since, and the thing I keep
coming back to isn't that she can do it. It's that somebody *told her how*,
in an afternoon, and there's a word for it, and there's a room somewhere
with a person at the front of it saying the word to eleven-year-olds."

Behind Kakashi, quite distinctly, the girl said, "*Oh*," in a very small
voice.

"That's it," said Sazare. "That's the entire ask. I want to be in the
building where somebody says the word."

---

"Three," said Kakashi.

He had not moved and his hands were still in his pockets and there was
absolutely nothing in his voice.

"What will you do if I say no?"

And there it was.

Sazare stood on the hardcore in the sun with his sleeves up and sixty
people laughing forty feet away, and he saw the whole of it, in about a
second and a half — saw exactly what the question was for and exactly what
the safe answer was and how easy it would have been, and how completely
that man would have known.

*Nothing. I'd go back to mending.*

That was the safe one. It was four words. It was reassuring and modest and
it would have got him nowhere and he knew that, but the knowing was not
what stopped him.

*There is a difference between a boy who ran and a man who left*, said a
flat dosage voice, twenty-two months in the ground in a valley in the Land
of Hot Water, *and there is a difference between a man who lies and a man
who doesn't.*

"There's a woman," said Sazare. "She came four hundred miles to find me in
the autumn because I'd left something in a wall, and she bought me a meal
and asked me for nothing at all, and she told me there is one person alive
who could tell me in a sentence why my work cracks at four seconds. She
wouldn't say where he is, and she was right not to, and she is the only
person in four years who has given me a thing I actually needed.

"I know roughly where to start looking. It would take me a season, maybe
two.

"If you say no, that's what I do. I'd go and find her, and I would take
whatever that offer turned out to cost, and I have a fairly good idea what
it costs, because I have just spent a fortnight in this country watching
what happens to people who get picked up by somebody who *wants* them."

The wind went across the causeway head.

"And I'm telling you that," said Sazare, "knowing exactly what it sounds
like, because you asked me a question and I don't lie to people. It's the
only thing I've got left that's worth anything and I'm not putting it down
for this."

---

Kakashi looked at him for a long moment out of one eye.

"Somebody oiled that sword," he said.

Sazare said nothing.

"I went back up the headland yesterday evening," said Kakashi
conversationally, "because I'm a professional and you always go back and
look at a thing you've left, and it had been out of the ground and put
back in. Levered, and the socket packed with turf and stones, and trodden
in all round, twice, and sighted true — which, incidentally, is better than
it was when I put it there.

"And oiled. Both faces, the back, round the inside of the cut-out, and
somebody had got into the drilled hole with a rag corner."

He tilted his head very slightly.

"So you had it flat on the ground on an empty headland with nobody within
a mile."

"Yes."

"And you know what it's worth."

"To about eleven thousand ryō," said Sazare, "and I know four people who'd
have moved it and one who wouldn't have looked in the crate."

"And you put it back."

"Yes."

"Why?"

And Sazare Ruri, standing on a causeway in the Land of Waves with his
hands out from his sides, gave the only reference he had.

"Because he told me how they work," he said. "In his house, with a cup in
his hands, about ten days before you killed him. He said the seven are
older than the village and nobody knows who made any of them and there's
no smith alive who could make another. He said they don't get made, they
get *handed down* — holder to holder, and you get it off the last man, and
it's not a ceremony, it's a chain, and the chain doesn't care who's on
it."

He heard his own voice go flat, and let it.

"And I wanted it. I want you to have that, because you'll find out
otherwise. I got it out of the ground and I sat on that hill with my hand
on it and I could have had it off the island inside a week and there is
nobody alive with a better claim than mine, which is to say nobody alive
with any claim at all.

"And nobody handed it to me.

"That's all. That's the whole of the reason and there isn't a better one.
My family lived four hundred years on a thing nobody handed them and it
killed all of them. My village took everything I had and never asked. My
sister used me for four years and never asked. I have spent my entire life
on the difference between being given a thing and taking it, and it turns
out to be the only thing I actually know, and I was not going to be the
next man on that chain by *helping myself* on an empty hill with nobody
watching."

---

There was a silence of about four seconds.

"Kakashi-sensei," said Naruto, "he can just come, right?"

"No."

"*Why not?*" It came out at about twice the volume of anything else on
that causeway. "He fixed everybody's hands! He was on the bridge — the
whole town's been going on about him for a *week* — and he's got nowhere
to go, and he just told you the truth about a thing that makes him look
bad when he could've just *not*—"

"Because it isn't mine to give," said Kakashi.

He said it without any heat at all and it stopped the boy dead.

"Listen, because this is the actual answer and you're going to be doing
this job for the next forty years." He turned round properly to face the
three of them, which meant he was no longer facing Sazare, which Sazare
understood immediately to be a deliberate courtesy and possibly the single
most reassuring thing that happened to him that morning.

"A foreign national with an unregistered kekkei genkai, wanted by a hidden
village, with no papers and no service record, asking for residence.
That is not a decision a jōnin makes on a road. It is not a decision my
commander makes. That goes up, and it gets read by people whose entire
occupation is thinking about what a thing like this is *for*, and they
will ask, correctly, whether he is a plant, and there is no answer he can
give that proves he isn't, because there never is."

"But he's *not*—"

"I know," said Kakashi. "I've been standing here for ten minutes and I'd
put money on it. That's worth exactly one line in a report and it should
be worth exactly one line, and if it were ever worth more than that our
village would have been taken apart from the inside eleven times over by
people considerably better at this than he is."

He put his hands back in his pockets.

"And there's the other half, which he's already worked out and I'd rather
say out loud in front of witnesses. There are people at home who will find
a sole surviving bloodline holder with no family and no country
*extremely* interesting, and I cannot promise you what that looks like,
and I would be lying to him if I pretended I could."

"Then what's the *point* of—"

"There's a registration procedure," said Sakura.

---

Everybody looked at her.

She had gone red to the ears and she said it anyway, fast, with her chin
up, in the voice of somebody who has read the whole book and is aware that
this has never once been useful before.

"For foreign nationals seeking residence. It's — there's a form, and it
goes to the administrative office, and it needs an endorsement from a
Konoha shinobi of chūnin rank or above who can *attest to direct
observation of conduct*, that's the actual wording, and then it's
conditional residence pending assessment, and there's a probationary
period, and monitoring, and—" she ran out of air and got some more "—and
it *exists*, sensei, it's a real procedure, it's in the field manual under
non-combatant persons and I read it on the boat because I was bored."

Nobody said anything for a second.

"You read the field manual," said Sasuke, "on the boat."

"*Shut up.*"

"Sakura," said Kakashi, and there was something in his voice that had not
been there before, "that is the single most useful thing anybody has said
on this causeway this morning and I want you to remember that for the rest
of your career."

---

He turned round.

"Here is what I can do," he said, "and it is less than you want and I'd
rather you knew the size of it now.

"I can walk you to the gate. That's eleven days and I'll not have you
travelling on your own with that entry live, and you'll do it as what you
are and not under any story, because a story that comes apart at a
checkpoint finishes you and me both.

"I can write what I saw. That's the endorsement, and I'll write it
straight, and it will say that you did field medicine on eleven of the
Land of Waves' people at your own initiative and that you gave me a full
and accurate account of a capital charge that I had no way of compelling
out of you. It will also say that you have been in the trade, because you
have, and I'm not writing a report with a hole in it.

"And I can be in the room when they read it, which is worth more than the
paper, and is about the end of what I've got.

"I can't promise you're admitted. I can't promise how long you're held
before anybody decides. I cannot promise who reads that file after I've
finished with it, and I would be doing you a very serious disservice if I
let you walk eleven days believing that I could.

"I can get you to the gate. What happens after that isn't mine to give."

Sazare stood on the causeway head with his hands out from his sides and
his heart going like a hammer, and found that he had absolutely nothing to
say and that his eyes had gone hot, and that sixty people were laughing
forty feet away about a bridge.

"That's more than I asked for," he said.

"It's a good deal less than you need," said Kakashi. "Get your bag. We
leave in an hour, and I'm not waiting, and neither is he — " a thumb at
the blond one, who was already shouting something at the crowd — "so if
you've got anything to say to anybody in this town, go and say it now."
