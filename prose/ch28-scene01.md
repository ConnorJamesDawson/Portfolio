# Chapter 28, Scene 1 — The Rest of the Building

*Act 4, part C. Konoha General. Sazare Ruri, age sixteen. POV: Sazare.
~1,205 words.*

---

Sister Kaburagi found him on the second floor at the end of his second
week back, doing observations from the evacuation chair with a speed
that had stopped drawing comment from anybody but the newest orderlies,
and stood at the foot of bed three until he had finished writing down
bed three's pulse.

"Finish the round," she said. "Then my office."

He finished the round. Bed one's crushed hand was healing crooked in a
way Fujita had already flagged for a second procedure. Bed five had
gone home. Handa's pulse was seventy, as it had been for eleven days
running, which Sazare had begun to suspect said more about Handa's
constitution than about anything the ward was doing for him.

"You're being summoned," Handa said, without opening his eyes.

"I don't know that yet."

"You know it. You've got the face you get when you're about to be told
something you already worked out three days ago and haven't wanted to
say out loud."

Sazare did not answer that, which Handa appeared to take as
confirmation.

---

Kaburagi's office still had the dressing bowl of names from the
funeral on the shelf, unemptied, and a duty roster on the wall with
more lines crossed out than filled in. She did not sit. She rarely did,
in that office, on the theory — stated to him once, in passing — that
a chair invited a conversation to run longer than the building could
afford.

"Second floor," she said. "You know it better than anyone alive."

"I know it well."

"I said better than anyone alive, and I meant it precisely. Yagi's in
surgical rotation four days out of six and will be for the
foreseeable. Fujita hasn't left theatre for more than six consecutive
hours since the invasion. Emiko has admissions, and admissions alone
would flatten most people I've worked with. I have the rest of the
building, which is considerably more of a building than it used to
be." She looked at the crossed-out roster rather than at him. "That
leaves the second floor without anybody who knows every patient,
every piece of equipment, every evacuation route, and which of the new
orderlies can be trusted with a scalpel and which can't yet be trusted
with a broom."

"There are people more qualified than me for that."

"There are. Name three."

He tried. Nurse Tomoe had the seniority but had lost two fingers on
the invasion's second hour and was still three weeks from full use of
the hand. The senior orderly from the western intake had the
experience but had transferred to the fever ward, where he was
needed worse. The third name did not arrive at all, because the third
name did not exist — every candidate he could construct had already
been placed somewhere the hospital needed them more.

"They are also somewhere else," Kaburagi said, before he had finished
failing to produce the third name. "Which is the whole of my point,
and I'd rather not spend the next ten minutes watching you arrive at
it by yourself when I could simply tell you."

"You want me running the floor."

"I want somebody running the floor who already knows it, so I don't
have to spend a fortnight teaching a stranger where the linen
cupboard is while eleven people who can't walk wait for their
observations. You are, at present, the only person who fits that
description and isn't currently needed somewhere worse."

"That isn't the same as being qualified."

"No," Kaburagi agreed. "It isn't. I am aware of the difference, and I
am choosing the person who's here over the qualification that isn't,
because a hospital doesn't get to wait for its ideal staffing before
it treats its patients, and neither do I." She turned from the roster
and looked at him properly for the first time since he'd come in.
"Until somebody more qualified survives long enough to replace you, it
is yours."

---

She set the terms out in the same flat register she used for
everything that mattered, which he had learned, over four months, to
trust more than warmth would have earned from her.

"You may assign the orderlies their rounds. You may reorganise
supplies, observations, and the order in which routine work gets done.
You may perform any dressing or procedure you've already been signed
off for, and no others. You may stop anyone doing something unsafe,
including a nurse senior to you, and you will be backed if you're
right and corrected privately if you're wrong. You may escalate
anything you're not certain of to Yagi or Fujita, and you will, every
single time, because uncertainty escalated costs nothing and
uncertainty sat on costs somebody a limb."

"And what I may not do."

"You may not diagnose independently. You may not prescribe. You may
not approve a surgical decision or alter a physician's treatment plan.
You may not enrol a single patient in anything experimental — that
includes your crystal, and I want that understood before you leave
this room, not discovered the first time you're tempted to skip a
signature because the queue for one is inconvenient. You may not
overrule Yagi or Fujita on a clinical matter, ever, regardless of how
correct you believe yourself to be." She held his eye through the
whole of it, the way she held everyone's eye through anything she
judged important enough to be misheard. "The moment any of that
changes, I will tell you directly. Until then, the list stands exactly
as read."

"That's a narrower authority than the title suggests."

"It's exactly the authority the title requires, no wider. A title
that outran what you were actually permitted to do would get somebody
killed within a month, and I have buried enough people this year
without adding administrative optimism to the list of reasons."

---

She wrote *Acting Second-Floor Charge* on the temporary roster herself,
in the same square hand she used for everything, and pinned it beneath
the line that already read *RURI, S.* — no ceremony, no witnesses
beyond the two of them and the empty dressing bowl on the shelf.

"Ashida will amend your pay grade. It won't be much of a change; the
village doesn't pay for acting authority the way it pays for earned
rank, and I'd advise you not to expect it to." She capped her pen.
"Any questions."

"Why tell me the restrictions before the authority."

"Because you're the kind of person who remembers whichever half of a
sentence he hears first, and I would rather you remember the fence
than the field." She opened the door for him, which he understood,
correctly, as the meeting ending rather than courtesy. "Go and tell
the new orderlies where the sterile gauze is kept. Half of them still
think it's in the cupboard we moved it out of in the third month."

He went back to the second floor and found both new orderlies
standing in front of the wrong cupboard, exactly as predicted, and
did not mention the conversation to either of them, though Handa
worked it out from his face within the hour and did not stop bringing
it up for the better part of a week.
