# Chapter 50, Scene 5 — Twelve

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard and the hospital
stairs. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,679 words.*

---

She found it at the reconcile, which was where she found everything.

The ninth month closed on a Thursday and they sat at opposite ends of
the awning bench in the last hour of the last working day and read
their books aloud at each other, line by line, the way they had done
every month for three years — and at about the four hundredth entry
Kanzaki stopped.

She did not say anything. She went back four pages.

Then she went back to the start of the month, and then she got up and
went inside, and came out with the eighth month's book as well and put
it on the bench beside the ninth, open, side by side, and stood over
the two of them with her hands flat.

"Sazare."

"Yes."

"Site rubble. Give me your figure for the ninth."

He had it because he always had it.

"Twelve," he said.

"Twelve."

"Twelve point one, on two hundred and nine articles, which is enough to
mean something."

"Forty-four in the fourth month," said Kanzaki. "Forty-one in the
fifth, thirty-nine in the sixth, thirty-eight and thirty-six across the
seventh and eighth, which is a normal drift for a man getting better at
a thing and I have been watching it come down all year and thinking
well done."

She turned the ninth month's book round.

"Twelve," she said. "In four weeks."

---

He gave her the whole of it and it took about nine minutes, and she
listened without interrupting once, which she did not do.

The take. A third over, every time, four thousand observations. The
grip — twice the effort on the hold, every medium, since he was seven.
Both of them invisible to him and immediately visible to a creature
sitting nine inches away who had assumed he knew.

"And the cause," said Kanzaki.

"No supervision. Ever." He said it flatly, because it was flat. "You
hold a thing as hard as you can when there's nobody to catch it, and
you take more than you need when nobody has ever told you what you
need, and after eleven years you can't feel that you're doing it,
because there has never been a minute when you weren't."

Kanzaki looked at the two books for a while.

"Right," she said. "Now the bad news, and it is a quartermaster's bad
news and you will find it small and I do not care."

"Go on."

"Your data is broken."

---

"Three years of it," she said, sitting down on the stool. "Three
hundred and eleven entries and then some, in two hands, against four
media, and every single figure in that press before the fourth of this
month was produced by a man doing it wrong in two specific ways that
have now stopped."

"...Yes."

"Which means it is not a series any more. It is two series with a wall
between them, and the wall is in the middle of a month, and everything
on the far side of it describes a process nobody is going to perform
again." She tapped the older book. "Your degradation curve. Fifty
measurements out to nine days. What was that curve measured on?"

Sazare put his hand over his mouth.

"Articles made at a third over," he said, "with twice the grip."

"So is it the curve, or was it *your* curve?"

"I don't know."

"No," said Kanzaki. "And you are going to have to run the whole of it
again to find out, and it is fifty measurements, and it took you eleven
weeks the first time."

---

She let him sit with that for about a minute and then gave him the
other one, which was worse and which she plainly did not enjoy giving
him.

"Eleven hundred and forty kilos," she said.

"I know the figure."

"That is what has gone through my books in three years and I have
signed every sheet of it. And what you have told me this afternoon is
that about a third of what you drew to make it was never used."

The yard went very quiet.

"It didn't go into the articles," said Kanzaki. "It didn't go into
stock. It didn't go anywhere I can account for, because you took it and
you didn't need it and — what did she say the word was?"

"It goes."

"It goes." She wrote something in the margin, which he could not see.
"So somewhere between three and four hundred kilos of a substance I
cannot name, whose provenance column reads *unknown* in ink in my own
handwriting three hundred and eleven times, was pulled out of
somewhere by a boy who did not know he was doing it, used for nothing,
and released."

"I don't know where it went."

"No. And I want it entered, and I want it entered in the ninth month
under your name and not mine, because I am seventy-two and I will not
be the last person to read these books." She closed the ledger. "That's
the entry. *Approx. one third of all material drawn 16y7m–18y9m taken
in excess of requirement and not used. Disposition unknown.* You can
word it better than that and you will, and it is going in."

"Kanzaki—"

"You want to tell me it is a small thing and that a third of eleven
hundred kilos of something that is in everything anyway is like
complaining about a bucket out of a river." She stood up. "You may well
be right. I have been a quartermaster for thirty-one years and I have
never once been allowed to be right about a thing because it was
probably fine. It goes in the book, and in forty years somebody with
better instruments than mine can take it out."

---

He saw Sakura on the south stair on the Friday.

He had not seen her properly in three weeks — she was on Ōkubo's list
and he had been on the second floor and then not on the second floor,
and the study had gone into its fourth month and the practitioners
reported to Yagi and not to him by design, so there was no reason at
all for the two of them to be in a room.

She was coming down with a chart under her arm and she stopped one step
above him, which put them at a height, and she looked entirely well.

"Ruri-san."

"Sakura."

"How's the hand?"

"Eighty-five. The last fifteen degrees of the index aren't coming
back." He turned it over without thinking about it. "It doesn't affect
the work and it takes me about a second longer to do a dressing, which
Emiko has noticed and has said nothing about, which I have also
noticed."

"She's like that."

"She is."

They talked for about four minutes and it was completely fine.

She had a thing about wound age she had taken to Ōkubo and Ōkubo had
taken to the board with her name on it, which she reported flatly and
which he could tell she had rehearsed reporting flatly. He had the
reject figures and gave her the short version. She asked after Katsuyu
and he said she was well and Sakura said *good* and meant it.

Nothing in it was wrong.

The satchel was over her shoulder the whole time and she did not open
it once.

---

He worked it out at the bottom of the stair and had the sense not to
turn round.

*Five months.*

*Since the second morning in the fifth month, on a landing at
twenty-nine minutes past seven, she has had that sheet out inside about
ninety seconds of every conversation we have had. Folded three times.
Carried in a satchel. Annotated down one margin in a hand that got
smaller towards the bottom because she ran out of room and refused to
start a second page, and then a second page anyway in the seventh
month.*

*Fifty-one conversations. I have the number because she gave me a score
in forty-four of them and I entered every one.*

*Four minutes on a stair, and it stayed in the bag.*

He stood at the bottom of the stair for a moment.

*She was pleasant. That is not a complaint and I want to be careful
about the word — she was pleasant in the way she is pleasant with a
consultant she does not know, which is a thing I have watched her do
about nine hundred times and admired.*

*The marking scheme was ours. It was the one thing in this building
that was ours, and she built it, and it existed because I could not do
a thing that everybody else can do, and she made it a game so that I
could be bad at it in front of somebody without it costing me
anything.*

*She has not taken it away. She has simply stopped bringing it out, and
if I ask her about it she will say something perfectly true about
being busy, and it will be perfectly true.*

*Kaburagi, in a linen room, on the seventeenth of the eighth month: the
ones who come and tell you straight have already decided to forgive
you. It's the ones who make it funny you've got a fortnight of work
with, and sometimes a year, and they will never once say a hard word to
you about it and you will never be entirely sure whether it is
finished.*

*It has been six weeks.*

He wrote it in the ledger that night, four lines, which for him was
almost nothing.

*Sakura, south stair, four minutes. Entirely pleasant. Hand, wound age,
Ōkubo, Katsuyu. Nothing wrong with any of it.*

*Sheet not produced. First time in fifty-one.*

*There is no move here. Asking about the sheet makes the sheet a thing
she has to explain, and she will explain it kindly and it will be over.*

*So: nothing, again, and this is the second time in five months I have
written that word about a person and meant something different by it
both times.*
