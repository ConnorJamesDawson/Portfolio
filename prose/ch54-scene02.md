# Chapter 54, Scene 2 — Thirty-Five Years

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's office. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1270 words.*

---

"Thirty-five years," he said.

"Thirty-five years."

"I've done the work on it."

"Of course you have."

"Do you want it?"

"Yes," said Tsunade, "I want it. I want to hear a boy who was born the
year I stopped being able to walk into a hospital do the work on
thirty-five years. Go on."

So he did.

---

"The objection has three parts and they're separable, which is the
first thing, because they get argued as one and they're not one.

"The first part is the difference in what we've each had. You've had
fifty-three years and I've had eighteen and about eleven of mine are
unusable for this purpose because I was a child for them, so it isn't
thirty-five, it's more like thirty-five against seven, and that's a
worse ratio than the number people say out loud.

"The second is the difference in what's in front of us. That's the one
everybody actually means. It's an actuarial statement and it's the only
part of this with numbers you can look up. Senju longevity is atypical
and the sannin figure is atypical inside that, and Jiraiya's fifty-four
and in the field, and your grandfather—"

"Careful."

"— and there is a published table and I've read it, and on the table
the difference between us at the point where it starts to matter is not
thirty-five years, it's between nine and fourteen, and I've got the
working."

"Have you."

"It's in the second drawer at home. I did it in the seventh month and I
did it twice because I got it wrong the first time and had you dying
before Shizune."

Tsunade put her hand over her mouth.

He did not know for about four seconds whether she was laughing or the
other thing, and then she took her hand away and she was neither, and
that was worse.

"And the third part," he said.

"Oh, do the third part."

"The third part is what it looks like, and that's the one that has no
numbers in it and it's the one that people actually run on, and the
honest answer is that I don't have a position on it that would satisfy
anybody, because—"

"Because you don't have a position on it at all."

"No," he said. "That isn't true."

---

"Isn't it." She came round the corner of the desk, and she was not
slow about it. "You've just stood a table up in front of me, Ruri.
You've done a longevity table. In your head, in this room, with a
figure in it for when I die."

"I did it at a bench. I didn't do it in—"

"Do you know what you've actually said to me?"

"I've said that the arithmetic people use is wrong by about twenty
years and it's wrong in a direction that—"

"You've said *the problem is smaller than you think it is*."

He stopped with his mouth open.

"Which," said Tsunade, "is the same thing you said to me in a gateway
in the tenth month, and I stood there for eleven seconds because I
couldn't work out how to tell you what it did without breaking you, and
I see you've written that one down as well and drawn absolutely no
conclusion from it whatsoever."

*Eleven seconds.*

*She counted them too.*

He had that, and it went through him sideways and did not come out
anywhere, and he sat and held it and could not use it for anything at
all.

---

"It's not a *smaller than you think*," he said, and heard how it
sounded, and kept going because he had nothing else. "It's a — the
objection assumes a shape of outcome and the shape isn't supported by
the—"

"Stop."

"—the reference class, and if the reference class is wrong then the
objection isn't wrong in strength, it's wrong in *kind*, and that
matters because—"

"Ruri."

"—because if it's wrong in kind then it isn't an objection I'm
overruling, it's one that was never—"

"*Ruri*."

He stopped.

She was about four feet away, which was where she had been in the last
scene of the worst afternoon of his year, and she had the cup in her
hand and she had not drunk from it since she picked it up.

"You want to know what thirty-five years is," she said.

"Yes."

"No, you don't. You want the objection. You've been handed an objection
and you've taken it away and dismantled it and brought me back the
pieces and you want a mark for it."

"That isn't—"

"Do you want to see it?"

---

He did not follow.

"Sorry?"

"The thirty-five years." She put the cup down on the corner of the
desk, and she put it down badly, and it rang. "You've read a table.
Nobody's ever shown you the object. You go on about the object all day
long, Ruri — put it on the felt, weigh it, don't talk to me about the
paper, where's the *thing*. Fine."

She lifted her hand.

"Do you want to see what you're actually asking for?"

And he understood, and the understanding arrived about half a second
before he could do anything about it, which was long enough to know
exactly what kind of moment it was and not long enough to be clever.

*This is not an offer.*

*This is the shape she puts up when she wants somebody to go away, and
it works because nobody has ever once said yes to it, and she has been
carrying it for thirty years and it has never been tested and she does
not know what it is.*

*And I have got about a second.*

He could have said no. He knew that afterwards for a long time and he
had it very clearly at the moment as well: *no* was on the table, *no*
was kind, *no* was what any person in the village would have said and
it would have cost him nothing and she would have taken it as the
answer and put her hand down and poured another one and they would have
gone on to the fourth thing.

*No is a lie.*

*She has asked me a question and the answer is yes and I have never
once said a thing I couldn't support.*

"Yes," said Sazare.

"What?"

"Yes. Go ahead."

---

Nothing happened.

He sat with his hands on his knees and looked at her, straight, the way
he looked at a joint under a lamp, without any face on it at all,
because he did not have a face to put on and had never learned one and
this was not the night to start.

"Go ahead," he said again, and it came out plainly, and it was not a
challenge, and that was the thing that did it. "I'd like to. I've asked
you for a thing and you're telling me I've asked without looking, and
that's a fair charge and I don't want it standing. So — yes. Please."

Her hand was still up.

"Ruri—"

"You said do you want to see it. I said yes."

"You don't know what you're—"

"That's your objection," he said. "That's the whole objection. That I
don't know what I'm asking for. So show me and then it's answered, one
way or the other, in about four seconds, and it's the only thing all
night that we can actually settle."

The lamp on the desk was doing what it did at that hour, which was
buzz, faintly, on the ninth day after it had been trimmed.

Her hand was still up.

It stayed up for a long time.
