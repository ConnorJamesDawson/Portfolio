# Chapter 61, Scene 7 — Exchange Three

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, T&I, and the north store. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2210 words.*

---

It came on the eleventh, through Ibiki, as everything did.

He read it in a room on the ground floor of the T and I building with a
man he had never been introduced to sitting at the other end of the
table, because that was the arrangement and had been since the fifth
month: he read it there first, once, in front of a witness, and then he
took the copy away and Ibiki kept the original.

Ibiki was not there.

"He's out," said the man at the other end of the table. "Fourth day."

"Where?"

The man looked at him.

"Fair enough," said Sazare, and opened it.

---

*To S. Ruri, Konohagakure General, fabrication —*

*or, as I am given to understand since the eleventh of last month,
Fabrication Research, Medical Corps, effective the first of the twelfth.
I hope you will not think it discourteous that I know. It is not
concealed and it was in a list that crossed my desk for other reasons,
and I would rather say I know it than pretend I do not and be caught in
about four lines.*

*Congratulations. I am not being ironic. A post created for one man over
seven objections is a rare thing and it is rarer still when the man did
not ask for it.*

*Now. Your second question.*

He read the second part twice on the first pass, which was a thing he
did not normally do in that room.

*You asked whether the three-day interval in the sixth-month cohort was
an artefact of the recording or a real feature.*

*It is real, and I can tell you why, and I do not think anybody in your
country can.*

And then eleven hundred words about it that were the best eleven hundred
words on the subject he had ever read.

---

He sat in a room in the T and I building and read a letter from an agent
of Otogakure that answered a question he had been unable to resolve in
fourteen months, completely, generously, with the working shown and two
places where the author said he was not certain and marked which.

*It is better than the study.*

*It is better than the study and the study cost four hundred hours and
eleven people and a rejection and a resubmission.*

*He has done it in eleven hundred words off the back of one question in
a letter and he has flagged his own uncertainties, which nobody in this
village does, and there are two figures in here that he can only have
got by doing something I do not want to think about.*

*And that is the third exchange and he has given it away in the first
half.*

---

The gift was on the fourth sheet and it was one paragraph.

*A separate matter, and you did not ask, and I have gone back and forth
about whether to put it in.*

*There is a Shōton user in the Land of Sound.*

*She is a woman of about thirty. She commands a facility. Her work is
not like yours — I have seen a great deal of it and it is combat work
and it is very fine and it does not last, and I do not believe she has
ever asked why it does not last, which I mention because I know that you
would have asked in the first fortnight.*

*I am not going to give you her name in a letter.*

*I am telling you because you believe you are the last one and you have
believed it for eight years, and I have known otherwise since before our
first exchange, and I have decided that continuing to sit on it while
accepting your letters is a thing I would rather not do.*

*You may make of that what you like. I am aware it is not free of
motive.*

---

He read that paragraph eleven times.

He knew it was eleven because he counted, from about the third, and he
went on counting because counting was what he had instead of anything
else and because the room had a man in it.

*Eight years.*

*Twelfth of the fourth month, out through the eastern lines at seven and
a half. And there was a period — it was about four months and it ended
in a shed in the ninth year — where I did not know, and then I knew, and
I have known since I was eight years old that there is nobody else.*

*It is in the front of the world bible. It is on the first page of the
file. It is the thing every single person in this village knows about me
before they know my name.*

*The last one.*

*I have built four hundred pages on the assumption. I have built a paper
on it. I have built the whole of the reparations claim on it and Kudō
has argued it in front of a determination board twice.*

*There is a woman of about thirty commanding a facility in the Land of
Sound.*

He put the sheet down flat on the table.

*And I have found out in a subordinate clause, in a letter, from a man
who works for the person who put wood into sixty children.*

*And he has told me because he decided that sitting on it while taking
my letters was a thing he would rather not do.*

*Which is either the most decent sentence anybody has written to me this
year or it is the most expensive one, and there is no version where it
is not both.*

---

The ask was on the fifth sheet and it was four lines long and it was not
about crystal.

*And now the third exchange, and the fee, which was agreed in writing in
the fifth month and which I have been careful to keep to.*

*I would like to understand how you work without seals.*

*Not the crystal. I have no interest in the crystal and I want to be
plain about that at the front, because you will assume otherwise and it
would poison everything after it.*

*I have read your splint study and your reject table and the four
Corps papers you are named on, and there is a thing in all four of them
and it is not in the results. It is in the method sections. You describe
operations in a way that nobody who works from a notation describes
them, and you have done it consistently since you were sixteen, and I do
not think you know you are doing it.*

*I would like the grammar.*

*I am not asking you to teach me anything. I am asking how it is
structured in your head, and I will take four paragraphs and be
grateful, and if the answer is that you cannot say then that is an
answer and I will take that too and the fee will be considered paid.*

---

He got back to the north store at about two and did not do any work for
the rest of the day.

He sat on the step of the cart door in the cold with the copy on his
knee and went through it.

*Right. The timing first, because it is the loudest thing and it is
probably the least important and I will not be able to think about
anything else until I have killed it.*

*The trial was the second. The weighing was twenty to one in the
morning.*

*This letter came through the gate on the tenth and Ibiki's office had
it on the tenth and I have got it on the eleventh.*

*Sound to here through the ordinary route is nine to eleven days and
Ibiki has told me twice that they do not hurry it because a letter that
moves faster than the traffic is a letter that has been noticed.*

*So it was written on about the thirtieth of the eleventh month.*

*Three days before the trial. Two days before anybody in this village
knew whether the chain would work at all — I did not know on the
thirtieth, Fukasaku did not know, and Jiraiya threw a chair at a wall on
the twenty-ninth.*

*It is clean.*

*It cannot be about the second and there is no version of the arithmetic
where it is.*

He sat with that.

*And I do not feel any better, and I have been sitting here for eleven
minutes waiting to and it has not come, and I would like to know why
not.*

---

He found it at about half past three.

*It is not the date. The date clears the trial and the trial is not what
is wrong.*

*It is the second sheet.*

He went back to it.

*Your second question. The three-day interval in the sixth-month cohort.*

*I asked that in the eleventh month. It went out on the ninth. It is a
medical question about a splint study and it is about tissue, and it is
the sort of question I have been asking him since the fifth month and he
has answered four of them.*

*And his answer runs eleven hundred words and about two hundred of them
are not about tissue at all.*

He put his finger on it.

*He has gone from the interval to the reason for the interval to a
question about why the interval is the same across three unrelated
materials, and then he has stopped, and he has stopped in exactly the
place a man stops when he has got somewhere he did not intend to go in
a letter.*

*The question underneath that paragraph is: why do these things all
fail at the same rate regardless of what they are made of.*

*That is my question. That is page eight. That is the six-day problem
and it took me four years and a rose and a hand at the carpus, and I
have never written one word of it to him.*

*He is three months behind me on the same road and he got there off a
splint study and four Corps papers and one question about a cohort.*

---

He sat on the step until it got dark.

*So. Two readings.*

*One: he derived it. Independently, off published material, because he
is extremely good and because the material is there for anybody who can
read it properly and nobody else has bothered.*

*That is entirely possible and I would say it is the likelier of the
two, and it is the one Ibiki will pick, and I am not certain he is
wrong.*

*Two: he has a source.*

*Not in that room — the room is six creatures and two of them are on a
mountain and I would put every one of the other four at zero. That is
not it and I am not going to let myself go there.*

*A source in the material. Somebody who has read my papers with the
right question in their head and told him what to look for, and there
are about eleven people in four countries who could do that and I could
name six.*

*And I cannot tell which, and I am not going to be able to tell, ever,
because the evidence is identical under both.*

He put the copy inside his coat.

*And the third thing, which is not a reading and is the actual finding
and I have had it since about two o'clock and I have been walking round
it.*

---

*He has a Shōton user.*

*A woman of about thirty, commanding a facility, whose work he has seen
a great deal of, and it is combat work and it is very fine.*

*And he is writing to a hospital fabricator four hundred miles away in a
hostile country, under a written arrangement, at nine to eleven days a
letter, and paying for it in advance with the best eleven hundred words
on soft-tissue intervals that anybody has written.*

*Why?*

*He has got one in the building.*

*If he wanted crystal he would have crystal. He does not want crystal —
he says so in the first line of the ask and I believe him, and the
reason I believe him is that he did not need to say it and saying it
costs him.*

*He wants the grammar.*

*And she cannot give it to him.*

He stood up off the step in the dark.

*She uses it. She has used it for thirty years and it is very fine and
she has never once asked why it does not last, and he put that in the
letter, and he put it in the letter for the same reason he puts
everything in a letter, which is that he does not waste a line.*

*He was not telling me about her.*

*He was telling me what she is not.*

*She is the best crystal user in the world and she cannot answer the
question, and he has looked at four Corps papers by an eighteen-year-old
in a hospital and worked out that the eighteen-year-old can.*

*That is not a compliment.*

*That is a man establishing which of the two of us is the one he needs.*
