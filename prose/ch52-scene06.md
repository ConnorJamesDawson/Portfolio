# Chapter 52, Scene 6 — The Comparison Set

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,497 words.*

---

It went on the last night of the tenth month and it went in the middle
of an ordinary sentence.

He was writing up the book — the accession card, the strike-off,
Ochiai's *it is not doing anything here*, the contents page read out in
a cold yard and the six reactions — and he got to the part about
chapter three and put down the thing he always put down, which was the
comparison, and it was:

*I have been unable to ask after anybody's affairs*

and then the clause that went after it, the one that had gone after
four hundred clauses like it, the one his hand produced without
consulting him.

It did not come.

He sat with the pencil on the page for about eleven seconds and it did
not come, and then he wrote it out deliberately, in full, to see what
was wrong with it:

*— for as long as I have been keeping a book, which is eleven years.*

He looked at the sentence.

*That is not a fact. That is a citation, and the source is bad.*

---

He worked it out over the next hour and it was not difficult and it was
extremely unpleasant.

*Kanzaki, at the reconcile, on the thirtieth of the ninth month, about
the ledger:*

*"It is not a series any more. It is two series with a wall between
them, and the wall is in the middle of a month, and everything on the
far side of it describes a process nobody is going to perform again."*

*She meant the reject figures. She meant three years of a man drawing a
third over and gripping at twice, and that every number in that press
before the fourth of the ninth month measures a fault and not a
capability, and that the degradation study is not the curve, it is my
curve, and I will have to run the whole of it again.*

*It is also true about me and I have not once applied it.*

He wrote it out properly, in order, because that was the only way he
could look at anything.

*1. I have used my own history as the comparison set for every
observation I have ever made. That is not a habit and it is not a
style. It is the method. When I want to know whether a thing is
unusual, I compare it to eleven years of me, because there has never
been anything else in the box.*

*2. That set was produced by a man working alone with two faults he
could not detect, in a room nobody else was ever in.*

*3. Ame: the grandchildren are better because the grandmother was in
the room. Which means my eleven years are not a baseline. They are a
measurement of an absence.*

*4. Therefore: comparing anything to them measures nothing. Every "for
eleven years" I have ever written is a citation to a broken series and
I have been citing it about nine times a week since I was nine years
old.*

He read that back and it was correct, and it was rigorous, and every
step of it followed, and he sat and looked at it and it did not account
for one per cent of how he felt.

---

He tried three more times to write the entry about the book and could
not, and at about one in the morning he stopped trying and wrote the
other thing instead.

*I want to state what is actually wrong, because the four steps above
are true and are not it, and I have watched myself put a correct
technical reason in front of an uncomfortable one four times this year
and I am not doing it a fifth.*

He stopped.

Then:

*"Since I was seven" is not a measurement.*

*I have known that for about three hours and I have been trying to get
round it since.*

*I was seven in a workshop in Kiri. Warashina had the bench under the
window and I had the stool and there was a graver I could not hold and
a knee that had gone in the spring, and it is the year I started, and I
have used it as the origin of everything I have ever counted.*

*My mother was alive. She was thirty-one and she had four years of
hospital shifts still to do and eleven of the ones after that. Kohaku
was three and had not started running yet.*

*They were alive until I was thirteen and a half.*

*Which means every single time I have written "since I was seven" I
have been drawing a line from now back to a house with three people in
it, and the line goes through the whole of the time they were alive,
and I have drawn it about four thousand times in eleven years and I
have never once known I was doing it.*

He put the pencil down.

*It is not a comparison set. It is the only continuous thing I have.*

*Everything else went. The house went and the workshop went and the
country went and the name went in a register in Kiri as *Cl. ext.* and
there is not one object in this room that was in that room, and the
last person alive who could say "you were like this at seven" was
executed in a yard when I was thirteen years and six months old.*

*Except the boy. The boy is still in the book. He is in every entry,
underneath, holding the far end of the line, and I put him there nine
times a week without deciding to, and the reason I cannot stop is not
that it is a habit.*

*It is that he was there.*

---

He sat on the floor with his back against the wall for a long time
after that.

*So the arithmetic is: the citation is bad and I have to stop using it,
and if I stop using it I stop putting him in the book.*

*And there is no third option, because the whole point of a bracket is
that you do not get to keep the reading and lose the evidence.*

He wrote one more line and then could not write anything else.

*I do not know what to compare things to.*

That was where it stopped.

He tried to go on and there was nothing to go on with — he had the
finding and he had the cost and he had no procedure whatsoever, and it
was the first time in eleven years he had written a fault report with
nothing in the second column.

---

He thought about it for four days.

*Who.*

*Kanzaki has the ledger half and it is her sentence I have borrowed and
she would give me twenty minutes and it would be about data.*

*Yagi would be kind and it would be four sentences and a warning.*

*Kaburagi told me to my face on the sixteenth that a man who hands a
document like that to a ward sister has either got it right or has
nobody, and I did not answer her.*

*Katsuyu is four hundred miles away with a book and will not be back
for a fortnight, and she has no history of her own and would not
understand the shape of the problem and would say so, honestly, and
apologise for it.*

*Sakura has not had the sheet out of the satchel since the twenty-ninth
of the ninth month.*

*Kurihara has forty stones waiting on her and a requisition with her
name on the personnel line and has not come.*

*Ashida would listen for two hours out of pure friendship and would be
bored inside four minutes and would not say so.*

*Handa is seventy-one and would be better at this than any of them and
would want to talk about it in a shed with a chisel in his hand, and he
has buried a wife and would know exactly what I meant, and I am going
to be honest and say that the reason I am not going to Handa is that
Handa is not who I want to tell.*

He looked at the list.

*There is one person.*

*She has not sent anything down that hill in seventeen days. There is
no business between us. I have got nothing to bring her — no article,
no finding, no protocol, no docket, nothing that could sit on a table
between two people and give either of us something to be looking at.*

*I would be going up there with a thing that has happened to me.*

*I have never done that. Not once, in three years, in about ninety
visits.*

He wrote the last line of the month at about two in the morning, in the
ordinary place, at the bottom, and it was six words long.

*Exercise 1: fifteen.*

*And I have no idea.*
