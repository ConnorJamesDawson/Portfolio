# Chapter 31, Scene 3 — Things Twelve-Year-Olds Do

*Act 4, part C. Konoha, the evaluation ground. Sazare Ruri, age
sixteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,512 words.*

---

Toriyama had aged eleven months and about four years.

Sazare had last seen him in a shed at the back of the quartermaster's
yard, running a capability assessment on a boy who was not allowed to
have any capabilities, and had put him down then as about thirty-five,
efficient, uninterested, and entirely fair. He was all four of those
things still. He had also lost about a stone, which showed at the
collar rather than the waist, and his flak jacket now sat on him the
way Ubukata's had — cut for a man who was no longer inside it.

*Rung one. The jacket doesn't fit.*

*Rung two. He hasn't had it taken in, and it's been long enough that
he could have.*

*Rung three—*

"Ruri," said Toriyama. "Stop looking at my jacket."

"Sorry."

"Don't be sorry, be quicker." He said it without any heat at all.
"Everybody who's been through the last year has one of these. Mine's a
jacket. Some of them are worse. Get in the square."

The square was chalked on packed earth, forty feet on a side, in a
corner of the evaluation ground where three other assessments were
running at the same distance from each other. Overcast, cold for the
month, with the wind coming off the training grounds carrying dust and
a smell of turned earth and, faintly, of the horse lines beyond the
fence. The chalk had been re-marked over an older square that didn't
quite align, so every line was doubled.

There were four people watching from the bench and Sazare read all of
them without deciding to.

**Toriyama**, running it, with a clipboard held in the crook of the
left arm and a pencil behind the ear rather than in the hand, which
meant he expected to write in bursts rather than continuously.

**A woman of about forty in a chūnin vest** with her hair in a scarf,
sitting with both feet flat and slightly apart and her hands loose on
her knees, entirely still — the stillness of somebody who has spent
years being able to move quickly and has stopped needing to
demonstrate it.

**A very young clerk**, maybe nineteen, with an ink stain on the
outside of the right middle finger, sitting forward.

**Kakashi**, thirty feet further back than any of them, against the
fence, reading.

---

He was bad at it.

He had known he would be and had not adequately imagined the texture of
it, which was that being bad at something in front of four people is
not one experience but about sixty consecutive small ones.

**Academy taijutsu forms, first set.** He had never been taught them.
He had been taught, at eleven, in a classroom in the fog, to name them
in order for a written paper, and had scored full marks, and had never
once seen anybody perform one. Toriyama demonstrated each in turn and
Sazare copied it, and what came out was recognisably the shape and
nothing else — a man doing an impression of a thing he had read about.

"Again. You're leading with the shoulder."

He led with the shoulder again.

"Again."

**Weapons.** Ten kunai at a board at twenty feet. He put four on the
board, none in the inner ring, and two of the six misses were wide
enough to be on the wrong side of the post. He knew exactly why —
he had thrown perhaps two hundred kunai in his life and about a
hundred and ninety of those had been in the last six weeks with
Kakashi standing behind him — and knowing why did not put them on the
board.

The young clerk wrote something down.

**Substitution.** He could do it. It took him a full second and a half
and Toriyama said "that's about four times long" without unkindness
and marked it.

**Transformation.** He could not do it at all. He had never held a
transformation for longer than the instant it collapsed, in nine years
of trying alone, because nobody had ever told him the thing about
maintaining rather than casting, and Kakashi had told him three weeks
ago and it had improved it from *impossible* to *four seconds and
visibly wrong*.

**The Clone Technique.**

Toriyama said the words, and Sazare made the seals he had been making
since he was eleven, and what appeared beside him was what had always
appeared beside him: a flat grey thing about his own height with no
detail below the chest, which existed for slightly under a second and
went out like a dropped lamp.

Nobody on the bench reacted. The young clerk wrote.

"That's a fail," said Toriyama. "Standard academy graduation
requirement, third of three. You want to try it again for the record
or shall I put it down as attempted?"

"Attempted."

"Attempted." He wrote it. "For what it's worth, that's the second most
common reason a candidate doesn't graduate at twelve, and the boy who
currently holds the village's worst recorded score on that particular
technique put a bridge in the Land of Waves out of business four months
ago."

"I know," said Sazare. "I was there."

Toriyama's pencil paused.

"Right," he said. "Yes. You were." He wrote something else. "Chakra
control. Leaf exercise, then the tree."

---

The control tests took nineteen minutes and reversed everything.

He held eleven leaves. The woman in the scarf sat up. He held them
through Toriyama walking round him, through a thrown stone, through
being asked a question and made to answer it, and he was still holding
eleven when Toriyama said "that's enough" in a different voice from the
one he had been using for the last hour.

The tree took him four attempts and he did it, badly, with the wrong
distribution and far too much output, and got twenty feet up a trunk
he had watched a twelve-year-old girl walk up in one go from sixty
yards away on a hill above Nami, eleven months and one entire life
ago.

He came down and did not say any of that.

"Ninety-four in control on the medical paper," Toriyama said, reading
off a sheet. "I'd assumed that was a medical-corps number. It isn't,
is it."

"I don't know what a medical-corps number is."

"It's a number that means good for a medic." He wrote. "That was not
one of those."

---

The last hour was the part that Sazare had not been braced for, because
nobody had told him it was on the list.

"Response to orders," Toriyama said. "Nomi will run it."

The woman in the scarf stood up. She was shorter than she had looked
sitting down and moved with her feet very close to the ground —
scarcely lifting them — and Sazare identified her gait as the one
Kakashi had spent six weeks trying to put into him and had described,
at one point, as *walking like you already know where the floor is*.

Her kunai pouch was on the left, low, with the strap deliberately
slack. The flap was worn evenly all over rather than in a band.

*Rung two. She doesn't draw from it the same way twice.*

"You'll get instructions," Nomi said. "Some of them will be stupid. Do
them anyway, and if you're going to object, object once, before you
start, not in the middle. Understood?"

"Yes."

They were stupid. He was told to move a stack of sandbags from one
corner of the square to another, and then back, and then to a third
corner. He was told to stand facing the fence and not turn round, and
left there for eleven minutes. He was told to describe the contents of
the square with his back still turned, which he did — fourteen
sandbags, a water butt with a split stave, the doubled chalk line, four
people, one of whom had moved — and Nomi said "the butt's got a split
stave?" and he said "third from the left, about nine inches, it's been
caulked with rag" and she went and looked.

He was told to carry a sandbag on his left shoulder, which put the
whole load through the repaired knee at exactly the angle Ubukata's
ceiling was written about.

He set it down.

"That one's outside my medical clearance," he said. "Left knee. I'll
carry it on the right, or I'll drag it, or I'll do something else."

Nomi looked at Toriyama. Toriyama wrote for slightly longer than the
sentence required.

"Objected once, before starting, with a reason," Nomi said. "Right
shoulder, then. Go on."

Afterwards, when it was finished and the young clerk was gathering
sheets, Sazare heard Toriyama say to Nomi, not quietly enough:

"That's the whole assessment, isn't it. Can't throw, can't clone, can't
copy a form, holds eleven leaves through a thrown rock, and knows the
number in his own medical file well enough to stop in front of four
strangers."

"He'll pass the afternoon or he won't," said Nomi.

"He'll pass the afternoon," Toriyama said. "That's what worries me
about him, and I've not worked out why yet."
