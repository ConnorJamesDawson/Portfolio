# Chapter 74, Scene 6 — The Thumb

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the north fields and the quartermaster's yard.
Sazare Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2,580 words.*

---

The Wednesday morning gave him back the two-feet band and charged the
usual for it, and at the end of it — the ground worse than Monday's,
his shoulder registering a complaint that would be Tsunade's problem
at noon — Jiraiya sat down on the fence rail and held up one hand with
the fingers spread.

Sazare stopped dead in the churn.

He had seen the hand before. Once, on the twenty-seventh of December,
behind the east bathhouse, with a rice ball in the other one: four
fingers counted off and named — *stone, seals, that sword, your own
two feet* — and the thumb folded down last and left there, and the old
man declining, cheerfully and completely, to say what was under it,
on the grounds that *you get worse when you are told, and this one you
are going to want to be told badly.*

He had thought about that thumb, at a conservative estimate, four
hundred times.

"Wednesday of the eleventh week," said Jiraiya. "Right. Sit down,
because I've been sitting on this since the twenty-seventh of December
and I have enjoyed every single day of it."

"Jiraiya-sama, I would like it on record that this has been—"

"Unbearable. I know. It was the point." He put the four fingers down
one at a time. "Stone. Seals. The sword. Your own two feet. Four
layers, one principle, take options away." The thumb came up. "Fifth.

"Wood."

The field did not change. Sazare stood in it and felt something
reorganise itself very quietly, the way a load finds a member you had
drawn but never sized.

"It is not a fifth range," he said.

"No it isn't. Say why."

"Because the other four are ranges and this one is not. It runs
*underneath* all four." He was seeing it, and it was arriving whole,
which was what always happened when a thing had been true for a long
time before anybody said it. "A vine on an ankle at twenty feet costs
me nothing. A branch that swats at forty is in the hole where I have
nothing — it is the only article I can put past forty feet at any
price. And under five, in the sword's band, a root that shifts an
inch under a man's back foot while he is committing—"

"—is worth more than the sword," said Jiraiya, "and costs about
nothing, and cannot be blocked, and nobody in the five nations
watches the floor. There it is. That's why it's the thumb. The
fingers are what you *do*. The thumb is what makes the hand a hand."

"And it has been available to me since the fourteenth of the tenth
month."

"It's been available to you since you were about nine. Vegetation is
already there, kid; you don't make it, you *ask* it, and asking things
that are already there is the entire trade you've had since before
anybody trained you." Jiraiya scratched his jaw. "So why'd I sit on it
for three months?"

"Because I would have gone at it."

"Because you'd have gone at it *from the wrong end*. Same reason I
sat on the creed." He got off the rail. "The wood layer is the
cheapest thing in your whole kit and it has a ceiling on it that is
going to make you insane, and I wanted the man who found that ceiling
to be a man who'd already had a governor taken off him and knew what
that felt like. A boy with a creed in the front of four books finds
that ceiling and files it under *fine, that's the limit, limits are
interesting.*"

*He is correct,* thought Sazare, and did not say so, because his
mouth had gone dry.

"And on Monday you took the governor off."

"On Monday I took the governor off." Jiraiya picked up the practice
swords. "Say the ceiling out loud, then. You've had it since the
fourteenth of the tenth. Say it."

"I can move wood," said Sazare. "I cannot make it.

"I match finished articles. That is the whole method, it has been the
whole method since I was seven, and it is why nobody can teach me and
why I have never needed anybody to. To make a thing I have to have
held a made one — not a *natural* one; a natural one is a different
object. A tree is not made wood. A tree is grown wood, it took forty
years, it has a history in it and no author, and I can no more match a
tree to make wood than I can match a seam of quartz to lay a graded
course.

"I made wood once, at nineteen minutes past nine on the fourteenth of
the tenth month, on a bench, with a Mokuton rose nine days old and
still saturated sitting four inches from my hand. It worked because
the reference was in the room. Take the reference out of the room and
there is no operation. There has never been an operation. There is
only ever a man matching something."

"And?"

"And there is exactly one source of made wood on this continent."

He heard his own voice do it — heard the sentence arrive at its
destination with the specific flat finality of a survey line closing
on a wall — and Jiraiya, who had been waiting three months, watched
him hear it, and did not help.

"Mm," said Jiraiya. "There it is. Go and have your noon."

---

He did not go and have his noon. He went and had his noon, because the
noon was hers and the noon was not negotiable and he had eaten two
mouthfuls under duress on Monday and was not going to make her do that
twice in a week; and he was in the yard at two, and he did not
remember a single thing anybody had said in between, which he entered
afterwards as a data point about himself that he did not enjoy.

The yard was at working strength. Second-run trays racked. The kettle
at temperature since half past one, as it had been every day since the
second month, by a mechanism nobody had ever discussed.

Tenzō was at his eight feet, reading a supply manifest.

*Right,* thought Sazare. *Audit. Properly, in order, on paper, the way
you would run it if the subject were somebody else's problem. And
begin by writing down the thing you want, because a want you have not
written down runs the audit from underneath.*

He got the notebook out and wrote it down.

*I want the fifth layer.*

*Statement of the want, in full and without decoration: I want to be
able to make wood in a field, at speed, without a bench and without a
reference in the room, and I want it because it is the cheapest
article in my kit and the only one that reaches past forty feet, and
because a root that moves an inch under a committing foot is worth
more than everything else I own put together, and because in eleven
months somebody is going to come for what is in that yard and I would
like the ground to be on my side all the way out to the wall.*

*And I want it for me. Not for the line. Not for the road. There is no
docket. It is the first thing in my life I have ever wanted for the
purpose of being harder to kill, and I was permitted to want it at
eleven minutes past seven on Monday morning, and I have had the
permission for two days and this is what it has produced.*

He looked at that for a while.

*Good. Now the ask.*

*The ask is not — and this is the trap, and I nearly walked into it in
the lane — the ask is not "let me examine you." I settled that on the
sixth of the eighth month at a table in his lodging and I have never
once been tempted since: I do not ask that man where he got it. That
question stays shut for life.*

*The ask is smaller. The ask is: make things. Ordinary things.
Regularly, for about a year, on no particular schedule, and let me
hold them while they are fresh. Nothing about his body. Nothing about
the procedure. Nothing about the sixty. A commission, like any other
commission, of the kind I place with Handa-san eleven times a year.*

*And it passes my own rule. That is the fact I want minuted, because
it is why the audit is necessary: the ask is fully compliant with the
line I drew in the eighth month and it is still wrong, and if I do not
find out why in the next ten minutes I am going to do it.*

He found it in four.

He found it, and it went through him like cold water, and he sat down
on the bench end for the second time in two days.

*Because that is precisely, exactly, and in every particular what the
shop did.*

*Root never asked him what he was. Nobody in that building ever
required him to disclose one thing about the procedure, because they
already knew, they had done it. What they required was OUTPUT. A man
who makes wood, making wood, on order, at intervals, for somebody
else's programme, indefinitely, and never once asked whether the
making was anything to him.*

*He told me the shape of it himself in this yard in January, off duty,
with a bag of chestnuts, and I wrote it in the book that night: they
started from the man they wanted and worked backwards.*

*And a commission is a specification, and a specification is a man
standing at the front of the article holding the order, and I have
spent three years writing about what it does to a person to be the
place where a technique is not — and I was about to place a standing
order with the only human being alive who was manufactured.*

He wrote the second leg underneath it and it was worse.

*He cannot refuse me.*

*Not "would find it difficult." Cannot. He is attached to this yard by
an order with her signature on it. His remit is three lines and one of
them is that he is able to refuse a live working on safety grounds and
that is the whole of his authority in this place. There is no clause in
that document under which the man watched can decline a request from
the man he is watching, because nobody drafting it imagined the traffic
running that way.*

*So any ask I make of him is made from inside a power relation that I
did not build and cannot step out of by being nice about it. I could
give him every exit in the world and the exits would be mine to give,
which is not the same object as an exit and I know it is not, because
I have spent four months writing a document about what it means when
the gap between what you take and what would have been given is
zero.*

*Third leg, and this is the one I would like on the record in my own
hand, because it is the one I would have missed a year ago:*

*The rose was a gift. He made it once, unasked, for a thesis that
belonged to a hospital, and he chose it, and it cost him something to
choose and he chose it anyway. If I now convert that gift into a
supply — if the thing he did once, freely, becomes the thing he does
on Tuesdays because the fabricator's style needs it — then I have
taken the single act of free making in that man's adult life and
turned it into a requisition.*

*I would not do that to a stranger. I have him at eight feet five
days a week and he brings me chestnuts on the bad evenings and has
never once mentioned it.*

*Finding: no.*

He sat with it.

And the thing that arrived then was not relief. It was the other
thing, and he made himself hold still and read it accurately, because
the accuracy was the whole of what he had:

*It is a real no and it has a real price and the price is the fifth
layer.*

*Not delayed. Not deferred to a better year. There is no other source.
Tenzō is the only made-wood article on this continent that I could
ever hold fresh, and I am not going to ask him for it, this year or
any year, and therefore the thumb stays folded — for me, permanently,
by my own decision, made on a bench on the eighteenth of the third
month at about twenty past two in the afternoon.*

*Jiraiya-sama built me a style with five layers and I have just, on
purpose, cold, with no hesitation at all, thrown one of them away.*

*And I found the decision in four minutes.*

*Both directions, he said. The no is the same muscle as the yes.*

*I did not understand that sentence in January. I understood it as
encouragement. It is not encouragement. It is a specification, and I
have just built to it, and it cost me the cheapest and best article
in my kit, and I would do it again in four minutes.*

---

He put the pen down and looked across eight feet of frost-bleached
yard at a mild man reading a manifest.

*And now the hard part,* he thought, *which is that I cannot let this
be a thing I decided about him without him.*

*Because that is also the shop. Weighing a man in a room he is not in
and delivering him the result as weather. He said it about the file
he came with — that in that building you never found out you had been
assessed; you found out from what happened to you afterwards.*

*I have just spent eleven minutes assessing him. He watched me do it
from eight feet with a manifest in his hands and he will have logged
every second of it, dateless, factual, interpretation withheld, and
he will never ask.*

*So he gets told. All of it. The want, the ask, the three legs, and
the answer. Out loud, to his face, today, with nothing softened —
which is a horrible thing to do to a man and is the only honest thing
available, and I am aware that "it is the only honest thing available"
has been my entire defence in every difficult room of my life and that
one day it is going to be wrong.*

*Not today. Today it is correct. Go.*

He crossed the eight feet, which he had not done, in ten weeks, for
anything except work.

"Tenzō-san," he said. "I need eleven minutes and I need you to have
an exit, and I do not know how to build you one that is not mine to
give, so I am going to say that out loud too and then begin.

"I have just decided not to ask you for something. You were the
subject of the decision, you were not in the room, and I am not
prepared to let that stand.

"So I am going to tell you what I wanted."
