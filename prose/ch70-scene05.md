# Chapter 70, Scene 5 — There Is No Form

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Shizune, age
thirty-one. POV: Shizune. ~880 words.*

---

Shizune had been keeping her own file for eleven days before she
decided to act on it, because eleven days was her standing
threshold for the difference between a mood and a condition, and
Wednesday was day eleven.

The file was small and it was damning and it was assembled
entirely from diary requests, because everything in this tower
eventually became a diary request, which was why the person who
ran the diary knew everything and said almost nothing, which was
the job.

*Item: three requests in nine days to know whether the design
board could move from afternoons to mornings. The board cannot
move to mornings. The mornings are the field. She knows the
mornings are the field. She signed the field.*

*Item: a query, framed as idle, about whether the second-run
fabrication ceiling could be met with reduced yard hours. It
could not. She knew it could not. The query was not a query. It
was a woman shaking a locked door to hear it rattle.*

*Item: the noon tray. She has started ordering for it
personally. Specifying it. Last Thursday she sent the first one
back — the FIRST tray in three years to be sent back — because
the tea was the afternoon blend and, quote, he only gets twenty
minutes and he is not spending them on the wrong tea.*

*Item: Tonton will no longer sit in the small office in the
evenings. Tonton has relocated to the stair landing. Tonton's
clinical instincts remain the finest in this building and
Tonton does not sit with brooding.*

She took the file in at seven on the Wednesday evening, with
tea, the right blend, and shut the door.

"I'm not here as the diary," she said. "Or the assistant. I'm
here as the other thing, and I'm invoking it, and you get no
say, that's how the other thing works."

Tsunade looked at her across the desk for a long moment, and
then sat back, and rubbed both hands down her face, and said,
through them, with the weariness of a woman surrendering a
fortress she had known perfectly well was indefensible:

"How long have you known."

"Eleven days by the file. Longer by the tea." Shizune sat, and
poured, and did not consult any notes, because you did not
bring notes to this. "Say it anyway. Out loud, once, to a
person. You've been saying it to a register — don't look at
me like that, I've watched you write in it at one in the
morning for three weeks; I run your lamp oil requisitions too."

There was a silence.

"I miss him," said the Hokage of the Land of Fire, "and he is
two floors down, and I have never once in my life been lonely
in this particular direction, and I don't know what to *do*
with it, and before you say anything: I have checked, and
there is no form."

"There's no form," Shizune agreed. "I checked as well. Years
ago. For someone else." She let that sit exactly as long as
it needed to and not one second longer, and then she put the
cup down and did the part she had come to do. "Two things.
First: I can fix the mechanics tonight. You know I can. The
docket bends, the board reschedules, nobody ever knows the
weather changed. Say the word."

"No."

"I know," said Shizune. "I put it first so you'd get to say
no to it out loud and hear yourself mean it, because you've
been saying no to it silently for three weeks and silent
no's don't hold, they just recirculate. Minuted: offered,
declined, correctly." She refilled the cup. "Second thing,
and then I'll take Tonton and go."

She had thought about how to say the second thing for most of
day ten and eleven, and had settled, in the end, on the
plainest version, because the plainest version was the one
with three years of standing behind it.

"You spent two months teaching that boy that wanting things
out loud is survivable," said Shizune. "You watched him learn
to ask — for the bath, for the day, for the Thursday squid,
for a market with no second part. Every one of them nearly
killed him and every one of them he asked anyway, and you
received every single ask like it was treasure, because it
was. He built the asking muscle from nothing, at eighteen,
with a nosebleed." She stood, and collected the tray, and
delivered the rest from the door, in the flat administrative
voice, which had always been where she kept her love. "You're
fifty-three, and yours has been in a sling since before I met
you. He goes to a field four mornings a week and finds out
what he can't do yet, on purpose, in the frost, in front of a
legend. It's Wednesday. Your field's two floors down, asleep
in his coat at a bench. *Ask.*"

The door was almost shut when the voice came, low, from the
desk.

"When did you get like this."

"Three years of watching the two of you take turns being
brave," said Shizune. "It's very instructional. Goodnight,
Tsunade-sama. The tea tomorrow will be the morning blend, and
I've moved your first appointment to ten, and I have not
minuted why."
