# Chapter 54, Scene 4 — Ask Me Again

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's office. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1330 words.*

---

The quiet went on for a while and neither of them did anything about
it.

He had never sat in a room with a person and not filled it. He had a
sense, sitting there with his hands on his knees and nothing coming out
of him, that he was doing something extremely expensive and that he did
not know the price yet.

Tsunade poured.

"Ask me again in a year," she said.

---

He did not answer straight away.

That was not restraint; it was that the sentence had gone into him and
started doing things and he wanted to see what they were before he
opened his mouth.

*That is a good sentence.*

*It is a very good sentence. It is not a no. It costs her nothing
tonight and it moves the whole thing outside the room, and if I take it
we both get to go home, and she knows I want to go home, and she knows
that better than I do.*

*And she has said it to somebody before. That is a sentence with
handling marks on it.*

"All right," he said.

"All right?"

"The twenty-second of the eighth month, next year. That's a Tuesday. If
you're in the village I'll come at the end of the day and if you're not
I'll come the first day you're back."

Tsunade stared at him.

"You're not serious."

"You said ask me again in a year."

"It's a—" She put the cup down. "Ruri, it's a *form of words*."

"I know it's a form of words."

"Then why in God's name have you just given me a date?"

"Because I'll be there," said Sazare.

---

Something went across her face that he could not name at the time and
could not name afterwards either, and he wrote it down that night in
four words and none of them were right.

"That's not — " She stopped and started again. "That's not what it's
for. It's for putting a thing down. You take it, and the year goes, and
by the time the year's gone the whole thing's smaller and you've met
somebody your own age and I've got a war on and neither of us ever
mentions it and it *dies quietly*, which is the kindest available
outcome and you have just walked straight through the middle of it with
a diary."

"Yes."

"Don't say *yes* like—"

"I'm agreeing with you," he said. "That's what it's for. I know that's
what it's for. I'm telling you it won't work on me, and I'm telling you
before you spend a year on it, because you spending a year on a thing
that won't work is worse for you than me being difficult tonight."

"It won't work on you."

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because a year isn't a long time," said Sazare. "I've got books that
took four. I waited eleven weeks to open a page in my own notebook and
the only thing that changed was that I got better at not opening it. If
you give me a year I'll do the year properly. I'll work, and I won't
raise it, and I won't be strange about it, and I won't be at your door,
and on the twenty-second of the eighth month I will come up the stairs
and ask you the same question in the same words, and I'll have a year
of not raising it behind me and you'll have a year of me not raising
it, and it will be worse for both of us than tonight is."

She was looking at the window.

"So I'm not going to take it," he said. "Not because I'm refusing you.
Because it's a bad instrument and you'd be using it on yourself."

---

She got up.

She took the bottle with her, which she had not done before, and she
went to the window at the end of the room and stood side-on to it with
her shoulder against the frame, and outside there was the village at
about half eleven at night with four lights on in the hospital and the
rest of it dark.

"You think you're being kind," she said.

"No."

"You think it's honest, then."

"I think it's the only thing I've got that's true."

"It's the same thing, with you." She drank out of the cup, standing.
"That's the bit nobody knows about you, Ruri. Everybody thinks you're
cold. You're not cold, you're just — there's nothing between the inside
of your head and the room. There's no *filter*. Whatever's true goes
straight out of you at whatever velocity it happens to have."

"Is that bad?"

"It's going to get you killed."

"Probably."

"Don't say *probably* like that either."

---

He sat and looked at her back and worked, because there was one thing
left that he had not done and it had been sitting there for about a
quarter of an hour.

*She has given me three objections she can defend and one she won't
say, and I have been trying to get at the one she won't say from the
outside, by elimination, which is how you find a fault in a lattice.*

*She is not a lattice.*

*And I have been asking the wrong question all night, which I know now
because she asked it first and I answered a different one.*

*Why me is not a selection question.*

He did not have the whole of it. He had about a third of it, and the
third he had was enough to know that the rest of it was not his to open
and would come out or not out of her.

So he took the only part he could stand on.

"Can I say the thing I think it actually is," he said, "and you don't
have to answer it."

She did not turn round.

"Go on."

"The question isn't *when*," said Sazare. "Everything I've done for
forty minutes has been about *when*, and the year is about *when*, and
the table's about when, and the four years until you stand down is
about when. And none of it's the question."

"Then what's the question."

"Whether you can do it again."

The bottle stopped on its way up.

"That's not mine," he said. "I want to be clear that I know that. It's
not a thing I can take apart and it's not a thing anybody gets to have
an opinion about from a chair, and I've got no data on it and I'm not
going to pretend I have. It's yours. It's the only bit of the whole of
this that's completely yours and I can't touch it."

She said nothing at all.

The lamp buzzed.

He had been in that office something over four hundred times and he had
never once heard the lamp from the chair.

---

"I've got one more thing," he said, "and then I'll go, and I mean it
this time."

"Say it."

"I said it in a gateway in the tenth month and you stood there for
eleven seconds and I've never worked out what the eleven seconds were,
and I've decided I'm not going to work it out, because I've had eleven
weeks at it and it's the only figure I own that hasn't produced
anything."

He stood up.

"I'll support you either way, Hime."

---

He heard her breathe in.

It was not a long breath and it was not a dramatic one; it went in and
it caught about two-thirds of the way, on nothing, the way a breath
catches when the ribs are doing something the person has not sanctioned.

He knew that sound. He had heard it about six hundred times, standing
at the head of a bed with two fingers on a wrist, and it did not mean
anything good and it had never once meant that the person was about to
be all right.

And then she turned round.
