# Chapter 61, Scene 9 — Unknown

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the hospital, the north store. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1910 words.*

---

He went back down to the store at about ten and lit the lamp and sat at
the new bench with two sheets of paper in front of him.

The left one was the reply.

The right one was a piece of the pressed leaf they had sent the review
on, because there had been three sheets spare and it had seemed
disrespectful to use it for anything ordinary, and because he had known
since the twenty-fourth of the eleventh month exactly what he was going
to use it for and had not been able to make himself start.

He sat there for about forty minutes and did not write anything on
either.

---

*They are the same question.*

He got that at about a quarter to eleven and it did not come sideways;
it came the way a fault comes when you have been holding a thing up to a
lamp for long enough.

*Fifth sheet: I would like to understand how you work without seals. How
it is structured in your head.*

*Question six: you have asked us two hundred and six questions about how
it works and not one about what it is for.*

*Those look like opposite questions and they are the same one, and the
thing they have got in common is that both of them are asking* me *to
state something that has never been mine to state.*

He put the pencil down and did it properly.

*Every single thing I have ever made has had somebody standing at the
front of it with the load already written down.*

*Yagi hands me a patient. That is a specification — it says what the
article has to survive and for how long and what it does to a person if
it fails, and it is a better specification than I could write and it
comes with an argument attached.*

*Kanzaki hands me a quantity and a grade. That is a specification.*

*The Corps board hands me a quota. The study handed me a question.
Ibiki handed me a fee and a form of words and both were in writing.*

*Even the exercise. Katsuyu handed me a count and I put it in a column
for eleven weeks without once asking what the number was for.*

*I have never in my life had to say what a thing was for.*

*I have said, out loud, in a room, that I am the man who takes the
object off the table and asks for the rest of it — no. She does that.
She does that to me and I wrote three pages about it and it is the third
of the three reasons and it is the only one I could not have got at a
bench on my own.*

*And nobody has ever done it about the thing at the front.*

---

He did the letter first because it was the smaller one.

That was a decision and he knew what kind of decision it was, and he
made it anyway at eleven o'clock at night in an empty dye-works.

*Draft one.*

*I cannot say.*

He looked at it.

*Right. Take it apart before you write another word, because if you let
that stand without doing the work you will be a different man by
Thursday.*

*Is it true?*

*It has been true since I was sixteen. I could not say it to Jiraiya on
the eighteenth of the eleventh month and I could not say it to two
mountains on the twenty-eighth, and I stood in that room and told them
what I had to hand anybody was the place where a technique is not, and
it was the honest position and everybody in there agreed with it.*

*And on the second of the twelfth month, at about eleven in the morning,
a toad's wife put her foot on a table and told me I had been wrong about
it for a fortnight.*

*An absence is not what moves. A removal is. And a removal is a positive
operation with a before and an after.*

*So there is an answer. It is not the answer he is expecting and it is
better than the answer he is expecting, and it is four paragraphs, and I
could write it in about eleven minutes and it would be completely true
and he would understand every word of it.*

*And it is the second of the twelfth month.*

He sat back on the stool.

*Now say what* I cannot say *is.*

*It is a true proposition. I am not permitted to say it. That is a fact
about the world as of two o'clock this afternoon in an office, and if
anybody put me under oath I would be able to defend the sentence
word for word.*

*And it is designed to produce a belief in him that I know to be false.*

*He will read it and he will conclude that I have got nothing — that the
thing in my head is not describable, that I have looked and found no
grammar, and that the road is shut.*

*And it is not shut. It is open. It was opened nine days ago on the
floor of a room in a tower by a woman who has been dead about four
hundred years' worth of arguments and did it with her foot on a table.*

*And he will stop asking about it.*

*Which is the whole of the reason I am going to write it.*

---

He sat in the north store for about half an hour and did not move.

*That is a lie.*

*I want to have it in the right words before I do it, because I am going
to do it, and afterwards I would rather have the accurate entry than a
comfortable one.*

*It is not a false statement. I have never made one of those and I am
still not going to. Every word of that sentence is defensible and I
would defend it in front of Ibiki tomorrow morning.*

*It is a true sentence deployed to produce a false belief.*

*And I have spent eleven years telling myself that the reason I do not
lie is that I cannot construct one, and that is not true either and I
have known it was not true since about the ninth month. I can construct
anything. I have constructed nine hundred articles and eleven pages and
a chain along four creatures.*

*The reason I do not lie is that I have never once needed to badly
enough.*

*And tonight I do.*

He picked the pencil back up.

---

He wrote three sentences.

*Your first two sheets are the best work anybody has sent me and I am
going to be using the sixth-month material for years. Thank you. I have
put the two flagged uncertainties into my own notes as flagged, which is
what you would want.*

*On the fourth sheet: I do not have a way of thanking you for that which
would not be worse than saying nothing, so I am going to say nothing
except that I read it eleven times and that you were right that I would
have asked in the first fortnight.*

*On the fifth sheet: I cannot say.*

He read it back four times.

Then he added one more line, because he could not stop himself and
because he had decided at about the third reading that he was allowed
exactly one.

*I want it on the record between us that I have thought about it
properly and that this is not a refusal made in four minutes.*

He put the pencil down.

*And that is true as well.*

*And it is there so that he will believe the rest of it, and I have put
it there for that reason, and I have just watched myself do it.*

---

He wrote the entry for himself afterwards, in his own book, at the
bench, and he did not soften it.

*11/12. Exchange three answered. Reply as attached copy.*

*I have written a true sentence in order to produce a false belief. It
is the first time. It took four hours and I did it deliberately and I am
not going to write down that I had no choice, because I had two and I
picked this one.*

*It is the correct decision. I would make it again tomorrow. I have
checked it against the alternative eleven times and the alternative is a
letter that puts the second of the twelfth month into the Land of Sound
inside nine days.*

*Recording it here because a man who does that and does not write it
down is a different man from a man who does it and does.*

*I would like to know how many of these there are before it stops being
worth writing.*

---

Then he pulled the leaf across.

It was the last thing and it was the one he had been sitting in that
room for two hours in order to avoid, and it was one line long and he
had known what it was going to have to be since about the twenty-fourth
of the eleventh month, standing in his own room, reading eleven sheets
by a lamp.

*Instrument. Nineteen-inch graded core, hilt permanent, blade drawn.
203 hours. Completed 4/11. Reviewed Shikkotsu 24/11.*

*Specification:*

He sat with the pencil above the line for a long time.

*Say what it is for.*

*It is a weapon. That is not a specification, that is a category, and I
would reject it off anybody else in about four seconds.*

*A specification says what the load is. Who is at the other end of it,
in what circumstance, at whose instruction, and what happens to that
person.*

*I have not got one.*

*I have not got one and I have had seven months and eleven sheets of
leaf telling me I have not got one, and I have had the best week of my
life and a woman told me I made history and I still have not got one,
and everything downstream of a missing specification is a guess that
happened to work.*

*So write what you have got.*

---

He wrote one word.

*unknown*

He looked at it.

*Three hundred and eleven.*

*Three hundred and eleven of those in the burial register in the Land of
Water, in ink, and a woman of seventy-two is filling them in in her own
evenings at the end of the day with a lamp she denies having put out.*

*And I have got about four hundred of them across fourteen books,
because I would rather put unknown in ink than write down a thing I
cannot stand behind, and it is the oldest rule I have got and I made it
at about nine years old on my own on a floor.*

*This is the first time I have put one on the front of something instead
of in the middle of it.*

He put the leaf on the shelf above the bench where he could see it, and
did not roll it up, because rolling it up would have been putting it
away.

Then he put the lamp out and locked the double doors and went up the
hill in the dark, and the thing on his hip sang the whole way, quietly,
four voices, seated, clean, at a hundred and seventy-one hours and no
degradation of any kind.
