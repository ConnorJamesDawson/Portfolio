# Chapter 62, Scene 3 — Four Seconds

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the road up to the tower. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1580 words.*

---

Naruto raised it on the hill, about halfway up, with the handcart between
them and Sakura eleven feet ahead carrying two blankets and a lamp and
refusing to give any of it up.

He raised it the way he did everything, which was without any preamble
at all.

"I had the same amount of stuff."

"Sorry?"

"When I got my place. I was twelve — no, I was younger, I was about
eight when they gave me the apartment, and Iruka-sensei came round on the
first night and looked at it and I could tell he was doing a thing with
his face."

Sazare pulled the cart.

"I had a futon," said Naruto, "and a kettle, and about four bowls
because they came with it, and a poster.

"That was it. And that was *four years* in, not the first week. Four
years in the same room and there was a futon and a kettle and a poster
and it never got any more than that, and I couldn't work out why, because
I had money — I had the orphan money, it wasn't much but it was money,
and I *bought* stuff sometimes and then a year later there'd still be
nothing in there."

---

Sazare stopped pulling.

He did not decide to. The cart went about another eight inches and
stopped and he stood in the middle of the road on the hill up to the
tower with the shaft in his hands.

"Say the rest of it," he said.

"There isn't a rest of it, I just—"

"There's a rest of it. You've worked out why."

Naruto scratched the back of his head.

"I worked it out on the trip," he said. "Not, like, sat down and worked
it out. Ero-sennin had us in about forty inns in two years and after a
bit I noticed I could tell in about four seconds which rooms had somebody
in them and which ones were just — rooms.

"And it isn't the amount of stuff."

"No."

"It's *who put it there*."

He took a hand off the shaft and turned it over.

"Everything in a room comes off a person. That's it, that's the whole
thing. Somebody gives you a thing and you keep it because they gave it
to you and it's got them in it, or you buy a thing and you buy it because
you're thinking about somebody looking at it, even if it's just you
looking at it later and being pleased.

"You don't fill a room for *you*.

"You fill it for somebody who's going to come in."

---

Sazare stood on the hill and did not say anything for some seconds.

"And nobody came in," he said.

"Nobody came in."

"So there was nothing to buy anything for."

"Yeah," said Naruto. "And you don't know you're doing it. That's the bit
that got me. It's not like I sat there thinking *nobody's coming so I
won't get a chair* — I just never got a chair, for four years, and there
was room for one, and I had the money, and it never once occurred to me
that a chair was a thing a person could have."

He looked at the box on the cart.

"You've got books, though."

"I've got books."

"Loads of books. Like, that's a lot for a room with nothing in it, so
that's the thing you did instead, right? Everyone does a thing instead. I
did ramen and I did the pranks and Sakura-chan thinks the pranks were
about attention and they *were*, I'm not stupid, but they were also—"

He stopped and evidently decided not to finish that one, which was, as
far as Sazare could tell, the first thing all morning he had decided not
to finish.

"Anyway," said Naruto. "Books. That's yours."

---

*Right.*

*Say it to myself first, in order, and do not say any of it out loud on
a hill.*

*I did the inventory at eight o'clock this morning and I did it properly
and I got to the end of it and the finding I wrote down was* everything I
own does a job*.*

*And I was pleased with that. I want to be honest — I was pleased with
it, I thought it was clean, I thought it was a nice hard finding about
a man who has never been sentimental about an object in his life.*

*It is not a finding. It is a symptom and I have written it down as a
virtue.*

*A room fills up because people come into it.*

*Ōtaki Sue has never been up that stair. She is seventy-three and it is a
stair. Kanzaki has never been up it. Yagi has not, Ashida has not, Emiko
has not, Ibiki has certainly had somebody up it and it will not have been
socially.*

*Sakura has never been in that room until this morning.*

*Katsuyu has never been in it, because I go to the yard.*

*Hime has never been in it.*

*In two years and three months, in a village of eleven thousand people,
with a street downstairs that gave me a bowl and a blanket and a slate,
nobody has come up those stairs.*

*Not because I was excluded. I want that accurate. Nobody has been kept
out. There was no barrier and there was no unkindness and the whole of it
is that I never asked one single person, ever, and it did not occur to
me that asking was a category.*

*And this one worked it out at eight years old in a room with a poster
in it and has just handed it to me in about ninety seconds on a hill.*

---

"Ruri," said Sakura, from eleven feet up the road. "The cart."

"Coming."

He picked the shaft back up.

"You all right?" said Naruto.

"You've just told me something I've had in my own book since eight
o'clock this morning with the wrong word at the top of it."

"...Is that good?"

"It's extremely good and it is not comfortable and both of those are
usual," said Sazare. "You're the fourth person this month."

"Fourth what?"

"Fourth person to look at a thing I have been carrying around for years
and put the right word on it in under two minutes."

"Who were the other three?"

"A toad," said Sazare, "a slug, and Shizune."

Naruto considered this.

"That's an amazing sentence," he said.

---

They got to the top of the hill.

Sakura had the door and was holding it with her hip and both hands full
and had refused help twice, and she said, "In the *hall*, not the office,
she's got the Suna people at eleven," and Naruto took the box off the
cart on his own and did not appear to notice that it weighed fourteen
kilos.

Sazare stood at the bottom of the steps for a second with the empty
handcart.

*Two things, and then I am going to stop.*

*One. He said* everyone does a thing instead*, and he said it as an
aside, and it is the single most useful sentence about human beings that
anybody has said to me this year. Books. Ramen and pranks. And there is a
woman in this village who does gambling and drink and a shape on her own
face, and I have got four hundred pages on her and I have never once had
that word for it.*

*Two, and this is the one I am going to have to be careful about.*

*He is sixteen.*

*He has been in this village his whole life, everybody knows his name,
he has just come back off two years with one of the three, and he is
about to be the most important person in the Land of Fire and about half
this village already knows it and the other half will inside two years.*

*And he had four bowls, a kettle, a futon and a poster.*

*And nobody came up his stairs either.*

He put the handcart against the wall.

*So it is not the accent.*

*I have had eleven years of a completely clean explanation for the whole
of my life and it is: I am foreign, and I got here at fifteen, and a man
who arrives at fifteen with a Kiri name is going to have four windows in
every wall for ever and it is nobody's fault.*

*It is a good explanation and it is true and I have never once looked
past it.*

*And there is a boy in that hallway who is from here, whose name is on
everybody's mouth, who has never been foreign anywhere in his life —*

*— and he got the same room.*

*Different reason, same room.*

*Which means the reason is not the reason.*

*Which means I have got a four-hundred-page explanation for the shape of
my own life and about a third of it is load-bearing on a thing that I
have just watched fail on somebody else.*

He went up the steps.

*I am going to have to go through fourteen books.*

*Not today.*

*But I am going to have to go through fourteen books with that question
in my hand, and I have got a fairly good idea already of how many entries
are going to come back wrong, and it is more than four hundred, and there
is nobody I can tell about it except a sixteen-year-old who does not
know what he has done.*
