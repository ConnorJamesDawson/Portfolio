# Chapter 61, Scene 2 — Chapter Four

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~2490 words.*

---

He woke at six and knew four things before he opened his eyes, in
order, at about a second apart, and the fourth one stopped him
breathing.

*One. Slept. Properly. Four hours and about forty minutes and I have not
surfaced.*

*Two. I have moved.*

*Three. I have moved a considerable distance.*

He did the position without opening his eyes, because he had done four
thousand of them on wards and it was the oldest reflex he had.

*Left arm underneath, dead from about the elbow, which is a positional
palsy and is going to be extremely unpleasant in approximately eleven
minutes.*

*Right arm over. Across her. My hand is on the bed in front of her and
it is not touching anything.*

*Face is in her hair.*

*My knees are — right. Yes. All of that.*

*We went to sleep holding hands on top of the covers with about nine
inches between us and I have crossed the nine inches at some point in
four hours and forty minutes and I have got no report of it whatsoever.*

*That is the third hour of my life I cannot produce in twenty-two days.*

And then the fourth thing.

---

*Oh no.*

He did not move.

He lay absolutely still in the dark with his eyes shut and did not move
one single part of himself, and his heart went up to about a hundred and
thirty, and the entirety of chapter four arrived at once, in full, at
speed, with the citation.

*Nocturnal penile tumescence. Ordinary. Universal in healthy males from
infancy. Occurs in cycles across the sleep period in association with
the light phases and is not associated with dream content. Persists on
waking in a substantial proportion of instances. Absence of it in a man
of eighteen is a finding and the finding is bad.*

*Chapter four. Page ninety-something. I have read it eleven times. I have
taught a section of it to first-years. I have explained it, out loud,
professionally, without embarrassment, to two men on a ward who were
frightened about it, and I was good at it, and one of them said
afterwards that I was the first person who had not made him feel like a
pervert about it.*

*I know more about this than almost anybody in this building.*

*It is not helping.*

*It is not helping at all. It is not touching the sides. I have got the
entire physiology and the citation and the teaching experience and I am
lying in the Hokage's bed at six in the morning with a positional palsy
in one arm and an erection and I would rather be back in the yard on the
fifteenth of the eighth month.*

---

*Right. Options.*

*One: move.*

He examined that for about four seconds.

*I cannot move. My left arm is under her and it is dead and the moment I
try to withdraw it the whole of her shoulder comes with it, and I know
that because I tried exactly that on the twelfth of the eleventh month
with two fists in my shirt and her head came off the pillow and I stopped
immediately.*

*And that is the smaller half of the problem, because the larger half is
that moving at all in this position is going to communicate*
significantly *more information than lying still.*

*Two: go back to sleep.*

*No.*

*Three: wake her deliberately, explain the physiology, and treat it as a
clinical matter.*

He got about eleven words into constructing that one before he stopped.

*Absolutely not.*

*I have got the whole of it and it is four sentences and every one of
them is technically correct and it is the worst available course of
action in the history of the world.*

*"Hime, before you move, there's an ordinary physiological event in
progress and I'd like to give you the mechanism."*

*No.*

*Four: wait.*

*Wait is not a plan. Wait is what I have got.*

---

He waited.

He lay in the dark and did not move and did arithmetic, because
arithmetic was the only thing available and because it had got him
through four thousand hours of considerably worse rooms.

*Duration. It resolves. It has resolved every morning of my life for
about six years and there is no reason for this one to be different and
the median is not long.*

*Except that the median is measured in men who are asleep and alone, and
I am neither, and there is a very large body of evidence — chapter four
again, at the bottom of the page, in smaller type — that the resolution
is affected by state.*

*And my state is—*

He stopped that one before it got anywhere.

*Do not think about the state.*

*Thinking about the state is going to be counterproductive in a way I
can predict with total confidence and can do absolutely nothing about,
which is the fourth time in three weeks that I have known the mechanism
perfectly and been unable to touch it, and I would like it recorded that
the pattern is now beyond argument.*

*Breathing. Fourteen. Even.*

*Not eleven.*

He went very still.

*She has been awake for some time.*

---

"Morning," said Tsunade.

He did not say anything.

"Ruri."

"Good morning."

"You've been awake for about six minutes."

"Four."

"Six," said Tsunade. "Your breathing went at four and then it went again
at about two, which is a man having two separate realisations, and then
you stopped moving in a way that I have not seen a person achieve since
I was in a field station."

He lay absolutely still with his face in her hair.

"I'd like to say something," he said, "and I want to be clear at the
front that it is a physiological event, that it is ordinary, that it
occurs in cycles across the sleep period, that it is not associated with
dream content, and that its *absence* in a man of my age would be—"

"Ruri."

"—would be a genuine clinical finding and I would want to examine—"

"*Ruri.*"

"Yes."

"Are you giving me chapter four?"

"...Yes."

"At six o'clock in the morning."

"Yes."

"In my bed."

"Yes."

---

Tsunade began to laugh.

It was not a large laugh and she did not do it for long, and he could
feel the whole of it through his arm and through her back and through
about four other places and it was the worst four seconds of the entire
morning.

"Stop," he said.

"I have not—"

"Please stop laughing."

"I am *trying*," said Tsunade, "and you are not helping, and I want you
to understand that the thing you have just done is quote a *textbook* at
me about your own—"

"I am aware of what I have done."

"— at six in the morning—"

"I am *extremely* aware."

---

She got it under control.

"Right," she said. "Now listen to me, because I am going to be sensible
about this exactly once and then I am never going to be sensible about
it again for the rest of your life.

"I'm a doctor.

"I have been a doctor for thirty-eight years. I have had my hands inside
about four thousand people and I have run a ward and two field stations
and I have seen every single thing a human body does, in every
circumstance, in four countries, and there is not one thing that yours
is capable of that is going to be *news* to me."

"Yes."

"You are eighteen years old. You are eighteen, you are healthy, you have
been asleep, and you have spent the night four inches from a woman
you told a fortnight ago you were going to be beside until you died.

"If that had *not* happened I would be worried about you.

"I'd have been mildly insulted, first, and then I'd have got over the
insult in about four seconds and started running through causes, and by
breakfast I would have had a list, and none of the things on it are
things you want.

"A man of eighteen who does not is a man with something wrong with him.
I would want a history and I would want him examined and I would not be
laughing then."

She shifted about an inch.

"So we are not going to have a *thing* about it," said Tsunade. "It is
not a thing. It is going to happen every single morning for the next
thirty years and it is going to go on happening long after either of us
finds it interesting, and I am not going to remark on it and you are not
going to lie there doing arithmetic at six in the morning like a man
waiting to be sentenced."

"Right."

"Say *right* again in a voice that means it."

"Right," said Sazare.

"Better."

---

He lay there and found that about eleven-twelfths of it had gone out of
him, and that his heart was down to about ninety, and that his left arm
was going to start hurting in approximately four minutes and that he did
not care.

*She has just handled that entirely as a doctor and it worked completely
and I want to put down why, because it is going to be useful for the
rest of my life.*

*She did not reassure me. Reassurance is a thing people do to patients
and it is worth nothing and I have watched about a thousand of them not
believe it.*

*She gave me the differential.*

*She told me what it would mean if it had* not *happened, and she made
it sound worse, and she was not being kind — she meant it, that is a
real list and I could write it myself and two things on it are things I
have seen.*

*She turned the frightening thing into the ordinary one by putting the
actual frightening thing next to it.*

*That is exactly what she did to me about the seal in the yard, and what
she did about the study, and what she does about everything, and I have
watched her do it four hundred times to other people and it is the first
time it has ever been done to me.*

---

And then she moved her hand.

It was not fast. That was the thing he thought about afterwards, at some
length, on several occasions: that it was not fast at all, that she did
it in about two seconds, unhurriedly, the way a person reaches for
something on a table they have used for years.

She got hold of him.

Not painfully. There was no pressure in it at all, at first, and it took
him a full second to understand what had happened, and by then it had
stopped being at all funny.

"Now," said Tsunade.

He did not move.

"There is one rule," she said, "and there is only one, and I have been
waiting eleven days for the correct moment to give it to you, and I am
not going to pretend I had planned it to be this one.

"This — " and there was, briefly, some pressure " — does not do your
thinking."

"...Understood."

"I don't think you have."

---

"I am not talking about other women," said Tsunade. "I want that out of
the way first because it is what you would assume and it is what
everybody assumes and it is not what I mean. I have got no opinion about
that at all and if I ever do I will tell you in a sentence.

"I am talking about your *judgement*.

"You have got no brake, Ruri. You have never had one. Hatake said it in
eleven words in the ninth month and I have had it read back to me by two
people on that council since and I have not been able to argue with any
of them.

"There is no interval between you deciding and the world finding out.
That is the whole of what you are and it is why you are the most useful
person in my building and it is why there is a governor with my name on
it.

"And ten days ago you sat in a yard on your own and held open the thing
that turns people into statues for forty seconds because you *wanted to
know*, and you were pleased about it, and the only reason I found out is
that you cannot lie.

"And you told me on Friday that the reason there was no fear in that
yard is the same reason your work has gone radiant, and it is one effect
and not two, and the observer is gone.

"So you have not got a brake and you have not got an observer and now
you have got *this* as well."

The pressure came back on, a little, and did not go away.

"If it ever starts doing the deciding," said Tsunade, "I will take them
off you.

"I am not being funny and I am not being coarse for effect. I have got
the training and the anatomy and the hands and it would take about four
seconds and I would do it in a corridor.

"Do you understand me?"

"Yes," said Sazare.

---

She let go.

He lay there for about eleven seconds and did not say anything, and then
did the thing that he did, which was the wrong thing at the wrong moment
in the wrong voice.

"That's a governor," he said.

"...What?"

"That's a governor. That's a brake." He got the dead arm out from under
her, finally, and it was as bad as he had predicted. "That's the first
one anybody has ever fitted to me that is attached to *me* and not to a
piece of paper.

"The hug box is a signature at the bottom of a docket. It's a
consequence you hang on afterwards. It cannot see me, it cannot stop me,
and it has been visibly inadequate since the eleventh of the eleventh
month and everybody including you can see it.

"Shizune was a control. Shizune was better, because Shizune was a person
in a room, and she stopped being one on the seventeenth when the box was
empty.

"You have just fitted a control that is in the room with me
permanently, that acts *before* the event and not after, that I cannot
argue with and cannot get round and cannot construct a distinction
inside in a second and a half.

"That's the only real one I've ever been given and I'd like it recorded
that I said thank you."

There was a silence.

"You are going to write this down," said Tsunade.

"Not the whole of it."

"Ruri."

"I'm going to write *governor fitted, 2/12*," said Sazare, "and nothing
else, and in about forty years somebody is going to find that line and
have no idea whatsoever."
