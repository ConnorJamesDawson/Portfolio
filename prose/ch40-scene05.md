# Chapter 40, Scene 5 — Hime

*Act 4, part D. Konoha. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare.
~1,295 words.*

---

She banned *Hokage* on the second Thursday of the third month, in the
middle of something else, which was how everything happened.

"Stop it."

"Stop what?"

"You've said *Hokage-sama* four times in eleven minutes and you're
doing it in here, and in here I have spent fourteen months being a
woman who is angry about a bridge." She put her cup down. "I get it in
the tower. I get it in the corridor, I get it from four hundred people
a week, and I have exactly one room in this village where nobody does
it and you have started doing it in it."

"I hadn't noticed."

"No. You never do." She said it without any edge. "It's since the
tenth."

Sazare put that beside the other things he had put beside the tenth
and did not say anything.

"So stop," said Tsunade.

"What do I use instead?"

"I don't know. Something."

"That's not a specification."

"It is *absolutely* not a specification, and I am not going to give you
one, because if I give you one you will use it exactly and forever and
it will be the least natural thing anybody has ever said in this
building." She picked her cup back up. "Work it out. You're supposed to
be good at that."

---

He worked it out over nine days and it took nine days because he did it
properly.

*Constraint 1: not Hokage, in any form, in that room. Stated directly
and without qualification. This is the whole of the requirement and
everything else follows from it.*

*Constraint 2: not her given name.*

He wrote that one out and looked at it and made himself write the
reasoning, because a constraint without reasoning is a preference
wearing a coat.

*I am an eighteen-year-old provisional genin on a support
classification. She is the head of this village. There are perhaps nine
people alive entitled to use it and they are all either dead, sixty, or
Jiraiya.*

*Also I would not be able to. I have tried it in this room, alone, out
loud, four times, and I could not get the second syllable out on any of
them, and I am recording that because it is data and because pretending
otherwise would make everything after it useless.*

*Constraint 3: not Tsunade-sama. That is what a clerk says. She would
hear a clerk.*

*Constraint 4: the short form of the given name is out on grounds of
sound, register and self-preservation, and I want it noted that I did
consider it for approximately four seconds and that I am dense rather
than stupid.*

Which left, as far as he could establish, nothing at all — and he sat
with nothing for four days before he did the only thing left, which was
to stop trying to invent one and go and look for one that already
existed.

---

He found it in an intelligence digest.

Not a new one. The consolidated Land of Water digest, from the fourth
month of his seventeenth year, the one Hazama had read to him for two
hours and eleven minutes in a room with a broken radiator — he had a
copy of the covering summary, because Ibiki had let him keep the
covering summary, and it was in a folder on a shelf under a paulownia
box.

*Konohagakure: leadership. Fifth Hokage, Senju Tsunade. Of the three
Sannin. Widely known by the epithet* Hime *(Princess), in general
circulation, non-honorific in origin, deriving from her summoning
contract; used in official Kirigakure correspondence without prejudice.*

He read it four times.

*It is documented.*

*It is not mine. It is not a thing I invented, it is not an intimacy I
have decided to claim, and it is not a diminutive I have constructed
out of a name I am not entitled to use. It is a public epithet in
general circulation which appears in the correspondence of a foreign
government.*

*I have had it in a folder on a shelf for two years.*

*I read it before I ever sat down at that table.*

---

He said it on the fourth Thursday, in front of Kanzaki, who was
arguing about the wine list, and O-Shige, who had come in for reasons
nobody established, and the woman behind the counter, and four other
people.

He said it entirely without preamble, in the middle of a sentence about
a supply figure, because the whole point was that it should not be an
event and because he had thought about that for two of the nine days.

"— which is why the fourth-quarter number is wrong, Hime, and Kanzaki
has already written that down twice."

The room did not stop.

That was the thing he had not modelled: the room did not stop, because
the room was six tables of people who had been drinking for two hours
and were not listening, and the only two people in it who registered
anything were Kanzaki Uta, who did not look up from the wine list and
whose eyebrows went up about a sixteenth of an inch, and Tsunade, who
went completely still.

Then she said: "Say that again."

"The fourth-quarter number is—"

"Not that part."

Sazare put his cup down.

"I've worked out what to use," he said, "and I'd like to give you the
reasoning before you rule on it, because if you rule on it first I'll
argue and if I argue I'll lose."

"Go on, then."

So he gave her the reasoning, out loud, in a room with four other
people in it, in about ninety seconds: the four constraints, in order,
with the self-preservation clause, and the four failed attempts at her
given name alone in his own room, which he reported flatly as data
because it was data; and then the digest, with the date and the section
and the phrase *in general circulation, non-honorific in origin*, and
the fact that it had been in a folder on his shelf for two years and
that he had read it before he had ever met her.

"So I didn't invent it," he finished. "I sourced it. It's in the record
and it isn't mine and it doesn't presume anything, and it's the only
thing left that satisfies all four constraints, and if it's wrong I'll
go back to nine days of nothing and you can pick."

---

Tsunade did not say anything for about four seconds.

Kanzaki turned a page of the wine list with enormous deliberation.

"You *cited* it," said Tsunade.

"Yes."

"You have stood in a public room and cited a foreign intelligence
digest at me in order to justify what you're going to call me."

"It seemed better than guessing."

"It is *insane*," said Tsunade. "It is completely insane and it is the
most thoroughly worked piece of nonsense I have ever had presented to
me in this room, and I have been coming here for eleven years."

"That's not a ruling."

"No, it isn't." She picked her cup up, and Sazare — who noticed
everything, and who had spent nine days on this and would spend rather
longer on the following four seconds — saw that she did not drink from
it. "Fine."

"Fine?"

"*Fine.* Use it. In here." She set the cup down again without having
drunk. "Not in the tower. Not in front of the Council, not in front of
a foreign delegation, and not in front of anybody who would have to
write it down, because there are about nine people in this village who
would make it a problem and two of them would enjoy it."

"Understood."

"And Ruri."

"Yes?"

"You are aware," said Tsunade, "that it is entirely inappropriate."

"Yes."

"Good," she said. "As long as one of us is."
