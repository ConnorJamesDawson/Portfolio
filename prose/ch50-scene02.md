# Chapter 50, Scene 2 — Not Mine to Take

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,851 words.*

---

She raised it on the second of the ninth month and she raised it as a
safety matter, which he had not expected and which made it a
considerably harder conversation than the one he had constructed.

"I should like you to take a contract," said Katsuyu.

He put the file down.

"Before you answer," she said, "I have thought about how to put this
and I am going to put it in the order that makes the case rather than
the order that is comfortable, because you do that and it appears to
work."

"Go on."

"On the twenty-second of last month, at attempt seventy-one, you were
open and still connected and it had begun to draw, and I said stop, and
you stopped." A slow settling on the corner of the bench. "I have
thought about that a great deal. I stopped it at a second and a half. I
have concluded I should have stopped it at one, and I have said so, and
I will do better.

"But the thing I keep arriving at is not the half-second."

"It's that you were there."

"It is that I was there *by chance*," said Katsuyu. "You asked me on the
Friday whether I was free on the Saturday, and I was, and so I came,
and so you have a hand. If I had not been free — if I had been called,
which happens, and it is not a thing either of us can predict — you
would have done it anyway."

"...Yes. I would have."

"You would have done it anyway, alone, at one in the morning, in a
yard, with nobody to say stop, and the second time you would not have
had a hand to lose because it would have been the arm." She did not
change register at all. "Tsunade-sama set you a term. *You do not do it
alone.* And at present your ability to keep that term depends entirely
on whether I happen to be at liberty on a given afternoon, and that is
not a term, it is a hope."

---

He sat with that for rather longer than he was comfortable with.

*She is right.*

*That is the whole of the first pass and I am not going to dress it up.
The term is not a term. It is a hope with a slug in it. And I have
written "you don't do it alone" in this book four times as though it
were a rule I was keeping.*

"And with a contract," he said.

"With a contract you call and I come," said Katsuyu. "From anywhere,
immediately, in the middle of anything, and it is not a question of
whether I am free because there is no such thing. You would be able to
keep the term whenever you chose to work, and you work at one in the
morning, and I would rather you worked at one in the morning with
somebody watching than at three in the afternoon without."

"You've built the whole case."

"I have had eleven days."

---

He did not say no immediately, and he was glad afterwards that he had
not, because the thing that got him to the right answer was not a
principle. It was a question, and the question arrived about four
minutes in, sideways, the way everything arrived.

"Katsuyu — how did the first one happen?"

"The first what?"

"Contract. Ever. Any of them, anywhere." He had the pencil going now.
"There are contracts and they are ancient and everybody treats them as
though they have always been there. But they haven't always been there.
There was a first one. Somebody wrote something on something and
somebody signed it, and before that day there were people and there
were slugs and there was no contract at all."

Katsuyu was quiet.

"So what was there instead?" said Sazare. "On the day before. What was
between whoever it was and whichever of you it was, at the point where
there was no paper?"

"...They talked to each other," said Katsuyu.

"That's it?"

"That is all I have ever been told. I do not know names or dates. I
know that it went on for a very long time before anybody wrote
anything down, and that the writing came later, and that it was — " she
hunted for it — "it was a *tidying*. It made a thing that already
existed into a thing that could be relied on."

Sazare put the pencil down.

*There it is.*

*Fourth time this year.*

*Bloodline is a word in the box where the mechanism goes. Source medium
is a provenance column that records the seed and not the substance.
Chain of command is a channel I assumed existed because the arrangement
looked formal.*

*And contract is the word in the box where the relationship goes.*

*It is not the thing. It is the paperwork on the thing. It came second,
it came a very long time second, and everybody alive treats it as the
precondition because nobody alive was there before it.*

---

"So I don't need one," he said.

"You do not need one to *talk* to me. No." Katsuyu shifted. "You need
one to be able to rely on me, which is a different thing and is the
thing I was offering."

"I know. That's why it's a good offer and it's why I've been sitting
here for eleven minutes."

"But."

"But." He turned his hand over on the bench, the one with the patch on
the heel of it. "Katsuyu. On the first morning — the Tuesday, in her
office, with about a minute of my four minutes left — I asked you
whether you were free."

"Yes."

"And you couldn't answer. It took you eleven seconds and then you told
me you'd been trying to work out what you would say if the answer were
no, and that you didn't have one, because there had never been a no
available for you to have."

There was no sound in the yard except the pigeons.

"Four hundred and eleven years," said Sazare. "Nine summoners. Every
one of them courteous and several of them kind, and not one of them
ever asked, and it would not have occurred to any of them, and you said
it did not occur to *you*."

"...Yes."

"If I sign that, I can call you and you have to come."

"Yes."

"Then there is no *free* any more," said Sazare. "Not with me. Not ever
again. The one place in the world where somebody asks you and you get
to decide would be the last place, and it would stop being that on the
afternoon I signed, and it would be me who did it."

---

Katsuyu did not say anything at all for about nine seconds.

He had learned, over three weeks, roughly four of her silences, and he
did not recognise this one, and he sat still through it and did not
fill it.

"I had not thought of that," she said.

"No."

"I had not thought of it at all. I have had eleven days and I built the
whole case and I went over it four times, and that did not — " She
stopped. "I was about to give away the only thing I have found in four
hundred years that I liked. I would have handed it to you this
afternoon and I would have been *pleased with myself*."

"You'd have been solving a real problem."

"I would have been solving a real problem," said Katsuyu, "and I want
you to know that I do not think that makes it better. It makes it
worse. It is exactly how a thing like that gets done."

---

He gave it back to her, which was the part that took him four separate
attempts to write up afterwards.

"That's my reasoning," he said. "And I want to be careful about what
I've just done, because I have made an argument about what is good for
you, at length, to your face, in a yard, and I have been on the wrong
end of that manoeuvre about nine hundred times and I have never once
been glad about it."

"You are not—"

"Let me finish, I've thought about how to do this and I'll lose it."
He put both hands flat. "The reason isn't mine. It's about your *free*,
and your free is yours, and if you want to spend it then that's a thing
you're allowed to do and I don't get a vote on it. I've told you what I
think it costs because I would rather you had the number in front of
you. That's all I've done and it's all I'm going to do.

"So: I'm not asking. If you offer again in six months I'll say the same
thing and I'll say it faster. But if you decide, having heard all of
that, that you want to sign one anyway — "

"I do not," said Katsuyu.

"You can take a minute."

"I do not need a minute," said Katsuyu. "I am four hundred and eleven
years old and I have just been offered the first no I have ever had,
and I should like to use it on the thing that would take the rest of
them away, and I should like to do it now, out loud, in a yard, in
front of the person who gave it to me."

---

So: she visits.

He wrote the terms out that night, properly, because a thing that was
not written was a thing two people could remember differently.

*No contract. Standing arrangement, agreed 2nd of the ninth month.*

*I cannot summon her. I cannot call her, send for her, reach her, or
find out where she is. If I want her I have to ask her in advance, in
person, when she happens to be in front of me, and she can say no, and
she is to be able to say no without giving a reason and without it
costing her anything, and if I ever make that cost her anything the
arrangement is over and it will be my fault.*

*Any slug may choose to talk to me. None of them has to.*

Then he wrote the other half, because the ledger got both.

*What this costs, honestly, since I am the one who chose it:*

*It is worse for safety and she was right about that and nothing in the
last hour has made her less right. The term stands and my ability to
keep it is now formally a matter of luck. If I want to work on this at
one in the morning I have to have asked somebody on a Friday.*

*Which means, in practice, that I work when she is available and not
when I want to, and I have never in my life worked when somebody else
was available.*

*I think that is probably also the point and I am not going to pretend
I arrived at it deliberately.*
