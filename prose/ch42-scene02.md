# Chapter 42, Scene 2 — The Marking Scheme

*Act 4, part D. Konoha General, the fourth-floor landing. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,520 words.*

---

She arrived on the second morning with a ruled sheet.

He should have expected it and had not, and he stood on the landing at
twenty-nine minutes past seven with the book under his arm and watched
a fourteen-year-old unfold a piece of paper that had been folded three
times and carried in a satchel and was already annotated down one
margin in a hand that had got smaller towards the bottom of the page
because she had run out of room and refused to start a second sheet.

"I've made a marking scheme," said Sakura.

"Of course you have."

"Don't say it like that."

"I wasn't—" He stopped, and rebuilt it. "I meant that as an observation
and it came out as something else. I've been told about this. It's the
same fault in both directions: everything I say sounds like an
assessment because everything I say *is* one."

Sakura considered that.

"All right," she said. "That's better than most apologies I get in this
building, and I've had four this month and three of them were from
grown men." She held the sheet out. "Ten marks. Four criteria. I've
written what each one fails like, because you'll want the failure modes
more than the definitions."

He read it standing up.

*1. Did you go in without a cargo? (3)*
*Fails as: SUPPLYING. You bring or invent a subject inside the first
fifteen seconds because the silence is unbearable. See: plaster.*

*2. Did you leave a gap the other person could put something in? (3)*
*Fails as: THE LECTURE. Technically no cargo, but you talk for ninety
seconds and there is no door in it anywhere.*

*3. Did you follow what they gave you, or the thing you had ready? (2)*
*Fails as: THE INTERVIEW. Four questions in a row off a list. You did
this to me on a staircase and I didn't mind but I noticed.*

*4. Did you stop when it was finished? (2)*
*Fails as: THE AUDIT. Summarising the conversation back at the end.
Nobody does this. Please stop doing this.*

"You've thought about this a great deal," said Sazare.

"I've been thinking about it for two years," said Sakura. "You just
made it a thing I'm allowed to say out loud."

---

They did not manage every day. She was on nights twice and he lost a
Tuesday to a road accident that came in at four in the morning with
eleven people in it, and the sheet skipped those, and she ruled the
dates in anyway and left the boxes empty, which he approved of so
strongly that he said nothing about it at all.

He scored four out of ten on the third morning.

He scored two on the fourth, which was worse than the first attempt,
and Sakura wrote *2* on the sheet without softening it and then wrote
*(harder conditions — Kaburagi walked past)* underneath, which he
looked at for some time afterwards, because it was an honest number
followed by an honest reason and he had never once seen anybody do both
in the same box.

He scored six on the sixth morning and was pleased with himself for
about four hours, and then went back over it and worked out that the
whole of the six had come from Sakura carrying it, and said so at half
seven the next day, and she said "yes" without hesitating and did not
change the score.

"Why not?"

"Because that's not cheating, that's how it works." She was eating
something out of a paper twist while she said it, one-handed, which was
a thing she did every morning and which he had begun to suspect was her
only meal before the second hour of the afternoon. "Half of it is
always the other person. You'd know that if you—"

She stopped.

"Say it," said Sazare.

"You'd know that if you'd ever been the other person."

---

The correction came on the ninth morning and it came sideways, in the
middle of an argument, which he would afterwards note was how every
useful thing he had ever been told had arrived.

He had said the book was wrong.

"It isn't wrong, you're just bad at it—"

"It's wrong. It says the chapter consists in going in without a cargo.
That's a negative instruction. It tells me what not to carry and it
tells me nothing whatsoever about what the activity *is*, and the
result is that I stand on a landing performing an absence, which is
what you scored a two."

"It's not an absence—"

"Then what is it. Give me the positive form."

"It's not *nothing*, it's—" Sakura made a shape with the hand that
wasn't holding the paper twist, a small impatient scooping gesture, and
he watched her hunt for it, and watched her find it. "Right. Okay.
There *is* an object. You've just got the wrong one."

"Go on."

"The object isn't a subject. The object is *them*." She had gone
faster, the way she did when she got hold of something. "You're not
filling a silence. You're putting down somewhere for the other person
to put a thing. That's all it is. The reason it doesn't work with a
topic in it is that a topic is already a place — it's *your* place, and
they can either come and stand in it or they can't, and mostly they
can't, so they say something polite and go up the corridor."

Sazare had the notebook out.

"Say the last part again."

"You're not filling a silence, you're offering somebody somewhere to
put something." She watched him write it. "And it works when they put
something in it that you didn't expect, and it's *supposed* to be
something you didn't expect, that's the whole point of not bringing the
topic, and I think the reason you can't do it is that you've decided in
advance you'd rather have a bad conversation you can predict than a
good one you can't."

The pencil stopped.

"That," said Sazare, "is the single most accurate sentence anybody has
said about me in two years, and you're fourteen, and I want you to know
that I'm not saying that as a compliment because I still can't do
those, I'm saying it as a finding."

"...Thanks?"

"The book has eleven pages on chapter two and it does not contain that
sentence. I've checked. Twice, since you said it, and I'm going to
check again tonight and I already know what I'll find." He shut the
notebook. "It's a two-hundred-year-old manual written by a man who
thought about this for forty-one years, and it has been out-taught on a
staircase by somebody who hasn't finished growing."

Sakura went scarlet and covered it by folding the marking sheet.

"That's a compliment."

"It's a measurement."

"It's a *compliment*, Ruri-san, it's got a comparison in it and
everything—"

"It has a comparison in it because it's a measurement. That's what a
measurement is." He put the notebook away. "I'll accept that they're
harder to tell apart than I've been assuming."

---

He asked her, on the eleventh morning, what she would score.

He asked it badly — he asked it as criterion three, off the sheet,
which was itself a fail on criterion three, and both of them saw it
happen and neither of them said so.

"Eight," said Sakura. "On two."

"And the others?"

"Seven on four. Nine on one, I never bring a subject, I'm fourteen, I
haven't *got* any subjects." She turned the twist of paper over and
found it empty. "Three's the one."

"Chapter three."

"Chapter three." She had stopped looking at him, which he had learned
in nine days meant the thing coming was true. "*Of Inquiry, and the
asking after a person's affairs.* I'd score about a one."

"You've asked me eleven questions this week."

"I've asked you eleven questions about *you*. That's not the same
thing." She stood up off the rail. "It's easy. It's the easiest thing
in the world. You ask somebody about themselves and they're pleased and
they tell you and they think it went well and it costs you nothing at
all, and I have been doing it since I was nine, and it is a very good
way of getting all the way to fourteen without anybody finding out one
single thing about you."

The trolley went over the join in the tiles below, twice, the way it
had every morning of both their lives in that building.

"That's not a fault in chapter three," said Sazare, slowly, because he
was assembling it as he said it. "That's chapter three used as cover."

"Yes."

"You've been doing to me what I do with a ledger."

"I don't know what you do with a ledger."

"You will," said Sazare. "It's the same move. You hand somebody
something true so they stop looking for the rest of it."

Sakura picked up her satchel.

"Half seven tomorrow," she said. "And you can have that one for free,
because you got there on your own and I wasn't going to say it."
