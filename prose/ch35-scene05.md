# Chapter 35, Scene 5 — Eleven Minutes

*Act 4, part C. Konoha, the administration building. Sazare Ruri, age
seventeen. POV: Sazare. ~1,240 words.*

---

They went on the Thursday, because the bag went on Thursdays.

Yagi came, which he had not asked for and had not been offered — she
simply appeared at the hospital gate at the second hour in her own
clothes on a day she was not working, and said, "Right," and started
walking, and did not explain herself at any point in the following
fourteen minutes.

Her own clothes were a shock. He had known her for four years and had
seen her out of uniform twice. She wore a dark red jacket, plain, well
made, about eight years old and cared for, and boots that had been
resoled at least twice by somebody competent, and she walked
considerably faster in them than she did on a ward.

*Rung two: she resoles rather than replaces, and she has the same
cobbler as whoever does Nemoto's.*

"Stop reading my boots."

"Sorry."

"Don't apologise, I'd have been disappointed." She did not slow down.
"I'm not saying anything in there, before you ask. I'm sitting down and
I'm going to be extremely boring and you're going to do all of it."

"Then why come?"

"Because there is a difference," said Yagi, "between doing a thing
alone and doing a thing by yourself, and you have never once in your
life had the second one."

---

Kudō had the form ready.

She had, in fact, had it ready for eleven days, five boxes completed in
her own small neat hand off the file, the sixth left empty, and she had
kept it in the top drawer rather than in a tray so it would not get
processed by anybody's six-month rule.

"Right," she said. "Sit. Both of you. This takes about four minutes."

It took eleven.

Sazare sat down at the corner of Kudō's desk with a pen he had not
brought, on a chair that did not match the other chair, in an office
that smelled of ink and floor polish and somebody's lunch two doors
down, and Yagi sat on the opposite side with her hands in her lap and
was as good as her word, which was to say that she did not say one
single thing for the entire eleven minutes and did not once look at
the paper.

*I, Ruri Sazare, of Konohagakure.*

That part he had written eleven times and it cost him nothing and it
came out in his mother's hand without his deciding it should.

*Formerly of Ward Eleven, Kirigakure.*

Kudō had told him how the relationship clause was stated, and it turned
out to be a standard formula, and it turned out that his fabricated
technical objection had had a four-word answer that any clerk in the
building could have given him in the corridor.

*Son of Ruri Kaede, registered holder.*

His pen stopped.

He had not written his mother's name since she died.

Four years. He had thought about it constantly and had said it perhaps
four times out loud and had written it, he was fairly sure, never — not
in a notebook, not on a form, not once in four countries — and it went
onto a reparations schedule in a foreign administrative office, in the
hand she had beaten into him before he was eight, and the *e* at the end
had her tail on it because that was how he had been taught to finish a
name.

Nobody in the room said anything.

He got to the last line.

*I claim as surviving member of the Ruri, and declare this claim made
in good faith.*

He wrote *surviving member of the Ruri* without stopping, which he had
not managed in eleven attempts over four evenings alone in a room, and
he did it in about four seconds, and there was no moment in it and no
weight in it and nothing whatsoever happened.

He signed it.

---

"That's it," said Kudō. "I witness it, it goes in the bag at four, it's
in Kirigakure inside a fortnight."

"That's it."

"That's it. I did warn you it was four minutes."

"It was eleven."

"Was it?" Kudō looked at the clock, genuinely surprised. "So it was.
That's the longest anybody's taken on one of these in this office and
I've done about ninety."

She stamped it, and signed underneath, and blotted it, and put it in a
buff envelope, and wrote the schedule reference on the front, and the
whole of that took another ninety seconds and was, Sazare thought, the
single most anticlimactic sequence of events he had ever participated
in.

"Right," said Kudō. "Two to three years. I'll write to you at the
hospital when there's a determination and I'll write to you at the
hospital if there *isn't* one within eighteen months, because a lot of
people spend two years assuming no news means no, and it usually just
means a backlog."

"Thank you."

"And Ruri." She had already turned to the next thing on her desk, which
was how he knew it was sincere. "Whatever they come back with, they're
answering a question about a schedule. They're not answering a question
about your family. Those are different questions and the form can't
tell the difference, and I've watched about nine people take a refusal
as though it could."

---

Outside, on the steps, Yagi said her first word in half an hour.

"Well."

"Well."

"That was extremely boring."

"Yes."

"Eleven minutes and a stamp." She put her hands in her jacket pockets.
"Five weeks, four nights, eleven attempts, one kidney dish, a corridor,
and at the end of it a woman in a cardigan puts it in a buff envelope
and asks whether you want a receipt."

"I didn't want a receipt."

"No, and I noticed you didn't take one, and you're going to want it in
about a fortnight." She started down the steps. "Go back and get it."

He went back and got it.

---

He wrote it up that night, at the bench, which he had not sat at in
five weeks.

*Filed. Eleven minutes. Two to three years to determination, probably
three with an appeal. Valuation forty per cent short and appealable.
Possible outcome: refused.*

*Note on the four days: the technical objection about the relationship
clause had a four-word answer that any clerk in that building would
have given me in a corridor. I did not ask any of them. I constructed a
problem I could not solve alone, in order to have a reason not to
solve it, and then I wrote the constructed problem in this book, which
means there is now an entry in here that I knew was false when I made
it.*

*It is the first one. I have gone back and looked. Four years, eleven
hundred entries, and that is the first thing I have written down
knowing it was not true.*

*I am leaving it in.*

He looked at that for a while.

Then he wrote, underneath, in the second column, where there was no
unit:

*Emiko: nine seconds. Yagi: eleven minutes in a corridor, standing,
saying nothing, on a day she was not working. Ōtaki Sue heard it stop
through a floor. Ochiai asked Ashida. Ashida asked Yagi.*

*Five people. None of them asked me anything for five weeks because
each of them thought asking would be an insult, and every single one of
them was wrong about that, and I am the reason they thought it.*
