# Chapter 69, Scene 8 — The Vector

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1240 words.*

---

Ino Yamanaka arrived at the yard on the Wednesday of the second
week, unannounced, mid-morning, through a gate that Tenzō — eight
feet away, at the bench-watch — later admitted she had talked her
way past in eleven seconds flat, which he reported not as a
security failure but as a professional observation, in the tone
of a man filing a weather event.

Sazare knew her by file and by column: Yamanaka, the flower shop,
Sakura's east-stair opposite, and — per three years of hospital
observation — the single most efficient information-distribution
node in the village, a one-woman signals corps whose transmission
speed he had once charted against the official notice boards and
found to lead them by a day and a half on any story worth having.

She came in like the story was worth having.

"So it's true," said Ino, without preamble, taking in the yard,
the racks, the bench, and him, in one sweep that missed, by his
count, nothing. "You're the underwear man."

"I am the fabricator of record for a medical support garment
pilot."

"You're the underwear man," said Ino, with satisfaction, and put
a list on his bench.

The list was written in violet ink, in a fast confident hand, and
it was numbered, and it was two columns, and the first column was
*names* — eleven of them — and the second column was headed, with
terrifying commercial instinct, *requirements*.

"Order forms don't exist yet," she said, "so I made one. Those
are confirmed — money-in-hand confirmed, not interested-confirmed;
I know the difference and so does my till. Four are kunoichi, six
are civilians, and before you say the pilot is roster-only—" she
had read the objection off his face before it reached the
docket-quoting stage, which put her read-speed in the top five he
had ever charted— "civilian demand is your volume, volume is your
price floor, and the eleventh name is my mother, and you do not
want to be the man who told my mother no. Nobody has survived it.
There's a plaque."

"The board holds design and release authority. I build to their
specification."

"Good answer, wrong century," said Ino. "Fine. I'll take it to
the board. Who chairs — " and then she stopped, because she had
that answer already, and the whole affect changed gear, downward,
into the serious voice that her file said she almost never spent.
"Actually, that's the other reason I came. Two things, underwear
man, and then I'll buy something and go."

---

"Thing one. The grey." She held up the pilot spec sheet, which
she had obtained — he chose not to ask — and tapped the colour
line. "Penitential. I heard the matron said it and the matron is
right. And I know what you'll say: function doesn't care about
colour. But listen to me, because this is my actual area." She
leaned on the bench, and the commercial gear and the serious
gear turned out to be the same gear, which he noted as the
finding of the morning. "Every woman on that list has spent her
whole working life being told that things built for her body are
either pretty or serious — pick one, and if you fight for a
living, it's picked for you. You've made the first serious thing
that actually works. If it's also *ugly on purpose*, you've just
agreed with everybody who says function is what you settle for.
Make it in colours. Not instead of the engineering — in ADDITION
to the engineering, same rigour, dye lots that survive field
washing, I'll write you the palette myself. Functional does not
have to mean penitential. That's not vanity, that's the actual
product."

He wrote it down. All of it, at speed, and under it: *this is
the leisure-wear thesis from the capital letter, arriving
independently, from primary source. Two independent derivations
= real. The board gets both.*

"Thing two." She straightened, and looked at the case of pilot
articles, and then at him, and the question came out sounding
like a merchandising query and had, underneath it, at a depth he
was professionally certain she could not resist sounding, a
probe. "The name. Hime. Whose idea?"

"Mine. Rationale is in the docket."

"I read the docket." Of course she had. "Rank without clan,
every woman can say it, very tidy. And I keep thinking—" the
blue eyes did one slow pass over him, reading whatever there was
to read, which for the first time in a long while he genuinely
could not gauge— "it's a *dedication* name. That's what it
sounds like. Like there's a specific one."

The yard held. Tenzō, eight feet away, achieved a stillness so
mild it was indistinguishable from furniture.

"Every product I have ever certified was for a specific one,"
said Sazare, level, in the register that had survived a Sannin
and a nosebleed. "The splints were for bed eleven before they
were for the ward. The stones were for one theatre before they
were for four towns. You design for a load case, not an
average — the board minuted the same principle last Friday.
If the name makes every woman who wears it feel it was
dedicated to her—" he closed the notebook, unhurried— "then
the name is doing what the engineering does, and I would call
that a product working."

Ino looked at him for three full seconds.

"That," she said, slowly, "is the best branding answer I have
ever heard in my life. *Every woman gets to be the Hime.* I'm
having that. I'm putting it on a card in the shop window and
you can't stop me." The gear shifted back up, the transmission
resumed at full power, and whatever the probe had touched, it
came away — he was almost sure; the file would carry *almost*
— holding the cover story so much better than the secret that
the cover story was already halfway to the market square. "Put
my eleven with the board, colours proposal to follow in
writing, and sell me one of those little warming stones for
the shop's till drawer, my fingers freeze counting change."

He sold her the stone at list price. She paid it without
haggling — "full price, underwear man; the till story's worth
more than the discount" — and was gone through the gate in a
swirl of violet ink and consequence.

*Entry: the Yamanaka vector has engaged with the line,* he
wrote, when the yard had settled. *Estimated effect on the
list: it is Wednesday; by Friday the number will have a
different first digit. Her colour thesis goes to the board
under her name — it is correct, it is load-bearing, and it is
a category claim from a primary source, which the board should
treat as market data of the first grade.*

*And she looked directly at the name, called it a dedication,
and left believing the best possible version of the truth: the
one where it is a dedication to all of them.*

*Which it also is. That is what made the answer safe to give.
The rationale that is not on request remains not on request —
but I have now field-tested the armour on the most dangerous
reader in the village, and the armour is the same armour it
has always been: everything I said was true.*

*Tenzō filed the entire exchange as furniture. His pattern
book, however, is one entry longer tonight. The inch of open
door in this life is never the one you are watching.*
