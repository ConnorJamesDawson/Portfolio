# Chapter 38, Scene 4 — Eleven Birthdays

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the south-east quarter. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,219 words.*

---

He started with Handa, because Handa would answer and would not make
anything of it.

"When's your birthday?"

Handa did not look up from the dovetail. "Second of the third month.
Why?"

"Chapter six."

"There's a chapter six?"

"Of Occasions, and the marking of them. It's about birthdays and name
days and the anniversary of a death and the day somebody gets a thing
they wanted." Sazare set the try square down. "I've realised I don't
know when anybody's is. Not one person. I've got about four hundred
facts on you and I've had to ask you the only one you'd have expected
me to have."

"That's about right, that." Handa turned the board. "Go on then, I'll
save you the trouble. Wife's is the ninth of the eighth. Daughter's the
twenty-first of the fifth, and she's thirty-one this year, and she
writes twice a year and one of the two is always late." He blew the
dust off. "And mine's the second of the third and I'm forty-six and the
last time anybody marked it I was in a hospital bed with a pelvis in
three pieces and a nurse brought me a bun with a candle in it that
she'd got off the kitchens, and I was extremely rude about it, and I
have thought about that bun approximately once a month ever since."

Sazare wrote all of it down.

"Who was the nurse?"

"No idea. Small, dark, cross." Handa considered. "Same one who did the
thing with the pillows."

"Tomoe."

"If you say so."

"She's got one and a half hands and she'd have carried that bun up two
flights."

Handa put the chisel down and looked at the wall for a moment.

"Right," he said. "Well. That's ruined my afternoon."

---

Ashida took nine days, which was eight and a half days longer than it
should have, and the reason was that Sazare kept getting to the gate
with the sentence assembled and delivering a different one.

He did it in the end by not assembling anything.

"What's your wife's name?"

Ashida stopped with the pencil halfway to his pocket.

"...Miyuki."

"Miyuki."

"Yes."

"Three years," said Sazare. "I've called her *your wife* for three
years. She wrote a note to you in a folder that was for me and I read
it about eleven times and it was signed *M.* and I never asked."

"You didn't have to ask, I could have—"

"You could have told me and you didn't, and I have worked out
recently that that is because you have spent three years taking your
cue from a man who never asks anything, and that I have trained about
eleven people to withhold ordinary information from me by never once
requesting any of it." He got the notebook out. "When is she born?"

"Fourth of the second."

"And the children."

"Kiyomi's four in the eleventh month, the twenty-third. Tarou was the
sixth of the first, so he's just gone one." Ashida's voice had begun to
do something. "Kiyomi asks about you, you know. She's got it into her
head that you're the man who makes the blue stones and she wants one
and I've told her about nine times that they're government property."

Sazare wrote *Kiyomi, 23rd/11, four* and *Tarou, 6th/1, one* and
*Miyuki, 4th/2* in a hand that came out less steady than usual.

"And yours."

"Nineteenth of the ninth."

"That's in three weeks."

"Is it?" Ashida looked genuinely surprised. "So it is. I don't
generally—"

"I'm going to mark it," said Sazare. "I don't know how yet. The book
has a page on it and the page is not helpful because it assumes you
already know the person's habits, which I do, in about four hundred
particulars, none of which are this."

Ashida Kōhei stood at a hospital gate for a moment.

"You could just come round," he said. "That's — people come round.
There's food and it's chaos and Tarou throws things and you'd hate
about half of it."

"Which half?"

"The half with people in it."

"That's the half I'm working on," said Sazare.

---

He got eleven by the end of the fortnight and it cost him more than the
gifts had.

Emiko's was the twelfth of the tenth and she told him without looking
up from the bed state and then, four seconds later, put the pen down
and said "why?" in a voice that made him explain the whole book, which
took nine minutes, at the end of which she said, "Right. Well. I'm
fifty-two on it and I don't want a fuss," in a tone that meant *I would
quite like a small fuss.*

Kanzaki's was the thirtieth of the sixth and she gave it to him
immediately and then said, "Do not make me anything," and then, after a
pause, "and if you do, make it small, because I have a shelf and it is
not large."

O-Shige told him hers and her husband's and her sister's and her
sister's husband's and two of the neighbours', unprompted, in about
ninety seconds, and appeared to find the question so unremarkable that
Sazare stood at the counter afterwards recalibrating.

Nemoto said, "What do you want to know that for," and then told him,
and then said, "Don't," and then, on the following Tuesday, without any
preamble whatsoever, told him what his wife's had been.

Ōtaki Sue wrote hers on the slate and then wrote, underneath it:

*NOBODY HAS ASKED ME THAT IN 20 YEARS*

*THEY THINK IT IS RUDE TO ASK AN OLD WOMAN*

*IT IS NOT RUDE. IT IS RUDE TO STOP*

---

The thing the book had not warned him about arrived somewhere around
the seventh name and he did not have a category for it for about two
days.

*They are pleased.*

*That is the finding and I have been turning it over since Tuesday and
I cannot make it sit still.*

*Every single person I have asked has been pleased. Not four of them.
All of them. Nemoto told me not to and then told me his wife's
unprompted six days later, which is not the behaviour of a man who did
not want to be asked.*

*And I did nothing. That is the part. I did not do them a service, I
did not mend anything, I did not make anything, I did not put
four-tenths of a gram of anything on a scale. I asked a question that
cost me four seconds and about eleven months of accumulated
cowardice, and eleven people were pleased, and there is no ledger in
which that transaction balances.*

*I have spent nine years making myself owed, on the entirely reasonable
grounds that being owed is the only reliable way to make people keep
you. And it works. It has saved my life twice.*

*And it turns out that the cheaper method has been available the whole
time and produces a better result and I did not use it because it
requires me to admit, out loud, in front of the person, that I want to
know something about them.*

He looked at that.

*Being the cause of that is harder to sit with than being ignored was.*

*I do not know why yet. I am writing it down so that I can find out.*
