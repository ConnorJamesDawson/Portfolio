# Chapter 72, Scene 8 — The Largest Creditor

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, Handa's workshop and Naruto's apartment.
Sazare Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1520 words.*

---

He ran the audit on the Friday night, because it had been
sitting in the ledger since the Sunday dinner asking to be run,
and audits did not age well.

It had begun as a small entry — *the chair at the tower was
his before he sat in it; establish why that is true* — and it
had gone down, the way his entries went down when there was
load underneath, through the following strata:

*Every article of my life in this village descends from her.
The trial that got me a bench. The ward that taught me
medicine. The board, the line, the paper, the tower, the
governor, the noons, the Thursdays, the count — trace any of
it upstream and every channel runs through one woman, and I
have known that for three years and never once asked the next
question, which is the only question an auditor is for:*

*what does SHE descend from? Why is she here?*

*And the record answers: because six years ago a woman who
had buried everything she loved, who had left this village
with no intention alive of returning, who was — her words,
once, in the dark, about that year — "done, and drinking to
stay done" — was found in a gambling town by a twelve-year-old
boy who needed her, and shouted at her, and bet her, and
BEAT her — beat the Legendary Sucker at her own table, won a
necklace with a curse on it and a village with a hole in it —
and hauled her home by sheer, unreasonable, weaponised faith,
because he had decided she was going to be Hokage and the
concept of her declining did not survive contact with him.*

*Nothing I have exists without that.*

*Not the bench. Not the board. Not the count. Not the
Thursdays. Not the daylight treaty in this book's back pages.
Remove one loud twelve-year-old from one gambling town and
the whole of it — every article, every entry, HER — never
reaches me. I would be someone else entirely. Somewhere on a
coast, probably, with a jeweller's roll and a six-day rule
and nobody's hands in my shirt at night, being the ghost of
a trade.*

*Finding: the largest creditor in my ledger is Uzumaki
Naruto, and the account is six years old, and he does not
know it exists — because to him it is not an account. It is
just a thing he did, the way weather is a thing the sky
does.*

*The debt cannot be paid. Debts of this class never can; I
learned that from a paper with two signatures on it. But it
can be ACKNOWLEDGED, formally, once, in the correct
currency.*

*And I know his currency. I ate it on Saturday, out of a
shared pot. A bowl put down without being asked. A name in
a diary. A chair.*

---

Handa Rokusuke's workshop was three streets off the timber
yards, and Handa Rokusuke — sixty now, spine fused, hands
still the finest he had ever watched work wood — listened to
the commission with the specification sheet held at arm's
length, and then lowered it and looked at Sazare over the top
with the expression he had been aiming at him since bed
eleven.

"Cedar," he said. "You've written cedar."

"Cedar is light, stable, and—"

"Cedar is what a stonemason thinks a chair is." He said it
with enormous satisfaction, and turned to his racks, and
Sazare surrendered the point with the particular pleasure of
being craft-corrected by the man he had once craft-corrected
a window for, seven years of debt and counter-debt between
them now, the interest paid in exactly this coin. "Zelkova.
For a chair that gets sat in hard by somebody who tips it
back on two legs — and he will; you've described the boy in
one page and he tips, I'd stake the shop — zelkova, through-
tenoned, and it'll outlive him, and it'll outlive whatever
fool building he keeps it in. Three weeks."

"Four days, if I supply a curing article for the joints."

Handa's eyes narrowed with the joy of a man being offered
sorcery in a currency he respected. "The splint stone? For
FURNITURE?"

"The line has a materials budget and I chair the line's
fabrication. It is, for once, entirely my call. Four days,
Handa-san. It is wanted for a Tuesday."

---

The chair was delivered on the second Tuesday of the month,
by its commissioner, personally, up the stairs of a building
in the old quarter, and Naruto opened the door mid-protest —
"we said Tuesday dinner, you're EARLY, the eggs aren't—" and
stopped, because the visitor was holding furniture.

"What's that?"

"A chair," said Sazare, and carried it past him, and set it
at the table, on the guest side, across from the sill, where
it fit — he had taken the room's numbers at the hotpot,
because of course he had — as if the table had been drawn
around it. "Zelkova. Handa Rokusuke's work; the joints are
mine. It seats a man who tips it onto two legs, which you
will, and it will outlive the building. It is not a gift for
the apartment." He straightened, and gave the rest of it in
the working register, level, because this was the load-
bearing clause and the register was how such clauses were
laid: "You told me the rule. A chair is a promise — you buy
the chair when there is someone the chair is for. This is
that chair. Tuesday evenings, standing, in my diary, in
yours. I am informing you of the appointment. You are the
someone."

Naruto looked at the chair for a long moment. When he
spoke, his voice had gone to the volume he'd used for the
promise at the gate, the one the file said he kept for the
things carried like a rank.

"Okay. Okay, but you have to—" he cleared his throat, and
pointed at it, and started again at proper amplitude,
which fooled neither of them. "That's MY side. You're in
my chair. We're switching. Guest gets the — you know the
rule."

"The rule stands. I am not the guest tonight. Sit down,
Naruto. There is one more finding, and then the eggs."

And because the record got the whole of it or the record
was worth nothing, he gave it whole: the audit, run
upstream, channel by channel — the bench, the board, the
line, every article of a life traced back through one
woman to one gambling town and one loud boy who would not
take no from a legend. He kept the daylight parts on the
right side of the treaty, and the load came through
regardless, because true things carried in the level
register always came through — that was the entire
technology.

"You were twelve," he finished. "You brought her back.
Everything I have descends from that day, and I would be
somebody else entirely — somebody smaller, somewhere
colder — in every version of the world where you did not.
You are the largest creditor in my ledger, Naruto, and
you did not know the account existed, and I am not able to
pay it, and I am not going to try. I am going to eat in
this apartment on Tuesdays instead. Acknowledgement, in
your currency. That is the finding. That is why the chair."

The apartment was quiet. Below the window the old quarter
went about its evening; on the sill, the plant grew; on
the table, between the one chair and the other one, the
eggs waited to go in at the end, per the trial.

"...Man," said Naruto, finally, thickly, wiping his face
with the heel of one hand, entirely unembarrassed about
it, the way he was unembarrassed about everything true.
"I just wanted her to come home and do her Hokage thing.
I didn't know I was — I didn't know it was gonna be
somebody's whole—" He stopped. He looked at the chair.
And then he arrived, the way he arrived at everything, by
not looking where he was going, at the true thing:

"That's how it works though, right? You never know whose
whole life you're carrying when you do a thing. You just
do the thing." He sat down in his chair, in his apartment,
across from his standing Tuesday, and pointed at the
kitchen with his chin, grinning through the last of the
wet. "Get the eggs, fabricator. You're not a guest, you
said it yourself. Guests don't cook. Family cooks."

*Entry, Tuesday, filed at the bench, midnight, under the
day's date and no heading, because some entries refuse
them:*

*The count stands at fifty-six.*

*Entries fifty-two through fifty-five were hers, this week,
per standing collection.*

*Entry fifty-six was mine, tonight, in a kitchen in the old
quarter, at the words "family cooks" — logged for the first
time in the ledger's history under BOTH columns, given and
received, because I have audited it four times and cannot
determine whose it was.*

*The methodology section can argue with me when it gets
home. The methodology section is asleep two floors up with
her hands in my shirt. The finding stands.*
