# Chapter 58, Scene 8 — What Are We

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1910 words.*

---

He had it built by the time he got to the landing.

*Four clauses. Statement of position, evidence, the question, and the
release.*

*It is four minutes. I have timed it twice and it is three minutes
forty at the pace I actually speak, and the release is the important
part and it goes at the end because if I put it first she will hear the
whole of the rest of it as pressure.*

He knocked.

"It's open."

She was on her own. There was no Jiraiya on the windowsill and no
Shizune and no fish in a paper, and the compensation files were on the
left at about eleven inches and the lamp was pulled over, and it was
eleven minutes past four on the twenty-first of the eleventh month.

He sat down.

"Can I say a thing," he said.

"You're going to anyway."

"I might not."

Tsunade put the pen down.

"Go on, then," she said.

---

*First clause. Position.*

"I don't know what this is."

"Ruri—"

"I want to get the whole of it out and then you can have it, because if
I stop in the middle I'll rebuild it and the rebuilt one will be worse.

"I don't know what this is. That's the position. It's not a complaint
and it isn't a grievance and nobody has done anything to me. It's a
statement of my actual state, which is that I have been in something
since the eleventh of this month and I could not tell you what it is."

*Second clause. Evidence.*

"And I've got evidence, and that's the problem, because all of it is
circumstantial and I have spent eleven years learning exactly how much
circumstantial evidence is worth.

"You come in at the fourth hour and I come in at the fourth hour and you
have not once told me to stop.

"You gave me a docket on the thirteenth that was yours to determine and
you sent it up with my name on the minute over the head of a man who
voted against my post.

"You have kissed me on the cheek twice, and both times there was a
witness in the room, and never once when there wasn't.

"You swear in front of me now. Four times in eleven minutes on Monday
morning, and none in three years and four hundred visits before that,
and it isn't drink and it isn't him — it's that I have been moved out of
the group you assemble for and into the one you don't, and I found that
out from two words about a schedule.

"You would not summon Katsuyu on Wednesday because it would have made
the thing she and I have got into a thing you did.

"You have never once, in any of it, said a sentence."

---

*Third clause. The question.*

"So what I have got is nine observations and no statement, and I can
build about four structures out of them and every single one of them is
consistent with the data, and two of them are wonderful and one of them
is ordinary and one of them is that I have made a very large mistake
very quietly and nobody has had the heart to say so.

"And I cannot tell which. I have been trying since Monday.

"I'm a material man, Hime. I want to be clear about what I'm asking for
and why, because it's going to sound like I'm asking for a promise and
I'm not.

"I don't want a promise. I want the *object*. I want one thing I can put
on a felt and weigh, because inference is what I do all day and I am
extremely good at it and it is the least reliable thing I own, and I
have been living on it for ten days.

"So — what are we?"

He stopped.

He had got to the end of the third clause in two minutes and fifty and
he had done it properly and it had come out almost exactly as built,
and he had about a second and a half of being pleased with himself.

Then the silence went on.

---

*Right. Fourth clause. The release.*

*And I built this one at half past two this morning and it is the best
thing in the whole of it, and she needs to have it, because otherwise
the last three minutes were a lever.*

"And I want to say the other half," he said, "because if I don't say it
then everything I've just said is a weight and I'd be putting it on you
at four in the morning.

"If the answer is that it isn't anything — that would be an entirely
acceptable answer."

Tsunade's chair moved slightly.

"I mean that. I'd take it. I want you to know that I would take it and
that nothing would change, and I'd like to say precisely what I mean by
nothing, because people say that and it isn't true and then they leave."

"Ruri."

"I'd still come at the fourth hour if you'd have me. I'd still do the
dockets. I'd have the same position on every single one of the things
I've got a position on and I would not be strange about it and you would
not have to manage me.

"And if it's less than the ordinary version — if it's that you've been
kind to somebody who—"

"*Ruri.*"

"— because I've thought about that one a great deal and it is completely
consistent with the data, and I want you to know that if that's what it
is then I'd rather have that than not have anything, and I'd take it on
those terms and I'd take it for as long as it was on offer, and I would
not ask you for a—"

---

She got up.

He had not noticed her stand. She had come round the corner of the desk
somewhere in the middle of the last sentence and he had gone on
speaking, because he had two more clauses and because the thing he was
doing had a shape and the shape was not finished.

"— for a single thing beyond it, and I'd like that written down
somewhere, because in about four years I might—"

She took hold of the front of his coat with her left hand.

"— be a different— "

And she kissed him on the mouth.

---

Everything stopped.

That was the whole of it and he was never able to get any further with
it, not that night and not for a long time afterwards: everything
stopped, all at once, the way a room goes when a fault finally lets go
and there is nothing left making noise.

He had four clauses in his head. He had had four clauses in his head
continuously since half past two that morning, alongside three
alternative openings and a fallback and a count of what he was going to
do if she said the ordinary thing.

They went.

Not one of them was retrievable afterwards. He looked for them for
several days and found nothing at all — not the words, not the shape,
not the order, not even the memory of having had them, and he had never
in his life lost a structure he had built.

*I have no readings.*

*I have got nothing. Nothing. There is no count, there is no duration,
there is no— I do not know how long this has been happening and I have
no instrument at all and I am not going to be able to write one single
line of this down.*

*I don't care.*

*I have never once not cared and I do not care and I would like that
recorded and there is nothing to record it with.*

---

It was not long.

He knew that only because of what came after, which was that his hands
were still on his knees, and he had not moved them, because moving them
had never occurred to him.

She let go of his coat.

She did not step back very far.

He looked up at her, and the lamp was behind her and doing what lamps
did, and she had one hand still on the arm of his chair and she was
about eleven inches away and she was looking at him.

"Right," said Tsunade.

He said nothing at all.

"That's the object," she said. "Weigh that."

---

Something happened to him then that he could not account for at any
point in the rest of his life.

It came up out of somewhere with no warning whatsoever, the way things
had been coming up out of somewhere for nine days, and it went through
the whole of him and there was nothing at any level that had any
capacity to stop it or shape it or put a bracket round it.

He laughed.

It was not much of a laugh. It got about two-thirds of the way out and
then stopped being a laugh and became something else with water in it,
and he put a hand over his mouth, which he had seen people do, and it
did not help.

"Oh, *hell*," said Tsunade.

"I'm all right."

"You are not remotely all right."

"No," he agreed.

He sat in a chair in the Hokage's office at nineteen minutes past four
in the morning with his hand over his mouth and his eyes shut and made
absolutely no attempt to do anything about any of it, because there was
nothing available and because he did not want there to be.

*This is euphoria.*

*I know the word. There is a page on it in chapter four and the page is
about pathology, because in a hospital it is a symptom and you look for
the head injury.*

*It is a symptom.*

*I have got it and I have got no head injury and I know exactly what
caused it and I have never felt anything remotely like this in eighteen
years and I would like it to go on for the rest of my life.*

---

"Say something," said Tsunade.

"I can't."

"You've been talking for four minutes."

"That was before," said Sazare.

He took the hand off his mouth and looked at her and could not
straighten his face out at all, and he was aware that this was probably
visible and that there was nothing to be done about it.

"There's a thing I ought to say and I've got no way of saying it and
I'm going to say the small one instead."

"Say the small one."

"Can I have another one," said Sazare.

---

Tsunade closed her eyes.

"You absolute little—"

"That's not what I—" He stopped. "It is what I meant. Both times. I'd
like it noted that it was what I meant both times."

"It is *four in the morning*."

"Yes."

"I have got a delegation at nine and ninety files and a temple that has
moved three times."

"Yes."

"And you have sat in that chair and offered, in full, at length, with
clauses, to be *nothing at all* for as long as I liked."

"...That was the release," he said. "It goes at the end. Otherwise the
rest of it is a lever."

Tsunade stood over him with her hand on the arm of the chair.

"Ruri," she said. "Shut up."

And she kissed him again.
