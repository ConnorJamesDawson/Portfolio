# Chapter 60, Scene 5 — Two Mountains

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, the long room. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1470 words.*

---

Jiraiya's hand stopped on the scroll.

"They're at the *gate*?"

"They arrived at the western approach at about the ninth hour," said
Katsuyu, "and they have come up on foot, and they are on the stair now."

"On foot."

"Yes."

"Fukasaku has not walked into a human settlement in ninety years. He
does not *walk* anywhere, he goes where he is called and he goes home
again and he has told me four hundred times that a summons is a summons
and that the whole of the arrangement is that you do not turn up—"

He stopped.

Sazare watched the entire thing arrive on him at once, which was
something he had not seen happen to that man in nine days.

"He's not coming as a summon," said Jiraiya.

"No," said Katsuyu.

"He's coming as a *guest*."

"Yes."

"Because you're here."

"Because I am here," said Katsuyu, "and because you cannot summon a
thing into a room where another mountain is standing and expect it to
mean anything afterwards. He is being extremely careful. I would like
somebody in this room other than me to understand how careful he is
being before he comes through that door."

---

They came through the door at four minutes past ten.

Fukasaku was about two feet high and very old and entirely unhurried and
had a stick that he did not need, and Shima was slightly smaller and
came in second and looked at the whole room in one movement, corner to
corner, in about a second and a half, and then looked at the sill.

Nobody said anything for a moment.

*They are looking at her.*

*Both of them, immediately, before the room and before us. Fukasaku got
about four feet in and stopped, and he has not moved since, and the
stick has not gone down.*

*And she has not come off the sill.*

---

"Katsuyu of Shikkotsu," said Fukasaku.

"Fukasaku of Myōboku."

"You have come."

"I have been here since the eighth hour," said Katsuyu. "I wanted to be
in the room before you rather than arriving into it. I thought it would
be easier for both of us and I may have been wrong."

"No," said Fukasaku. "That was correct."

And that was all of it.

There was nothing else. There was no greeting after that and no
ceremony and nothing that anybody would have written down, and the two
of them looked at each other across a scrubbed table in a tower in the
Land of Fire for about eleven seconds and then Fukasaku put the stick
down and turned round.

*That was not warm.*

*I want to be careful, because I have got no basis for reading either of
them and I have four hundred pages on people who read a species they do
not know.*

*But it was not warm and it was not hostile and it was not the thing
Jiraiya was frightened of.*

*It was two very old creatures being enormously polite to each other in a
situation neither of them has a precedent for.*

---

"You'll be the woman who holds the tower," said Fukasaku.

"Tsunade."

"I know your name. I have known your name for forty years and I did not
use it because I do not know what you would like to be called in your own
house."

"Tsunade's fine."

"Then we'll have the first thing," said Fukasaku, "and then I'll sit
down."

Tsunade stood at the head of the table.

"This is my house," she said. "Authority in this room is mine, and it is
mine because it is my house and not because of any rank I hold in this
village, and nothing that happens in here is Konohagakure's business or
under its arrangements.

"And there's a second half.

"If the boy says stop, everything stops. Anybody, any point, any reason
or none, and nobody asks him why."

Fukasaku looked at her for a moment.

"Say the second half again," he said.

She said it again.

"Right," said Fukasaku. "Good. That's better than I expected and I'd
like it minuted that I said so."

Shizune wrote it down.

---

He sat.

Shima came round the table and sat next to him and had still not said
anything, and Sazare had been watching her since she came in and had got
nothing at all off her, which was unusual and which he wrote down later
as the first honest reading of the day.

"The boy," said Fukasaku.

"Sazare Ruri."

"You've got a name, then."

"You didn't have it in the letter."

"I did not have it in the letter because Jiraiya would not put it in a
letter that had to cross water," said Fukasaku, "which was correct of
him, and I wanted you to see that I had noticed. That was the whole
purpose of putting you third. I am told you notice things."

"I noticed."

"Aye. Well." He put both hands on the table. "Second condition. Stand up
and tell me what's being transferred, in your own words, plainly, and
don't dress it, and don't be modest about it either, because modesty and
dressing are the same thing coming from opposite directions and I have
had ninety years of both."

---

Sazare stood up.

*Right. Say it the way you worked it out at the window and do not improve
it on the way out of your mouth.*

"Not crystal," he said.

"Go on."

"Crystal is the test article. It's the test article for two reasons and
neither of them is that it matters. It's the only operation I can
definitely perform, so it's the only clean thing to try it with. And
nobody can fake one — you can pretend to understand a lesson but you
cannot pretend to be holding a piece of Shōton, and it can be put on a
table and picked up by anybody in this room.

"What's being transferred is the absence of a notation."

Fukasaku's head came up about an inch.

"Everybody in this village makes a shape by running a seal," said
Sazare. "A seal is writing. It's a compression — somebody worked a shape
out once and found a way to write it down so that it could be handed to
a person who had not worked it out, and so it could be handed back to
themselves reliably on a bad day. It does about nine parts in ten of the
shaping and you carry forty of them.

"I can't read one. Not can't in the sense that nobody taught me — I was
taught twice. The positions go in and nothing comes back, and that is
the one medium in the world that is shut to me, and every technique in
history is written in it.

"So I hold the whole shape myself, entire, every time, from nothing.
That is not a discipline and it is not impressive. It is what is left
when you cannot write anything down. I have got three operations and a
genin with a scroll outnumbers me by Thursday.

"And that is the thing Jiraiya has asked for. Not the crystal. The
working without the writing.

"And I cannot teach it, because I have got nothing to hand him, and
because what I would be handing him is not a technique, it is the place
where one is not.

"You cannot demonstrate a thing that isn't there.

"So the proposal is to move it along a chain of creatures who do not use
notation either, in this room, one link at a time, with me present to
say when a link has come out wrong.

"That's it. That's the whole of it. It's four sentences and I have not
got a fifth."

He sat down.

---

Nobody said anything for some seconds.

Then Shima spoke, and it was the first thing she had said since she came
into the building.

"You're wrong about one part of that," she said.

"Which part?"

"*I have got nothing to hand him.*" She had a voice like somebody
sharpening something. "That's the bit you've built the whole of it on
and it's wrong and you've been wrong about it for a fortnight."

"Then correct it."

"Not yet," said Shima. "You'll get it in about an hour and you'll get it
off the work and not off me, and if I tell you now you'll do what your
kind always does, which is understand it perfectly and be no better off."

*That is Katsuyu's sentence.*

*That is Katsuyu's sentence almost word for word and she has never met
this creature.*

He looked at the sill.

Katsuyu had not moved.

"Well," said Fukasaku, into the quiet, "that's the first interesting
thing that's happened in ninety years."

He put his hands flat on the table.

"Now," he said. "Fourth condition. What have you given Shikkotsu?"
