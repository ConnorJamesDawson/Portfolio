# Chapter 43, Scene 4 — Committing

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, training ground eleven. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,234 words.*

---

Training ground eleven had gone from mud to dust in about nine days,
which was the fourth month doing what the fourth month did, and by the
sixth evening of falling on it Sazare had stopped noticing the taste
and started noticing that it got into the crease of the elbow and
stayed there through two washes.

Warm, and the light going orange along the west treeline, and the
crickets starting up in the long grass at the far end at almost exactly
the same minute each evening, which he had checked twice before
admitting to himself what he was doing.

"Again," said Kakashi.

He fell again.

"That's eleven," Kakashi said, "and every one of them is the same
mistake, and I've let you do all eleven because you're the sort of man
who has to be allowed to exhaust an approach before he'll hear that
it's the wrong one. Are you exhausted?"

"Yes."

"Good. Sit down."

---

Kakashi did not sit. He stood with his weight on the back foot the way
he always did, one hand in a pocket and the book in the other, closed,
a thumb in it.

"You're resisting the fall," he said. "All the way down. Every time.
Your shoulder goes out, your hand goes out, your hip stays back, and
you take the ground in four places instead of one, and every one of the
four is a place you didn't choose."

"I'm trying not to hit it."

"You're *going* to hit it. That was decided about a second and a half
before you started arguing with it." He turned the book over. "A fall
you fight takes you in four places, chosen by the ground. A fall you
commit to takes you in one, chosen by you. It hurts more in the moment,
occasionally, and it costs you nothing afterwards, and the entire
difference is whether you spent the second and a half deciding or
objecting."

"That's not intuitive."

"No. It's the least intuitive thing I teach and it's the first thing I
teach, and the reason is that everything else is a version of it." He
nodded at the dust. "Again. And this time pick the place."

Sazare fell. He picked the place. It was the outside of the left
shoulder and it went through him properly and he lay in the dust for a
moment and then found that he was up, without having made a decision
about being up.

"There," said Kakashi.

"That was better."

"That was correct. Do it four hundred more times and it'll be better."

---

They walked the long way back, because Kakashi always walked the long
way back and Sazare had stopped asking about it in the second month.

He reported the board, because reporting was what he did and because
Kakashi was on the short list of people who got the whole of a thing
rather than the half that was their business.

He got through the first mistake and Kakashi said nothing at all.

He got through the second one — fifty-eight point one, page four
hundred and eleven, a room of eleven going quiet — and Kakashi laughed
once behind the mask, which was rare enough that Sazare stopped
walking.

"Sorry," said Kakashi, not sorry. "Go on."

"He came down a flight of stairs afterwards and asked to be one of the
six."

"Of course he did." Kakashi started walking again. "Do you want the
useful version of this or the comfortable one?"

"The useful one."

"Everybody in that hospital is going to tell you the lesson is that you
shouldn't correct senior men in public. That's the comfortable version
and it's wrong, and if you take it you'll spend forty years watching
bad numbers go into wards because you learned the wrong thing at
eighteen." Kakashi put the book away. "Your problem isn't that you
corrected him. Your problem is that you have never once, in your life,
decided *not* to."

Sazare walked about six paces with that.

"That's the same shape as the fall."

"It's exactly the same shape as the fall, which is why I let you have
eleven of them before I said anything." Kakashi's hand went back in the
pocket. "You've got a reflex that fires before the decision does. You
think the work is getting rid of it. It isn't — the reflex is the best
thing you own, it's how you found a wrong barrier on a diagram nobody
was ever going to check, and if you sand it off you'll be a pleasanter
man and a considerably worse one."

"Then what."

"You put a governor on it." Kakashi said it without any particular
emphasis, which was, Sazare understood immediately and with a
sensation like cold water, deliberate. "You don't stop it. You put the
limit on first, underneath, so it can't go past a line you set when you
were calm. And then when it fires, it fires inside the thing you
already decided, and it looks from the outside like restraint and it
isn't. It's a fall you picked the landing for."

Sazare stopped walking again.

"I built that," he said. "Last month. In crystal."

"I know," said Kakashi. "Kanzaki told me. It's why I used the word."

---

He wrote it that night with the lamp low and both halves in order and
the left shoulder stiffening the way it was going to be stiff for two
days.

*Eleven falls, all resisted, all four-point. One fall committed, one
point, chosen. The chosen one hurt more and cost nothing and I was up
before I decided to be up.*

*He said the resisting is a second and a half of arguing with something
that was already decided. That is a description of about a third of my
life.*

*And then he pointed the governor at my mouth, which I did not see
coming and should have, because I designed the governor four weeks ago
and I have applied it to exactly one material and it did not once occur
to me to apply it to the only material I actually live inside.*

He looked at the page.

*The reflex is not the fault. I have been treating it as the fault for
eleven years — every entry I have ever written about the barrier and
the camber and the fifty-eight point one has been an entry about a
thing I should stop doing, and I have never once stopped doing it, and
the reason I have never stopped is that it is load-bearing.*

*Set the limit first. Set it calm, in advance, when there is nothing in
front of me. Then let it fire.*

*Ōkubo in a corridor: correct in public, and it produced a volunteer.*

*Ōkubo's junior in the third week of last month, over a dressing, in
front of two orderlies: correct in public, and it produced a man who
now walks the long way round the second floor.*

*Same reflex. Same accuracy. Two outcomes.*

*The variable is not whether I am right. I have been right every single
time and being right is not the input.*

He wrote the last line and did not like it and left it standing.

*The variable is whether the person can afford it. And I have never
once, in eleven years, asked that question before opening my mouth,
because it is not a question about the number.*
