# Chapter 74, Scene 2 — The Date

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the north fields. Jiraiya, age fifty-four.
POV: Jiraiya. ~3,780 words.*

---

Jiraiya had taken the brakes off a boy before.

It had been a long time ago and a long way from here, in a country
where it rained without stopping, and the boy had been small and
sick and frightened of his own eyes, and Jiraiya had sat him down
in a wet field not unlike this one and told him that he was going
to change the world. He had meant it. It had been true. He had
been so entirely certain of it that he had left, afterwards, and
gone home, and got on with a life, and the boy had changed the
world exactly as advertised and then died of it, and Jiraiya had
found out the way you always found out about Ame, which was late
and from a stranger.

He had never once decided that he had been wrong to say it.

He had also never once said it again, to anybody, in thirty
years, and he was aware — standing in ruined ground on a Monday
in the third month with an eighteen-year-old getting to his feet
in front of him — that he was about eight minutes from saying a
version of it now.

*Different boy,* he thought. *Different mechanism entirely. That
one had the power and needed the reason. This one has the reason
and has spent his whole life declining the power.*

*And the same trade, old man. Exactly the same trade. You take
the governor off a good one and you find out what he was for, and
you do not get to know in advance, and there is no version of
this where you keep your hands clean and also hand her back a man
who survives spring.*

"Say the creed," he said again.

The boy said it.

"*I am not going to get stronger. Find out how it works.*"

He said it the way you say a thing you have said ten thousand
times — flat, quick, no weight anywhere in it, the delivery of a
man reciting his own address — and Jiraiya let the field hold it
for a second and then went in.

"What's it for?"

"It is a method statement," said Sazare. "It says: strength is not
the object, the mechanism is the object. Every article I have ever
made came out of asking why a thing holds instead of asking how
hard I can push it. It is the reason I am useful. It is also the
reason I am—" he stopped, and corrected, because he corrected
himself in front of people, which after nine weeks Jiraiya still
found faintly extraordinary— "it is the reason I am *interesting*,
which is a different claim and a smaller one, and I am aware I
was about to make the larger one."

"Good. That's the version you tell boards." Jiraiya walked a
half-circle round him through the churn. "Now do it the way you'd
do it if somebody handed it to you on a bench. It's an article,
kid. Somebody made it. Read it."

He watched the boy's face change, and it was the change he had
been fishing for since the first week: the moment the instrument
came round and pointed at its own housing, and the housing turned
out to have been made by somebody with hands.

"...Stroke width," said Sazare slowly. "Order of laying. Where the
maker got tired."

"There he is."

"It is two sentences and they are not the same age." He had gone
still, in the way he went still over a casting. "The second one is
the older one. *Find out how it works* is eleven years old — that
is a child of seven in a shop with a cracked sample-piece, that is
the whole of what I am, and it was never in question. The first
sentence was laid on the front of it afterwards, at fifteen, on a
deck." His hands had come up slightly, the way they did when he was
holding a thing that was not there. "The first sentence is a
*closure*. It was laid last, over a going concern, to shut it."

"And what's it shutting?"

Nothing for a moment. The crow. The ground draining.

"It is a governor," said Sazare.

Jiraiya stopped walking.

"Say the rest."

"It is a governor and I did not know I had written one." The voice
was still level and had begun, very slightly, to cost. "Eleven
clauses on the sword. One clause on me. Same architecture — a
ceiling laid as the deepest course so that every fragment inherits
it, and the whole thing works precisely because it operates without
the maker's attention. I have been carrying a governed article for
three years and admiring the design, and it has been in the front
of four notebooks in my own hand the entire time."

"And the trigger," said Jiraiya. "Come on. You built it. What does
it fire on?"

He watched him get there. It took eleven seconds and it was not
pleasant to watch.

"Capacity," said Sazare.

"Say it in full."

"It does not fire on danger and it does not fire on other people
and it does not fire on wanting. It fires on *capacity*. It is not a
rule about what I do." He had gone somewhere very quiet. "It is a
ceiling on what I am able to do at all."

---

Jiraiya let him stand in it and did not sit down and did not soften
his face, because there were four or five places in a man's life
where the kindest available thing was to stay exactly where you
were.

*Here it comes,* he thought. *He'll fight now. He'd better fight
now. If he takes this lying down I've got the wrong reading and we
start again on Tuesday.*

The boy fought.

"Then let me put the reasoning on the record," said Sazare, "before
you take it off me, because I have never once said the whole of it
out loud, and if it is going to be struck this morning it is going
to be struck accurately.

"On the day, my hands came up.

"There was no second in which I decided. I want that stated
precisely, because I have gone looking for that second for five
years and it is not in the record and it never happened. I was
thirteen and a half and eleven yards back, and my hands came up the
way they came up over a frame — the exact motion, both hands out,
heels turned in, which is how everybody in my family started for
four hundred years — and I put a spike through an executioner one
hundred and forty seconds before nineteen people in that square
were ready to move.

"Twenty-two men on that barrier had a standing order, and the order
was not cruelty and it was not complicated. If a sentence is
interrupted, you carry out the sentence. There were four men on the
platform already, because there were always four men on the
platform, and they were nine feet closer than the eight coming over
the back."

He was not looking at Jiraiya. He was looking at the churn, and his
voice had not moved off level by one degree, which was, Jiraiya
thought, the worst thing in that field all morning.

"My uncle went first. Then my mother. Then my father. Then my
sister, who got both bound hands up, because they had tied them in
front, because the steps were steep.

"That is the timing, and the timing was mine, and I have never once
claimed I did not see it.

"So." He turned round. "Run the design problem, Jiraiya-sama. A
component in me produced a lethal article without consulting me, in
public, at thirteen, and killed four people by the timing. What is
the remediation?

"You cannot govern a decision that does not occur. There is nothing
to put a clause on. Consent, restraint, judgement, discipline —
every one of them requires a decision to attach to, and there was no
decision, and a man with no access to his own initiative cannot
regulate it.

"Which leaves exactly one variable in the entire system.

"If the hands are going to come up on their own, then the only
available safety measure is a hand that cannot reach.

"That is the sentence. It was never humility and it is not a
philosophy. It is a ceiling, laid as the deepest course, so that
whatever comes out of me without permission is small enough to be
survived. I wrote it at fifteen with the data I had, and it is the
soundest piece of engineering I have ever done, and if the next
thing you say is that it was a frightened child's mistake then you
have not read the file and I will not accept the finding."

The field was quiet. Fukasaku, on the cloth, had not moved at all.

"It isn't a mistake," said Jiraiya.

Sazare blinked.

"It's a good clause. Correctly drafted, addresses the actual fault,
obeyed to the letter for three years without one lapse — and if
you'd brought it to me at fifteen I'd have signed it as witness."
He came forward through the churn. "And I'm going to condemn it in
one sentence, and you're going to hate the sentence, because you
already have the evidence for it.

"It doesn't work.

"You wrote the proof yourself. It's in an incident report with her
signature on it."

---

He watched that land, and he watched the boy not understand it for
about a second and a half, and then understand it completely.

"Twentieth of the twelfth," said Jiraiya. "Say what happened to
your hands."

"...They came up without me."

"Louder. You said it in this field on day one and you said it
better than I could."

"It was not a decision," said Sazare. "It was a side effect. A
reflex wearing my hands, arrived and regretted in the same second.
There was no lever there to pull."

"Same mechanism."

"Same mechanism."

"Ten years after the square. With the clause in force, in the front
of four books, in your own hand, obeyed every single day, never once
revised." Jiraiya stopped four feet off. "And what came out of your
hands on the twentieth wasn't a spike, kid. It was a thing that
*grows*, and feeds, and turns the ground into more of itself, and it
went into a boy's leg — and if it had gone a hand's width the other
way he'd have bled out on a training ground in front of the Hokage
of the Land of Fire, and there is not a medic breathing who'd have
got to him.

"So run *your* design problem. Three years of *I am not going to get
stronger*, perfectly obeyed. Did the ceiling hold?"

Nothing.

"Did the ceiling hold, Sazare."

"...No."

"No. Capacity arrived anyway. It arrived because you are a man who
finds out how things work, and that is a machine no promise on earth
switches off — your own second sentence went round the back of your
first one and built the exact thing the first one existed to
prevent, and it took ten years and never once slowed down." He let
it sit. "That clause has been standing on the neck of the only
useful thing in the sentence since the day you wrote it, and the
useful thing won anyway, and got no supervision at all while it was
doing it. That's the finding. Your two sentences have been at war
for three years. The wrong one was winning the argument and the
right one was winning the work."

---

"Now the part you'll want to fight and won't be able to," said
Jiraiya. "What stopped the twentieth from being the square?"

The boy stood in the ruined ground and did not answer.

"You know the answer. It's the same answer as the one about your
hand and the plinth and the fifteenth of the eighth, and I've read
that report too."

"...The close."

"The close. You got there and you shut it, and the boy kept his
leg." Jiraiya put both hands in his belt. "And you could shut it
because by then you'd spent eleven years finding out exactly how
that material behaves — because you'd got *better*, kid, not
smaller — and at thirteen and a half you had nothing in the world
but a reflex and forty thousand mornings over a frame.

"Your hands are going to come up again. That's not a threat and I'm
not going to pretend I can train it out of you; I've been trying for
nine weeks and it's the one thing in your file I can't reach. They
came up in a square, they came up on a training ground, and one day
they'll come up on a road.

"So the variable was never how far they reach. You picked the wrong
one, with the best available reasoning, at fifteen, and I'd have
picked it too.

"The variable is *what you know when they get there*.

"A boy with a reflex and no craft kills four people by the timing. A
man with the same reflex and eleven years of craft puts a thing
through a shin and then closes it in front of witnesses, and
the boy walks out of theatre two. Same hands. Same absence of a
decision. The entire difference is the eleven years — and your creed
has been calling those eleven years a *betrayal* the whole time."

The boy had gone very white.

"Say it back to me," said Jiraiya, "in your own words, because
you'll never believe it in mine."

It took a while.

"Staying small does not make the accident small," said Sazare. "It
makes the accident *unskilled*."

"There it is."

---

"And the last thing," said Jiraiya, "and then the old man gets his
turn and I'll stop.

"Say who you made the promise to."

He did not answer for a long moment.

"Kohaku."

"Yes." Jiraiya nodded once. "So striking that sentence is breaking a
promise to your sister. That's what's happening this morning, out
loud, on a date, in a field, and I want it named accurately before
you do it."

"You are not going to tell me that she would have—"

"No. I'm not." He picked his coat up off the fence post and shook
the mud off it. "I never met her, I have no idea what she'd have
wanted, and men my age telling boys your age what their dead sisters
would have wanted is the oldest cheap trick in this trade and I have
watched it ruin better than you. She's not available for comment.
That's exactly what makes it a real promise, and it's exactly why
breaking it costs.

"So it costs. You break it, out loud, on a date, in front of
witnesses, and you carry that, and I'm taking none of it off you.
That's the whole ceremony. That's why we're not doing footwork."

---

"Boy," said Fukasaku.

He had come off the cloth at some point without either of them
registering it, which after four hundred years he could presumably
do in a room full of sensors, and he stood in the churn at about
knee height with his arms folded.

"The wager," he said. "Three questions, one question in three
coats. You have had the first coat. You will have the second one
now, because it is due this morning and not in June, and because
your teacher has been running my mountain's rite on you for nine
weeks and has not once had the courtesy to say what it was."

Jiraiya said nothing whatsoever, which was as close to a guilty
plea as he intended to come in front of a student.

"How is a sage trained," said Fukasaku. "First coat: the body.
Graduation, a medium, a dose no ambition can increase. You have that
one. Second coat: *the mind that consents to get out of the way* —
and every tadpole thinks that means calm, and every tadpole is
wrong, and Ma and I have spent four centuries correcting it.

"It does not mean calm. A calm mind still grips. It means a mind
with nothing left to defend at the moment the flood arrives.

"And a mind gets there one way only. Not meditation. Not years.
Certainly not talent. It gets there by having *settled what it is
for*, out loud, in advance, where others heard it." The old toad's
yellow eye came round. "On our mountain no tadpole goes near the
oil until he has stood in front of the pool and said what he intends
to be for. Nine years old. Said again at twenty, and at forty, and
every single time it changes, on a stated day, witnessed — because
an unsettled account is a *grip*, and a mind that grips is a place
where the world stops moving through and starts collecting."

"I have an answer to that question," said Sazare. "I have had one
since I was fifteen."

"You have a *verdict*," said Fukasaku. "That is a different article,
and I would like you to hear the difference, because it is the whole
of the second coat.

"A boy of thirteen ran an inquiry with a sample of one, on the worst
morning of his life, with no counsel and no witness and not one
elder left alive to check his working. He returned a finding against
himself, and he has been serving it for five years without appeal,
and the sentence he passed was that he would intend to be nothing in
particular, permanently, as a safety measure.

"That is not what you are for. That is what you decided you must not
be allowed to become.

"And then you went into the flood at seventeen carrying it." The
arms unfolded. "I am not going to explain the connection, boy. You
have read the physics and you are quicker than I am. A man holding a
verdict on himself is holding something. He grips. What comes up out
of the world does not pass through a grip — it stops there, and it
collects, and where it collects is where the stone starts, and you
have a hand that by rights ought to be standing on a plinth in a
garden on my mountain."

Nobody said anything for a while.

"It is not a punishment and it is not your fault and it is not the
only cause," said Fukasaku. "It is a *mechanism*, and you have been
living beside it for seven months calling it an accident. Now: your
teacher is about to give you a date, and you will think it is about
your character. It is not. On my mountain it is safety equipment, it
is four hundred years old, and we have buried the ones who skipped
it."

---

"Right," said Jiraiya, into the silence, briskly, because somebody
had to move the morning along and it was in the job. "Sixteenth of
the third month. Say the time, Fukasaku-sama, you time everything."

"Eleven minutes past seven."

"Eleven minutes past seven. First half of the creed: struck.
Effective now. It comes out of the front of all four books in your
own hand and it does not sit there one more day pretending to be the
standing text."

"...And the replacement," said Sazare.

"Not this morning."

The boy looked up.

"You'll want to write it inside twenty minutes, because you're a man
who cannot stand an open finding, and you'll write something
beautiful, and it'll be a slogan, and slogans go in the front of
books and rot there." Jiraiya put the coat on at last. "It's due
Friday. Six sharp, this field, in your own hand, in all four books,
and you bring me book one to read it out of, because I want it in
the same object as the sentence it replaces.

"And one condition, or you'll get this wrong: don't write me a
creed. You're no good at creeds — the last one took you three years
to notice was load-bearing. Write me a *specification*. Something a
man could build to.

"Four days. Go and have your noon; you look like a casualty, and if
she sees you like this before you've eaten I'll hear about it in a
memo."

---

He watched the boy go up the lane with his coat and his notebook and
his four days, and he stood in the ruined field with an old toad at
his knee, and did not feel anything as simple as satisfaction.

*Done,* he wrote at noon, on the wall, in the shorthand.

*Governor off. 16/3, 07:11, witnessed. He found the trigger himself
inside eleven seconds, which is eleven seconds, and he fought the
finding first with the best defence anybody has ever handed me, and
that is the only reason I'd trust the surrender.*

*Note for my own file, since nobody else will ever want it: he has
spent five years believing the fault was the reaching. It was the
timing. A hundred and forty seconds. Nineteen people in that square
were going to move regardless, and four of his were going to be
standing on those boards when they did, and the only world in which
that family walks off that platform is one where nobody anywhere
ever interrupts a sentence in Kirigakure, which is not a world that
has been available since before he was born.*

*He would not accept that from me and I did not offer it. Handing a
man absolution he has not asked for is how you lose him. It stays in
this book.*

*He is going to write something on Friday that I will be quoting for
the rest of my life. I know that the way you know weather.*

*And the other thing, entered once and never again: I have done this
exactly one time before, in the rain, to a boy who deserved it, and
I was right, and he is dead.*

*So: watch him all year. Every week. The brake is off, and the brake
was the only thing standing between that yard and four countries, and
I took it off in a wet field on a Monday because the alternative was
handing her a corpse in a good coat.*

*She will never ask me whether I was sure.*

*Which is the whole of what that woman thinks of me, and it is more
than I have earned, and I intend to spend the year earning it.*
