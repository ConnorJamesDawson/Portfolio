# Chapter 45, Scene 1 — The Second Paper

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,444 words.*

---

The paper was thirty-one years old and it was about splints.

That was the whole of it. A staged comparison of four immobilisation
methods for the mid-shaft tibia, run at a field station in the Land of
Hot Water during a border action nobody had bothered to name, by a
medic called Wakabayashi whose forename had been abbreviated to an
initial on the title page and whose entire subsequent career appeared
to consist of not publishing anything else. Ochiai had given it to him
off the fourth bay with three others and had said, in the voice of a
man handing over something he thought was a waste of everybody's time,
*you asked for protocols and this is a protocol.*

Sazare had taken it for the method. He had wanted to see how somebody
else built an arm and matched a cohort and reported a failure, and he
had got nine pages into it at half past one in the morning in a hot
room with a comparison table face down on the table behind him, and it
was a perfectly ordinary piece of work, and then he read this:

*Immobilisation was applied without chakra assistance in all four arms,
in order that the comparison should be of the mechanical properties of
the splint and not of the practitioner. Where chakra assistance was
subsequently trialled (see Appendix C) it was restricted to shape
transformation only; no nature transformation was permitted at any
stage, both because of the burn risk to the periosteum and because the
two produce effects of an entirely different order which the design
could not have separated.*

He read it again.

He read it a third time.

Then he sat up.

---

It was not the content. The content was about splints and he could have
written a better paper about splints at seventeen.

It was that Wakabayashi had used two categories, side by side, in a
subordinate clause, in the middle of a methods section, without
defining either of them.

*Shape transformation.*

*Nature transformation.*

The way you would write *length* and *weight*. The way Kanzaki wrote
*tare* and *gross*. As things everybody reading the document would
have, because of course they would have them, because they were the
first two words you learned.

Sazare got up and went to the window and stood there.

*I do not have those.*

*I have never had those. I have never seen either of those phrases in
my life and I have just this second understood both of them from
context in about four seconds, which means they are not difficult, which
means they are not advanced, which means the reason I do not have them
is not that they are hard.*

He came back to the table.

*It is a splint paper. It is thirty-one years old and it was rejected —
it has the rejection annotation on the cover sheet in a hand that is not
Wakabayashi's — and it is about the mid-shaft tibia, and a man writing
it in a tent in the Land of Hot Water assumed that any medic who picked
it up would know the difference between changing what chakra IS and
changing what chakra DOES.*

*I have been changing what chakra does since I was seven years old.*

*I did not know it had a name.*

---

He did not sleep, and there was a shift at six, and he worked the whole
of it and was competent throughout and could afterwards remember almost
none of it.

What he did instead, in the gaps — in the four minutes between the
dressing round and the second-floor handover, at the sluice with his
hands under the tap, walking the corridor with a chart he was not
reading — was go back through eleven years and re-read it.

*I lay a course. The course is an instruction and the crystal takes it
up and the crystal is a material. That has been the entire model since
I was nine. Everything I have ever written is inside that model, and
the model works, and the model produces correct predictions, and I have
three hundred and eleven entries in a ledger proving that it produces
correct predictions.*

*And in eleven years the model has never once contained an account of
what I am actually doing with my hands.*

*I have described what happens to the stone. I have never described what
happens before it gets to the stone.*

He stopped in the corridor outside the second-floor treatment room and
stood there for long enough that Emiko came out and asked him if he
wanted something and he said no and she looked at him and went back in.

*Twenty-two people in a street. Six short faces instead of one long one.
A slab on my own forearm in about a second and a half.*

*Nobody has ever asked me how I did that. Kakashi asked me what I would
build differently. Kanzaki asks me what it weighs. Yagi asks me what it
does to a patient. Ibiki asks me what it can be used for.*

*Not one person in four countries has ever asked me what the actual
operation is, and the reason is obvious and I have never once seen it:
they all assume it is the bloodline. The bloodline is the answer to
every question anybody has ever wanted to ask me about the mechanism,
and it is not an answer, it is a place where an answer is supposed to
go.*

---

He went to Yagi at the end of the shift, in the sluice, because the
sluice at the end of a shift was where the two of them said things.

"Can I ask you something that's going to sound stupid."

"You've never once asked me anything that sounded stupid," said Yagi,
"which is a fault and not a compliment, so please go ahead."

"What's shape transformation."

Yagi stopped drying her hands.

"...In what sense?"

"In the sense that I don't know what it is and I need somebody to tell
me."

She looked at him for a moment with an expression he could not read at
all, which was rare, and then she folded the towel over the rail and
leaned back against the bench and gave it to him straight, which was
why he had come to her and not to anybody else.

"Chakra does two things," she said. "You can change what it is —
that's nature. Fire, water, earth, wind, lightning, and the ones people
have that are combinations. And you can change what it *does*: the
shape it takes, how fast it moves, how it's held, how much of it goes
where. That's shape. They're independent. Most techniques use both. A
few use only one."

"That's it?"

"That's the first day. That is *literally* the first day — it's the
opening lecture of the first term at the Academy, and every child in
that building gets it at eleven, and they get it before they're allowed
to touch a kunai." She folded her arms. "Ruri. Are you telling me
nobody's ever—"

"I came in at sixteen," said Sazare. "Through the hospital. As a
fabricator, on a work order, with a file that said what I could already
do."

Yagi shut her eyes briefly.

"Of course they didn't," she said. "Of course nobody did. You arrived
able to do the thing, so every single person who might have taught you
the basics looked at you and concluded somebody else already had."

"Yes."

"Four years."

"Two years in this village. Nine before that in three others." He put
his hands in his pockets, which he did not do. "The column in
Kanzaki's ledger. The paragraph on natural energy in the exam I sat at
seventeen. This."

"That's three."

"That's three," said Sazare, "and I've stopped calling it a
coincidence."

---

Yagi walked out with him as far as the stair.

"Go to the library," she said. "Not to me — I'll give you the medic's
version and the medic's version is four sentences and a warning about
burns. Ochiai will find you the real one."

"I've had four books out of Ochiai this week."

"Then have a fifth." She stopped at the turn. "And Ruri — ask him for
the beginner's material. Not the advanced. I know exactly what you're
about to do, which is walk in there and ask for something that sounds
appropriate to your standing, and you'll come out with a monograph
you can't use and you'll have wasted a fortnight being too proud to be
eleven."

Sazare thought about that.

"That's accurate," he said.

"I've known you two years."
