# Chapter 66, Scene 1 — Seven Versions

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2400 words.*

---

By the second of the month there were seven versions, and he had all of
them in the back of the grey notebook in the order they had arrived,
which was not the order of their quality.

Version five had him underground for the whole fight, which would have
been sensible and had not occurred to him. Version six gave Naruto a
broken arm — the arm was fine; the arm had never been touched — and had
him carried from the field by six men, when it had been one stretcher,
two bearers, and a medic who would not let anybody else steer. Version
seven, new that morning off a woman queueing for bread, had the whole
thing lasting an hour. It had lasted six minutes and forty seconds and
he had the count.

The fishmonger's boy's version still stood alone at the top of the
page, eleven words long, and still the only accurate account in the
village including his own.

*He made the crystal come alive.*

*Everything else in circulation is wrong in my favour,* he wrote under
version seven. *The story continues to buy me more than the fight won.*

He was weighing reject aggregate when the gate went, and he knew the
gate wrong before he knew it at all, because the rhythm of it was wood
on wood — two beats, a knock and a lean — and nobody leaned on that
gate.

"It's open," he said, which was true for one person in the village and
had been true for exactly that person since the seventeenth of last
month.

Naruto came in on crutches.

He came in the way he did everything, at whatever volume it had, no
bracket needed, nothing to construct — a man standing in a field, even
on two sticks with his left trouser leg cut up the seam to clear the
frame. The frame was Corps issue, four rods and six lashings, and
somebody had signed it along the outer rod in paint.

Sazare read the signatures upside down from four feet. Sakura's. Two
names off the ward. Something in a circle that was probably Konohamaru.

"You're not walking," said Sazare.

"I'm *crutching*," said Naruto. "It's faster than walking. Watch." He
crossed the yard in four swings, put himself onto the bench by the
scale with a thump that would have cost anybody else a wince, and
leaned the crutches on the reject bin like a man racking tools. "Ten
days, the bone. That was Tuesday. Baa-chan says three weeks before the
leg takes me, six before I run on it. She says if I get on it early
she'll break the other one where the paperwork's easier."

"That is in character."

"Yeah, she seemed really comfortable saying it." Naruto looked around
the yard with open interest, the way he looked at everything, as if the
world were obliged to be worth it and generally was. "So this is where
you live."

"This is where I work."

"Same thing though, right?"

Sazare considered the sentence, found it structurally sound, and let it
stand.

*He does that,* he wrote later. *Arrives at the true thing by not
looking where he is going. Sixth documented instance.*

---

"I need to ask you about the leg," said Sazare. "For the record I keep.
You can say no."

"You can't ask me how I am first?"

"How are you."

"Great," said Naruto, delighted. "Ask about the leg."

He got the notebook. Naruto watched him get it with the particular
satisfaction of a man watching a heron do the thing herons are known
for.

"The forty seconds," said Sazare. "From the article entering to the
close. I have my account from the outside. Nobody has the inside and
you are the only person who will ever have it. What did it feel like?"

Naruto was quiet for a moment, and the quiet was itself unusual enough
that Sazare noted the length of it. Four seconds.

"Cold," he said. "First just cold, like river cold, and I thought,
fine, it's stone, stone's cold. But then—" He frowned at his own knee,
hunting, and Sazare did not help him, which cost the usual amount.
"Then it was drinking. That's the only way I can say it. Not the leg.
*Me.* Like it found the middle of me and put a straw in. And the
drinking didn't hurt — that's the bit I keep thinking about. The bone
hurt like anything. The drinking felt like—" He stopped.

"Say it wrong," said Sazare. "I'll take it wrong. Wrong is data."

"It felt like being *useful*," said Naruto. "Like the thing was really,
really glad I was there."

The yard was quiet. Somewhere over the wall a cart went by with one dry
axle, and Sazare heard himself not say four different things.

He wrote: *Subject experienced intake as directional and personal.
Article read to its host as glad.*

His hand was level. He made it be.

"Is that bad?" said Naruto, watching him. "You've gone still. Stiller.
You're normally about a chair's worth of still and you've gone full
furniture."

"It is the most useful sentence anybody has given me since the
twentieth," said Sazare, which was true, and, "I do not like it," which
he had not planned to say and said anyway.

"Yeah," said Naruto. "That's why I said it. Sakura-chan says you write
everything down. I figured if it was going in a book it should be the
real one."

---

They ate in the yard, because Naruto had brought lunch in a sling bag
without mentioning it and unpacked it without asking, which was four
buns and a flask of soup, and the arithmetic of it — that a man on
crutches had carried soup across half the village on the second day
of the year — sat in Sazare's chest at an angle he decided not to
measure.

"So what did you get?" said Naruto, around a bun. "For winning. There
was a bet, right? The old pair had a bet, everyone says so, and
Baa-chan doesn't lose quietly, and she *won*, which — nobody knows what
that means. Iruka-sensei says an era's ended. What was the prize?"

"A piece of paper."

Naruto stopped chewing.

"You got a *paper*."

"A good paper."

"They gave me a *contract with toads* and you got a *paper*." He shook
his head slowly. "You need better people doing your deals. When I'm
Hokage you'll get proper prizes. A month of ramen. A house."

"I will hold you to the ramen."

"See, that's a deal," said Naruto, jabbing the last of the bun at him.
"That's how deals sound." He looked at the leg stretched out in front
of him in its rods and lashings, and flexed something experimentally,
and Sazare watched the wince arrive and get overruled. "The new bit
itches. Inside. Baa-chan says that's the muscle knitting and itching
means it's winning. Six weeks, and then you and me go again."

"You will win," said Sazare.

"Obviously," said Naruto, cheerful as weather. "That's not why."

Sazare put down the flask cup.

"Then why?"

"Because you're *there* now," said Naruto, as if it were the easiest
thing in the world and he was being asked to confirm the sky. "First
time we fought, you weren't. You said it yourself — I asked where you
wanted to be and there wasn't one. At the end there was one. That's the
guy I want. Fighting people who aren't there is—" he hunted briefly —
"it's like arm-wrestling a coat."

*Like arm-wrestling a coat,* Sazare wrote, and under it: *He has
diagnosed in four days of lying down what took Jiraiya-sama a page of
questions. Different instrument, same reading. The reading is now
unanimous.*

"Also," said Naruto, standing his crutches up and getting onto them in
one practised heave, "when the frame's off, you're coming to mine. I
can't cook till I can stand at the pot, so it'll be after the three
weeks. You've had me at yours twice now. That's a debt. That's how it
works — Sakura-chan explained it once about something else and I've
decided it applies."

Sazare stood in his own yard and experienced, in order: the impulse to
say that nobody owed him anything; the observation that this was the
first reciprocal invitation of its kind he had ever been issued; and
the decision, arriving with unusual speed, that only one answer kept
faith with the man who had carried soup on crutches.

"Yes," he said.

"Good," said Naruto, already swinging for the gate. "Don't bring a
notebook. Bring — actually bring the notebook, I want to see what you
write about my cooking. Write something *nice*."

"I will write something true."

"Even better," said Naruto, and knocked the gate twice on the way out,
wood on wood, for no reason at all except that it was a thing a person
could do, and was gone up the lane at a pace no man on crutches had any
business making, and the yard settled back around the absence of him
the way water settles, gradually, and not quite to the old level.

---

He worked until the light went. Then he took out the grey notebook and
did the day's honesty before the honesty could age, because a note
written late is a note written by a different man.

*2/1. Seven versions and the boy's still leads. N. came to the yard
uninvited (second instance; the category now has a population). Bone
knitted day ten as projected. The boundary she showed me holds: the
half it accounts for is knitting on schedule and I am not going at the
other half. Not for eleven months.*

*He gave me the inside of the forty seconds. The article read to him
as glad. I have built nine hundred articles and not one of them has
ever been described to me as glad, because not one of them has ever
been inside a person's chakra before, because until the twentieth I
had never done that, because it was never, at any point in eleven
years, a thing I imagined doing.*

*Note to the file I have not opened yet, the one Jiraiya-sama starts
on the thirteenth: an informed opponent will not need to look at the
floor. He will feel the drinking begin. The stealth of the technique
was never stealth. It was strangeness, and strangeness is a coin you
spend once.*

*Last. He is coming back in six weeks to lose to me or beat me, he
does not greatly care which, and he called the version of me that can
now be fought "the guy I want." Filed without comment. The file is
the comment.*

He closed the book, and banked the brazier, and went up to the tower
through the early dark, past the lit window on the second floor where
the diary said senjutsu and the diary, as always, did not lie.
