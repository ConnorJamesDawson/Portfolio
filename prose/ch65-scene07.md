# Chapter 65, Scene 7 — The Debt

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the north store. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1950 words.*

---

It had been in his coat for eleven days.

He got it out on the thirty-first at about nine at night, at the bench,
with the lamp on and nothing else going, and put it on the felt and
looked at it the way he looked at an article that had come back from a
ward.

*Four lines. Her hand. The twentieth of the twelfth month.*

*No reference number.*

*Unconditional. Any time, any purpose, no expiry. She does not get to ask
what it is for and she does not get to refuse it.*

*I have carried it for eleven days and I have taken it out four times and
I have never once got as far as thinking about what to spend it on,
because I have been stuck on the step before that one the entire time.*

---

*Right. Do it properly and stop going round it.*

*Why can I not use this?*

*One: I do not want anything.*

*That is the answer I have given myself four times and it is not true and
I have known it was not true since about the twenty-second. I want a
great many things. I have got about four hundred impossible items on a
roll of paper on that shelf and I want every one of them.*

*Two: it feels wrong.*

*That is not an answer. That is a reading with nothing under it and I
have got four hundred pages on people who stopped there.*

*Three, and this is the one, and I got it on the twenty-fourth in a
corridor and have been refusing to write it down for a week.*

He got the book out.

*Tenth month. The yard. I asked her how the first contract came about and
she told me and then I thought about signing one.*

*And I decided not to, and the reason was eleven words, and I have said
them to two mountains and to a Hokage and to a toad:*

If she is contracted, she comes because she is contracted, and then I
would never be able to tell the difference again.

He put the pencil down on the bench.

*It is the same shape.*

---

*Say it in full and do not let yourself off any part of it.*

*I refused a contract with Katsuyu because a contract removes the
question. She would come, and she would be doing it because she was
obliged to, and there would be no way on earth for me to know whether she
would have come anyway, and the knowing is the entire content of the
thing.*

*I have got a piece of paper in my hand that obliges the Hokage of
Konohagakure to do one thing of my choosing, unconditionally, without
asking why and without the option of refusing.*

*It is a contract.*

*It is a contract with one clause in it and it runs the other way and I
have been carrying it round for eleven days as though it were a prize.*

*And the moment I use it, whatever she does in response to it is a thing
she was obliged to do.*

*And I will never be able to tell the difference again.*

He sat on the stool at the bench.

*I have spent five weeks with a woman who has arranged the whole of it so
that nothing false is ever said. She has not lied to me once. She refused
to say a sentence at twenty past four in the morning on the
twenty-first because I would have gone back and looked at the conditions
it was produced under, and she was right, and it is the most careful
thing anybody has ever done about me.*

*And I have got, in my coat, an instrument that would let me take
something off her and never know whether she meant it.*

*That is not a prize. That is the exact opposite of everything I have
built with that woman, and she handed it to me on the twentieth in four
lines with no reference on it, and I took it.*

---

*Right. So the options.*

*One: never use it.*

*Which I have been doing for eleven days and which is worse, and it took
me until tonight to see why.*

*An unused one is not neutral. It sits there. It sits there for forty
years and every single thing that woman ever does for me, for the rest of
both our lives, is going to be done by somebody who owes me an
unconditional favour.*

*She will not think about it. She will have forgotten it inside a year.*

*I will not. I have got four hundred pages that prove I will not.*

*So carrying it is the same corruption, applied continuously, at a lower
rate, for ever.*

*Two: destroy it.*

He looked at the lamp for about four seconds.

*No.*

*She wrote it on the twentieth after four hours in a theatre having
watched a thing on a training ground that put her the colour of the
underside of a leaf, and she had about eleven reasons not to and she did
it anyway, and she said the terms out loud on the Wednesday in front of a
witness specifically so that she could not decide afterwards that it did
not count.*

*Burning it is me deciding afterwards that it did not count.*

*It is the same act with better manners on it and she would know inside
about four seconds, and she has been paid the compliment of not being
managed by me for three years and I am not starting on the thirty-first
of the twelfth month.*

*Three.*

He stopped.

---

He got the small orange book out of his coat.

It was on the bench with the felt and the IOU and the lamp, and it had
about eleven of his own pencil marks in the margins and one folded corner
that he had made on the twenty-eighth and had not been able to account
for at the time.

*Chapter seven.*

*Nineteen pages. The two of them go somewhere for a day.*

*That is the whole of it. They get on a boat in the morning and they walk
about somewhere with a market in it and they eat something and they
argue about the price of a hat and they come back in the evening, and
there is nothing in it.*

*I read it twice.*

*It is the only chapter in that book with nothing wrong in it and it took
me until the second reading to work out why, which is that there is
nothing in it to be wrong. There is no figure. There is no anatomy and no
duration and no dimension. Nobody does anything that could be checked by
anybody.*

*And I folded the corner over and I did not know I was doing it and I did
not put a note on that chapter at all.*

*It is the one I liked.*

*Nineteen pages of two people buying a hat and I have thought about it
about nine times in three days and I put fourteen notes on the rest of
the book and none on that.*

---

*Right. Now the third option, and I have had it since about the eleventh
minute of this and I have been assembling the reasoning so that I do not
have to admit that I had the answer first.*

He picked the IOU up.

*It only stays an instrument while there is something on the other end of
it that she would not have given.*

*That is the whole of the mechanism and it is not complicated and I have
been three weeks getting to it.*

*The corruption is not the paper. The paper is four lines. The corruption
is the gap — between what I ask for and what she would have done anyway —
and everything in that gap is a thing I have taken and cannot verify.*

*So close the gap.*

*Spend it on something she would have given for nothing.*

*And then there is no gap, and the paper is worth exactly nothing the
instant it is used, and it stops existing, and nobody has taken anything
off anybody.*

He put it down on the felt.

*And it has got to be something I would never have asked for on my own.*

*Because if I would have asked for it, then I do not need the paper and
the paper stays in my coat, and I am back where I started with an unused
one sitting between us for forty years.*

*So: a thing she would give me for nothing.*

*That I have never once asked for.*

*And there is exactly one of those and I have known what it is since
about the eleventh minute of this.*

---

He sat at the bench for a while.

*Three years.*

*Four hundred visits up that hill and about nine hundred dockets and
about six hundred hours in that office, and eleven weeks of the fourth
hour, and twenty-four nights in a room on the second floor.*

*And there has not been one day, ever, that was not about a thing.*

*Not one. There is no instance in three years of the two of us being
anywhere for any reason other than an article, a docket, a study, a
patient, a council, a report, a fight, or a wound.*

*The eleventh of the eleventh month was about a hill and eleven pages.
The twelfth was a kitchen and it was about the worst night either of us
has had. The twenty-first was about a question I had built for four days.
The second of the twelfth was about nought point one one grams.*

*Every single one.*

*Even the good ones. Especially the good ones.*

*Nineteen pages of two people buying a hat.*

He picked up the pencil and wrote one line on a fresh page and then sat
and looked at it, and it was the shortest entry he had made all year.

*I am going to ask her for a day.*

---

*And I want the reasoning on the page before I do it, because at some
point in about nine years I am going to want to know whether I meant it
or whether it was a manoeuvre, and I would rather have it in ink on the
thirty-first than reconstruct it.*

*It is a manoeuvre. Half of it.*

*It is the only way I have found to discharge an instrument that should
never have been made without either using it against her or carrying it
between us for forty years, and I built it as a solution and I am not
going to pretend otherwise in my own book.*

*And it is the thing I want.*

*Both. I have got no way of separating them and I have been trying since
half past nine and I do not think they come apart, and I have decided
that a thing being useful does not stop it being true.*

*She is going to be angry.*

*Not at the day. At the size of it. She has handed me the largest object
in the Land of Fire and I am going to spend it on a boat and a market and
an argument about a hat, and she is going to look at me for about four
seconds and then she is going to say something.*

*And I have got no idea what.*

He put the pencil down and looked at the lamp.

*Which is the first time in three years that I have gone into a room with
that woman without a model of what she is going to do.*

*Good.*
