# Chapter 68, Scene 3 — The Oil Problem

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the north fields. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1540 words.*

---

On the Tuesday the field had a different geometry. The swords stayed
in the ground. Fukasaku's cloth had been moved to the centre, a
brazier had appeared beside it from somewhere with no visible
logistics, and the arrangement said, in the universal grammar of
arrangements: today is a classroom.

"The wager," said Fukasaku, without preamble, when they were
settled. "Shikkotsu won it fair, and the terms were three questions
answered in person, truthfully, at length, at the winner's
choosing. The slug chose. All three of her questions turned out to
be one question wearing three coats, and Ma and I argued the
protocol of that for two days and then gave up, because the terms
say at length, and one question asked three ways simply gets
answered at three lengths." He settled his arms deeper into their
fold. "How is a sage trained. Sit still, boy. This is the first
answer, and I have been giving some version of it for four hundred
years, and I have never once given it off the mountain."

Sazare sat still.

"You cannot train a sage," said Fukasaku. "That is the whole of
the first answer, and everything after it is the length. What you
train is a body that does not die of nature energy, and a mind
that consents to get out of the way — and then the sage arrives
or does not, on its own schedule, like weather. Anyone who tells
you otherwise is selling a mountain.

"Now the length. Nature energy is not chakra. Your chakra is
yours — it answers you, it has your grain in it, your hand. What
comes up out of the world answers nobody. Take in more than the
body can hold in balance and it does not injure you the way
overwork injures you. It *converts* you. Stone, on our mountain.
The statues in the garden of Myōboku are not statues. That is the
first thing every tadpole is shown, and the showing is the whole
of the first year."

*Petrification,* wrote the part of Sazare that never stopped
writing. *He is describing the square. He is describing my hand.
They have a garden of it.*

"So the training is graduation," said Fukasaku. "You do not teach
a body to survive the flood by flooding it. You teach it in
increments — a controlled dose, held, survived, released, and the
body's tolerance rebuilt a fraction wider, and again, and again,
over years. And because a tadpole cannot meter what he cannot
feel, the metering is done from outside. Two ways. A teacher's
hand on the boy the whole time, feeling what he cannot — that is
the expensive way, one teacher per student, years per student.
And the oil."

The old toad let the word sit.

"Myōboku has a spring. The oil that comes up carries nature
energy the way water carries salt — dissolved, dilute,
*graduated*. A tadpole bathes in it and takes in at the skin a
dose no ambition of his can increase, because the oil decides the
concentration, not the boy. Do you understand what I am telling
you? The mountain's whole method — four hundred years of sages,
every one — stands on having a *medium*. A substance that holds
nature energy at a known strength, releases it at a known rate,
and cannot be talked into more by a boy in a hurry. The oil is
the syllabus. Fools think it is a shortcut. It is the opposite of
a shortcut. It is the thing that makes the long road survivable."

He looked at Sazare with four hundred years of level yellow
patience.

"And Shikkotsu has no spring. That is the answer to the question
the slug was actually asking, and she knew it before she spent
the wager, and she spent it anyway to hear me say it where you
could write it down. The slug road has the trigger — you found it
in a ditch, holding — and the slug road has the teacher's reason,
which is that forest's own patience. What the slug road has never
had, in the whole history of the world, is the oil. No medium.
No graduated dose. Which is why in four hundred years the slugs
have trained *no one*, and why every drop of nature energy you
have ever held arrived either as a flood or not at all. Your
twentieth of December was a boy falling into the spring. The
mountain's whole art is making sure nobody ever has to."

The brazier ticked. The frost held.

"Uncap it," said Jiraiya quietly. "Now. Read it to them exactly
as you wrote it."

He took out the grey notebook, and opened it to the ruled line,
and read it aloud in the field:

"Q. Where does an open article keep what it draws?"

---

He gave them the rest, because a question read to a class deserves
its workings: the blade, sixteen days open and now twenty-seven,
the month sheet, the mass unchanged, the order *improved*, the
income exceeding any imaginable upkeep. And then, because the
field had gone very quiet and the quiet had the specific density
of two old teachers arriving at the same place at the same speed,
he said the rest of it out loud for the first time:

"Crystal draws nature energy. That has been in a Corps file since
the autumn. An open article draws it continuously and — the
finding — retains something, somewhere, in a form I have not
located. I know how to write *store* — it is the thirteenth
element I own, and I have built a keyed store that fired. I know
how to grade a release the way I grade everything, because a
graded release is the whole of my craft. So the question under
the question, which I capped eleven days ago because Katsuyu-sama
correctly identified it as a fuse:

"If an article can take in nature energy, keep it, and give it
back at a rate I set in the lattice — then that article is a
medium. A known strength. A known rate. A dose that cannot be
talked into more, because the stone decides, and I have never
once built a stone that could be talked into anything."

He closed the notebook.

"Shikkotsu has no spring. But I grow the only substance on the
slug road that could be *made into one*, and I have been carrying
the first working prototype on my back for a month without
knowing what it was."

Nobody said anything for eleven seconds. He counted, because he
counted.

"Pa," said Jiraiya, at last, conversationally, to Fukasaku, "I
want it noted that I have known about that rule in his book since
Sunday, and I still didn't see the bottom of it, and you didn't
either, so neither of us gets to be wise about this afterwards."

"Noted," said Fukasaku, who had not taken his eyes off the
notebook. "Boy. Three things, and they are not suggestions. One:
that sentence does not get said again outside this field. Not to
the slug's Fridays, where it belongs and will go, with these two
present — but nowhere else, to no one, in no notation anybody can
read, and the reason is that you have just described, to the two
living people qualified to check your working, an artefact that
turns sages from a mountain's miracle into a *manufactured good*,
and there are perhaps four powers on this continent who would
burn a city to own the man who can grow one." He said it without
heat, as arithmetic. "Two: it is years away, and the years are
the safety, and any step that shortens the years gets my veto,
and I am telling you now I will use it. The oil took the mountain
generations to learn to keep. You will not out-hurry four hundred
years just because you are clever and in love.

"And three." The old toad's voice changed by a degree — the same
degree Tenzō's had changed over the teacup, the register of a
thing said once. "If it works — and I have sat on that mountain
long enough to know the shape of a thing that is going to work —
then the slug road opens. Not for you. You are the toll somebody
already paid. For whoever comes after you, on a road that has
been shut since the world was young because nobody could find
the spring. You would not be making a weapon, whatever the four
powers think. You would be making a *syllabus*. Hold the work to
that standard and Ma and I will hold the secret to ours."

Sazare sat in the cold with the notebook shut in his lap.

*The battery was never the invention,* he wrote that evening, on
the walk home, standing in the lane because it would not wait.
*The invention is the spring. I have been trying to build myself
a shortcut and the old man has re-specified it in one morning as
infrastructure — as the thing my whole line has always actually
made. Not weapons. Wells.*

*Three keepers now: a sage, an elder, and — Friday — a slug.*

*And her. The diary says senjutsu, and for once the diary is
going to underdescribe the evening.*
