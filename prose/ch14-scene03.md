# Chapter 14, Scene 3 — Things Get Fixed

*Act 2, part D. The Land of Hot Water. Jirō, age sixteen. POV: Jirō. ~2,400
words.*

---

The corridor was three men and a coastline, and the work was exactly what
he had said it would be in the interview, which pleased the part of him
that had built the answer and appalled some other part he kept in the
chest with the flag.

They moved as merchants' guards, papers good, masks packed. Rei ran the
detail — thirty, spare, a tracer of the paper school, all collation and
corroboration, decent enough to work for and impossible to drink with —
and the third was Kōga, retrieval-trained, who spoke perhaps forty words
a week and moved like the answer to a question you'd regret asking. They
had four files in the folder. Three were debt-grade, deserters of no
particular value, cover-work to justify the route.

The fourth was the fourth.

Jirō had read it so many times he could recite the annex, and the annex
was where the whole file lived, as far as he was concerned — one line of
some district clerk's careful hand, boiled to a caveat: *anomalous
aptitude, interrupted-cycle growth, recorded age 10.* Ten. He'd have been
in the second row by the window that year, two desks from — he did not
finish these thoughts any more; he had a place for them; the place was
full.

The trade was slow, and the trade was talk, and Jirō was rated best in
his intake because he had understood from the first week what the paper
school never quite believed: that a coastline is not a line of ground,
it is a line of *conversations*, and you do not sweep it, you sit in it.
You buy fish. You mend a wheel. You lose small change at cards to men
who'll remember you fondly. And you ask nothing, ever, because a
question is a stone dropped in still water and every ring of it says
*someone was asking* — you just talk, the way his whole family had
talked at every table of his childhood, and you let the coast do what
coasts do, which is mention things.

It took the coast five months to mention the stone-mender.

---

It came the way the true ones always came: sideways, as garnish, in a
story about something else entirely.

A drover in a harbour town two days south of the exchange at Shiranui,
complaining at cards about his sister's wedding — the cost of it, the
cousins of it — and in the middle of the complaint, like a currant in
dough: *"...and the pendant, her whole dowry-piece, chipped to bits on
the well-head, and the jeweller down here wanted the price of a calf,
so my brother-in-law carts it clear up the spring towns to some lad
who—"*

"Some lad who what?" said Jirō, pushing his losing hand in, keeping his
eyes on the deal.

"Mended it so you'd swear the chip was meant," said the drover. "Made
it a *style*. Charged fair, and all. My brother-in-law says half the
valley wears his work now. Says the boy looks at a broken thing like
it's telling him what it wants." He gathered the cards. "Wasted up a
mountain, my brother-in-law says. Lad like that could keep a shop in
the capital."

"What's he charge, then?" said Jirō, tradesman-idle, dealing the next
hand — and took the town's name, and the valley's, and the fact that
the boy was young, and quiet, and *looks at a broken thing like it's
telling him what it wants*, and banked all of it behind a losing pair
of sixes and a face he had learned at his mother's table.

*Wherever he stops, things get fixed.*

He had said it in an interview as an asset's pitch, a line built to
stand on. He had not expected — this was the thing he turned over that
night, on his back, in a harbour inn, listening to a foreign sea — he
had not expected it to be *load-bearing*. Four years, a war, a price on
him you could retire on, a whole apparatus of offices holding its
breath — and the trace, when it finally surfaced, was a dowry-piece
mended so kindly that a drover two valleys away was still talking
about it.

He'd have known it blind. He'd have known it off one fact in a
thousand. There was exactly one person on this coast who would take a
broken thing that could be hidden whole, and choose, for reasons no
report box would ever hold, to make the damage honest and call it the
design.

Rei marked the report *possible, unverified* off Jirō's own written
words, because Jirō wrote the number he held.

It was the hardest sentence he had ever written straight.

---

Yumoto, when they walked its lanes eleven days later as three guards
between contracts, was over.

He knew it within the hour — knew it the way the drover's coast knew
things, out of the grain of the talk — and spent two days confirming
what the first hour gave him, because the paper school required it, and
because every detail of the confirming hurt in a place the mask was
starting to fit over.

The stone-mender was gone. Left in the spring, the town said, easily,
to anyone; no mystery in it; trade's trade, boys move on. The porch at
the last house before the town — a treatment house, an old Kiri medic,
*retired, decades back, keep your voice down about it all the same* —
held a workbench, and a rail where something had stood and weathered a
rectangle into the wood, and nothing else. The medic himself came out
to them unasked, looked at three merchants' guards with a flat
unhurried assessment that Jirō's training recognised and his nerves
did not enjoy, and offered, before anyone had asked him anything:

"If it's the springs you're after, the west houses are cheaper and the
water's the same. If it's medicine, surgery's at the fourth hour. And
if it's the boy who mended things—" the old man's face did nothing at
all, which was itself a dialect Jirō knew from home — "you're the
third lot asking since spring, and you'll get what the other two got,
which is what I know: came up sick, worked honest, learned some
doctoring, left orderly. Settled his accounts. Even left the tray."

"Tray?" said Rei, pleasantly.

"Seed tray," said the medic. "For the herb beds." And looked at Jirō —
only at Jirō, out of the three of them, one beat too long, with eyes
that had read bodies for thirty years and were reading the set of a
shoulder, the wear of a strap, the *age* under the guard's kit — and
said, mildly, generally, to the porch at large: "Good teachers, that
boy had. Somewhere. You can always tell. Nothing on a road will kill
him quick, and nothing polite will catch him slow." He picked up his
basket. "Surgery's at the fourth hour, if any of you needs it. You'd
be surprised what men your trade carry and never mention."

They lodged two nights. Jirō did the lanes, the bathhouses, the
weighbridge, the ford — talk, cards, small change — and the town
mentioned things, because towns did, and every mentioned thing went
into him like grit.

A door-keeper at the new bathhouse, old ruin of a swordsman, who'd
taught the mender lad of a morning — *for the exercise*, said the town,
fondly — and who, when Jirō drifted the yard at first light to see for
himself, was standing in the middle of it stone sober doing a single
slow cut, over and over, to a count nobody living was keeping.

A smith who repriced Jirō's re-shod heel to half when he heard which
road they'd come by, *any friend of the springs*, and whose daughter
wore at her throat, on a work-day, over flour, a stone with two flat
honest facets where the eye went first.

And on the third morning, on the road out, at a wayside shrine above a
blood-warm pool, where Kōga washed and Rei checked the map and Jirō
stood with his cap off because his mother had raised him — a small
crooked blue thing on the offering shelf, upright against the back
wall, between a clay horse and a blank coin. Weathered. Tended. Hollow
at one end.

He looked at it for as long as a man decently looks at a shrine, and
then he put his cap on.

*Grown work,* said the part of him that had sat on a wall at eight
while a friend's whole face lit up over a hollow failure. *He's
growing again. He got it back. Somewhere in a country that doesn't
matter, somebody gave him back the—*

*Ornamental,* said the report boxes. *Small. Devotional context.
Provenance unestablishable.*

He wrote, that night, in the field ledger, in his plain rated hand:
*Yumoto: subject resident approx. 3 yrs, departed spring, direction
unconfirmed. Local attachment strong; obstruction probable but
passive. No indication of insurgent handling.* He looked at the last
sentence for a long time, at the shape of the hole where the corridor
brief expected *handlers* to be, because the brief's whole theory was
that the network had taken the boy and was running him, and five
months of coast and three years of this valley said otherwise, said
worse, said something no box was built for: that the boy had been
raised — by a coast, by a doctor, by a drunk, by a fat horse and a
country that didn't matter — into exactly what he'd been at eight,
which was a person things came to broken and left mended.

*Don't round up.*

He left the sentence standing. Rei read it, and looked at him, and
marked the file *active, southern axis*, and on the first of the month
the detail took the coast road south, three merchants' guards with
their masks packed, eleven days behind a cart that smelled of lamp
oil.
