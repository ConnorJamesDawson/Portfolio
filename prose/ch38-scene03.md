# Chapter 38, Scene 3 — One Side

*Act 4, part D. Konoha General, the sluice. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1,163 words.*

---

He asked Yagi about the ear on the Thursday, in the sluice, at the end
of a shift, because the sluice at the end of a shift was where he and
Nurse Yagi had conducted every important conversation of the last two
years and neither of them had ever remarked on it.

"Can I ask you something."

"You can ask." She was counting swabs. "I've a long history of
answering, which is different from her."

"Your ears."

Yagi's hands stopped on the tray.

"Go on."

"The left one's pierced and the right one isn't. It's been like that
for the whole time I've known you. The left hole's old — twenty years,
maybe more, it's stretched about a third of a millimetre and it's
stretched *evenly*, which means whatever was in it was in it constantly
rather than occasionally." He kept going, because stopping in the
middle would have been worse. "And there's nothing on the right at all.
Not a closed one. Never done."

"You've had that catalogued for two years."

"Yes."

"And you've never asked."

"No."

"Why now?"

"Because I have been told twice in four months, by two different
people, that I have never once asked anybody a question about
themselves that wasn't in front of me," said Sazare, "and both of them
were right, and I've read a book about it, and I'm working through the
chapters."

Yagi put the tray down.

"You're working through the *chapters*."

"There are six."

"Of course there are." She leaned back against the bench and folded her
arms, and it was not the folding of arms that meant a refusal; he had
had two years to learn the difference. "Right. Sit down, this is longer
than a sluice."

He sat on the step stool.

---

"My sister did it," said Yagi. "Both of them, the same afternoon, when
she was sixteen and I was fourteen. On the back step at home with a
sewing needle and a cork and a bag of ice off the fishmonger, which was
what everybody did and which is a filthy way to do it and I have spent
twenty-two years telling girls not to."

"She did both."

"She did both. Left one first, and she made a proper job of it, and
then she started the right and I moved."

Yagi held up two fingers about an inch apart.

"She went in about that far and I moved, and it hurt considerably more
than the first one had because she was through the skin and not through
the back yet, and she stopped, and I said *leave it*, and she said *don't
be so soft*, and I said *leave it* again in a voice that meant it, and
she left it."

"And you never had it done."

"I never had it done." She looked at the wall for a second. "And she
mentioned it, on and off, for about fifteen years. Not unkindly. It was
one of those things a family gets — *Ren's got one ear*, and everybody
laughs, and it comes out at weddings."

"What happened to her?"

Yagi looked at him.

"That," she said, "is the first time you have ever asked me a second
question."

"Should I not have?"

"You absolutely should have. It's the whole of the technique and it
took you two years." She unfolded her arms. "She died. Nine years ago,
in the winter, of something that would take about four hours to treat
now and took eleven days to kill her then, and I was a chūnin
medical-nin with three years' service and I was two hundred miles away
on a border rotation and by the time I got the letter she had been in
the ground a week."

"I'm sorry."

"Don't be sorry, be quiet for a minute, I'm not finished."

He was quiet.

---

"Somebody asked me, about a year after," said Yagi. "Another nurse.
Perfectly kind, no harm in it at all — she said *why don't you just get
the other one done*, and I said I'd think about it, and I went home and
sat on the floor and did not think about it for about four years."

"Because it was hers."

"Because it was hers, and because the hole in my left ear is the last
thing my sister made." Yagi's voice did not change at all, which he had
learned was how she carried the things that were heaviest. "It's a
hole. It's not an object, there's nothing in it, I don't wear anything
in it and I never have. It's an absence in a piece of me that she put
there with a needle on a back step when she was sixteen and I was
fourteen and neither of us knew anything about anything."

"And the right one is the part she stopped."

"The right one is the part she stopped because I told her to." She
picked the tray back up. "And I have been told twice by well-meaning
people that it looks odd, and I have been asked about it four times in
twenty-two years, and you are the only one who noticed it was *never
done* rather than *closed up*, which is the entire difference and I've
never had to explain it to anybody before."

Sazare sat on the step stool in a sluice room.

"Two years," he said.

"Two years."

"I've had it in the file for two years and I could have had that at any
point by opening my mouth."

"Yes."

"And you'd have told me."

"I'd have told you the first week," said Nurse Yagi. "I'd have told you
the first week and I'd have made you feel like an idiot for about nine
seconds first, and then I'd have told you, and it would have cost you
nothing and it would have got you two years of knowing something true
about somebody who has held your kneecap in place for
forty-three minutes."

---

She stopped him at the door, which she never did.

"Ruri."

"Yes?"

"The book. Six chapters. Which one are you on?"

"Three. I'm doing three and six at the same time, which the book
advises against and which I am doing anyway because there's a date
involved."

"What's six?"

"*Of Occasions, and the marking of them.*"

Yagi's face did something.

"And what's the date?"

"It's already gone," said Sazare. "The eleventh of last month. I only
found out about it three weeks ago and by then it had been and gone,
which as far as I can establish means the matter is closed."

There was a silence of a very particular quality.

"Right," said Nurse Yagi, in the voice she used on a ward when
something had just presented that was going to require several people.
"Off you go. Go on. I've swabs."

He went.

He got about nine feet down the corridor before he heard her put the
tray down again, considerably harder than she had picked it up.
