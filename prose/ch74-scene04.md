# Chapter 74, Scene 4 — The No-Warning Case

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Hyūga Hinata, age
nineteen. POV: Hinata. ~2,340 words.*

---

The certification took forty minutes and she did it properly, which
meant with the veins up, which meant that for forty minutes on a
Tuesday afternoon in the third month Hyūga Hinata stood in a
quartermaster's yard seeing everything in it.

She had asked permission first. She always asked permission first —
her father had drilled it into her before he drilled anything else,
and the drilling had been about war and she had spent her whole
adolescence applying it to tea rooms — and Sazare-san had said,
"Granted, and thank you for asking, and please note that nobody else
has ever asked me for anything in this yard, they simply look," which
was, she had worked out by now, how he said *yes* when he was pleased.

The articles were clean.

*Certification note. Cohort two, forty-one articles, slate blue.
Inert-passive throughout. No pooling. No pattern that could be read
as notation by a teammate at any range. The clan's constraint is met
in full and it is met by margin, not by luck — the lattices are laid
so that nothing in them makes a shape at all. He did not simply avoid
seal-forms. He engineered the articles to be, to my eye,
uninteresting. That is a harder specification and he met it without
being asked twice.*

*Certified. Both copies. Licence extends.*

It was while she was signing that she looked, without meaning to, at
the man rather than the merchandise.

She had not intended it and she did not stop it, and afterwards she
would enter that honestly, because a Hyūga with the veins up in a
room is responsible for everything in the room, and *I did not mean
to* was the oldest excuse her clan owned.

He was carrying the sword. He always carried the sword. She had seen
it a dozen times as an object — a plain thing, unremarkable, worn
across the back under the coat like a tool.

Under the eye it was not an object at all.

*It has no source.*

She stood very still with the brush in her hand.

Everything in the world had a direction. That was the first lesson and
the whole grammar of the eye: chakra came *from* somewhere and went
*to* somewhere, and you read a person or a jutsu or a barrier by the
traffic, the way you read a page by the order of the strokes. A seal
array had a flow. A ninja had a flow. A tree had a slow one. Even the
dead had a going-out.

The thing on his back had incoming and no outgoing and nowhere for the
incoming to be coming from. It was, to the eye, a small quiet
inexhaustible *arrival* — a thing filling from a room that was not
there, and had been filling, by the look of the gradient, for a very
long time, and was not full.

*That is not possible. Not as a property of an object. Objects are
things somebody put chakra into and they run down like everything
runs down, and this one is—*

She put the brush down and let the veins go.

It took her about four seconds to arrive at what she was going to do,
and it was the same four seconds it had taken her at a street corner
in the second month, and it went the same way, because it was the
same question with a different coat on.

"Sazare-san."

"Hyūga-sama."

"I raised the eye to certify the articles and it was up for a
half-second longer than the articles required, and in that
half-second I saw something that is not mine." She kept her hands
folded and her voice where the format kept it. "I am not going to
describe it, to you or to anyone. I am telling you it happened
because you would want the security finding and because I would
rather you heard it from me on the day than worked it out from my
face on some later one. And I would like to state, so that it is in
your record and not only in mine, that I am not curious. That is not
modesty. I have decided not to be. It is available to me to be
curious about it and I have declined."

She had expected — she was not sure what. Something.

What happened was that he stood absolutely still for a moment and
then sat down on the bench end, quite suddenly, like a man whose legs
had made a scheduling decision without him.

"That is the second time," he said.

"Yes."

"You have now held two things of mine, neither of which I gave you,
and reported both inside a day, and asked for nothing either time."
He looked up. "May I say something that is going to be
uncomfortable?"

"...Yes."

"The village has a story about your clan and it is the wrong story.
It says the Hyūga see everything. That is a description of an
instrument and it is not interesting. The interesting property — the
one nobody outside your walls has any idea about, because you have
all been extremely disciplined about it for eight hundred years — is
that the clan's whole civilisation is a *disposal system*. Anybody can
be given a Byakugan. What has to be built, one child at a time, over
generations, is what a person does with the seen in the eleven seconds
afterwards." He said it flatly, in the working register, which she had
come to understand was how he handled things too heavy to lift any
other way. "You are the finest instrument I have ever stood in front
of. That is the least remarkable thing about you and I would like it
minuted that I said so twice."

Hinata looked at the yard for a while.

"My father," she said, "would like you very much, and would never say
so, and would be extremely rude to you for approximately three years
first."

"That is the most Hyūga sentence I have ever heard and I am going to
write it down."

"Please don't."

"I have already begun."

---

The consultation was after, at the kettle, because everything in that
yard was after and at the kettle.

She had brought her notebook and the amendment, written out in her
careful hand on the Saturday, and she put it on the bench between them
and did not look at it while he read, because looking at a thing while
somebody read it was a habit she was training out.

*Consultation, session four. Amendment requested: the no-warning
case. Rationale: the format did not fail on the fourth of the third
month. The format never deployed. Every drill to date has begun from
readiness. The load I am training for does not arrive from readiness.
It arrives through a gate.*

"Accepted," said Sazare. "And now I am going to tell you the problem
with it, because you will find it yourself in about a week and it is
better if we lose the week."

"The problem is that you cannot rehearse a surprise."

He looked at her.

"...You did find it yourself."

"On the Sunday." She turned her cup. "I sat down to draft the drill
and I could not get past the first line. Every version I wrote had me
knowing about it. And a drill that I know about is a drill from
readiness, which is the thing we have already got, and it works, and
it is not the thing that fails."

"Then say the rest of it."

And here it was.

She had known it was coming since the Sunday. She had, in fact,
rehearsed it — walking the compound's north wall, twice, in the dark,
saying it to a wall the way she had once said a name to a wall — and
the rehearsal had been useless the way rehearsals always were, and she
found now, sitting on a bench in the third month with a cup going cold
in her hands, that the only thing that was going to get her through
the sentence was the thing he had written on a page in a meeting room
in the second month and she had copied out in her best hand.

*Do not walk up to him with a feeling. Walk up to him with a task
that contains the feeling. Put the true sentence inside a format your
voice already knows how to carry, and let the format walk it across
the street, and pay the blush like a toll on the far side.*

*Very well,* she thought. *A commission, then. I have written four
of those this month.*

"The rest of it is that the load has to be supplied by somebody who
is not me," said Hinata, and her voice held, because the format was
holding it, "and there is only one person who supplies it, and you
have declined to arrange anything involving him on the grounds that
you are my engineer and not my broker, which I read in your margin
in the second week and which was correct and which I have been
grateful for every day since.

"So it cannot be arranged by you on my behalf. It can only be
*commissioned* by me.

"Therefore." She put her hand flat on the notebook, because her hand
wanted something to do. "I am commissioning it. I would like you to
put me in front of him without warning. At a time of your choosing.
Inside the next fortnight. Not told, not signalled, not made
comfortable, and specifically not made comfortable, because a
comfortable one is worth nothing to me. And I would like it noted
that I have asked for it in writing and that no part of it was your
idea, so that when it happens and it goes badly — and the first one
will go badly, I have costed it and I am buying it anyway — you are
not carrying any of that. It is mine. I ordered it."

She got to the end of the sentence.

Then she paid the toll, in full, at the going rate, which arrived in
her face and her ears and the backs of her hands and stayed for
somewhere between forty and sixty seconds while a young man on the
other end of the bench looked politely at a kettle and did her the
enormous courtesy of continuing the conversation as though nothing
were happening at all.

"Received," said Sazare. "Reference number to follow, because I
issue reference numbers, it is a known defect." He was writing. "Two
counter-terms and then I accept.

"One: I will not manufacture the encounter. I will not invent an
errand, or a reason, or a coincidence. The whole value of the drill
is that the load is real, and a staged one teaches your instrument
that ambushes are staged, which is worse than no training. What I
will do is *decline to prevent* one. He is in this yard on no
schedule whatsoever. He arrives like weather. I will simply stop
arranging your afternoons around the forecast — which I have been
doing since the fourth, quietly, and which I should have told you
about at the time and am telling you about now."

She stared at him.

"...You have been."

"Every session since the kilns. It was well-intentioned and it was
brokering by omission and it was not mine to do." He did not look
up from the page. "Consider it stopped as of this sentence, with an
apology attached that I am not going to elaborate because
elaborating apologies is a way of making the other person carry
them."

"That is—" She stopped. She started again. "Thank you."

"Two," said Sazare. "The bridge does not have to hold. That is the
counter-term and it is the important one, so I am going to be
blunter than is polite. You have written *the first one will go
badly, I have costed it and I am buying it anyway*, and I believe
you, and I want you to hear what that sentence sounds like from the
other end of a bench. It sounds like a woman who has decided that
the drill is only worth doing if she wins it.

"You will not win it. Not the first one. Possibly not the fourth.
The instrument you are training has eleven years of load on it and
you are asking it to take a shock in a fortnight." He put the pen
down. "So the acceptance test is not *did the sentence arrive*. The
acceptance test is *did the structure stand up afterwards*. Nineteen
minutes, on the fourth, from total failure to full function in a
licensing review. That is the number that matters and it is the only
number I am going to ask you for.

"Agreed?"

Hinata sat with her cold cup and her burning face in a yard full of
articles that were, by her own certification, uninteresting.

"Agreed," she said.

"Then it is commissioned, and I will not tell you when, and I am now
going to change the subject with great violence because you have
been extremely brave for about four minutes and there is a limit."
He turned a page. "The rotation spec. Your seamstresses have raised
eleven questions and nine of them are better than mine."

*Report, Tuesday,* she wrote that night, in her own room, in the
format.

*I commissioned an ambush.*

*I have no idea when it is coming. I have fourteen days of not
knowing and I have done this to myself on purpose with a brush and
a notebook, and the eye is no use to me at all, and I have never in
my life been so frightened of a fortnight.*

*Finding: this is the first thing I have ever asked anyone for.*

*Second finding, entered because the record gets the whole of it: I
am also, underneath, in a place I do not have a word for —*

*— glad.*
