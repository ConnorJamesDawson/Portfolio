# Chapter 60, Scene 8 — The Worst Article in the Land of Fire

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, the long room. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1840 words.*

---

It took four days to get the count out of him.

Not four days in the room. Four attempts across the afternoon of the
twenty-eighth and then three more evenings, because a thing that has
been in a man for thirty-one years does not come out because somebody
has named it, and Sazare had known that and had said so and had been
right and had hated being right.

On the second evening Jiraiya threw a chair at a wall.

On the third he got it twice, at about a third of his ordinary speed,
and sat on the floor of the long room afterwards for eleven minutes
saying nothing at all.

On the fourth he did it four times out of six and one of them was fast.

---

And on the fourth evening Sazare said the other thing, which he had had
since about the second and had been going round the outside of.

"I was wrong about the second obstacle as well."

"Which second obstacle?" said Fukasaku.

"I said he can't match a finished article because he can't make one. I
said it in this room on the twenty-eighth and I said it to him in an
office on the eighteenth and I have had it as a fixed point since about
the tenth month, and it's circular and I have never once looked at it."

He put the tin on the table.

"I can't make wood," he said.

"...No," said Jiraiya.

"On the fifteenth of the eighth month I held a rose that somebody else
had made and I tried to make my own alongside it, and nine inches of
stem came out of my palm at half past ten at night.

"I couldn't make wood. I made wood.

"Matching isn't a thing you do because you have the ability. Matching is
*how you get it*, and I am the only recorded instance of it, and I have
had the instance in my own book for three months and I have been telling
people it doesn't transfer."

"Why did you think it didn't?" said Shima.

"Because it took my hand off at the carpus," said Sazare.

---

"That's the honest answer and I'd rather have it in the minute than a
better-sounding one.

"I have not been reasoning about matching. I have been *avoiding* it. It
is the only thing in my life that has ever nearly killed me and I built a
rule that made it impossible for anybody else to do it and I never once
checked the rule."

Fukasaku made a noise.

"And is it safe?"

"No," said Sazare. "But I know why it wasn't, now, and I did not know in
the eighth month.

"The rose was open. It was nine days old and saturated and still
drawing, and I had it in a bare palm with nothing in between, and I was
in the circuit and the intake came through me and it did not stop when I
did.

"That is not a property of matching. That is a property of matching
against an *open* article."

He opened the tin.

"These are closed. Every one. Laid on the twenty-second, closed at the
point of laying, and they've got two days left in them and after that
they're chalk. There is no live route in any of them and there is
nothing for anybody to stand in.

"They are the safest objects in this building."

He put one on the scrubbed table.

"And they are still Shōton."

---

They set it up properly and it took an hour, because Tsunade would not
allow one part of it to be skipped and neither, it turned out, would
Fukasaku.

Shizune at the end of the table with the pen. Tsunade four feet from
Jiraiya with her sleeves back, doing nothing whatsoever, which was the
most useful thing anybody in that room could have been doing. Katsuyu on
the felt at nine inches, watching. Shima on the table, watching. Sazare
standing where he could see the hand and reach it.

"And the boy says stop," said Fukasaku, "and everything stops."

"Everything stops," said Tsunade.

Jiraiya sat down on the floor with a piece of somebody else's crystal in
his left hand.

"What do I do?"

"Hold it and try to make one," said Sazare.

"That's it?"

"That's the whole of it. That is the entire method and there is nothing
else in it and I am aware of how that sounds. Hold the finished thing
and try to produce the same thing next to it.

"Don't think about how. There isn't a how. If you find yourself
building a procedure, stop, because that's the count coming back in a
coat."

"And the gather?"

"Without the count."

"I can do that four times in six."

"Then do it four times in six," said Sazare, "and we will be here until
the sixth one."

---

They were there until about one in the morning.

He made nothing at all for the first two hours, and it was not
interesting and there was nothing to report and Shizune minuted eleven
attempts with the word *nil* against each of them, and about halfway
through Katsuyu said "his shoulders" and they went back and started
again with his shoulders down.

At about eleven Jiraiya's hand got warm.

That was all it did. It got warm and it stayed warm for about four
seconds and then stopped, and everybody in that room went very quiet
and nobody said anything for a while, and Sazare wrote *4s, thermal,
no product* on a page and did not let his hand shake while he did it.

At twenty past midnight there was something on the floor.

---

It was about the size of a grain of rice.

It was grey. It was not clear and it was not opaque, it was *grey*,
which was a thing crystal did not do and which he had never once seen in
eleven years and had no entry for. It had no shape whatsoever — it was
not a course and it was not a lattice and there were no ways in it in
any direction he could find, it was a piece of aggregate that had been
persuaded very badly to be something else and had got about eleven per
cent of the way.

It sat on the boards.

It made no sound at all when he put his palm on it. Not one voice. Not a
wobble, not a beat, nothing — the flattest, deadest, most completely
silent piece of matter he had ever touched.

It came apart at four seconds.

Not degraded. Not gone cloudy. It simply stopped holding, all at once,
the way a thing stops holding when there was never any order in it to
begin with, and it went to a small grey smear on the boards of the long
room.

---

Nobody said anything.

Sazare crouched on the floor of the second-floor room of the Hokage's
tower at twenty past midnight on the second of the twelfth month and
looked at a smear.

*Right.*

*Say it in order and do not go fast.*

*One. It was crystal.*

*I want to be exact, because this is the only thing in this room that
matters and everybody is going to want me to be pleased and I am going
to be pleased in a minute and I would like the finding first.*

*It was Shōton. I had my hand on it for two and a half seconds. There is
no order in it and no ways and no note and it is the worst article I
have ever touched by an enormous distance —*

*— and it is not stone, and it is not glass, and it is not a mineral
that happened. It is nature chakra given a structure by a human being's
chakra, and the structure is atrocious, and it is a structure.*

*Two. It is worse than the first thing I ever made.*

*Ninth year. A floor, a bit of gravel, four seconds, no order, written
up as failed, and I have had that page in a box for nine years and I
went and looked at it in the tenth month for a completely different
reason.*

*Mine had a note. It was one voice and it was flat and it was wrong and
there was a note in it.*

*This has got nothing. This is a worse article than the one made by a
nine-year-old with no teacher in a room, and it was made by one of the
three strongest shinobi alive with four observers and eleven hours.*

*Three, and this is the entire thing, and I am going to write it in the
plainest words I have got.*

*Jiraiya of Konohagakure has no clan and no line and no bloodline of any
kind. His father sold eels. He was the worst of three for eleven years
and he has never in his life had a kekkei genkai and there is not one in
his family and there never was.*

*And at twenty past midnight on the second of the twelfth month he
produced Shōton.*

*Badly. For four seconds. In front of five witnesses and a minute book.*

*And it went on a scale.*

---

"Weigh it," he said.

"...Ruri, it's gone."

"Not that one. The smear — Shizune, is there a paper in the—"

"There's a paper," said Shizune, who had already got up.

They scraped what was on the boards onto a sheet and Sazare took it down
to the annexe at half past midnight with Fukasaku on his shoulder,
because Fukasaku said he was not going to hear about it secondhand at
his age, and they put it on the small balance that Kanzaki kept for
seed-grade work.

Nought point one one grams.

He wrote it down.

*Nought point one one grams of the worst article in the Land of Fire.*

*And that is the whole of the paper.*

*Nineteen pages and a slug's word and four hundred years of oral
tradition out of Shikkotsu, and none of it was worth anything in a room,
and I wrote that on page fourteen myself in a hand I did not enjoy.*

*And this morning it stopped being testimony.*

---

He came back up the stairs at about one.

They were all still in the long room. Nobody had gone to bed and nobody
had said anything about going to bed, and Jiraiya was sitting on the
floor exactly where he had been with his back against the table leg and
his hands hanging over his knees, looking at the boards.

"Nought point one one," said Sazare.

Jiraiya did not look up.

"Say the other thing," he said.

"It's the worst article in the Land of Fire."

"Aye," said Jiraiya. "It is."

And then he put his head back against the table leg and laughed until he
could not breathe, and it went on for a very long time, and he had to
put a hand over his eyes at the end of it.
