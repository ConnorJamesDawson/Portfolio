# Chapter 64, Scene 4 — The Lighthouse Goes Out

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the second training ground. Sakura Haruno, age
nineteen. POV: Sakura. ~2080 words.*

---

At five minutes she had written four things in her head and was ready to
call it.

*Rib, left, sixth or seventh, lateral. Not broken — he moved wrong for
about nine seconds and then moved right, which is a man who has assessed
himself and found nothing structural.*

*Thigh, right, deep. He is favouring it and he does not know he is
favouring it.*

*Two to the head, both glancing, neither worth anything.*

*And he is beaten. He has been beaten since about three minutes and
everybody on this ground knows it including him.*

She had her hand about a third of the way up.

*One more and I call it. He's got nothing. He has built about nine
hundred pounds of crystal and Naruto has gone round every single piece of
it, and the last wall bought him four feet and now he's on the ditch and
there is nowhere—*

He stopped.

---

She had her hand at half height and she did not put it up.

*He's stopped.*

*Not cornered-stopped. He's been cornered twice and both times he went
somewhere, he went round the post and he went through his own wall and
whatever else that man is he has not once stood still.*

*He has put his feet down.*

She looked at his hands.

*Nothing in them. Nothing coming. He hasn't got the felt roll off his hip
all morning — he has not touched it, not once, and I have been waiting
for it since half past nine.*

*He is standing on the lip of a ditch with a wall on one side and a post
on the other and a hundred and twenty feet of open ground in front of him
and he is doing* nothing.

*Which is what he has been doing all morning.*

*So why does it look different?*

---

Naruto stopped at twenty feet.

That was the thing that made her put her hand all the way down again.

*He never stops.*

*I have fought that boy about four hundred times and he does not stop, he
does not have a stop in him, he has been going forward since he was nine
and he has just put his weight back at twenty feet against a man who
cannot hurt him.*

*He can feel something.*

*He has always been able to feel things. He could feel Gaara in a
corridor and he could feel Ero-sennin coming up a road, and he has never
once been able to explain it and I have stopped asking, and he has just
stopped at twenty feet.*

She looked at the western treeline.

Jiraiya-sama had come off the trunk.

---

He had been against that tree with his arms folded for eleven minutes and
he was standing clear of it now, about four feet out, with his hands
loose at his sides.

*That's a man about to move.*

*He is going to go onto that ground and I do not know which one of them
he is going to and I do not know why, and there is nothing happening.*

*There is* nothing happening.

And then Fukasaku said, from the middle post, "Oh."

---

Sakura had been about eleven feet from the north post all morning and she
heard the whole of it.

"Look at his feet," said Shima.

"I am looking at his feet."

"He has not moved them."

"I *know* he has not moved them."

"Fourteen seconds," said Shima.

"I know how long it is, woman, I have been counting since he put them
down." Fukasaku's voice had gone somewhere else. "That is not a man who
has stopped because he is cornered. Look at the shoulders."

"They have come down."

"They have come *down*," said Fukasaku, "and I have watched about four
hundred humans get cornered on that mountain and not one of them has ever
had their shoulders come down, and I want to say now, before anybody says
anything else, that I have got no idea what I am looking at."

And Katsuyu said, "Do not close."

---

She said it across a hundred and twenty feet of open ground.

Sakura had never heard her raise her voice and had not known she could,
and it was not raised, exactly — it was the same mild voice she used for
everything and it simply arrived at the far side of the bowl as though
the distance were not there.

Ruri did not react.

*He hasn't heard it.*

*He has heard everything all morning. He heard her at two minutes and he
answered her and he shouted, and I have never heard him shout.*

*He hasn't heard that.*

---

"Katsuyu," said Shishō.

She had come off the eastern edge and was about eleven feet along the
ditch and she had not been eleven feet along the ditch a moment ago.

"What is he doing?"

"He is holding," said Katsuyu.

"He's *what*?"

"He is doing the exercise," said Katsuyu, "and he is doing it at the
eastern edge of a training ground with eleven people coming at him
because he has run out of directions, and I want to say clearly and in
front of witnesses that I did not teach him to do this, that I have never
seen it done, and that there is nobody on my mountain who has."

"Then why did you tell him not to close?"

"Because he is going to want to," said Katsuyu. "In about eleven seconds
he is going to want to very badly, and if he closes he will be a man
standing in a ditch with nothing, and if he does not—"

She stopped.

"I do not know what happens if he does not," she said.

---

Sakura watched him.

*Twenty seconds.*

*Twenty-two.*

*Nothing is happening. There is nothing coming off him, there is no
light, there is no — I have seen Shishō raise chakra and I have seen
Kakashi-sensei do it and there is always something, there is always a
pressure and it puts your hair up and you can feel it in your teeth.*

*There is nothing.*

*He is standing in a ditch with his shoulders down and his hands at his
sides and it is the most ordinary thing on this ground.*

And then she noticed the rubble.

---

She noticed it because she was a medic and because she had been tracking
feet for eleven minutes, which was what you did — you watched where
everybody's feet were going to be, because the injuries came out of the
ground and not out of the hands.

There was about nine hundred pounds of broken crystal on ground two.

Two collapsed walls. A thirty-foot bar in four pieces. Four blunt spikes
in a scatter that nobody had stepped on. Nine reinforced ones. The
remains of a box, in about eleven pieces, spread over a twelve-foot
circle where he had come out of the back of it at a run.

It had all been there for four minutes and she had been walking her eyes
over it since the second wall went down.

*The pieces have moved.*

She looked at the box.

*They have moved and I did not see them move and I have been looking at
them.*

*That piece was eleven inches from the post. It is about four now.*

*And there are more of them.*

*There are — there were eleven pieces and there are not eleven pieces,
there are about thirty, and they are smaller, and the ones at the edge
are*

*they are joined to the ground*

---

"Jiraiya," said Shishō.

He was already coming out of the treeline.

"Don't," he said.

"Don't what?"

"Don't call it and don't go on that ground and *don't touch the boy*." He
came up beside her at a walk that was not a walk. "Not yet. Give it four
more seconds."

"Jiraiya, there is something happening to the—"

"I know what's happening to the ground."

"Then—"

"Hime," said Jiraiya, "look at his *face*."

---

Sakura looked at his face.

She had fifty-one sheets on that man. She had a column for eye contact
and a column for volume and a column for whether he finished sentences,
and since the twenty-fifth of the eleventh month she had had one more
that she had ruled in herself in her own book and never shown anybody, and
it did not have a heading on it because she had not been able to think of
one that was not embarrassing.

There had been something on his face since the twenty-first of last
month.

Every day. All day. In a lane and on a hill and in a store and at a fork,
and she had told him so at that fork and he had said *I know* and she had
said *I've got a column for it now*, and he had not smiled about it
because he did not smile, and it had been there anyway.

It was gone.

---

*It's gone.*

*Not covered. He can't cover things. He has never covered a single thing
in his life and I have got nine hundred marks that say so.*

*It has* gone*.*

She took two steps along the ditch, which she should not have done, and
Jiraiya put an arm out and she stopped.

And at about thirty feet, in ordinary daylight, on the twentieth of the
twelfth month, she saw his eyes.

---

*They've gone clear.*

*That's not — eyes aren't clear, eyes have got a colour, his are grey and
I have got that in a column because I put it in one on the second session
and never used it again.*

*They have gone clear all the way through and the light is going into
them and coming out and it is coming out* even*, and I do not know how I
can see that from thirty feet and I can.*

*And there are marks.*

*Under both. From the inner corner, running down, about an inch, pale
blue, and they were not there four minutes ago and I have been looking at
that face all morning.*

*I know those markings.*

She turned her head about eleven degrees and looked at the north post,
where a slug about nine inches long sat with a pale blue line running
down from the inner corner of each of her eyestalks, in exactly that
shape and exactly that colour, and had gone completely still.

"Katsuyu," said Sakura. "What—"

"I do not know," said Katsuyu. "I want it on the record that I do not
know, that I told him on Thursday evening that his eyes had gone clear
and that he had my markings, and that he laughed and said it was the first
joke I had ever made.

"And I let him."

---

"You can't tell what he's looking at," said Sakura.

Nobody answered.

"I can't — " She put her hand up and then did not know what to do with
it. "I've got fifty-one sheets on him and the *whole point* of him is
that you can read him off a wall at forty feet, everybody in this village
can, I've said it in a briefing, and I am thirty feet away and I cannot
tell what he is looking at."

"No," said Jiraiya.

His voice was not right.

"Jiraiya-sama—"

"Sage mode," he said.

---

Sakura turned round.

"That's—"

"That is sage mode," said Jiraiya, "and it is partial, and it is on his
eyes and nowhere else, and it took me thirty-one years and I cannot do it
without two toads sitting on my shoulders and I have never had a clean
one in my life.

"And that boy has done it standing in a ditch because he was frightened
and ran out of places to go."

"Does he know?"

Jiraiya did not answer for about two seconds.

"He does not know it exists," he said. "I told him he was a sage on a
roof on the sixteenth of last month and I did not tell him there was a
*state*, because it had not once occurred to me that anybody could arrive
at it by accident, and I have been going over that conversation since
Tuesday and I have got about four things wrong in it.

"He thinks he is holding his breath."

---

Out on the ground, twenty feet from a man standing in a ditch, Naruto
Uzumaki put his weight forward.

"Oh, don't," said Shima.

And Fukasaku said, "Boy, get *back*—"

And Naruto went.
