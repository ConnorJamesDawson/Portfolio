# Chapter 51, Scene 1 — The Half-Made Thing

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard, after hours. Sazare
Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2,158 words.*

---

He started it on the third of the tenth month and he knew what he was
doing before he had picked up the file.

*A drainage seat for the third-floor stumps.*

*It is a real problem. Emiko has raised it four times in two years and
the standard article is a moulded tin cup with a rolled lip which is
correct in every respect except that the lip is rolled by a man in the
Land of Fire who has never seen a stump, and it bears on the scar at
about eleven o'clock in roughly a third of below-knee cases and there
is nothing anybody can do about it because it is a bought article and
we buy nine hundred a year.*

*A crystal seat, laid to the individual, would bear nowhere. It is four
hours' work per patient and it would cost the hospital nothing but my
evenings.*

*It is a good idea. I have had it since the seventh month and have done
nothing about it because there has been no reason to prioritise it over
about eleven other things.*

*And a new class of article in contact with a healing wound cannot be
issued without the medical board.*

*Which is one signature.*

He wrote all of that out first, honestly, because a thing you would not
write down was a thing you should not do — and then read it back and
saw that he had written the last two lines in a different order than he
had thought them, and that the honest order was:

*It is one signature.*

*And therefore what can I build.*

---

He built it anyway.

Four hours on the Friday and three on the Saturday, and it was good
work. A blank first, in aggregate, to get the geometry off a plaster
Emiko had kept from a case in the spring. Then the seat proper, in
granite stock, laid slow because he had time now and because everything
laid slow was better. Wall thickness under two millimetres at the rim
and holding. The lip rolled outward and *thinned* at the point where
the standard article bore, which was the entire idea, and which had
taken him about nine minutes to get right and which he had been quietly
delighted by.

Katsuyu sat on the corner of the bench and watched him do it.

She said nothing for a long time, which was not like her, and at about
the eleventh hour of the Saturday she said:

"You are working badly."

"The article's fine."

"The article is very fine. I did not say the article." A slow settling.
"You have laid four hundred courses in front of me since the fourth of
last month and I have got quite good at the ordinary shape of you, and
this is not it. You have taken over on eleven of the last twenty. You
have gripped on all twenty. And you did the thing at the rim twice
before you did it right and neither of the first two was a technical
error, they were — "

She stopped.

"I do not have the word," she said. "You were not *there*."

---

He put the file down.

"No," he said. "I wasn't."

"Is something wrong?"

"Yes."

"Will you tell me?"

"No," said Sazare, "and I want to be clear that it isn't a refusal
about you. It's that I have not finished working out what the answer
is, and if I say it out loud now I will say a tidy version, and then I
will believe the tidy version because I said it, and it will take me
about four months to get back to the true one."

"That is a very complete way of saying no."

"I've had practice."

Katsuyu was quiet.

Then she said, in a completely different register, one he had not
heard: "May I say something? I have been trying to work out how to say
it for eleven days and I have not managed it, and I have concluded that
I am not going to manage it, so I am simply going to say it and it will
come out wrong."

"Say it wrong."

"I should like to help you."

---

"You are helping me."

"No," said Katsuyu. "I am *telling* you. Those are not the same and I
have known they were not the same since about the twentieth of last
month and I have not known how to raise it."

He waited.

"I have said *now* to you four thousand times," she said. "I have told
you about the take and I have told you about the grip and you have done
extraordinarily well with both, and I want to say that plainly first
because what comes next may sound as though I do not think so.

"But I have been describing a thing to you. That is all it is. I say
*a third over* and you have to go and find, inside yourself, in the
dark, with no report, the thing I might mean — and you have found it,
and it has cost you four days for one of them and a fortnight for the
other, and I have watched you do it and it has been like watching a
man learn the shape of a room by being shouted at from outside it."

Sazare sat down on the yard stone.

"Yes," he said. "That's exactly what it's been like."

"I could show you."

---

He did not say anything for about nine seconds.

"Say what you mean by show."

"I would be — " She hunted. "I am going to do this badly. When you
reach and take and hold, that is a thing that happens, and I am beside
it, watching. I could be *in* it. Not doing it for you. Alongside, with
my own — with what I have — doing the same thing at the same time, so
that there is something for you to feel your own against."

"A reference."

"If that is the word. I do not know the word."

"It's the word." He was aware that his hands had gone still on his
knees. "Katsuyu, it's more than the word. It's the whole thing. It's
the only thing that has ever worked on me.

"I can't take instruction. I've never been able to — there's no
notation and there never was and Kakashi told me in the eighth month
that the entire transmission medium of this profession is shut to me
and it always will be. Everything I have ever learned in my life I
learned by holding a finished one and going at it until mine agreed
with it. A bezel at eleven. A retaining figure at seventeen. A *rose*,
in four minutes, with my eyes shut, because there was one in my other
hand."

"Then—"

"Nobody has ever been able to put a finished one in my hand for *this*."
He held both of them up, palms in, the bad one and the good one.
"There's no article. It's the operation itself. It's in here and there
has never been a way to get a second one next to it."

Katsuyu said, quietly: "Then I should have offered eleven days ago."

"Why didn't you?"

---

That took her a while.

"I did not know how to ask," she said. "I have been asked things all
year and it has been the best year I can remember and I have thought
about very little else. Nobody had ever asked me anything until the
Tuesday in her office. I did not know there was anything in me that
wanted to be asked.

"And it did not occur to me until about the twentieth of last month
that I have also never *offered* anything, to anybody, in four hundred
and eleven years. Not once. I am summoned and I am instructed and I do
the thing and I am released, and in all of it there has never been a
moment where I said *shall I* — because there has never been a shape in
which that would go."

She settled slightly.

"I have watched you for a month," she said, "trying to work out how a
person offers, and I did not manage it, and in the end I have said it
plainly because plain is what I have."

Sazare looked at the bench.

*I cannot compliment anybody. I have never done it once. It has been in
a column since the ninth month of last year with the word "cannot"
against it and Sakura marked me for it four times.*

*She cannot offer.*

*Same thing, opposite direction, and neither of us was ever given the
chance to practise, and I am not going to say any of that out loud
because it is rung four and she has not asked for it.*

"Yes," he said. "Please."

---

It took about twenty minutes to work out how, most of which was them
both being extremely careful and getting nowhere.

Then they stopped being careful, and it was immediate.

He reached, the way he had reached four hundred thousand times, and she
reached alongside it — and there was, for the first time in eleven
years, a second thing in the same place doing the same job, and it was
not like his at all.

He made a sound he did not intend to make.

"That's *nothing*," he said. "That's — you've taken nothing. That's a
tenth of what I—"

"It is what the work is."

"Then what am I doing?"

"You are pulling," said Katsuyu. "That is the word I have been trying
to find since the fourth of last month and I could not find it because
I had nothing to compare you to either. I say *you take a third over*
and it is true and it is not the point. The point is *how*. You take it
the way a man hauls on a rope. You have been hauling since you were
seven and there is nothing to haul against — it is not resisting you,
it has never resisted you, it simply comes."

"And the grip."

"The grip is the same thing at the other end. You are holding it the
way you would hold something that wants to leave." Her mantle shifted.
"It does not want to leave. It does not want anything. You have spent
eleven years in a fight with a thing that was never fighting you."

---

He sat in a yard for a long time after that.

Then he got up and looked at the drainage seat.

It was on the felt where he had left it, three-quarters finished, wall
under two millimetres, the lip thinned at eleven o'clock, four hours of
his evenings and three of his Saturday, and it was — he could see it
perfectly well — the best small article he had made all year.

*It is a good object.*

*It solves a real problem. Emiko has raised it four times and about a
third of below-knee cases have a scar bearing on a bought lip, and I
have made a thing that would not.*

*And it is a pretext.*

*I began it on the third because I worked out on the last night of the
ninth month that a new class of article in contact with a healing wound
needs the medical board, and the medical board is one signature, and
the signature is on the second floor of a building I have not been into
for thirty-nine days.*

*I wrote in this book on the thirtieth that I would not construct a
reason to go up that hill.*

*I began constructing one inside seventy-two hours and I did it in a
material, which is the only way I know how to lie, and I have never in
my life made an article that was for something other than the thing it
was for.*

He put his hand on it.

*And I cannot finish it.*

*Not because I have caught myself and repented. I want that recorded
accurately, because in four months I will remember this evening as a
moment of character and it was not one.*

*I cannot finish it because a slug spent eleven days trying to work out
how to offer me something and then said it plainly in a yard, and I now
know what my own hands have been doing since I was seven, and this
object has been sitting nine inches away from that the entire time.*

*It is a good article and it is a false one and I can see both, and I
have spent eleven years being able to see both in other people's work
and it has never once been mine.*

He wrapped it in a cloth and put it in the drawer under the bench,
unfinished, and did not throw it away, because he did not destroy
records.
