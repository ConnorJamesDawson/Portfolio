# Chapter 64, Scene 8 — Four Hours

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, ground two and the hospital. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2150 words.*

---

He had to open it to get the ankle out.

There was no other way and he ran it four times looking for one, and
every version that did not involve opening it involved taking the collar
off mechanically, and a collar with a taper on the inside comes off a
broken ankle in about eleven pieces and takes the ankle with it.

So he knelt on the packed earth of ground two with about eleven people
looking at him and put his hands on a thing he had made and took the
close off it.

Nobody said anything.

That was the part he had not costed. He had costed the ankle and he had
costed the two seconds and he had costed the risk, which was low and
which was not zero, and he had not costed the fact that he was going to
have to do it in front of the Hokage of Konohagakure and two elders of
Mount Myōboku and a woman with fifty-one sheets on him, eleven seconds
after the same operation had put fourteen inches of crystal inside a
boy's leg.

Nobody said anything at all.

It came off in about two seconds and he shut it again immediately and the
ankle came out and the foot was the colour it should not have been and
Sakura had it in a splint inside eleven more.

---

They had him off the ground at about four minutes.

Tsunade carried him, which nobody argued with, and she did it the way you
carry a spinal even though it was not a spinal, and Sakura went beside
her with the head and Sazare went beside the leg with both hands on it
and did not take them off between ground two and the second floor of the
hospital, because there was fourteen inches of dead article in a femoral
compartment and if a piece of it moved on the way there was going to be
nothing anybody could do.

He did not look at anybody.

He had a great deal to be doing with and he had one thing to be doing
with that was not medical, and he put it in a corner of himself with the
lid on and did not open it for four hours.

*They know what is in that boy.*

*Both of them. And there is a whole village that does not, and I am in
that village.*

*Not now.*

---

Yagi met them on the stair.

She took one look and said, "Theatre two," and did not ask one question
until they were through the doors, and then asked exactly three and all
of them were about the leg.

"Whose is the crystal?"

"Mine."

"Is it live?"

"It's closed. It has been closed for eleven minutes and it is going to
stay closed."

"Can anybody but you take it out?"

"No," said Sazare.

"Then scrub," said Yagi, "and I want somebody who is not you on the head,
and Haruno is not going in that room because she is nineteen and she has
been on a training ground for two hours and she is going to sit down and
drink something and I will have that argument with her in the corridor
myself."

---

It took four hours and eleven minutes.

*Method: it cannot be pulled. It has grown along the vessel and it is not
adherent everywhere — about a third of the length is lying in the
compartment and about two-thirds has taken, and taken means it is in the
tissue and the tissue is in it.*

*So it comes out in sections and every section has to be separated from
what it is holding before it is moved, and the separation has to be at
the boundary, and the boundary is about a fortieth of an inch wide and
irregular over fourteen inches, and there is one instrument in the world
that will do that.*

He stopped with his hands over the field.

"Hime."

She was at the head end, gowned, because she was the only other person in
the Land of Fire who could have done the head if the head had gone wrong.

"Say it," she said.

"On Thursday afternoon you made me write *not to be used* under a line on
a sheet and put the date on it," said Sazare, "and you told me you wanted
the decision made in a yard on a Thursday and not in a third of a second
in four years' time.

"That was forty-eight hours ago."

"I know how long it was."

"I cannot take fourteen inches of this out of that compartment without a
scalpel and neither can anybody else, and if it is not out by about the
sixth hour the compartment is gone and the leg goes with it."

"Then use it."

"I want it said out loud in this room."

Tsunade looked at him across the table.

"Sazare Ruri is instructed by me, by name, in theatre two, in front of
Yagi and two nurses, to use a chakra scalpel in the course of a
surgical procedure on the twentieth of the twelfth month," she said.
"Write it up tonight and put the time on it and I will sign it in the
morning.

"Now get on."

---

He got on.

It was, on any reasonable measure, the best work of his life, and he did
not enjoy one second of it and did not know that it was until about eight
days later when Yagi told him.

*Forty-one sections.*

*Every one separated at the boundary, every one lifted clear, every one
checked against the piece that came out before it because the whole of it
has got to add up to fourteen inches and if it does not then there is a
piece left in there and a piece left in there is going to be found in
about nine months by somebody who is not me.*

*It adds up to fourteen and a quarter.*

*Compartment: gone on the lateral, about sixty per cent. The medial has
survived and I do not know why and I will take it.*

*Femoral: intact. Three places where it was within a fortieth and one
where I have got a partial and I have repaired it and it will hold.*

*Tibia: displaced spiral, reduced and pinned at the fourth hour by Yagi
while I was on the vessel.*

*Nerve: I do not know. There is no way to know today. He will find out in
about a fortnight and so will everybody else.*

He came out at about half past two in the afternoon and stood in the
corridor with his hands still up and could not work out what to do with
them.

---

Tsunade came out four minutes later.

She stood next to him against the wall of the second-floor corridor and
neither of them said anything for a while, and there were people going
past both ways because it was half past two and it was a hospital.

"Prognosis," she said.

"You give it. I'm not fit to."

"You're the most fit person in this building to and you know it, and
you're going to have to say it to about nine people this week and you
might as well get it wrong in front of me first."

"All right."

He put his hands down.

"Ordinary man of sixteen," said Sazare. "Sixty per cent loss of the
lateral compartment with the tibia displaced and four hours of ischaemia
in a limb.

"You do not save that leg. You try, because he is sixteen, and you spend
about eleven days trying and then you take it at mid-thigh, and if you
are extremely lucky you keep it and he is nine months on crutches and
walks with a stick for the rest of his life and cannot run.

"That's the figure and it is not a pessimistic one and I would defend it
in front of anybody."

"And?"

"And that is not what is going to happen," said Sazare, "and I have known
it was not what was going to happen since about the third hour, and I
have not got a mechanism, and I would like one."

---

"Tibia?" said Tsunade.

"It was knitting on the table."

"Yes."

"Not starting. *Knitting*. I have had my hands on it for four hours and
it has changed under them and I have put a pin in a bone that was
already going, and there is no figure in any text I have read for that
and I have read the ones from four countries."

"Ten days," said Tsunade.

"...Ten."

"Ten days for the tibia. The compartment will fill in about three weeks
and it will fill properly — not scar, *fill*, and the function comes back
with it. He'll be walking at about three weeks. He will be running badly
at five and running properly at six and by the eighth month there will be
nothing to find.

"The nerve will come back. It always comes back. I have watched that boy
take things that should have finished him about eleven times since he was
twelve and I have stopped putting figures on him."

Sazare stood in the corridor.

"That's not a person," he said.

"No."

"Hime, that is not — I have got four thousand ward notes and about two
thousand hours and there is nothing on any page anywhere that does that.
The nearest thing I have got is the Senju line and I have read the
longevity table and it is *nothing like this*."

"Uzumaki blood," said Tsunade.

---

He looked at her.

She was against the wall of a corridor with her arms folded and she was
looking at the far end of it, and she said it in exactly the voice she
used to read out a bed count, which was flat and unhurried and had
nothing in it anywhere.

"Uzumaki blood," she said again. "It's a clan. It was a clan. They were
at Uzushiogakure and they had a reputation for it and it was not
exaggerated, and there are about four people left in the world who carry
it and one of them is on that table.

"Vitality. Longevity. They knit. It is documented, it is in the
Konohagakure archive under the alliance papers, and you can read the
whole of it any afternoon you like with a Corps card."

"That's true," said Sazare.

"It's true."

"Every word of it."

"Every word of it," said Tsunade.

---

*Right.*

*And it is not the whole of it and both of us know that, and she has just
handed me a true and completely checkable answer that accounts for about
half of what I watched happen in that theatre, and she has handed it to
me in a corridor at half past two in the afternoon in the voice she uses
for a bed count.*

*And she is not lying. She has not lied to me once in three years and she
has not started today.*

*She has given me the part I am allowed to have.*

*And she has done it in a way that means I can go and read the alliance
papers and verify it and be satisfied, and be satisfied *incorrectly*,
and she knows I will not be.*

*So it is not a lie and it is not a cover. It is a boundary, and she has
put it there in front of me deliberately, and she has done it in a way
that lets me see it is a boundary.*

*That is not concealment. That is her telling me there is a wall and
where it is.*

He put his back against the corridor wall.

"Thank you," he said.

Tsunade's head came round about four degrees.

"For what?"

"For not making it up," said Sazare. "You had about four minutes in that
theatre to build me something that would have gone all the way and I'd
have taken it, because I am tired and I have been on a table since ten
and I want an answer, and you gave me a true one that stops in the middle
and you did not dress the stopping.

"I know where the wall is now. I'm not going to go at it."

"Ruri—"

"I'd like it recorded that I said that on the day," he said, "because in
about eleven months I am going to want to, and I would rather have it in
writing that I knew what I was agreeing to."

Tsunade did not answer for some seconds.

"Go and sit down," she said. "You've got a rib and a thigh and you have
not looked at either of them since eight o'clock this morning."

"They're fine."

"I *know* they're fine, I looked at them on the stair while you were
holding a leg. Go and sit down."
