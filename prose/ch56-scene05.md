# Chapter 56, Scene 5 — Authority

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, outer office. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1540 words.*

---

Shizune found it at about four in the afternoon.

He knew before she said anything, because she had been going through
the done pile at the ordinary rate of about one every three seconds and
then she was not, and the not lasted for a considerable while.

"Ruri."

"Yes."

"Come here."

---

It was a dispatch cover.

Four lines. A consignment reference, a quantity, a destination and an
authority, and the whole of it existed so that a quartermaster at the
other end could look at the fourth line and know whose word the goods
travelled on.

Destination: Sunagakure, with the delegation, out on the nineteenth.

*Authority:*

*Senju Tsunade.*

---

He looked at it for about four seconds.

"That's wrong," he said.

"Yes."

"That should be *the office of the Hokage*, with the standing
reference, because the authority isn't a person, it's a post — the
whole point of the line is that the goods travel on the post and not on
whoever's holding it, and if she's dead in the fourth month the
consignment is still good."

"Yes."

"It's not even a hard field. It's the same eleven characters on every
one of these and there are four hundred of them in that box and I have
done all four hundred and they are all right and this one is—"

He stopped.

"Say the rest," said Shizune.

"It's in my hand," said Sazare.

---

He took it and held it under the lamp that did not work, which was a
thing he did with his hands and not his head, and looked at the ink.

*Nothing wrong with the stroke. No hesitation, no correction, no
lift-and-restart. That is not somebody uncertain and it is not
somebody tired.*

*That is a hand writing a thing it knows perfectly well.*

*Which means I did not make a mistake. A mistake has a shape and I have
looked at about nine thousand of other people's and I know exactly what
one looks like on a page.*

*I wrote what was in my head.*

"When?" said Shizune.

"Sixth bundle. It'll have been in the run I did straight after the
flimsies, so between about half eleven and midday."

"You know that?"

"I know it to about eleven minutes," he said, "because the flimsies
were the thing I was doing when she came through at eleven, and I lost
a count, and it took me four attempts and eleven minutes to get a
figure I'd normally have in nine seconds, and then I went straight on
to the covers because they're easy."

Shizune sat down slowly.

"You lost a *count*."

"Yes."

"And you didn't say."

"There was nothing to say. It's not in a file and nobody was ever going
to know. I was going to write it up tonight." He put the cover down.
"This one's in a file."

---

He went back through everything.

He did it at speed and he did it completely, because there was no
version of the afternoon in which he did not, and it took him
thirty-five minutes: four hundred and six dispatch covers, eleven
weeks of acknowledgements, the sixth-month reconciliation, both
directions, every field.

There was nothing else.

That was worse and he understood why immediately.

*If there were nine of them it would be a misunderstanding. It would
mean I had the wrong template in my head and had been applying it
consistently, and consistent is fixable — you find the template, you
correct it, you re-run the batch and you write a note at the front of
the book so it does not happen again, and I have done exactly that
about fourteen times since I was twelve.*

*There is one.*

*One, in four hundred and six, in a batch that ran at thirty an hour
with no errors either side of it, eleven minutes after she went past
the end of my desk.*

*It is not a template. It is not a system fault and there is nothing to
correct and there is no note to write at the front of the book.*

*The system is fine. The system produced four hundred and five correct
covers today.*

*Something else got in and used my hand for one of them.*

---

"Do it again on a fresh one," said Shizune.

He did it again on a fresh one.

It took ninety seconds, including the reference lookup he did not need
to do and did anyway, and he read it back four times, which was three
more than was reasonable, and put it on the desk.

Shizune checked it.

Then she picked up the spoiled one.

She did not put it in the waste and she did not put it in the
destruction tray, which was on the corner of her desk and was emptied
by a clerk on Fridays with a witness, and she did not put it anywhere
at all.

She folded it once and put it in the inside pocket of her coat.

---

He watched her do it.

"That's a spoiled form," he said.

"I know what it is."

"Spoiled forms go in the tray. There's a procedure and the procedure
has a witness in it and the reason it has a witness in it is that a
spoiled form with a real reference on it is a live document until
somebody sees it destroyed."

"I know, Ruri."

"So what you have just done," said Sazare, "is remove a live document
from the room."

Shizune did not answer.

"That's the second one," he said.

---

She looked up at that.

"Second what?"

"Thing." He was standing on the other side of her desk with his hands
at his sides. "The first was on Wednesday morning, in that kitchen, at
about ten past eight, when I said somebody has to tell Ibiki and you
said not yet and I said all right. That took two seconds and it has now
been sixty-eight hours.

"This is the second. And they are not the same size — I want to say
that, I am not pretending a folded cover is the same as an unreported
vulnerability. But they are the same *shape*, and you have done both of
them, and both of them are you standing between me and a procedure that
exists for a reason, and I have said all right both times."

"Do you want me to put it in the tray?"

"No," said Sazare.

"Then what are you telling me for?"

"Because I'm going to want it on the record somewhere that I noticed."

---

Shizune sat with her hand flat on the pocket of her own coat.

"That's fair," she said. "Say it again on Monday and I'll write it
down."

"All right."

"Sit down, Ruri."

He sat down.

"Now listen to me, because I have had two days of watching this and
I've got one thing and then I'll leave you alone.

"You are not going to be able to fix it."

"I know."

"You don't. You *think* you know, because you've written it in a book
somewhere with the word *no remedy* in it, and then you have got up
this morning and turned up here at the fourth hour and you will do it
again tomorrow, and every single thing you have done for three days
has been you running the experiment again to see if the result holds."

He opened his mouth.

"Don't tell me it isn't," said Shizune, "because I have watched you do
it to a rock."

He shut his mouth.

"It holds," she said. "It's going to hold. It held for me at nineteen
and it holds for absolutely everybody and there is nothing clever in
it, and the sooner you stop looking for the fault in your own hand the
sooner you'll be able to put a number on a form.

"There's no fault. Your hand's fine.

"You've just got somebody in your head, and that's all it is, and it
happens to about four billion people and every single one of them
thinks they're the first."

---

He sat there.

*She is right.*

*She is right and it is the third correct thing she has said to me in
three days and she is going to be right about the next one as well, and
I am going to have to find some way of not treating that as a resource.*

*And she is wrong about one part, and it is not worth saying, and I am
going to say it to myself and leave it here.*

*It is not that I think I am the first.*

*It is that four billion people got this at about fourteen, over some
months, with somebody their own age, in a village where the entire
machinery exists to catch it — and I got it at eighteen, in one night,
at four inches, off the Hokage.*

*There was no ramp.*

*There is nothing in my books. There is nothing in anybody's books that
is any use, because everybody who wrote one had already had the fifteen
years of practice that I did not have.*

*And the only person I would ordinarily go to with a thing I have no
data on is her.*
