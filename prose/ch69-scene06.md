# Chapter 69, Scene 6 — The Workbench

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the second floor. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1300 words.*

---

The first fitting ran Thursday evening, upstairs, under the
working protocol, and he prepared for it the way he had learned
not to prepare for a bath: no theatre, and the instruments doing
the room's talking.

They were laid out on the cloth in working order. The tape. The
calipers, cork-tipped. The reference cards for the fit series.
And the four load-reading articles he had grown for the purpose —
small flat ovals, warm as hands, each one reporting the pressure
across it as a change in its own standing note, pitched below
hearing and read off his fingertips like braille. He had built
them in an afternoon and they were, by a wide margin, the finest
instruments in the project, and they existed because the project's
lead model was the one wearer whose measurements he could not
survive taking by eye alone. Necessity, as ever, running the
research division.

She arrived at eight in the calibration rig — the plain fit-frame
Sonobe's linen room had run up to his diagram — with the border
digest under one arm, on the theory, stated at the door, that she
could read while he worked.

The digest did not survive the first ten minutes. That was the
evening's first finding.

"You're reading the load off *sound*," said Tsunade, abandoning a
trade dispute mid-column to watch him work the second oval along
the anchor line, fingertips resting on it like a man taking a
pulse. "That's — give me that. That is a palpation technique.
That is *my* palpation technique, the pressure-mapping, the one I
have spent twenty years failing to teach because nobody's hands
listen — you've built my hands into a *stone*."

"I built four of them," said Sazare, in the working register,
which was holding, which he noted in the running log with
cautious satisfaction. "The technique is in the ward
documentation under your name, which I have read eleven times.
The articles are the same finding at a different scale: the
structure reports, if the instrument is patient. Arm up,
please. Breathe at working depth."

And she did — that was the second finding, the one he had not
forecast and would spend the walk to the field tomorrow
re-reading: she *complied*, instrument-precise, medic to
engineer, and then leaned into the work itself, and within
twenty minutes the fitting had stopped being a procedure
conducted on her and become a thing being built by both of them,
because of course it had, because he had somehow — planning the
insulation, planning the clipboard — failed to plan for the one
property of hers that three years of the ledger documented more
thoroughly than any other: she could not stand next to work
being done well and not get her hands in it.

They argued about strap geometry for eleven minutes. Properly
argued — she held that the load wanted to come off the shoulder
entirely, onto the frame band, citing four decades of shoulder-
groove injuries; he held that a zero-load strap was a stability
loss under inversion, citing Akimoto's henge-and-handspring
spec; and they went at it with diagrams, her sketching on the
back of the abandoned digest, him countering on the reference
cards, until they arrived — simultaneously, in the manner of
two people reaching the same ledge from opposite faces — at the
graded-transfer answer, strap load falling to nothing at rest
and waking under acceleration, which the seal family could do,
which nothing but the seal family could do, and which neither
of them had held in their hands before the argument built it.

"That's new," said Tsunade, looking at the composite diagram
with the specific hunger she brought to a promising chart.
"That's actually new. Nobody has ever built that because
nobody who could build it ever had the — " she waved the digest
at the whole of it, the rig, the ovals, the evening — "the
*access*."

"The access," he agreed, deadpan, and she pointed the digest at
him like a summons and went back to the diagram, and the log
recorded minute thirty-one, register holding, and underneath
the register, where the log did not go, something that had been
true for a month finished becoming obvious:

*This is the bench. This is what the small rows were reaching
for and could not name — not a moment; moments are what she
makes. A workbench. A thing that is ours, that neither of us
brought to the other finished, that needs both trades to
build. The holiday was hers. The bath was mine. This we are
standing at together, and it does not care that one of us is a
Hokage and one of us is eighteen and the whole village would
require a chair if it saw the diagram we have just drawn on
the back of a border digest.*

*I came here to survive proximity. I appear instead to have
found the room where we live.*

---

The measurements themselves, when they finally happened, were
the easiest quarter hour of the month — that was the theory
vindicated, and he logged it honestly at the time and stood by
it after: the working register, loaded with a real problem and
a real collaborator, carried him through territory that had
been lethal in November. The numbers went down. The ovals sang
their quarter-tones. The vocabulary held — anchor, span,
moment, distribution — and if the instrument beneath it ran
warm the entire time, warm was within tolerance, warm was
*documented*, warm had a whole appendix now.

She waited until minute forty-one.

The session was closing — instruments back in their roll, the
last figures entered, the register beginning to stand down like
a guard at shift's end — and Tsunade, still in the rig,
stretched. Once. Completely. Unhurried as the crawl had been
unhurried, a woman easing a long day out of her spine in a
manner that was fully, medically justifiable and had no
business whatsoever being also what it also was, and said, to
the ceiling, in the flat administrative voice:

"For the minutes. Best fitting I have had in fifty years. The
fabricator is a thorough professional. His protocols held the
entire session." A pause, measured in half-seconds, surgical.
"*Nearly* the entire session."

The copper patches arrived on schedule. The vocabulary went the
way it went. The tape measure, abandoned at the wrong moment by
every department responsible for it, unspooled to the floor
with a small administrative clatter, and she watched the whole
cascade go down with the satisfaction of a researcher whose
long-running study had just returned its eleventh consecutive
confirmation, and handed him back his clipboard with both
hands, courteous as a colleague.

"Noted for the file," she said, warm as the ovals. "Occupied
instruments overload slower. They do still overload. Same time
next week, fabricator — and bring attachment thirteen,
whatever it turns out to be. I find I'm a collector."

*Entry, Thursday, one size, for honesty:*

*Theory: partially confirmed, which from this researcher is a
career best. Forty minutes at ranges that were lethal in
November; the armour is real; the armour has a scheduled
failure at whatever minute she chooses, because the metering
was never mine, and I have stopped wanting it to be.*

*Findings that outrank the theory, in order: we have a bench
now, both of us at it, and it built something tonight that
neither trade owns alone. Her palpation technique is in my
ovals and my grading is in her diagram and the strap answer
belongs to the argument, not to either arguer.*

*The study continues. The study, it turns out, was never the
point. The bench was the point.*

*She commissioned attachment thirteen. She has no idea what it
will be. Neither do I. We are both looking forward to it, and
only one of us has admitted it, and for once — the file will
note the date — it is me.*
