# Chapter 61, Scene 4 — Custody

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~2020 words.*

---

She did it at two that afternoon, with Shizune in the room and the door
shut and the minute book on the desk between them, and she did it the
way she did a determination, which was to state it and then take
objections and then not move.

"Four things," said Tsunade. "Custody, access, succession, and one about
him.

"Custody. It stays in this building and it stays in my personal keeping
and it does not go in the archive, the annexe, the vault or the sealing
office. It is not a village document and it is not going to be given a
reference, because a reference is a thing people can request against.

"It goes in the drawer with the fragment and the eleven pages."

Sazare said, "That drawer is nine inches of softwood with a warded lock
that I could open."

"I know."

"So could four people in this village and two of them are on that
council."

"I know that as well," said Tsunade. "Say the rest of it."

"There's no safe place."

"There is no safe place," she agreed. "That is the first honest thing
about any of this and I want it said out loud in front of a witness at
the beginning rather than pretended about at the end. Every option is
bad. I am picking the one where the failure mode is a burglary rather
than a *requisition*, because I can do something about a burglary."

---

"Access.

"Five people were in that room and five names are in that book. Me,
Ruri, Shizune, Fukasaku and Shima — and Katsuyu, so six, and I'll come
to why she is not on the list in a minute.

"Nobody reads it who was not there. Not the Corps board, not the
council, not Yagi, not Kanzaki. Not Jiraiya."

"Jiraiya's *in* it," said Shizune.

"Jiraiya is in it as the subject. He isn't a witness, he was the
article." Tsunade turned the book round. "And he can read it any time he
likes and he is not going to ask, and if you want the whole of that
argument you can have it off him and not off me."

She put her hand flat on the cover.

"And the rule for anybody else, ever, is that it requires all five to
agree. In person. Not in writing.

"Which means it requires two toads to walk four hundred miles."

---

Sazare stood very still.

*She has just made it unreadable.*

*Not sealed. Not classified. There is no notice on it and nothing for
anybody to appeal against and no register that says it exists.*

*She has built a condition that cannot practically be met by anybody at
any time without an event that would be visible from the moon, and she
has done it in one sentence, and the mechanism is that Fukasaku
withdrew his copy this morning.*

*If he had taken it, the mountains would be inside the chain and the
chain would run at the speed of a scroll.*

*He gave it up and the effect of giving it up is that the door now
requires him to come through it on foot.*

*I do not think either of them planned that.*

*I am not going to say so.*

"Objection?" said Tsunade.

"No."

"You've gone quiet."

"I've gone quiet because it's better than anything I had," said Sazare,
"and I'd like about a day before I say why."

---

"Succession," said Tsunade.

"If I die, it goes to Shizune. Not to the office — to *her*, by name,
and she can have that argument with whoever is sitting in this chair and
she is going to win it because I am going to write it down this
afternoon in four lines and sign it.

"If Shizune dies, it goes to Ruri."

"That's the wrong way round," said Sazare.

"It is not."

"I'm eighteen. Actuarially I outlive both of you by—"

"Ruri," said Shizune, "don't."

"— it's a table, it's not an opinion—"

"*Don't.*"

He stopped.

"And if all three of us go," said Tsunade, "it goes nowhere."

He looked up.

"Nowhere. Not to a successor, not to a mountain, not to a vault. I am
going to write that down as well and it is going to be the fourth line
and it is going to say *and thereafter it is not to be preserved*."

---

"That's a finding that can be lost," said Sazare.

"Yes."

"You are deliberately building a document that can go out of existence."

"Yes."

"That's—" He stopped and started again, because the first version had
come out with heat in it and heat was not going to help. "A finding
which can be lost is not a finding. It's a rumour with a date on it.

"The whole of the difference between last night and everything before it
is that we stopped having testimony. That is the *only* thing that
happened. And you have just put a terminus on the one copy in the world
and written it into the succession as a positive instruction."

"I have."

"Why?"

Tsunade sat back.

"Because I have thought about who is standing in this room the day all
three of us are dead," she said, "and I do not know that person, and
neither do you, and there is no version of that morning where the book
is safer than the world is.

"You have got a paper about it. Page eleven. You wrote that the short
list of families that are genuinely heritable is a targeting document
and that publishing it would get people killed, and I agreed with you on
the first of the eighth month and I have not stopped.

"That book is worse than the paper. The paper is an argument and
arguments can be disputed. That is a *demonstration* with a weight on
it, and the first thing anybody does with a demonstration is repeat it,
and the second thing they do is repeat it on somebody who did not
volunteer."

She turned the book round again.

"There are sixty children in a hole somewhere because a man wanted to
find out whether it could be done," she said. "It is not a hypothetical
and you know the name and so do I and neither of us has said it out loud
in this building in five days.

"He did it *without* this book.

"I am not leaving a copy of the method lying around for the next one, and
I would rather it went out like a lamp than that."

---

He stood there for some time.

*She has used my own sentence.*

*Fourteen hours. She has had it fourteen hours and she has turned it
round and put it against me, and it is a completely fair use of it and I
would have done the same thing.*

*And she is right.*

"You're right," he said.

"I'd worked that out."

"I want to say the cost out loud anyway, because if I don't say it now I
will be arguing with it in a book at eleven o'clock tonight.

"The cost is that it might go. And if it goes then everything from
midnight last night is gone with it, and somebody in a hundred years
does the whole thing again from the beginning, and there is no way for
me to leave them a note.

"And I have got no answer to that and neither have you and there isn't
one."

"No," said Tsunade. "There isn't."

Shizune, at the end of the desk, said, "Fukasaku."

They both turned.

"He's got it," said Shizune. "That's the answer to it, isn't it. It's
in a toad who is going to be alive in three hundred years and who has
already carried one for four hundred, and it is not written anywhere
and it cannot be taken and it goes on existing as long as anybody keeps
asking him.

"That's not nothing."

"That's not nothing," Sazare agreed, "and I'd like it on the record that
I said this morning it was the strong version and I have spent the last
ten minutes arguing as though it weren't."

---

"Fourth thing," said Tsunade.

"Go on."

"It doesn't go in your book."

He did not say anything at all.

"Not the method, not the result, not the weight, not the date. Nothing
about the second of the twelfth month goes in a notebook that lives in a
room over a persimmon tree with a door that does not lock, or in the
inside pocket of a coat that you take up a hill at midnight."

"I don't live there any more."

"You live in this building because I put you in it eleven days ago and
you have got four notebooks and a box of fourteen still in that room,"
said Tsunade, "and I want that dealt with as well, and it is a separate
argument and we are going to have it this week."

---

"That's a concealment," said Sazare.

"It's a—"

"No, let me have it properly, because it's the only one I have got and I
would rather lose it out loud.

"I write everything down. That's not a habit, it is the entire method
and it is the only thing I have ever had. I have got four hundred pages
on people who did not write down the bad ones and I have got nought
point one one grams because a slug watched my hands and I wrote down
what she said.

"And a man who has made a finding and has not put it in his book is a
man holding something that nobody knows he is holding, and I have said
that in this room before — I said it about the paper and I gave you the
only copy so that I would not be that man.

"You are asking me to be that man on purpose."

"I am," said Tsunade.

"Why is this different?"

"Because when you gave me the paper there was one copy and you knew
where it was," she said. "This is not you holding something nobody knows
about. Six of us know. Shizune has minuted it. Fukasaku is walking home
with it in his head.

"It is not concealed, Ruri. It is *placed*.

"And your books are a beautiful, complete, indexed, entirely unsecured
record of everything you have ever thought, in the hand of a man who has
never once had to be careful, and the only reason nobody has taken them
is that until the eleventh of last month nobody wanted anything."

---

He looked at the desk for a while.

"Agreed," he said.

"That was faster than I expected."

"It was faster than I expected as well and I want to say why, because it
is not because you have persuaded me."

"Go on."

"It's the fifth time."

Shizune's head came up.

"Twelfth of the eleventh, in a kitchen, Ibiki, and I said all right in
two seconds," said Sazare. "The fourteenth, a spoiled form in a coat
pocket. Some time before the twenty-second, the paper going to Jiraiya,
and I found out by walking into a room. The seventeenth, a request
backdated to the twelfth.

"And now a finding that does not go in my own book.

"All five were correct. Every one. I have checked all five and I would
have done four of them myself and the fifth one I have just lost the
argument about in front of a witness.

"That is not the thing I am watching.

"The thing I am watching is that on the twelfth of last month it took me
two seconds and I wrote four pages about it that night. And this
afternoon it took me about eleven and I am not going to write anything
at all.

"I am getting used to it."

Nobody said anything.

"That's all," he said. "I'm not asking anybody to do anything about it.
I would just like it to have been said in a room with three people in it
on the second of the twelfth month, and now it has been."
