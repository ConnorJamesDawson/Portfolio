# Chapter 58, Scene 2 — Six

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1250 words.*

---

He sat down on the floor at nine that evening with the lamp low and did
the exercise for the first time in six days.

*Ex. 1. Twenty-first attempt at fifteen or above. Conditions ordinary.
Observer: none, which is standing.*

*Best: fifteen, eleventh of this month, on a hill, and it was not hard
at the end.*

He put his hands on his knees and went still.

---

It did not come.

That was not alarming in itself. It had not come on about four
occasions in eleven weeks and the cause had always been obvious — cold,
or a hand that hurt, or the one time in the ninth month when he had
been so tired that he had fallen over sideways and Katsuyu had told him
to go to bed in the flattest voice he had ever heard out of her.

He waited.

*Nothing.*

*Right. Again.*

He went still.

And this time he felt it start, and it was there, and he had about a
second and a half of it coming in the way it came in — which was not
like anything, which was the thing he had never been able to write down,
which was less like filling than like a room getting larger —

*That's the gather.*

It stopped.

---

He sat on the floor with his eyes shut and his hands on his knees and
had, for the first time in eleven weeks, absolutely nothing.

*Say it.*

*I thought a sentence in the middle of it.*

*I have never thought a sentence in the middle of it. There has never
been anything to think. It has been two minutes of nothing since the
eighth month — you sit down, it comes, you hold it, you count, you let
it go, and I have written down the number nineteen times and never once
written down anything about the middle because there is nothing in the
middle.*

*And on Monday morning a man on a roof told me what the middle is
called, and now there is something in the middle, and it is me
watching.*

He tried again.

He got about four seconds and it stopped, and it stopped in the same
place, and he knew exactly what had happened because he had felt himself
do it.

*I checked.*

*I was in it and I checked whether I was in it.*

---

He got up and walked to the window and stood there for a bit.

*This is the observer problem and it is the oldest one there is and I
have been on the right side of it my entire life.*

*You cannot measure a thing without touching it. Chapter one of every
book anybody has ever written about anything. I have four hundred pages
on it. I have a whole method built on it — you do not read an article
while it is under load, you read it before and after, because the read
is a load.*

*And I have spent eleven years being the instrument and never once the
article.*

He put his hand flat on the frame.

*It requires me to go still.*

*He said it on the roof. You go still the way water goes still —
everything stops, and something comes in, and you spend it, and you are
back before most people have finished blinking.*

*And I cannot go still while I am watching myself go still.*

*Nobody can. That is not a fault in me. That is what still means.*

---

He sat back down and did it eleven more times.

He got six on the fourth attempt and he was pleased about the six for
approximately one second, until he worked out that the six had come
because he had been irritated and had stopped paying attention, which
meant the six was not a recovery, it was an accident.

He got four twice.

He got nothing seven times.

At about twenty past ten he stopped, because the eleventh attempt had
produced a thing he did not like: a half-second of the gather starting
and then a very clear, very cold arrival of an entirely different
thought.

*If I take too much of this I turn to stone.*

He sat with that for a while.

---

*Right. Write it up honestly and do not soften it.*

*Ex. 1, 17/11. Twelve attempts. Best 6, accidental. Seven at zero.*

*Cause: identified, complete, and I have no remedy.*

*For eleven weeks the most important operation I have ever learned has
run entirely below the level of my attention. I did not know what it was
called. I did not know what it was doing. I did not know it had a
ceiling and I did not know that the ceiling had killed most of the
people who ever went near it.*

*I have got all four of those now, in one morning, off a man who was
being kind.*

*And the operation requires the absence of exactly the faculty I have
just pointed at it.*

He stopped and read that back and then wrote the rest, because it was
the honest half.

*It is not the word that has done it. Knowing the word is worth having
and I would not give it back.*

*It is the stone.*

*I have got a fear in the middle of it now, and the fear is correct —
it is the most correct fear I have ever had, it is supported by a
patch on my own palm — and correct fear is still fear, and fear is
attention, and attention is the one thing this operation cannot survive.*

*Second time in ten days that knowing the mechanism has made a thing
worse rather than better.*

*I have got no entry anywhere in fourteen books for that pattern and now
I have got two in a fortnight.*

---

He put the pencil down and did not put a column figure in, because there
was not one, and then went back and wrote *6 (acc.)* in the column
because leaving it blank was a decision and putting the honest number in
was not.

Then he sat against the wall for a while and looked at the ceiling.

*Katsuyu will know what to do about this.*

The thought arrived complete and without any warning, the way things did
now, and he sat with it and found that it had two halves and that the
second half was considerably worse than the first.

*She will know. She will know inside about four seconds, because she
found eleven years of faults in my hands in one evening and she has
never once been at a loss with anything I have brought her.*

*And I am going to walk into that yard on Friday and the first thing out
of me is going to be a problem.*

*That is what I do. Somebody who has given me nineteen evenings for
nothing is four hundred miles away and I have not been near her in
thirteen days, and I have just caught myself looking forward to Friday
because I have got something to ask.*

He looked at the tin on the low table for a bit.

*No.*

*Friday, she gets the apology, and then she gets the whole of Monday
morning, and then if there is anything left in the evening at all she can
have the six.*

*And if there isn't, then there isn't, and the six can wait a week, and
I have never once in my life let a fault wait a week.*

*Start there.*
