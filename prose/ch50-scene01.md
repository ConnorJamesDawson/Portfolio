# Chapter 50, Scene 1 — Four Days

*Act 4, part D. Konoha General, outside the records annexe. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,514 words.*

---

He counted them, because he counted everything.

*Day one.* Nothing. He worked a shift, went to the yard, put a hundred
and forty hours into a rod that was now sixteen inches of the twenty-one
it would need to be, and did not go up the hill.

*Day two.* Nothing.

*Day three.* Nothing, and at about the fourth hour of the morning he
wrote a line in the notebook and struck it out and did not write it
again, which he entered as an event because striking a line out was not
a thing he did.

*Day four.*

---

Shizune came out of the records annexe at about half past eleven with a
folder under her arm and stopped when she saw him.

That was the first thing. She *stopped* — not slowed, not adjusted; she
came round the corner at the pace of a woman with eleven things left
before two o'clock and put a foot down and did not put the next one
down, and stood in a corridor looking at him for about a second and a
half.

"Sazare-kun."

"Shizune-san."

"I—" She looked at the folder. "You've not been up."

"No."

"No. Right." She moved the folder to the other arm, which she did not
need to do. "I was going to leave it and then I thought — no. If it
were me I'd want to know, and I have been arguing with myself about
that for four days and I have not won either side of it, so I'm just
going to say it and you can do what you like with it."

"All right."

"I was in the doorway."

He did not say anything.

"I had the eleven o'clock signatures and she doesn't sleep, so I go up
at about two if the light's still on, and the light was still on, and I
was in the doorway with the folder when it — when Katsuyu-sama arrived
on the desk." Shizune's voice was doing something at the edges that she
was managing and not entirely managing. "With a flower."

"Yes."

"You knew?"

"I made it," said Sazare. "I made it at about half past one in a yard
and I asked her how I was supposed to give it to anybody and she said
*ah, this one is easy*, and then she took it, and she was extremely
clear that she was not carrying it for me and that whatever she said
when she got there was hers."

Shizune shut her eyes briefly.

"It was hers," she said. "It was very much hers."

---

She told him what Katsuyu had said, and she got most of the way through
it in an ordinary voice and did not manage the last four words, and
then had to stop for a moment in a corridor with a folder under her arm
in a building where four hundred people were working.

Sazare stood and let her.

He had learned that in the fifth month from a fourteen-year-old on a
landing and had been bad at it for two years, and he was still bad at
it, and he did it anyway.

"Sorry," said Shizune.

"Don't be."

"I'm not — it isn't sad. That's the *problem*, it isn't sad, and I've
been carrying it round for four days and I can't put it anywhere,
because there's nobody I can tell." She wiped under one eye with the
back of a knuckle, briskly, the way you deal with a bench spill. "And
now I've told you, which is probably the worst available choice, and I
would appreciate it enormously if you never mentioned that I did."

"I won't."

"You say that very easily."

"I've got about nine things in a notebook I've been asked not to
mention and I have not mentioned any of them," said Sazare. "One of
them's from the fifth month and it's about a person in this building
and I'd take it to the ground."

Shizune looked at him.

"...Yes," she said. "Yes, all right. I believe you and I'm sorry I
asked."

---

He asked the question then, because it had been sitting in him for four
days and he had constructed nine versions of it and the nine versions
had turned out not to matter.

"Where is it?"

Shizune's face closed.

It was not unkind. That was the thing he noted at the time and wrote
down afterwards — that it was a door shutting rather than a wall going
up, and that she did it with a certain amount of regret and no
hesitation whatsoever.

"I'm not going to answer that," she said.

"Right."

"I want to be clear that I'm not saying *I don't know*." She squared
the folder. "I know exactly. And it is not mine, and it is not yours,
and if you get it from anybody it will be from her and it will be
because she decided to, and I am not going to be the reason you found
out four days early."

"That's fair."

"It's *not* fair, it's correct, which isn't the same." She started to
go and then stopped. "I'll give you something else instead, because
you've been standing in this corridor being decent about it and I would
rather not walk away leaving you with nothing."

"You don't have to."

"I know I don't." Shizune looked at the wall for a second. "She didn't
say anything.

"Katsuyu-sama said her piece and set the thing down on the border
reports, and the Fifth did not say one word. Not to her, not to me.
For about a minute, which is — I have worked for that woman for a long
time and I have seen her handle a great many things, and I have never
seen her take a minute.

"And then she sent me home."

---

He walked back up to the second floor and worked the rest of the shift
and was competent throughout.

He wrote it that night with the window open and the eighth month
finally breaking, the first cool coming down off the hill at about
eleven.

*Day four. Shizune, corridor outside records, approx. 11:30. Reported
the delivery. She was in the doorway.*

*She would not tell me where it is and gave a reason which is correct
and which I would have given myself in her position, and I want that in
the entry because my first reaction was not gratitude and I had to go
and get gratitude from somewhere else about nine seconds later.*

*What I have: it arrived. It was set down on the border reports. What
was said was said and it was Katsuyu's and not mine.*

*What I do not have: where it is.*

He sat with that for a while.

*And I am not going to get it, and I want to be honest about the size
of the thing I am not getting, because it is small and I have been
carrying it around for five days as though it were large.*

*It is an object in a room. That is all. There are four places it can
be and every one of them is defensible:*

*— On a desk, which is the one I have been assuming without permitting
myself to assume it.*

*— In a drawer with the fragment and the eleven pages, which is the
correct security decision and which she would have reached inside four
seconds, because it is an open article and it draws and it hums and
that is evidence sitting on a windowsill in the Hokage's office.*

*— Given to somebody. Sent somewhere. Destroyed, which I do not
believe.*

*And the reason I want to know is not the object. I have made about
nine hundred objects and I have never once wanted to know where one of
them went.*

He looked at the page.

Then he wrote the honest half, because the ledger got both or it got
neither, and this one had been standing behind everything for five days
and had not been allowed forward.

*Six days ago I sat in a yard and gave five reasons why I could not
hand her a thing, and the fifth was that she would read the whole of me
in about four seconds, because she reads me faster than I read myself
and always has.*

*Katsuyu solved that. She took the object and left me out of the
delivery entirely and it was, on every measure I own, a complete
solution to the problem I stated.*

*And it has produced a room I am not in.*

*She took a minute and said nothing and sent Shizune home, and every
part of that happened at two in the morning in a building on a hill
while I sat in a yard putting a lamp out, and I have no report of any
of it and there is no version of the last five days in which I would.*

*I asked for a delivery that did not have me standing in the room at
the end of it.*

*I got one.*
