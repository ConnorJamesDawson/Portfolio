# Chapter 52, Scene 4 — One Hundred and Forty-One

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,527 words.*

---

He went and looked at the hundred and forty-one because a number on a
sheet was not a finding and he had said so to himself in a gateway
eleven days earlier.

It took an evening and most of them were nothing.

*Working brackets, approx. ninety of a hundred and forty-one. Reads a
document in an order that is not front to back. Signs with the arm and
not the wrist. Moves a sheet from the left stack to the right at a
rate that has nothing to do with reading, which is triage and not
carelessness, established third month of last year.*

*Physical brackets, approx. thirty. Short nails, unvarnished. The third
finger of the writing hand ridged where the brush sits. No rings, ever.
The haori is over the chair more often than on her and the chair has
taken the shape of it — shoulders held, back gone soft, the red circle
creased across the middle where it has been folded over the same rail
some thousands of times.*

*The bells that do not ring: four brackets, all of them queries, none
of them answered.*

*The gamble kanji: two.*

*Remainder, approx. twenty: things about her face that I could not
read, recorded because I could not read them, which is what the bracket
is for.*

He worked through the whole of it and it was, on the evidence, the
record of a man observing a superior he worked closely with, at about
the density you would expect from somebody who observed everything.

He had almost finished when he found the one he had never re-read.

---

*15/8. 23:05.*

He knew the entry existed. He had written it left-handed on the
sixteenth in a room over a persimmon tree with a patch on his palm the
size of a large coin, and he had written the whole of that night
because he wrote the whole of everything, and he had gone back to it
perhaps nine times since for the timings and the readings and the four
attempted closes.

He had never read the top of it.

He had, he established, sitting on the floor with the lamp down, gone
into that entry nine times and started at *22:24* every single time,
and the page above it had been there the whole while, and he had not
once let his eye go up.

*Right.*

He turned back to the top of the page.

---

*15/8, approx. 23:05. She came down the outside stair.*

*(I had four versions of the next ten minutes constructed on the way up
the hill and every one had me going up and being taken through a door.
Instead there was somebody coming down fast in the dark.)*

And then, in the bad left-handed writing, in the ordinary bracket, in
the ordinary place — because he had a stone hand and a rose in his
pocket and a thing on the end of his arm that had his fingerprints on
it and was not his, and had recorded it anyway, because recording was
not a thing he decided to do:

*(No haori. Sleeves already pushed back to the elbow, which she does
before she works and which means she had done it on the stairs. Hair
not up — I have never seen it down and I did not know it was that
long, and it is heavier than it looks and it moved wrong for something
that light, which means there is more of it than the arrangement
admits.*

*Shirt, not the good one — the working one, and it is cut for somebody
who does not intend to be looked at, and it does not achieve that,
and I want to record that I noticed the failure of the garment before
I noticed anything else, which is the trade and is not a defence.*

*The line from the shoulder is not the line the coat gives her. The
coat is doing a great deal of work and I had not understood how much
until it was not there.*

*She is not thirty. She is not thirty by a considerable margin and the
technique holds the face and does not hold the way a person carries
weight, and everything below the collarbone is the age it actually is,
and it is —)*

The bracket stopped there.

It stopped mid-clause, with the pencil having gone through the paper
slightly on the last stroke, and then there was a line struck through
the whole of it — one line, ruled, edge to edge, drawn with something
straight, which meant he had got up and found a rule to do it with.

And then, under the strike-through, in the same bad hand:

*Leave it. It is there. Striking a thing out does not remove it and I
have never once pretended otherwise about anybody else's records.*

*22:24 approx — contact.*

---

He sat on the floor for a long time.

*So I did look.*

*I have written in this book, twice, that I decided not to look, and
the second time was in the fifth month and I was extremely satisfied
with the sentence — I did not decide to do nothing, I decided not to
look — and I have quoted it to myself since.*

*It is not true and it has not been true since the fifteenth of the
eighth month at five past eleven at night, and the evidence is in my
own hand, in the ordinary notation, with a rule through it and a note
underneath explaining why the rule does not count.*

*I looked. At the worst possible moment, in the worst possible
condition, at somebody who had come down a staircase at a run because I
was standing at the bottom of it with a hand going to stone — and my
hands wrote it down, because that is what they do, and they got four
clauses in before whatever it is that is supposed to stop a person
caught up and stopped them.*

*And then I went and found a rule.*

He read the struck-out bracket again.

*It is not an ungenerous description. I want that established because
it is the thing I would attack first if somebody else had written it.
There is nothing in it that is a leer. It is an inventory of fabric and
line and load and what age does to a thing, in the only vocabulary I
have got, and every clause in it is a true clause and about half of
them are about tailoring.*

*And it is completely transparent and I could not see it at the time
and I can see it now, and the tell is not any of the clauses. It is
that I recorded the failure of the garment before I recorded anything
else about a woman who was at that moment about to spend six hours
taking a substance out of my arm by hand.*

*The trade is not a defence. I wrote that at the time as well.*

---

He did the honest half at about two in the morning and it took him
three attempts.

*Two things and I am going to put the smaller one first because it is
the one I would otherwise leave out.*

*One. I did not go back to that page nine times by accident. I went to
22:24 nine times and it is at the bottom of the sheet and my eye went
past four inches of my own handwriting every time and did not go up,
and that is not an oversight, that is a manoeuvre, and I have been
running it on myself since the sixteenth of the eighth month.*

*Two. I am not going to strike it out again.*

He looked at the page.

*The rule stays where it is. It was drawn on the sixteenth by a man who
thought the noticing was the fault, and it was the correct instinct
about the wrong object, and I am leaving it because I do not remove
things.*

*But I have read it now, on purpose, at twenty past one in the morning
on the twenty-sixth of the tenth month, having gone and found it, and
that is a different act from writing it with a stone hand at eleven at
night and I am not going to let it be filed under the same heading.*

*I looked, and I have now looked again, deliberately, once.*

*And I am not going to do anything about it, which is the same sentence
I wrote in the fifth month and means something different now, because
in the fifth month it meant I was refusing to find out and tonight it
means I have found out and am declining to act.*

*Those are not the same and I have needed five months and a stone hand
to be able to tell the difference.*

He closed the book.

Then he wrote one more line on the outside of the page, small, in the
margin, where he put things he wanted to be able to find:

*If I am ever asked, I looked. Do not let me say otherwise.*
