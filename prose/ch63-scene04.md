# Chapter 63, Scene 4 — Twenty Feet

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the yard behind the north store. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2330 words.*

---

They did the table on the Thursday and it took from the fourth hour until
about two in the afternoon and it was the best day's work of his year.

*Conversion. What one minute of hold buys.*

*Method: gather to a known figure, hold, spend against a stated article,
and take the time off the clock at the point the hold breaks.*

*Katsuyu holding the count because I cannot hold a count and spend at the
same time, which is itself the first finding and I should have had it in
the eighth month.*

He wrote it out at noon with his back against the cart door.

*One minute of hold, at working density, buys approximately:*

*— 0.9 cubic feet of graded article at three-deep, which is the ordinary
splint standard and which will stop a person and will not stop anything
thrown at it.*

*— or 0.22 cubic feet at eleven-deep, which is what the wall wanted and
which will take a hit.*

*— or about forty of the small ones. Spikes, bars, collars, a trip.
Forty. That figure is the one that surprised me and I have run it three
times.*

*So: reinforcement costs roughly four times volume, and the small things
are almost free, and the whole of my capacity converts into about
seventeen minutes of walls or about four thousand spikes and there is
nothing in between that is worth having.*

He looked at that for a while.

*And I am at six.*

*Six minutes. Not fifteen. So the figures above are the fifteen figures
and I have got two-fifths of them, and the honest sheet says: about five
and a half walls that will take a hit, or about two hundred and forty
small ones, or some mixture, and after that I am a man standing in a yard
with a lamp.*

*I have known my own capacity as a number in a column for eleven weeks
and I have never once known what it was worth.*

*It is worth two hundred and forty spikes.*

*That is either an enormous amount or nothing whatsoever and I have got
no idea which, because I have never watched a fight.*

---

Range was the afternoon and range was not what he had expected.

He had expected a wall. Twenty feet and then nothing, because twenty feet
was the figure he had, and the figure had come from a bench and a yard
and about nine hundred articles laid within arm's reach or on a felt.

It was not a wall.

*Twenty feet: ordinary. Costs what it costs at nine inches. No
difference I can measure.*

*Twenty-five: costs more. About a third more for the same article and I
have run it eleven times and the third is consistent.*

*Thirty: about double.*

*Forty: about four times and it is *slower*, which is new — it is the
first time in my life that an operation has taken measurably longer
because of where it was and not what it was.*

*Fifty-five: I got one. Once. It cost me about forty seconds of hold for
a thing that costs a second and a half at nine inches, and it came up
wrong at the second junction and I would not have accepted it off
anybody.*

*Sixty: nothing. Four attempts, nothing at all, and the fourth one gave
me a feeling in the left hand that stopped the experiment.*

He wrote it up on the step.

*Finding: range is not a limit. It is a price.*

*It goes up sharply with distance and it goes up in both currencies —
capacity and time — and there is no cliff anywhere. It simply becomes
too expensive to be worth doing, at about forty feet for anything
sizeable and at about fifty-five for anything at all.*

*I have been carrying "twenty feet" round in my head as a wall for two
years and it is not a wall, it is the point at which it is still cheap.*

---

And then, at about four, he found the cheat.

He had a spike left over from the range work at thirty feet — blunt, four
inches, laid and closed — and he was walking back to the bench and he
thought about moving it without thinking about thinking about it, and it
moved.

He stopped.

*What did that cost?*

He did it again. And again, and eleven more times, and then he sat down
on the step of the cart door with his hands shaking slightly, which had
happened twice before in his life and both of them had been in a yard.

"Katsuyu."

"Yes."

"Moving one that's already there is free."

"Say it precisely."

"It's not free. It's — " He looked at the sheet. "It is about a fortieth
of the cost of making it, and it does not go up with distance in any
amount I can measure out to fifty-five feet, which is as far as I have
got a spike.

"Making a thing at forty feet costs four times what it costs at nine
inches.

"*Moving* a thing at forty feet costs the same as moving one at nine
inches."

"Yes," said Katsuyu. "That is the invasion, is it not."

---

*It is the invasion.*

*It is the eleventh month of the year I was seventeen and about eleven
minutes of it that I have never written up properly because I did not
have a frame, and I have got four lines about it and one of them is* did
not need to be near it.

*I have known I could do that for over a year and I have never once
costed it, because at a bench everything is within arm's reach and the
question does not arise.*

He wrote it out in capitals, which he did not do.

*RANGE IS A PRICE. PLACED ARTICLES ARE NOT.*

*Therefore the whole of the problem is not how far I can reach.*

*It is how much of the ground is already mine.*

*A thing laid on the floor at the start of a morning, closed, inert,
costing me a second and a half, is a thing I can use at fifty feet for a
fortieth of a spike for as long as it holds order.*

*Which is six days.*

*And an open one is not six days.*

He put the pencil down.

*And that is the whole tactical answer and it took me four days and I
have been able to do every single part of it since I was seventeen.*

---

Tsunade came out at about five with two cups of something and sat down on
the step and read the sheet without asking.

"Why aren't you using scalpels?"

"...Sorry?"

"Chakra scalpels. You've got the control for it — you've got better
control than anybody on my ward and I have said so to Yagi twice. Why
isn't it on the sheet?"

He sat there.

*Because it did not occur to me.*

*It did not occur to me for four days. It has not occurred to me at any
point since Monday and it did not occur to me on the eleventh when I sat
in an office and told a room that I had three operations, and it is not
three operations, it is four, and I have been doing the fourth one since
I was sixteen.*

"I've got them," he said.

"I know you've got them. I've *watched* you use them. You took a
subperiosteal in the fourth month with a scalpel because Yagi asked for
clean margins and you gave her clean margins."

"That's surgery."

"Ruri."

"That is not me being clever. I want to be precise about it because it is
a genuine finding about myself and it is not a good one." He put the cup
down. "It is in a different place.

"Everything I have got is in a book with the operation at the top of the
page. Crystal is in the yard log. Wood is in the small book. The gather
is in the column at the bottom.

"The scalpel is in the ward notes.

"It is not in the same *volume*. It has never been in the same volume. It
went in under *procedures* in the ninth month of my sixteenth year with
about four lines on control and a note about margins, and it has been
sitting there for two years and three months next to a thing about
sutures, and I have not once had a thought that put it in a column with
crystal in it."

---

"And now you have," said Tsunade.

"And now I have."

"So say the rest of it."

*Right.*

*She is going to make me say it and she is right to.*

"A chakra scalpel is a blade about two inches long that will go through
anything a knife goes through and will not go through anything a knife
does not," he said, "and its entire value is that it is precise, and I
am precise, and I have got four years of ward notes and about two
thousand hours of anatomy.

"I know exactly where a human being comes apart.

"Not approximately. I have had my hands in four of them and I have
assisted on about ninety and I have written the notes on four thousand,
and I could put a scalpel through the axillary in about a third of a
second from a position I could reach on any person who came within arm's
reach of me, and they would have about ninety seconds and there is nothing
on a training ground that would stop it.

"I could do that on Friday.

"I am not going to do that on Friday and I am not going to do it on any
other day, and the reason is not that it would be wrong, although it
would.

"It is that I am the man who is standing on the other side of it at four
in the morning telling somebody's mother in a corridor."

Tsunade did not say anything for a moment.

"Put it on the sheet," she said.

"Hime—"

"Put it on the sheet. Not as a technique. Put it under a line and write
*not to be used* and put the date on it."

"Why?"

"Because in about four years somebody is going to be standing over you
with something, and you are going to have about a third of a second and
a decision," said Tsunade, "and I would rather you made that decision on
a Thursday afternoon in a yard with me sitting next to you than at the
time."

He put it on the sheet.

---

They did one more hold before the light went, because Katsuyu wanted a
long one and because the watching had been wearing out all day.

He got eleven minutes.

It was the longest since the eleventh of the eleventh month and he did
not chase it and did not congratulate himself about it, and it came
because he had been working for nine hours and had stopped being
interested in himself, which he had begun to suspect was the only
mechanism there was.

He let it down at eleven minutes and about forty seconds and sat back
against the cart door.

"Ruri," said Katsuyu.

"Mm."

"Your eyes have gone."

---

He opened them.

"Gone where?"

"That is not the word. I do not have the word." She was on the step at
about nine inches. "They have gone *clear*.

"Not pale. Clear. The light is going into them and coming back and it is
coming back without the scatter, which is a thing I have said to you
about four hundred articles and never about a person, and I am aware of
how that sounds."

He looked at the back of his own hand and then at the lamp, and then
blinked twice, which did not do anything.

"It'll be the lamp," he said.

"It is not the lamp. I have been at nine inches for nine hours."

"Then it's the hold. Vasomotor — you get it in the sclera after
sustained exertion and I have seen it about eleven hundred times on a
ward, and it goes off in about four minutes."

"Perhaps," said Katsuyu.

"You don't think so."

"I have no view. I have no framework at all and I am not going to invent
one at half past five in the afternoon." She paused. "There is a second
part and I have decided to say it and I am aware that it is going to
sound like a joke, and I do not make jokes."

"Go on."

"You have got my markings."

---

He looked at her.

"...Sorry?"

"From the inner corner of each eye, running down. About an inch. They are
the shape of mine and they are the colour of mine and I have had them for
four hundred years and I have never seen them on anything that was not a
slug."

Sazare sat on the step of a dye-works in the yard behind a hospital and
laughed, which he did about four times a year.

"That's the first joke you've ever made," he said.

"I have not—"

"That's *very* good. That's genuinely very good and I did not think you
had it in you and I am going to write it down."

"Ruri—"

"Nineteen evenings and about two hundred corrections and not one of them
had anything in it," said Sazare, still laughing, "and then on a Thursday
in the twelfth month you sit on a step and tell a man he has got your
face on him.

"I'll allow it. Chapter four of the book says you're supposed to allow
it."

Katsuyu did not say anything at all.

He wiped his eyes with the back of his wrist and got up and went to see
to the lamp, and by the time he came back it had been about four minutes
and she did not raise it again.
