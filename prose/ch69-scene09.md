# Chapter 69, Scene 9 — Functional Leisure Wear

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower and the village. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1210 words.*

---

The pilot ran two weeks, and the pilot report, when Shizune
assembled it, was the shortest and strangest document ever to
carry his name.

*Adverse effects of prolonged crystal contact: none. n=31.
Article performance: within specification in all cases.
Prototype return rate at study close: zero of thirty-one.*

"There is no box," said Shizune, presenting it to the board
with the flat delivery she reserved for her best material,
"on any form this hospital owns, for *the cohort has refused
to conclude the study*. I have had to invent one. I would
like my invention minuted: the box is called *retention*, and
the pilot scored a hundred per cent in it, and in nineteen
years of protocol work I have never once seen the number."

That was the Tuesday. By the Thursday he was famous, and he
found out the way he found out everything about himself now,
which was in public, sideways, at speed.

It happened on the walk from the field, at the junction by the
east bathhouse: two kunoichi he had never met — jōnin flak,
mud to the knee, straight off a return — and the taller one
clocked him, stopped dead in the street, and said, "You're
the crystal one. The— " and did not say the word *bra* out
loud, because they were in a street, but performed instead a
small economical gesture at shoulder height that conveyed the
entire project with terrifying efficiency.

"Yes," said Sazare.

What followed he later entered in the book as *the pattern*,
because it repeated, in the identical order, in every one of
the eleven instances that week: first the gratitude —
delivered fast and slightly ferocious, the register of
somebody thanking you for a thing they had privately given
up on, the taller one saying *my sister does courier routes,
she cried, I want you to know that, she cried and then she
did handsprings in the yard* — and then, with no seam at
all, at a distance of approximately four seconds:

The requests.

Could it come in a halter cut for mesh armour. Could the band
sit lower for weapon harnesses. Was there going to be one for
under plate. Her squadmate had a shoulder thing, old injury,
could an article be graded asymmetric. Did he take
commissions. When. Where was the list. Who held the list.
*Was there a list.*

"There is a list," said Sazare, eleven times, that week,
because by Wednesday there was — Shizune had opened it after
the fourth petitioner reached her office by other channels —
and the list, at Friday's count, stood at one hundred and
forty-one names, for a pilot line of thirty-one articles, in
a village where the entire active kunoichi roster was not
four hundred.

*Entry: I am, apparently, famous. Not for the reef, which
made the ground come alive in front of six witnesses, and
not for the sage business, which has three keepers and a
veto. For underwear. The fame arrived in under a fortnight,
travels woman to woman at a speed the Corps signals office
should study, and is composed of one part gratitude to four
parts requests, and I have concluded the requests ARE the
gratitude — nobody petitions a thing they expect to vanish.
Filed accordingly. Warmly, if the file must know.*

---

The board met on the Friday for a double agenda — the colour
hour, as promised its own hour by the chair, and then the
numbers — and both halves left the road he had planned for
them.

The colour hour opened with Ino Yamanaka's palette proposal,
submitted in writing as threatened, read into the minutes by
Shizune with a completely level face: eleven colours, each
with a name, a dye specification, *and a field rationale* —
she had matched shades to terrain classes, forest and stone
and river-winter, "because," the proposal said, "if it ever
shows through torn cloth on a mission it can do camouflage's
job instead of resigning; function all the way down,
gentlemen," and the *gentlemen* was addressed to a board of
five women and one fabricator and was, Shizune ruled,
reading on without pause, rhetorical.

The matron moved acceptance of three for the first run. Akimoto
amended for the forest set on operational grounds. Sakura,
holding the clinical corner to the last, required dye-fastness
data against the one form, and got it minuted, and then voted
for the colours anyway with the speed of a woman who had been
grey's enemy for years. Carried, five to nothing, the
fabricator not voting and not asked, and he recorded in the
margin of his own copy that the first run of the Hime line
would ship in *slate blue, iron red, and winter pine*, and
that the room in which grey was defeated had conducted the
whole engagement in under eleven minutes with better staff
work than most border actions.

Then the numbers, and the numbers were where the afternoon
left the road entirely.

Shizune did the arithmetic standing up, off two pages, in the
voice of a woman detonating a controlled charge: the list at
one-forty-one and doubling weekly. The roster ceiling at four
hundred domestic — but the ward matron's cousin ran a
clothier's in the capital, and the matron had mentioned the
pilot in a letter, *once, in passing*, and the reply had come
back by fast post with a purchase inquiry for *whatever
quantity can be supplied* and a second sheet asking, with
capital-city directness, whether the maker did outerwear.

"Outerwear," said Shizune. "And I will read the next line as
written, because I could not improve it: *there is a market
here for garments that do something, for women who do things
— call it functional leisure wear — and whoever holds this
crystal holds the category.* At projected volumes, domestic
plus one trade city—" she turned the page— "the line clears
two million ryō in its first year. The village's share funds
the hospital's winter deficit. Twice."

The board looked at him.

He was aware of the look, and aware of the number — two
million; charcoal at two-twenty the sack; nine thousand
winters of charcoal — and aware, mostly, of a feeling he
took the whole walk home to identify, because it was
arriving from eleven years away.

*My family sold ornament to women for four hundred years.
Pins, combs, frames, the trestle grades and the kept-back
pieces. It was a good trade and it ended in a night, and I
have spent eleven years being its ghost — the last pair of
hands, keeping the method alive with no shop to put it in.*

*And the shop is back. It is not the same shop. It does not
sell ornament; it sells load paths; the Hokage chairs its
board and its first product is named for her, privately, in
a rationale I withheld. But it is the family trade, resumed,
adapted, solvent — women's work taken seriously by Ruri
hands — and the queue is out the door, and Jiraiya's only
condition sits over the export question like a customs
officer, and I have somehow become a fabricator with a
waiting list, a trademark, and a category.*

*Kohaku would have run the counter. I am not going to write
the rest of that thought tonight.*

*Entry closed. The winter deficit, twice. That part I am
framing.*
