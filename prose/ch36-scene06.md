# Chapter 36, Scene 6 — Surplus

*Act 4, part C. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,422 words.*

---

He worked it out properly on a Sunday night in the sixth month, at the
bench, with the shutter open and the persimmon in full leaf and nothing
in particular having happened that day.

That was the part that made it possible. Nothing had happened. He had
done a shift, and gone to Handa's and cut four dovetails badly, and
walked home past O-Shige's and been given something he had not ordered,
and Nemoto had been on the stool and had said *evening* and Sazare had
said *evening*, and Ōtaki Sue's light had been on and he had not
stopped because there was nothing to write.

He sat down at the bench to work on the bezel and did not work on the
bezel.

He got the notebook out instead and ruled a page, and at the head of
the first column he wrote *WHAT I HAVE*, because he had been circling
something for about three weeks and had run out of ways not to look at
it.

*A ward. Second floor, eleven beds that can't walk on a bad week,
thirty-one on a full one. Two orderlies I trained. A standing
instruction sheet in my own hand that Kaburagi has not amended in
fourteen months.*

*A programme. Eleven patients, three failures entered, one letter of
apology delivered by hand.*

*A classification. Support, fortification and casualty management, six
deferrals, one of them lifted.*

*A contract. Four years, four kilos a month, eleven per cent in the
fourth quarter.*

*A room. A bench. Six grains. A box.*

*Ashida. Kanzaki. Yagi. Emiko. Kaburagi. Handa. Ōtaki Sue. O-Shige.
Nemoto. Kudō. Hazama, probably. Urushibara, until the winter. Anko, on
a fortnightly schedule she still pretends is coincidence. Kakashi,
sometimes, at the gate, about nothing.*

*Tsunade. Thursdays.*

He looked at the column.

It was, by a considerable margin, the best inventory of his life. He
had done four of these — at nine, being counted; at thirteen, in a
station abroad, with one arm that did not work; at fifteen, in a room
over a stable, listing eleven lodgings; and at sixteen, on the ninth
night above a mudflat in Nami, when it had come to *a bad knee, a bag
of tools, four seconds, and no jutsu at all.*

This one ran to twenty-two lines and every single line was good.

---

He sat back.

And then, because he had spent four years learning not to stop an audit
at the flattering place, he ruled the second column.

He did not know what to put at the head of it for some time.

In the end he wrote: *AND.*

*And I never wanted any of it.*

He looked at that for a long while with the lamp going and the
persimmon moving outside.

*I want to be exact, because being inexact about this is how a person
ends up wrecking something good.*

*I am not unhappy. I have checked, the way I check everything, and the
finding is that I am not. I would not swap the ward. I would not swap
Handa's shed or Thursday nights or the eleven people or the two nights
a week at this bench. If somebody offered me the workshop at ward
eleven back tomorrow, intact, with my mother in it, on condition that I
had never come here — I do not know what I would say, and four years
ago I would have said yes before the sentence finished, and that
change is real and I did it on purpose and I am not going to pretend
otherwise.*

*But I did not choose one single piece of this.*

*I did not choose the amnesty; I chose between it and a road. I did not
choose the ward; Kaburagi ran out of people. I did not choose the
crystal, the classification, the contract, or the headband. I chose to
stay on a roof, which is the only genuinely free decision I have made
since I was nine, and everything I now have came out of that one
afternoon and not out of any of the four thousand days either side of
it.*

*And the thing I did want — the only thing I ever wanted, which was a
bench in a room in nine streets that no longer exist — is gone, and was
gone before I was old enough to have any say in it, and there is
nobody to be angry at about that either, because the man who signed the
order was a hostage and the woman who succeeded him has apologised and
meant it and it changed nothing.*

*So the shape of it is this.*

*Everything works. None of it is the thing. Nobody did that to me.*

---

He put the pencil down.

The honest observation, which arrived about four minutes later and
which he wrote down because he had a rule about writing down the ones
that arrived late:

*This is not tragedy. I want that on the page, because I will read this
in nine years and I would like to be accurate.*

*A great many people find out at some point that the life they have
built works and is not the one they wanted. Handa wanted to be on
roofs. Kanzaki has never once said what she wanted and I am not going
to ask her. Yagi is thirty-eight and a chūnin and better at medicine
than four doctors I have watched, and there is a reason she is not one,
and I do not know it and I would bet it is not a good reason.*

*This is the ordinary thing. I have simply arrived at it eleven years
early and with more of the evidence than most people get.*

*It is a floor. It is not a wall and it is not a ceiling and I want to
correct my own language, because I have been calling it a ceiling for a
month and a ceiling implies something above me that I cannot reach.*

*There is nothing above me. That is precisely the problem. There is
nothing I am being kept from.*

*It is a floor. It is the place I have got to, and it is a good floor,
and it is level, and I could stand on it for forty years.*

---

He got up at about the second hour and stood in the middle of the room.

Then he did the arithmetic again, on the back of the same page, because
it was the only thing available to do with two in the morning.

*Thirty kilos. Sixteen months, keeping everything.*

*Current: 4 kg/month quota. Reject 22%, Kanzaki says 18% achievable and
she is not usually optimistic. Fourth-quarter increment: 11%.*

*Available surplus at 18% reject, keeping four bench nights and
Thursdays and Sundays: approx. 1.9 kg/month.*

*Thirty divided by one point nine is fifteen point eight.*

*Sixteen months.*

He looked at the figure.

Four letters were somewhere between a fortnight and never. There might
be a communal ground with a stone on it that said a year and a number.
There might be four offices with four answers. There might be a
survey with a clipboard note about a cleared plot and nothing else at
all, and he might travel six weeks and one hundred and forty miles and
a crossing to stand in a materials yard with a harbour works going on
around him and no idea which forty feet of it to stand still in.

He wrote, underneath the sum:

*I am going to do it anyway.*

*Not because I expect a grave. On the evidence I would put it at about
one in three, and if Hazama's temple letter comes back empty I will
revise that down.*

*Because it is thirty kilos and a form, and it is the first thing I
have ever wanted that had a price I could actually pay, and I have
spent my entire life being told that the things I want are not for
sale.*

*This one is for sale. It costs sixteen months of working slightly
harder at a job I would be doing anyway.*

He put the notebook down, and blew the lamp out, and lay on the mat,
and did not get to sleep for some time, and was not, on the whole,
unhappy about it.

In the morning he went to the yard before his shift and asked Kanzaki
about the reject rate, and she had four per cent for him inside forty
minutes, and it was good work, and he took it.
