# Chapter 41, Scene 4 — The Second of the Eighth

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the Hokage's office. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,402 words.*

---

He did not plan it, which was the only reason it worked.

The third of the month, the last hour of the afternoon, the thaw two
days old and the whole village smelling of wet stone and the underside
of old snow. Water came off the tower's eaves in a line of separate
falls that did not quite keep time with each other, and somewhere below
in the copy hall a stamp came down on paper at about one impression
every four seconds, steady as a metronome, the sound of somebody who
had done it for thirty years and no longer needed to look.

He was there for four minutes' business. The quarter's surplus
certification for the trade agreement: thirty-one point four kilograms
against a covenant figure of twenty-eight, signed by Yagi, countersigned
by Emiko, entered in Kanzaki's hand at the foot because Kanzaki did not
trust anybody else's arithmetic including his. Shizune took the folder,
checked the three signatures without reading a word of the body, said
*thank you* in the particular voice of a woman who had eleven of these
left before she could go home, and went out with it under her arm.

The door closed.

Tsunade signed. She had a stack at her left hand and a shorter stack at
her right and she moved sheets from one to the other at a rate that had
nothing to do with reading, which he had worked out eighteen months ago
was not carelessness but triage — she read the ones that mattered and
she read them slowly, and the rest went across on the strength of whose
seal was on them. Her sleeves were pushed back to the elbow. The green
haori was over the chair and not on her, and the chair had taken the
shape of it: the shoulders held, the back gone soft, the red circle on
the reverse creased across the middle where it had been folded over the
same rail some thousands of times. Short nails, unvarnished, the third
finger of the writing hand ridged where the brush sat. No rings. Never
any rings.

The bells at her collarbones did not ring. They never had.

He was at the door. He had his hand on it.

"Third," Tsunade said, without looking up. "You've been standing there
for eleven seconds. Either the number's wrong or you want something."

"The number's right."

"Then it's the second one, and I'd like it inside a minute, because
Shizune comes back with another folder in about ninety seconds and
after that we're both stuck for an hour."

He let go of the door.

"When were you born," he said.

The brush did not stop. It finished the character it was in the middle
of, which he noticed, because he had spent two years learning to read
hands and a hand that finishes its stroke is a hand that has decided
not to react.

Then she put it down.

"The second of the eighth month," she said. "It's in the almanac. Page
one, under the seals, with the other four. Every child in that academy
copies it out in their second year in a hand that hasn't learned to
close a loop yet, and if you'd walked forty metres to the records annex
you'd have had it off a shelf in under a minute without troubling me."

"I know."

"You know."

"I checked whether it was on record before I came up. It's on record.
It's on record in three places I could have got at without asking
anybody." He stayed where he was. "I didn't want it off a shelf."

Tsunade sat back.

She looked at him for what he estimated afterwards at four seconds,
which was long, and her face did the thing it did perhaps twice a
season — the whole working apparatus of it went still, all at once, so
that what was left was not an expression but the absence of one, and he
had never once been able to read what was underneath it. Amber eyes.
The diamond on her forehead, dark against a skin that insisted on being
thirty and had not been for a very long time.

"Nobody has asked me that," she said, "since I was eleven years old."

"That seems unlikely."

"It's arithmetic, not luck. After eleven, everyone knew. It stops being
a question and starts being a date, and a date belongs to whoever's
organising the thing, and by the time I was twenty there were four
separate offices that had it written down and not one person left alive
who'd needed to hear me say it." She picked the brush back up and did
not use it. "That's not a complaint. It's just what happens to a name
that goes on a page."

"What do you do on it."

"Now? Work. Same as the day before it and the day after it, and I sign
the same forty things, and about four in the afternoon somebody who
means well leaves a bottle outside the door and doesn't knock, which is
the correct instinct and I've never told them so." She turned the brush
over. "Before — for twenty-odd years there was no village in it at all.
I was somewhere with a table and a bad line of credit and it went past
me, generally about two days after the fact, and I noticed it the way
you notice a road sign for a town you've already gone through."

"Five weeks."

"What?"

"Mine went past me by five weeks. The eighth of the eleventh month. I
found out about it in the twelfth, from a duty roster." He said it flat,
because it was flat. "So I have some idea of the shape of that. Not the
twenty years. The going past."

Tsunade put the brush down again, properly this time, in the rest.

"The eighth of the eleventh," she said. "That was four months ago."

"Yes."

"And you're telling me now."

"I didn't know it at the time either. That's the part I'd like on the
record, because it's worse and I think it's also the funnier one."

She laughed — one syllable, out through the nose, and she did not
entirely get it back under control before it had shown on her mouth,
which he logged and did not analyse, because analysing it was the
failure and he had four separate entries in a notebook proving it.

"Next year," Tsunade said, "the eighth of the eleventh, you will be
somewhere I can find you at about six in the evening. That is not a
request and it isn't going in a diary. I'll remember it."

"You don't have to —"

"Ruri."

"Yes, Hime."

"Get out of my office. Shizune's on the stairs."

---

He wrote it down that night, in the back of the notebook, in the column
that still had no unit at the head of it, underneath the four entries
already there.

*Sixth item. Second of the eighth month.*

*It was on record in three places. I asked anyway and the asking was
the entire content — she had the answer out of her mouth in under two
seconds and then sat still for four, and the four seconds were not
about the date.*

He held the pencil over the page for a while after that, because there
was an obvious next move and it was standing directly in front of him
with its hand out: five months of notice, a requirement he could
actually establish for once, and a chapter-four problem he had failed
at eleven weeks running and burned one design over.

He did not open the file.

*I am not going to make her anything. Not this year.*

*The last four things I built for people worked because I already knew
what they needed and the thing arrived without being announced. This
one I would build because I have a date, and a date is not a
requirement, it is a deadline, and every single object I have ever made
to a deadline has been the worst one in the case.*

*Also — and I want this written in the honest half — I would be doing
it so that she had to look at it.*

*Leave it. Five months is a long time to find out what she actually
needs, and I have never once found that out by planning.*
