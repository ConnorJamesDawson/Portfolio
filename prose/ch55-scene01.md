# Chapter 55, Scene 1 — The Metric Fails

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's house. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1670 words.*

---

He woke at about a quarter past seven and knew immediately that it had
been the best night's sleep of his life, and could not make the figures
say so.

*Six hours and twenty minutes. Unbroken, as far as I can tell, which is
not far, because the whole point of unbroken is that there is no
observer.*

*The record is eight hours flat, sixteenth of the eighth month, on the
floor of my own room with my coat on, and I woke out of that one like a
man coming up out of water.*

*This is six twenty and it is not close and it is better by a distance I
cannot express in any unit I own.*

He lay still and worked it, because it was the first thing available
and because working it was what he did instead of opening his eyes
properly.

*Temperature. Warmer than my room by about four degrees and I have not
slept warm since the ninth month.*

*Surface. Worse. A bed I am on top of rather than in, fully dressed,
with my belt on.*

*Duration. Worse.*

*Position. Considerably worse. My right arm has been under a load for
six hours and there is no sensation below the elbow, which is a
positional palsy and will resolve in about eleven minutes and hurt
during nine of them.*

*So: three of the four inputs are worse than the record and the outcome
is better than the record by a margin that is not marginal.*

*The metric fails.*

He opened his eyes.

---

Her hands were still in his shirt.

Both of them. They had not moved and the shirt was ruined, and he lay
and looked at the ceiling for about four seconds with the whole of the
previous night arriving at once in the correct order, which was not how
things usually arrived, and then he turned his head.

She was asleep.

*Fourteen. Even. No catch on the third or the sixth.*

*Colour is good. Better than good — better than it was at the desk
yesterday afternoon and considerably better than it was at eleven last
night, and some of that is horizontal and some of it is six hours.*

He should have stopped there.

He knew afterwards, with total clarity, that the place to stop was
*colour is good*; that a respiratory rate and a colour was the whole of
what he was entitled to; that everything after it was a different
activity with a different name.

He did not stop.

---

He looked at her face.

He looked at it the way he looked at a joint under a lamp at two in the
morning, which was the only way he had ever looked at anything, and it
took about ninety seconds and at the end of it he could have built her.

That was not a figure of speech and he did not let it be one. He ran
the check the way he ran it on the bench: *if the lamp went out now,
could I lay this from memory.* Line of the jaw from the point to the
ear, and the angle it made with the neck, which was three degrees off
what a person would guess. The exact place where the hair started at
the temple, which was not where hair started on anybody else he had
ever looked at. Two lines at the outside corner of each eye that were
not there when she was awake and using the face, and were there now
that she was not.

The mouth, which was slightly open, and which was the only part of her
he could not have laid, because it was doing something with no shape to
it.

*Yes.*

*I could make that.*

*Nineteen inches of prepared conductor and about four hundred hours and
I would have it to within a thousandth, and it would sing, and it would
last six days.*

*And I am never going to.*

He had that, and he put it away, and it went in the same drawer as the
other thing and did not lie flat in there either.

*She told me last night what that is. She said I had taken the only
adult who was ever decent to me and built a beautiful thing out of her
and that it was wrong.*

*A bust is that sentence made out of stone.*

---

Then he looked at the diamond.

He had seen it four hundred times. It was on every published likeness
and it was on the coinage of two countries and it was the single most
recognisable object in the Land of Fire, and he had looked at it
approximately as often and as carefully as he had looked at the crest
on the front of the tower, which was to say constantly and never once.

At four inches, in daylight, with nothing else in the way, he saw what
it was.

*That's a seal.*

He went absolutely still.

*That is not a marking and it is not a clan thing and it is not
decorative. It has a boundary and the boundary is deliberate and there
is a — that is a closing stroke, that is a stroke put down last, you
can see the order it was written in.*

*Somebody wrote on her.*

*She wrote on herself.*

He lay four inches from it and looked at it and did not understand a
single character of it, because there were no characters; there was a
shape, and the shape was language, and it was the one language on
earth that was shut to him.

*I cannot read it.*

*I have never been able to read one and I am never going to be able to
read one, and I have made my peace with that eleven times in four years
and it has never once cost me anything, because the things written in
it were techniques and I do not want techniques.*

*This is not a technique.*

*This is on her forehead. This is a thing about her, written down, four
inches from my face, in the only notation in the world I am excluded
from.*

---

And then the second thing came, and it was worse, and it came the way
findings came, which was sideways and unasked for and at the wrong
moment.

*It stores.*

He did not know how he knew. He went back over it afterwards several
times and could not reconstruct the step; there was a boundary and a
closing stroke and something about the way the density sat toward the
centre rather than the edge, and it was not a deduction, it was
recognition, the same as knowing a rod was hollow before you picked it
up.

*That is a store. That is a thing with a volume, and a way in, and it
has been open for a long time.*

*It does not close.*

He stopped breathing for a moment.

*Mine closes.*

*Mine closes because I close it, at seven years old, without being
told, and it is the only reason I have ever been safe and it is the
reason everything I have ever made is dead by Friday.*

*I have spent eleven years unable to build a thing that draws and stays
open. I have got a rose in a yard that lasted six days and a hand that
went to stone at the carpus, and the whole of the fourth quarter of my
life is that one problem.*

*And there is one on her head.*

*There has been one on her head in every room I have ever stood in with
her.*

He was, he realised, going to have to stop looking at it.

That was a strange sentence and he had it in exactly those words. Not
*I should stop*. *I am going to have to*, in the future, as a task
requiring effort, the way you get a bad hand off a rail.

He got about two-thirds of the way and it came back.

*Angle of the outer boundary. If I had eleven seconds and a lamp—*

He shut his eyes.

*Stop.*

*She said it to me nine hours ago. She said you are doing the thing
where you solve it and she was right and I stopped, and it cost me more
than anything I have ever done, and it has taken me nine hours and
twenty minutes to start again.*

*And I am doing it to her face while she is asleep.*

---

He opened his eyes and looked at the ceiling instead and did the
arithmetic on his pulse, because it had been wrong since he woke up and
he had been letting it be wrong.

*Ninety-four.*

*Resting is fifty-eight. It has been fifty-eight since I was fifteen. I
have taken it four thousand times and it does not move — it did not
move on a stair with a hand going to stone and she said it was
ridiculous and I wrote it down as a compliment.*

*Ninety-four, supine, six hours' sleep, no exertion, no pain, no
stimulant, ambient temperature high.*

*There is no cause.*

He lay there and produced, without effort, because it was in chapter
four and he had read chapter four eleven times, the entire pathway: the
axis, the gland, the two hormones and where they went and what they did
when they got there, the vasomotor half, the pupillary half, the
timings, the half-lives.

He could have written it out. He could have written it out neatly, with
the citation, and got full marks.

*So that is what it is.*

*Right.*

He waited for that to help.

It did not help. It sat there, complete and correct and entirely inert,
and his pulse stayed at ninety-four, and he lay in a bed he had no
business being in with two fists in his shirt and understood that he
had just met the first system in his life that he could describe
perfectly and could not touch.
