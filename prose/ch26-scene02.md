# Chapter 26, Scene 2 — The Roof

*Act 4, part B. Konoha, the hospital street. Sazare Ruri, age
sixteen. POV: Sazare. ~2,017 words.*

---

He did not fall cleanly.

The crystal plate caught on the tiles and turned him. His right shoulder
struck first, then his hip. He slid across three rows, broke one tile with his
heel and stopped against the rain gutter with his injured leg folded beneath
him.

The pain arrived white and complete. For several seconds the roof, hospital
and invasion reduced themselves to the exact position of his kneecap.

"Don't move," the Hyūga called.

Sazare moved his right foot. Then his right hand. The left hand remained
trapped by the plate but the fingers answered. He lifted enough weight to draw
the bad leg straight. Something inside the joint went back with a click he
felt in his teeth.

"You were specifically told—"

The rest disappeared under a noise from beyond the southern wall.

It was not an explosion. Explosions ended. This gathered depth until the roof
timbers vibrated and every surviving window in the hospital gave the same thin
rattle. Smoke shifted above the village. Far beyond the stadium quarter,
something the colour of sand rose higher than the roofs.

At first Sazare took it for a collapsing building. Then it continued upwards
and acquired an arm.

The hand at the end was large enough to close round the hospital.

One of the Konoha shinobi in the road said, very quietly, "Tailed beast."

Sazare had never seen one. He knew the inventory categories: jinchūriki,
strategic asset, village-level deterrent, numbers assigned according to tails.
He knew Kirigakure had possessed more than one and that children in the
compound had been told not to ask which bodies contained them.

None of the categories included the effect of seeing fingers the size of
buildings move against the sky.

The hospital roof ceased to seem like a good position.

Sazare put his right hand beneath the gutter, grew a narrow grip from the
stone cornice and lowered himself over the edge. The Hyūga reached the
forecourt below him and looked up in disbelief.

"There are stairs."

"The inside route is longer."

"Your leg is broken."

"Not exactly."

"I can see it."

That removed one useful category of omission.

He descended using the drainpipe, the crystal grip and one leg. Eight feet
above the ground, the pipe separated from its upper bracket. He landed on the
hospital steps with enough control not to put the left foot down and not
enough to remain upright.

Ashida crawled out from under the stall roof while Sazare was getting up. His
flak jacket was torn across the back and blood covered one side of his face,
but he used both arms. Daichi came after him with help from a Konoha medic.
Mika appeared from the north carrying no weapon and returned to the bend
before anybody could stop her.

The cavalry had not arrived as cavalry. It arrived as twelve exhausted people
who had won or escaped fights on adjacent streets and found somewhere else
that needed them. More followed. A Hyūga jōnin took the road. A scarred chūnin
took the roofs. Two genin began checking bodies and were told to stop touching
crystal.

The hospital approach became a Konoha position again.

It did not become safe.

The first reinforcement through the gate stepped on a blue fragment and
stopped with his foot in the air until Natsumi told him it was inactive. The
second ignored her, kicked the piece aside and received a kunai through the
shoulder from the eastern roof. Two Sound shinobi remained inside the
pharmacy. A Sand genin had hidden beneath the stationer's counter and came out
fighting when a medic approached. Somebody on the hospital roof shouted that
more movement was coming from the south.

Natsumi established boundaries with chalk taken from the stationer's broken
display. White lines marked routes through the crystal. Konoha shinobi carried
casualties along them one at a time while she stood at the centre with the
Byakugan active and corrected every footstep.

"Left," she told one carrier.

He moved right.

"Your other left."

"There's a body."

"The body is safer than the paving."

They stepped over it.

Nobody attempted to move the enemy dead. Too many remained fixed to the road
or to each other by short growths. The street had become a material record of
the final minute: one man suspended against the pharmacy shutter by both
sleeves; three fallen in the crossing lattice at the gate; a row of punctures
following the gutter where attackers had tried to use the wall. The crystals
were small enough that the quantity of death seemed impossible until seen from
the angle that joined them.

Sazare did not look from that angle twice.

Sazare went through the broken window because the admissions desk still
blocked the doors.

Inside, the fighting had left three bodies and a stripe of plaster dust down
the centre of the hall. Nurse Emiko had converted the space around both
without moving either. A yellow-tag casualty lay partly across a dead Sound
shinobi's leg while a nurse splinted his wrist. The porter sat against the
wall with his calf bound and held a bowl for somebody else to be sick into.

The admissions clock had stopped at nine forty-six. One hand hung loose behind
the glass and moved when the building shook. Beneath it, the casualty board
showed thirty-two names written in four different hands. Several had no names,
only clothing, forehead protector and injury. One line read *boy, approx. 12,
red scarf* and had been amended twice as people disagreed about the colour.

The hospital had begun using doors differently. The west treatment room
admitted through the medicine corridor and discharged through the broken
window. The laundry hatch passed sealed instruments from the steriliser to the
front hall. A line of green-tagged civilians waited in the rear yard, each
holding the shoulder of the person ahead because smoke made the covered
passage difficult to see.

On the stairs, two orderlies carried bed nine from the men's ward on a canvas
sheet. The patient swore at every step. Handa followed behind them on a second
sheet, directing the angle at which they took the turn.

"Inside shoulder up," he said. "You'll put me through the rail."

"You're not walking," an orderly told him.

"I can still see."

Handa saw Sazare kneeling on the chair at the bottom.

"Window held," he said as they passed.

"For now."

"Told you. One rain."

The exchange occupied four seconds. It was enough to put Sazare's hands back
under his control.

Emiko saw Sazare.

"Are you bleeding?"

"Yes."

"Where?"

He looked down. There were too many sources on his clothes to answer by
colour. "Mouth. Nose. Left eyebrow. Possibly right shoulder."

"Chest?"

"No."

"Abdomen?"

"No."

"Then you're carrying."

She pointed to a stretcher.

That was how he knew he had got back.

For the next twenty minutes he worked without a left leg. He put the knee of
that side on the wheeled evacuation chair and pushed himself with the right
foot, which was faster than hopping and left both hands nearly available. He
moved dressings from stores. He carried tagged notes between the treatment
rooms. He identified crystal embedded in three casualties and marked it with
blue pencil so nobody mistook it for glass.

The repaired brake held whenever he stopped.

At the medicine room, he found the stonecutter from bed one sitting on the
floor with his crushed hand tied against his chest and his good arm around one
of the separated children. Somebody had evacuated him and then needed the
chair for another patient. He had remained where they put him.

"Is it over?" the child asked Sazare.

Beyond the rear wall came another explosion. Dust moved out of the ceiling
seam.

"No," Sazare said.

The stonecutter gave him a look.

"Is this building safe?" the child tried.

Sazare considered the load-bearing walls, the damaged windows, the intact
roofline and the defenders outside.

"Safer than the road," he said.

The child accepted this more readily than the stonecutter did.

The first piece was in a Konoha shinobi's forearm. Sazare put two fingers
against it and attempted recall. The crystal softened slowly. The medic beside
him watched the wound rather than the technique.

"It's getting wider," she said.

Sazare stopped. The fragment had barbs he could not see beneath the skin.
Recalling the base first was allowing the outer shape to move.

"Cut to the end," he said. "Expose the whole piece."

"That adds an inch."

"Pulling it adds more."

She cut. When the blue point lay visible, Sazare recalled from the tip
backwards. Mineral slumped into grey grit and washed out under saline.

The second casualty was Sound and unconscious. The point had entered below the
ribs. The duty medic looked at the forehead protector, then at Sazare.

"Yours?"

"Yes."

"Can you remove it?"

"Possibly."

"Do so."

There was no discussion about whether the man deserved treatment. The hospital
had doors and he was inside them.

Sazare found the line more easily the second time. Contact at the exposed
base, follow the course to the furthest point, pull from there. The crystal
degraded in sections. Twice it cracked and left small fragments that required
forceps. It took six minutes and more chakra than growing it had.

The Sound shinobi remained alive when they finished.

His pulse weakened during the removal. The medic used pressure, then green
chakra, then called for blood that the hospital did not have under the man's
unknown type. Sazare held the wound open with two retractors while ordinary
mineral washed out. The patient wore a cursed mark at the base of the neck:
three black curves inside a circle, unlike any seal notation Sazare knew.

The shape did not follow the muscle beneath it. It sat in the skin and
continued deeper in lines only medical chakra made visible.

Writing with the body as substrate.

Sazare wanted to ask how it had been inscribed. The wanting arrived sharp
enough to make him lean closer.

The medic said, "If you faint into the field, I'm leaving you there."

He straightened.

"Guren," Sazare said.

The medic looked up. "What?"

"Nothing relevant to this."

He moved to the third casualty.

At ten forty-one, Nurse Yagi came through the rear entrance with six arena
casualties and blood up both sleeves. She found Sazare kneeling on the
evacuation chair beside the oxygen rack.

"What have you done?"

"The front approach is open."

"To your leg."

"It was hit by a building."

"Buildings don't hit people."

"Part of one did."

"Ruri."

"The lateral structures have failed."

"Your knee is pointing east."

"Yes."

"The rest of you is pointing north."

"I had noticed."

Yagi seized the chair handles and pushed him into the west treatment room
despite his objection that it was already occupied. She cut the stretcher
strap from the knee. The swelling beneath had doubled the joint's width and
the skin was purple along the old surgical line.

"How old is this?"

"The swelling is about an hour."

"The scar."

"Nine years."

Her fingers found the displaced kneecap. Sazare gripped the edge of the chair.

"Who treated it?"

"A woman in Kiri."

"Name?"

"I don't know."

"Follow-up?"

"No."

"Rehabilitation?"

"I walked on it."

Yagi looked at him with the particular stillness medical staff used
immediately before anger became too large for a ward.

"Nine years," she said.

"It generally worked."

"You work in a hospital."

"For four months."

"Long enough. Bed."

"There are casualties in the corridor."

"And now there is one in this room."

"I can still carry."

"You are sitting in the thing you are using to carry."

Somewhere behind her a bowl hit the floor. Sazare turned towards the sound.
The room narrowed, recovered and narrowed again. The edges of Yagi's face
became indistinct.

"How much chakra?" she asked.

He tried to calculate it. Nineteen grains at an average extension, eleven
certainly spent, six remaining, fragment growth impossible to price, recall
losses perhaps fifty-eight per cent, reversed pathways—

"Ruri. How much?"

"More than I had," he said.

The answer appeared to concern her.

Yagi caught him before he reached the floor.
