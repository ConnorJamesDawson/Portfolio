# Chapter 59, Scene 6 — Myōboku Replies

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1690 words.*

---

Jiraiya came through the window on the Wednesday at the fourth hour with
a scroll in his teeth and put it on the desk in front of Tsunade without
saying anything, and then sat down on the sill and ate a persimmon while
she read it.

"Well?" he said.

"You know what it says."

"I know what I *think* it says. I've read it four times and I've been
wrong about Fukasaku before."

Tsunade turned the scroll round on the desk.

"Ruri. Come and read this."

---

*From Fukasaku of Mount Myōboku, to Jiraiya, and to the woman who holds
the tower, and to the boy.*

He stopped at that.

*He has put me third and named the other two by what they are.*

*And he has called me the boy, which is not rude, because he does not
have my name, because Jiraiya will not have written it down in a letter
going out of the country, which is correct.*

*He is telling me he knows that.*

*He will come.*

He read on.

---

*I will come.*

*I am bringing Shima. This is not negotiable and I am aware that
Jiraiya will have already told the room that he intends to argue about
it and lose. He is correct on both counts and he may consider the
argument had.*

"Right," said Jiraiya, without looking up from the persimmon. "There it
is."

*I want four things established before I set foot in a human building,
and I would rather have them established in writing than discover them
in a room, and if any of the four is refused I will still come but I
will come knowing.*

*One. Who has authority over the room.*

*Two. What is being transferred, in the plainest words available.*

*Three. What record is kept.*

*Four. What the boy has given Shikkotsu.*

---

He read the fourth one twice and then looked up.

"That's the one," said Tsunade.

"Yes."

"Jiraiya says that's the one."

"He's right," said Sazare.

He read the rest.

*On the first, I do not much care, but somebody must say it out loud
before anything begins, because when a thing goes wrong in a room with
two mountains in it the argument about who was in charge is worse than
whatever went wrong.*

*On the second: I have been told this is about the gathering, and about
how a human does a thing with no seals, and I want it stated plainly at
the start by the boy himself, in his own words, out loud, in front of
everybody, because a thing that cannot be stated plainly at the start is
usually four things.*

*On the third: something must be written. I am aware of what I am asking
and I am aware who I am asking. Jiraiya has told me that the boy cannot
read a notation and does not produce one. I accept that he cannot do it
himself. Somebody else in that room can hold a pen.*

*Nothing has ever gone out of Myōboku unrecorded and I am not beginning
at four hundred and eleven.*

---

*On the fourth.*

*I asked Jiraiya what the boy has given Shikkotsu and he told me the
answer and I did not believe him, so I am asking directly.*

*Everything Myōboku has ever given a human has been given under a
contract. Every one. There is no exception in our whole history and there
is no ambiguity about what the human owes and there never has been,
because the obligation is the thing that keeps it honest at both ends.*

*Jiraiya signed at twelve years of age and he has paid, and he has paid
badly in places and well in others, and he knows to the day what he owes
us.*

*And I am told that this boy has been taught for three months by a
Shikkotsu elder with no contract, no term, no obligation of any kind and
nothing rendered in return.*

*I would like to know whether that is true.*

*I would like to know it before I am in a room, because if it is true
then either the slugs are doing something we have never done, or the boy
has taken something without paying for it, and those are extremely
different situations and I am not walking into a building without
knowing which one I am in.*

---

He put the scroll down.

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"He thinks I've robbed her," said Sazare.

"He does not think you've robbed her," said Jiraiya. "He thinks it's
*possible* you've robbed her and that nobody's checked, which is a
different thing and is what he'd think about anybody, and he's right that
nobody's checked."

"It's true, though. All of it. There's no contract and there's no term
and there's nothing rendered."

"I know."

"I refused one," said Sazare. "In the tenth month. She told me how the
first contract came about and I asked her about signing and I decided
against it, and the reason was that if I signed she would come because
she was contracted to and I would never be able to tell the difference
again."

Jiraiya put the persimmon down.

"Say that to him," he said.

"That's what I'd say."

"No — I mean say exactly that. Those words. On the day, out loud, in
front of everybody, when he asks, and he will ask inside about four
minutes." He wiped his hands on his knees. "Because that is going to be
the single most interesting thing that has happened to that toad in
about ninety years and I would quite like to be in the room."

---

"Explain it," said Tsunade.

"They run on obligation, Hime. All of it. That's the whole architecture
— you sign, you owe, they give, and the owing is what makes it safe,
because a thing you owe for is a thing you cannot pretend was a gift.

"I have thought about it that way since I was twelve. I have never once
thought about it any other way, and I have had thirty years to and it
has never occurred to me that there might be a mountain where it works
differently.

"And now there's a boy who asked a slug on eleven Fridays and got yes
eleven times and does not owe her one single thing, and it has not gone
wrong, and it is the only teaching arrangement in the world that isn't
underwritten by anything at all."

"Is that better?" said Sazare.

"I don't know," said Jiraiya. "That's what I'm telling you. I genuinely
do not know and I have not known since Wednesday and it has been
bothering me at night.

"Fukasaku won't know either. And he is four hundred years old and he has
never in his life met a thing he did not know about, and he is going to
come into that room and find one, and I want you to be ready for what
that looks like, because it will not look like curiosity."

---

There was one more part and it was three lines at the bottom in a
different hand, and he nearly missed it because it was written across the
grain.

*A last thing, and Jiraiya will tell you I do not write these.*

*I have met one before. One of theirs. It was a very long time ago and it
was not on either mountain and I have not spoken about it in some
centuries, and I am not going to put it in a letter that has to cross
water.*

*Ask me when I am in the room, if you still want to, and I will decide
then.*

---

He read that three times.

"Did you know about that?" he said.

"No," said Jiraiya.

"You've known him thirty years."

"I have known him for thirty years and I have asked him about most
things and I have never heard one word about a slug, and if you had
asked me on Monday whether the two mountains had ever had anything to do
with each other I would have said no and I would have been sure."

"And now?"

"Now I've got three lines across the grain of a scroll," said Jiraiya,
"written by a toad who does not write things like that, and a fourth
question about obligation that he has put at the bottom of a list of
four and which is obviously the only one he cares about."

He looked at the window.

"There's something under this."

"Is that a problem?"

"It's the twenty-eighth in three days and I've spent my whole life
walking into things with something under them," said Jiraiya, "so no.
But I'd rather you knew, and I'd rather she knew, and I would like it
written down somewhere that I said so on the twenty-fifth."

---

"Right," said Tsunade. "My room, then. The four things.

"One: it's my building, it's my tower, and I have authority over the
room. Not as Hokage — I'll say that out loud on the day. If two of them
start something, I stop it, and if the boy says stop then everything
stops, and I'll put that second one in writing before anybody arrives."

"He'll like that," said Jiraiya.

"Two: Ruri states it plainly at the start, in his own words, in front of
everybody."

"Yes," said Sazare.

"Three: somebody holds a pen. Shizune."

"...Shizune?"

"Shizune has taken the minute of every difficult room in this village
for eleven years," said Tsunade, "including four I would rather did not
exist, and she has never once put a word in that was not said and never
once left one out that was.

"And she is the only person in this building I would trust to write down
a thing she does not understand without improving it."

"And four," said Sazare.

Tsunade looked at him.

"Four's yours," she said. "I'm not answering four. Nobody in this room
is answering four except you, and you're going to answer it on the day,
out loud, in front of two mountains, and Kami help all of us."
