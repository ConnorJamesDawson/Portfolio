# Chapter 72, Scene 4 — What The Fuss Is About

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the hospital forecourt. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~890 words.*

---

The telling-off happened on the Tuesday, in the hospital
forecourt, in front of eleven witnesses, and Sazare had the whole
of it because he was crossing from the board room with the
second-run manifests when the detonation occurred forty feet to
his left.

The detonating agent was one sentence of Naruto's, delivered in
complete innocence to Sakura, who had just come off shift, on the
subject of a delivery cart being unloaded at the stores entrance:
crates, stencilled, *HIME — slate blue — cohort 2*.

"I still don't really get the underwear thing," said Naruto.
"Like, it's cool that Sazare invented — whatever it is. But it's
*underwear*. Everyone's acting like he invented the Rasengan.
It's kind of a stupid thing to be famous f—"

He did not complete the word, because Sakura had turned around.

Sazare stopped walking. Around the forecourt, with the smooth
unanimity of a trained population, several other people also
stopped walking, and two nurses on the steps sat down.

What followed lasted four minutes and was, by his professional
assessment, the finest piece of instructional demolition he had
witnessed since Urushibara — delivered at parade volume,
structured, sourced, and building through its movements like a
prosecution: the sixteen feet of cloth, demonstrated, on herself,
with gestures; the sixty-one presentations in three years, *and
those are the ones with the honesty to say so*; Tachibana Rei's
rib, named, because Naruto knew her, *your own courier, Naruto,
her bindings finished what the fall started and NOBODY HAD EVER
ASKED*; the retention statistic, produced from memory; the
choosing-between-two-injuries-since-age-thirteen line, delivered
at close range with a finger in his flak jacket; and the
conclusion, at full amplitude, to the forecourt at large:

"—so the next time you feel like calling the single most
important piece of equipment this village has issued in forty
years STUPID, you can first spend one training day with your
chest strapped flat enough to survive your own Rasengan,
and THEN, if you live, you can come and apologise to every
kunoichi on the active roster ALPHABETICALLY."

Silence, respectful, forecourt-wide.

"...Can I see it?" said Naruto.

Sakura blinked. "What?"

"The thing. The — articles, the crystal, how it works." He had
his hands up in the universal grammar of honest surrender, and
his face had the expression Sazare had logged at the hotpot and
was coming to regard as the boy's core technique, deadlier than
any clone: total, weaponised sincerity. "You just told me it's
the most important equipment in forty years and I called it
stupid because I didn't know anything about it, which is — yeah.
That's on me. So show me. I want to get it. I want to be the
guy who gets it, not the guy who—" he gestured at himself,
retroactively, with disgust— "that guy."

And Sakura, artillery still deployed, visibly found herself
with nothing to fire at — Sazare watched her run the same
finding he had run at the hotpot: *there is no cover to
attack; he simply comes around the argument and joins you
behind it* — and lowered the finger, and said, with the
weariness of a woman conceding a decade-long pattern, "Fine.
FINE. Yard tour. But you're getting the full brief, Naruto,
the LOAD PATHS and everything, and if you yawn—"

"When has he ever yawned at a brief in his life," said
Sazare, arriving into the conversation because the
manifests gave him standing and the moment was too good to
audit from range. "He is the only person I have ever
briefed who asks the question the brief was hiding. Bring
him Wednesday afternoon. The cohort review is Wednesday; he
can see the operation working instead of a demonstration
arranged for him. Demonstrations lie. Operations don't."

"Wednesday," said Naruto, delighted, already recovered,
already somehow the host of the arrangement, "it's a — hang
on—" and he produced, from his jacket, with enormous
ceremony, a small and brutally creased notebook, and wrote
in it, tongue between his teeth, while Sakura stared and
Sazare experienced the specific vertigo of watching his own
disease achieve its first airborne transmission.

"Since when do you have a notebook?!"

"Sazare put me in a DIARY, Sakura-chan. Like a dentist. I'm
adapting. It's called professionalism." He pocketed it,
gravely. "Wednesday. The fuss. I'm gonna get it so hard."

*Entry, Tuesday,* wrote the fabricator, that evening. *The
line has survived its first hostile review. The reviewer
recanted under fire and converted to the cause inside four
minutes, which is the standard trajectory of everybody who
argues with Sakura on clinical ground — the column she
keeps on me is reciprocated; I keep one on her, and today
it gained: 'artillery, but only ever on behalf of others.
Note the targeting doctrine. She has never once fired for
herself.'*

*Cross-reference, filed under things the yard is teaching
me about the village: the two loudest people I know — her
forecourt, his sincerity — are both, on inspection,
delivery systems for care that would otherwise have no
route out. Loudness as load path. The clipboard principle,
inverted, at volume.*

*Wednesday: the cohort review, plus one. The operation will
be observed by the most disruptively honest instrument in
the Land of Fire.*

*Note to self: warn Hinata-sama she will have an audience.*

*Second note, appended after consideration: do not warn
Hinata-sama. The consultation's whole curriculum is formats
that hold under live load. Wednesday is live load.*

*The training proceeds.*
