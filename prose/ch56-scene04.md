# Chapter 56, Scene 4 — The Count

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, outer office. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1210 words.*

---

He was counting a bundle when it happened.

That was the part he kept coming back to. Not reading, not writing, not
thinking — counting, which was the floor of him, the thing underneath
everything else, the operation he had been running continuously since
he was seven years old and which had never once required any part of
his attention at all.

Dispatch flimsies, ninth month, tied in fifties by somebody who had not
counted them properly. He had four bundles done and was about two-
thirds through the fifth.

*— thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-three —*

The door of the inner stair opened and Tsunade came through the outer
office.

She was going down. She had the haori on and Shizune half a step
behind her with the folder and she was in the middle of a sentence
about the Suna delegation, and she did not look at him, and she went
past the end of his desk at a distance of about four feet and out
through the far door and down, and the whole of it took perhaps six
seconds.

*— thirty-two, thirty —*

He stopped.

He sat with a flimsy in his left hand and a stack in his right and
absolutely nothing in his head.

---

*Where was I.*

Nothing came.

*I was in the thirties. I know I was in the thirties because I have got
thirty-one and thirty-three and I do not know which of those is the
last one I actually said.*

*Right. Recount.*

He put the stack down and squared it and counted it again from the
beginning, properly, with the thumb, the way he had counted about four
million things.

*Forty-eight.*

He counted it again.

*Fifty-one.*

He sat back.

---

*I have counted the same bundle twice and got two numbers.*

He did not move for a while.

*That is not a lapse of attention. I want to be exact about this
because I am going to have to look at it later and I would rather have
it clean.*

*A lapse of attention is losing the thread of a thing you are thinking
about. That happens to people. It has happened to me perhaps four
times, all of them at the end of a nineteen-hour day, all of them
recorded, all of them recoverable — you go back to the last fixed point
and you start again and you get the same answer.*

*I have gone back to the fixed point twice and got two different
answers, and the fixed point is a stack of paper on a desk that has not
moved.*

*So the count is not the thing that failed.*

*The thing that failed is the part underneath the count. The part that
holds a number while the rest of me does something else. The part I
have never had to think about, ever, because it has never been off.*

*And it went off for approximately six seconds and I did not notice it
go off and I did not notice it come back on.*

He looked at the door she had gone out of.

*I have got no duration for that.*

*And I cannot get one, because the only instrument I have for getting
one is the thing that was not working.*

---

*Second observation and I am going to write this one down properly
because it is worse than the first.*

*I did not look up.*

He went back over it, carefully, the way he went back over a failure on
the bench.

*She came through the door on my left and went out through the door at
the far end and she was in my field of view for the entire crossing,
and I have got: the haori, on. Shizune half a step behind and on the
left, which is where she goes when they are going down and not up.
Sentence in progress about Suna, third or fourth clause. Pace slightly
faster than ordinary, which is her going somewhere she does not want to
be late for and does not want to be seen hurrying to.*

*Hair up but done fast — the front is not right and she did it on the
stairs.*

*That is nine observations in six seconds and I did not turn my head
once.*

He put the pen down.

*So it is not that I looked at her.*

*It is that I did not have to.*

---

He sat at a second desk in an outer office at about eleven in the
morning on the fourteenth of the eleventh month and had, for the first
time, the whole of it at once.

*Sakura is right and Sakura is out of date and both of those things are
about the same faculty.*

*Nothing gets in. That is completely true. That has been the single
most reliable fact about me since I was seven years old and it is the
entire reason I can do the thing I do — you put an article in front of
me and the world goes off, and that is not discipline and it is not
concentration, it is that there has never been anything else in the
room.*

*And it is not a door I ever installed. It is a room with nothing in
it.*

*Something is in it now.*

*And the faculty has not weakened. That is the finding and it is the
one I do not like: I have not got worse at attending. I attended to
nine things in six seconds without moving my head, which is better than
I have ever done.*

*It has simply stopped being mine.*

*It goes where it goes. I do not point it any more. And it went, and it
took the count with it, and it will do it again this afternoon and
tomorrow and every single time she crosses a room for the rest of my
life, and I have got no procedure, and there is nothing in chapter four
about it, and I have read chapter four eleven times.*

---

He counted the bundle a third time and got forty-nine, and then a
fourth, slowly, out loud under his breath, and got forty-nine again.

*Forty-nine. Bundle tied as fifty. One short, which is the ninth-month
clerk and not me.*

*And I have taken four attempts and eleven minutes to establish a
figure I would ordinarily have had in nine seconds, and the eleven
minutes is not in the file, and nobody is ever going to know except me.*

He wrote *49 (t. as 50)* on the wrapper and put it on the done side and
got the next bundle.

*Entry, tonight, and I am not going to soften it.*

*14/11. Lost a count. First time. Cause identified with total
confidence. No remedy identified. No remedy expected.*

*I have spent my entire life being a man who can be relied upon to have
the number.*

*That was not a virtue. That was a room with nothing in it, and I have
been taking credit for it since I was nine.*

He started the sixth bundle.

*— one, two, three —*

Two floors down, a door went, and it was the wrong door and it was not
her, and he found that he had stopped again anyway.
