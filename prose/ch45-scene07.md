# Chapter 45, Scene 7 — A Long Thin Object Under Load

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard, after hours. Sazare
Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,696 words.*

---

He stated the problem on the last night of the fifth month, at the
awning bench, with the dishes back in their tray and the yard empty
and the whole village going down to that particular quiet it had at
about eleven in warm weather.

*I have a rating and no instrument for measuring it.*

*I know that a fast article carries four seconds and a slow one carries
more than forty, because I ran two and stopped the second because my
arm gave out before the crystal did.*

*My arm gave out. That is the only number I have. I do not know the
ceiling of the seven-hour piece because I cannot reach it, and I do not
know the shape of the curve between one minute forty and seven hours
because I have two points on it, and a curve through two points is a
straight line drawn by a man who wants one.*

*So: build the instrument.*

---

He worked out what it had to be over about an hour, ruling and
crossing out, and the answer was disappointing in the way that correct
answers frequently were.

*It has to be long, because a short article gives me nothing about
where a failure starts and a long one lets me see whether it goes at
the near end, the far end or the middle.*

*It has to be thin, because a thick section has too much redundancy in
it and will simply route round a failure and hide the thing I am trying
to see.*

*It has to be held at one end, because that is how I use everything —
every operation I have ever performed goes in at my hand and out at the
far end, and an instrument that is loaded in the middle is measuring a
different article than the one I actually make.*

*It has to survive being taken to failure repeatedly, which means the
highest grade I can lay, which means the slowest, which means months.*

*And it has to be a shape that is already solved, because I am not
also going to spend four months finding out the correct proportions of
a long thin object under end load when there are people who have been
doing that for nine hundred years and have written it down.*

He sat and looked at what he had written.

*It's a sword.*

*A long thin article, held at one end, loaded to failure, in a
geometry four centuries of smiths have optimised and published. Every
proportion I need is in a book Ochiai can get me by Thursday.*

He wrote, underneath:

*I want it on the record that I arrived at this from the specification
and not the other way round, and I want it on the record that I know
exactly how that is going to sound to anybody I say it to, and that I
have already decided not to say it to anybody.*

---

He costed it on the second sheet, because a thing that was not costed
was a thing you had agreed to without knowing.

*Seven hours ten produced a thumb-sized article of an unknown but very
high grade. Assume the relationship is linear in volume, which it will
not be, and take the shortest blade in the book — a short sword, one
foot six of edge — which is a hundred and forty times that volume.*

He did the arithmetic and looked at it and did it again.

*Not linear. It cannot be linear, because I am not laying a hundred and
forty separate articles, I am laying one continuous set of ways and the
long runs are the expensive part.*

*Call it: four hours a night, five nights a week, if I hold the pace.*

*Four to six months.*

He put the pencil down.

*Four to six months.*

*I have not committed to anything longer than nine days since I came
into this village. The degradation study was eleven weeks and it was
eleven weeks of doing a small thing fifty times, which is not the same.
The jewellery is two nights a week for two years and it is two years of
separate objects, any one of which I could abandon on a Tuesday and
nobody would ever know.*

*This is one article. It is either finished or it is nothing. If I
stop in the eleventh week I have four hundred hours of nothing and a
bar of unfinished crystal that I will have to look at.*

---

He started it that night, which was not the plan, and he was aware
while he was doing it that starting a four-month article at twenty past
eleven at night was itself a piece of data about him.

He laid the core.

Not the blade — the core, a rod of about four inches, from the seeded
stock in the tin, the six grains from the invasion that had sat
untouched on a shelf in a treatment room for twenty-five months because
he had never found a use he was willing to put them to.

He used one.

He laid it slow. Slower than the seven-hour piece, deliberately, because
if seven hours had produced ways four times the width then eleven
hours would produce something he had no measurement for at all, and the
whole point of the instrument was that it had to be better than
anything he could reach.

It took him until four in the morning and he got about an inch and a
half.

He weighed it before he went home, on the small brass balance, because
Kanzaki weighed everything and he had picked it up off her without
ever deciding to, and wrote the figure in the log in the column she had
added for the crystal doctrine three months before he had ever brought
her a reason to need it.

Then he wrote, underneath the weight:

*Article 1. Instrument. Started 31/5, 23:20. Core laid to 1½ inches
at 04:10.*

*Grade: unknown and intentionally so. This is the first thing I have
ever made where I do not know the answer before I start.*

---

He cut the guard blank three nights later, out of ordinary stock,
because the guard was not part of the instrument and did not need the
grade and he could do it in an evening.

It was a simple oval, four inches across, with the ordinary aperture,
and it took him about two hours and it was the best piece of plain
work he had done in a year, because plain work went well when the rest
of him was occupied.

He set it on the felt under the lamp and looked at it.

There was a face on it about two and a half inches long, flat and
unornamented, on the side that would sit against the hand.

He had left it flat.

He had not decided to leave it flat. He had cut a guard and had, at
some point in two hours, made about nine small decisions that had the
effect of producing an unbroken surface exactly the size and shape of
a maker's panel, on the inside face, where nobody but the man holding
it would ever see it.

He put the graver down.

*I have left a panel.*

*I do not know what goes on it. I want that written down tonight rather
than in four months when I know, because in four months I will
reconstruct this evening as a decision and it was not one.*

*I have made perhaps nine hundred articles with a maker's face on them
and I have never once put anything on one. Not a mark, not an initial,
not a date. Kanzaki has asked me twice. Ōtaki Sue asked me at the
turned tables in front of six people and I changed the subject and she
let me.*

*So it is not that I don't sign things.*

*It is that I have never had a name I was willing to put on the inside
face of an object where only the owner would find it, and I have
apparently just spent two hours preparing one for it anyway.*

He put the guard in the drawer under the bench with the felt over it.

---

The sphere was still two.

He did it every night before he went to sleep, ninety attempts a week,
in a hot room on the floor with his back against the wall, and by the
last night of the month he had had three seconds twice and could not
repeat it and had begun to suspect that the two occasions had been
different things happening to look the same.

*Exercise 1: two. Two threes in nine days, both unrepeatable.*

*Saeko says: you can't hold the outside, there isn't an outside. I have
had that for four days and I have not been able to use it once,
because I do not know what she means and I cannot ask her.*

*I want to record the shape of the month, because it is not the shape I
would have predicted on the twenty-second.*

*I found out that the thing I do has a name that eleven-year-olds have.
I found out that I cannot do the first exercise in the first term. I
found out that two hundred and nine failed articles in a back room
have been telling me for three years that I burn out my own work, and
that I have been writing "medium failed" over the top of it in my own
hand.*

*And the single most useful sentence anybody said to me in nine days
came from a woman who took it back inside two seconds and then made
herself give it to me anyway, and who has arranged her next two months
so that she is only in that yard by contract.*

*Position: I am eighteen. I have four hundred hours of a four-month
article and an inch and a half of it done. I have a count of two.*

*I have never in my life been this far behind and I have not once,
since the twenty-second, wanted to stop.*
