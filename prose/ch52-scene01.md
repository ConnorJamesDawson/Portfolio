# Chapter 52, Scene 1 — The Freeze

*Act 4, part D. Konoha General and the quartermaster's yard. Sazare
Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,494 words.*

---

Nothing came down from the hill.

He had expected something and had not admitted to expecting it until
about the fourth day, at which point he wrote down what he had been
expecting so that he could stop pretending he had not been:

*A slip. Two lines, unsigned, saying that the arrangement of the second
of last month is withdrawn and that Katsuyu is not to be in that yard,
and I would have folded it and filed it and gone to work.*

*I have been listening for it every morning for four days and I have
been telling myself I was listening for the batch schedule.*

It did not come.

On the fifth day he stopped listening for it, and on the ninth he
understood that its not coming was the loudest thing that had happened
since the gate did not bang.

*She could withdraw her.*

*It would take one sentence. It is her contract and her summon and
there is no arrangement between me and Katsuyu that survives her
saying otherwise — the arrangement of the second is a thing two people
agreed in a yard and it exists entirely inside a permission that
belongs to somebody else, and both of us knew that when we made it and
neither of us said so.*

*Nine days.*

*She has not done it, and she is not going to, and I do not know what
that is.*

---

Katsuyu came on the seventeenth as though nothing had happened, and on
the nineteenth and the twenty-second, and by the third of those he had
stopped bracing when she arrived.

She did not raise it.

That was not tact — she did not have any and he had established that
comprehensively — it was that she genuinely did not appear to think
there was anything to raise, and after four evenings he asked her, and
she said:

"I have been going over it. I have concluded that I did four things
wrong and that three of them were unavoidable and one of them was not,
and I have not raised it because I do not know whether raising it would
be a fifth."

"Which one wasn't unavoidable?"

"I should not have said *then another evening*." She settled. "She had
said the thing, and I heard it as a time and it was not a time, and you
knew that inside about a second — I felt you know it — and I did not,
and I went on being reasonable at her for four more sentences."

"You didn't know."

"No," said Katsuyu. "And I have been four hundred and eleven years not
knowing, and this year I have found out that a great deal of what I
took for the shape of the world was people not saying things, and I
would like to be better at it, and I do not think that is going to be
quick."

He did not say anything for a moment.

"For what it's worth," he said, "I said the worst available sentence
about ninety seconds before you did."

"Yes," said Katsuyu. "I heard it. I have been trying to work out
whether it would help you to know that I did not think it was the worst
one."

---

He filed the protocol with Kaburagi on the sixteenth, because she had
said Kaburagi and not the Division.

It was the first time in eleven weeks that he had been on the fourth
floor for anything other than a handover, and the linen room door was
open, and she took the two sheets off him and read the top one standing
up.

Then she turned to the second sheet and read that, and stopped, and
went back to the top of it and read it again.

"This is new," she said.

"That's what she said."

"I imagine it is." Kaburagi held the sheet at arm's length, which she
had started doing in the spring and had not yet admitted to. "*Known
risks to the operator. Petrification, from the fingertips, painless,
decelerating and not certainly self-limiting.*"

"Yes."

"*Consent: mine, informed, and I have written the risk line myself
rather than putting none identified and underlining it.*" She lowered
the sheet. "Sazare. What is this?"

"It's a protocol."

"I have read four thousand protocols and about nine of them had a
person in them, and every one of those nine was written by somebody who
had already had something go wrong." She looked at him over the top of
it. "When?"

"The fifteenth of the eighth month."

"That would be the fortnight you came onto my floor with your left hand
in your pocket and did not take it out for nine days," said Kaburagi.

He did not say anything.

"I am not asking," she said. "I want that clear. It has the Fifth's
name on the bottom of it and a date, and that is the whole of what I am
entitled to and I am not a woman who goes looking for the rest." She
put it in the folder and put the folder in the second drawer and locked
it, which she did with about four things a year. "But I will say one
thing and then I will let you go to work."

"Go on."

"You have written the honest version of a document that could have
ended you," said Kaburagi, "and you have handed it to a ward sister to
file. Which means one of two things, and one of them is that you are
being extremely correct."

"And the other?"

"That you have nobody to hand it to," said Kaburagi, "and I would
rather it were the first, and I have known you three years and I would
not put money on it."

---

He worked.

That was most of the fortnight and it was most of what he wrote down.
Two hundred and forty hours on the instrument by the twenty-second and
the core at twenty inches of twenty-one, which meant the tenth month
after all and not the eleventh. Forty stones for the third and fourth
quarter medical, ready and waiting on a sealwright who had not come.
Nine evenings with Katsuyu, and the count at fourteen, and no
explanation for what happened past eleven and no way to ask.

And nothing from the hill.

*Day fourteen.*

*I have written the number at the top of the page every morning since
the fifteenth and I have not once written what it is a count of.*

*It is not a count of anything. It is not a deadline, there is nothing
due, there is no business between us and there has not been since the
fourteenth of this month when she signed a protocol on a bench and
walked out of a gate.*

*I have been keeping a tally of a silence.*

He looked at that.

*And I want to be accurate about what I would do if a slip came
tomorrow, because I have been assuming for a fortnight that I would be
relieved.*

*I would not be relieved. A slip would be an instruction, and I would
comply with it, and it would be the end of it, and the arrangement I
would rather have is exactly the one I have got — which is nothing, and
no instruction, and a summon in a yard nine evenings out of fourteen
that she has not withdrawn.*

*Which means I have spent a fortnight hoping for nothing to happen and
recording it as waiting.*

---

Kanzaki said one thing about it, on the twenty-third, and picked her
moment the way she picked everything.

He was at bench three with the core out and the lamp down, and she came
past with the day book and put it on the end of the bench and did not
sit.

"Twenty inches," she said.

"Twenty and a quarter."

"Mm." She turned a page. "The Fifth came through that gate on the
fourteenth at four minutes past nine and went out of it at about half
past ten, and I was in the office with the shutters over and I did not
come out, and I want you to know that I did not come out on purpose and
that I have not asked anybody anything about it since."

"...Thank you."

"I'm not being kind, I'm being a quartermaster. What happens in this
yard after six is not in my book." She closed the day book. "But the
stool is still out at the end of bench three with a lamp on it, and it
has been out every evening for two months, and I put it there for the
slug."

"I know you did."

"Then you will notice that I have gone on putting it there," said
Kanzaki, "on nine evenings when the slug came and on five when she
didn't, and I would rather you did not read anything into that
whatsoever, and I am going home."
