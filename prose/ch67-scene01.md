# Chapter 67, Scene 1 — The Missing Rows

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1150 words.*

---

They were back in the village by two on the ninth, and by six the day
at Tarugi had already begun to behave like a thing that had happened
to two other people.

He noticed the behaviour because he was watching for it. Re-entry had
a documented shape: the diary reasserted itself at the gate, the
tower put its weight back on her one floor at a time, and by the
evening the woman who had ruined a hat man for sport was signing
border correspondence with her hair back up, and the man who had
mended a luck charm for an audience of one lamp was checking a
requisition against a delivery sheet in the yard.

*Fifty-two hours ago she crawled a market lane for a paper pinwheel.
The pinwheel is on the shelf above her desk as of four o'clock. That
is the entire surviving evidence, that and a hat.*

The sake was drunk at seven, as contracted.

He was permitted to attend on the grounds that a settled wager wants
its subject present, and he sat with a cup he mostly held while
Shizune and the Hokage of the Land of Fire honoured the terms of a
bet about which of his own objections he would voice first, both of
them entirely certain they had won, both of them with a signed margin
note supporting the certainty, and the north-cellar sake growing
steadily less relevant to either position.

"The record shows a withdrawn objection," said Tsunade, "and a
withdrawn objection is a courtesy, and courtesies are not *dislikes*,
and therefore the first dislike on the minute is the bench clause, as
predicted, sight unseen."

"The record shows," said Shizune, "the words *and now the thing I do
not like*, spoken by the subject, immediately after the first
objection, which grammatically identifies the first objection as a
thing he did not like, whatever its procedural fate."

They both looked at him.

"I am not ruling on this," said Sazare. "I have read the governor's
clause about conflicts of interest and I wrote it."

"He's learning," said Shizune, to her cup, with enormous
satisfaction, and poured the third round, and the matter was minuted
as *resolved, twice, in opposite directions*, which as far as he
could tell was the desired outcome and possibly the whole point of
wagers between people who had worked together for three years.

---

He did his own accounting later, at the bench in the upper room, by
lamplight, with the tower quiet under him and the snow that had
followed them home ticking at the window.

It had started as the ordinary evening entry and turned into
something else in the third line, the way a routine survey turns into
a finding, and he followed it the way he followed everything, all the
way down.

*Milestones, dated, all of them in the book. The night of the
eleventh of the eleventh. The morning after. What-are-we, answered.
The declaration. The tower. The governor. The spar and everything the
spar broke open. The holiday. The paper, completed, both names
hers.*

*Eight weeks and five days. I have dated every stone in it and every
stone is real and none of it is the finding.*

*The finding is the gaps.*

He ruled the page into two columns, which he had done ten thousand
times, and headed them, which took him longer than any ruling had
taken him in years.

*Things we have done: everything large.*

*Things we have never done:*

*Walked anywhere without a destination. Tarugi had a ledger at the
end of it. Even the market lane was bounded by an audit.*

*Eaten at a stall, standing up, for no reason.*

*I have never given her a gift I chose. The rose was not delivered
and not chosen. The hat ran the other way. The pinwheel she won
herself. Three years and the column is empty.*

*I have never asked her a question that was not load-bearing.*

*I have never made her laugh on purpose. Nine times on the eighth,
and I have the count, and I treasure the count, and every one of the
nine was an accident.*

He sat back and looked at the two columns, and the lamp did what
lamps do to an honest page, which is refuse to soften it.

*It has gone past too fast. That is the sentence, and I have been
carrying it since the ferry and disliking it, because it sounds like
a complaint against the best eight weeks of my life, and it is not.
Nothing on the left of the page is wrong. The left of the page is a
list of miracles.*

*The failure is columnar. We built the cathedral first because the
cathedral kept getting handed to us — every large thing arrived on
its own schedule, an incident, a paper, a fight, a wager — and
nobody ever scheduled the small ones, because small things do not
generate paperwork, and I am a man who navigates exclusively by
paperwork.*

*She has done them. Note that, because it is true and the page wants
it: the bench in the sun was hers. The hat was hers. We'll-wait was
hers. The kiss that answered what-are-we was hers. The reason the right-hand
column has any entries at all near it is that she keeps making them
unilaterally, out of some competence she has that I have not got, and
I have been receiving romance for eight weeks the way the ward
receives supplies, and contributing incidents.*

*That is the gap. She is owed courtship, and she has never once asked
for it, and she would never ask, and every day it is not supplied she
will go on saying nothing, because she has decided I am worth the
shortfall.*

*I do not intend to be worth the shortfall. I intend to close it.*

He wrote, at the bottom of the right-hand column, in the space where
a protocol writes its objective:

*Objective: supply the small rows. Method: unknown. Precedent: none.
Literature: one volume, annotated, in my coat.*

And then he sat for a while with his hand flat on the closed
notebook, in the lamplight, listening to the snow, in the posture of
a man who has just discovered that the only textbook on the syllabus
is the one book in the Land of Fire he could never, under any
circumstances, be found holding.

*Well,* he thought. *I have read it fourteen notes deep as an editor.
Nobody said anything about reading it as a student.*

*Nobody said anything at all, in fact, and there is a reason for
that, and the reason is that if she ever finds out where the syllabus
came from, the man who gave it to me is going to need the hospital he
is banned from.*

*Through and out. Tomorrow is Friday and I owe a slug a market.*
