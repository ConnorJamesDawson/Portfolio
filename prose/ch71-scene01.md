# Chapter 71, Scene 1 — Rules of Engagement

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower and the yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~850 words.*

---

The war had rules, because everything between them acquired rules
the way wet ground acquires footprints, and by the Friday after
the first Thursday he had them drafted in the study file under a
heading he had considered for some time and entered without
apology.

*THE CAMPAIGN. Objective: her, off balance, at the frequency she
has enjoyed having me off balance since a towel in December.
Doctrine: her own — increments, delivered level, through the
correct channel, never faster than the recipient can bear and
never slower than she can grow. Rules of engagement: nothing
false, nothing public, nothing that spends what it cannot
afford. Assessment of enemy capability: total superiority in
every category except one.*

*The one: she has never once been on the receiving end. Forty
years of being the confident party in every room of her life.
The entire architecture of her is built for outgoing fire.*

*Exploit accordingly.*

The opening engagement was Saturday breakfast, and it was six
words long.

She was mid-document, hair down, reading glasses on — the
glasses were themselves a disclosure eleven weeks old, a thing
the village did not know existed — and he watched her turn a
page and said, in the working register, level as a bench rule:

"You are beautiful in the mornings before you are beautiful on
purpose."

The page stopped turning.

He returned to his tea with his pulse at a hundred and forty
and the blush arriving on schedule — that was the finding of
the new era, confirmed now across nine engagements: the blush
still came, every time, the systems still lit, every time. But
the white noise did not follow it. The love went through the
noise now the way a graded article went through disorder — the
signal arriving intact on the far side, the cost paid in
copper patches instead of blackout — and what was left was a
man, pink to the ears, hand steady on a teacup, entirely
conscious, watching the fifth Hokage of the Land of Fire fail
to find the line she had been reading for eleven seconds.

"You," said Tsunade, finally, with the care of a woman
stepping onto ice she had personally reported as thin, "are
going to be a *problem* now. Aren't you."

"I have a syllabus," said Sazare, "and a great deal of

"There's a drawer."

"There is not. I built your filing system a hairbrush,
Tsunade. I know what's on the shelf."

She threw the border digest at him. He caught it, which
Jiraiya's four weeks were owed credit for, and logged the
engagement as won on points, and the laugh — entry

What he did not log, because it happened at the pace at which
footprints happen, was the drift.

It began that same weekend, as a byproduct of victory: the
war required proximity, proximity bred habit, and habit —
habit was a solvent. It worked on the seams of the protocol
the way steam worked on the Tarugi floor, finding the fall
lines, pooling where nobody checked.

On the Sunday, crossing the yard while she reviewed the
second-run manifests, he took a stray hair off her shoulder
in passing — one motion, unthought, husbandly — with a
runner from the records office forty feet away at the gate.
The runner was reading a manifest. Nothing was seen.

On the Monday, at the close of the board's Tuesday-memo
review, she said "good, fabricator" in the wrong register —
the private one, the one that lived on the second floor,
its weight unmistakable to anybody with an instrument for
weight — in an open corridor, with a clerk four doors down.
The clerk was counting requisitions. Nothing was heard.

On the Tuesday he touched the small of her back for half a
second, steering past her in the doorway of the small
office, the way you steer around furniture you own. Shizune
was in the room. Shizune was, of course, a signatory.
Nothing was noticed that had not long since been minuted as
*not minuted*.

Each instance survived on the same actuarial truth: the
village was not watching. The village had a story about the
Hokage and her strange young fabricator — a good story, a
docket-shaped story, reinforced weekly by real dockets —
and people who have a story stop collecting data. He knew
that principle better than anybody alive. He had built his
entire security on it: *nobody audits the ordinary.*

The corollary — which he also knew better than anybody
alive, which he had written in the margin of the misdirection
file in his own hand in December, and which the war's
happiness had quietly filed somewhere behind the campaign
syllabus — was that the principle held right up until the
day somebody arrived for whom watching was not a choice but
a birthright, an art, and a family religion.

The appointment request came through the proper channel on
the Monday evening. Clan business, garment line, emissary to
attend Monday, tower meeting room, protocols standard.

The signature block said *Hyūga*.
