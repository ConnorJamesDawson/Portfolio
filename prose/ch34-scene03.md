# Chapter 34, Scene 3 — Item Four

*Act 4, part C. The Hokage's office. Sazare Ruri, age seventeen. POV:
Sazare. ~1,941 words.*

---

The slate in the corridor said:

*1. Border and transit*
*2. Fisheries — Nagihama and the strait*
*3. Reparations, fourth tranche*
*4. Personnel — outstanding orders*
*5. Any other*

He stood in front of it for rather less time than he had in the
autumn of his sixteenth year, and then went in, because Shizune was
holding the door and there was no version of standing in that corridor
that improved anything.

They had taken the desk out.

That was the first thing and it changed the whole room. The round
office at the top of the tower had a long table down the middle of it
now, borrowed from somewhere, with eleven chairs, and the four faces
on the cliff came through the middle window at the far end so that
anybody sitting on the near side spent the entire meeting looking at
them.

*Rung two: somebody chose which side of the table the delegation sits
on.*

Seven Konoha. Six Kiri.

He counted the room the way he counted every room and then made himself
stop at four seconds, because there was one person in it he was going
to look at for longer than four seconds and he wanted to have finished
everything else first.

**Ao** was second from the left on the Kiri side and Sazare knew him
before anybody said a word, because Hazama had read his name aloud in a
flat voice in a room with a broken radiator and because there was an
eyepatch and because the visible eye had the particular fixity of
somebody using the other kind of sight and being polite about it. He
was about fifty. He sat with both hands on the table, flat, which
nobody does unless they have decided to be visibly unarmed.

**Terumī Mei** was at the centre.

She was younger than he had constructed her, which he registered as an
error in himself rather than a fact about her — he had built her out of
a name on a wanted notice and eleven years of a war, and had arrived
at somebody in her sixties. She was perhaps thirty. Auburn hair, worn
long and over the right side of the face in a way that was deliberate
and not decorative, because the fall of it had been cut to hold that
line and cutting hair to hold a line takes a good barber and repeat
appointments.

Green coat over the blue, the Mizukage's, and it fitted, which after
two years of looking at Konoha's establishment was the most surprising
thing in the room: everybody in that village was wearing something made
for a different body. Hers had been made for hers, recently, by
somebody good.

*Rung two: she had it made after the appointment. Not inherited. Not
altered.*

*Rung three: the office is new enough that she's still establishing
what it looks like, and she has decided it looks like her.*

Her hands were on the table too, loosely, and there was a callus across
the right palm in a place a sword does not put one, and Sazare could
not read it and filed it.

Nobody looked at him at all.

---

Items one through three took ninety minutes.

He stood at the wall behind and to the left of Kōji, with Amagai's
second chair empty beside him because Amagai was at the table, and he
listened to two governments talk about fish.

It was, he thought afterwards, the single most useful ninety minutes of
diplomatic education anybody could have given him, because the fish
were real. There were four villages on the strait that had been
fishing water that had changed hands twice during the war and neither
administration had a defensible claim and about eleven hundred people
ate off it. Tsunade argued for forty minutes about a boundary in a
piece of water she had never seen. Mei conceded a third of it and took
something else in exchange that Sazare did not understand the value of
until Kōji explained it that evening.

Nobody performed. Nobody made a speech. Two women who between them
could have levelled the building sat and argued about a fishery for
forty minutes because eleven hundred people ate off it.

Reparations, fourth tranche, took twenty minutes and he stopped
breathing properly for about four of them, and it was schedules, and
the schedules were long, and his family's name did not come up, and by
the end of it he had understood that item three was money and property
in aggregate and that whatever Kōji had been warned about was not in
it.

"Item four," said Tsunade. "Personnel."

---

It took eleven minutes and he was discussed in the third person for
nine of them.

Amagai put the threat-review extract on the table. The Kiri delegate to
her right — a man of about sixty whose name Sazare did not catch and
never learned — did not look at it, because he had a copy, and said
that the order was withdrawn effective on signature and that the
registry had already prepared the amendment.

"And the charge," said Amagai.

"Vacated. You have the paper."

"We have the paper. I'd like it said at the table."

"It is said at the table," the delegate agreed, without any visible
irritation, and said it, and a clerk wrote it down.

Then they talked about the fabrication agreement for six minutes, and
Sazare stood against a wall and listened to two governments agree on
what would be done with the output of his hands for the following
four years, in a tone appropriate to a discussion of tonnage.

He had known it was coming. He had read the four pages twice. The
figures did not surprise him and the review provisions did not surprise
him and the clause reserving Konoha's operational priority did not
surprise him.

What surprised him was that it was *dull.*

*This is the fourth time,* he thought, standing against a wall with his
hands at his sides. *Kiri's inventory when I was nine. The book with
the tab. Kanzaki's Thursdays. And this.*

*And this one is the least frightening of the four and it is the one
that will last the longest, and the reason it is not frightening is
that everybody at this table is being extremely reasonable, and the
reason they can afford to be reasonable is that nobody has to persuade
me of anything.*

"Agreed," said Tsunade.

"Agreed," said Mei Terumī.

And the clerk wrote it down, and Amagai closed the folder, and Kōji
began to shift the papers for item five.

---

"A moment," said the Mizukage.

The room stopped in the particular way a room stops when the person who
has stopped it does not usually.

"Before item five." She turned in her chair, and looked past Kōji, and
for the first time in a hundred and eleven minutes somebody in that
room looked directly at Sazare Ruri. "You're him."

"Yes."

"Come here, please. To the table. Not to sit."

He went. His knee, which had not troubled him in four months, chose
that moment to remind him of its history, and he was aware of every
step of the eleven feet.

Mei Terumī looked up at him.

"How old were you when you left?"

"Thirteen."

"Where from?"

"Ward eleven."

Something moved behind her face and was put away, and Sazare — who had
been taught for six weeks by a man who could see it and could not say
it — read the whole of it and had no idea what to do with any of it.

"I'm going to say something now," she said, "and I want to be honest
with you about what it is, because you look like a man who would rather
have the truth than the courtesy. This is not spontaneous. I have said
it before. I have said it to about four hundred people in the last two
years and I will say it to several thousand more before I am finished,
and every single time I have meant it, and every single time it has
been insufficient, and I have not found a way to make it otherwise."

"All right."

"On behalf of Kirigakure." She did not stand, and she did not raise her
voice, and she did not look away. "There is no apology that heals that
wound. I am not going to offer you one and pretend it does. What I can
tell you is that the administration that did it is gone, and that I
was on the other side of it, and that a great many people I loved died
in the same years and for the same reason as yours. And that the whole
of what I have to give you is the hope — not the promise, I will not
promise it — that the thing we are building now is not tainted by the
thing it replaced."

The room was extremely quiet.

"Thank you," said Sazare.

"You don't have to thank me. Most people don't."

"I know."

---

And he had the sentence.

It was there, whole, complete, four years old, and it took no assembly
whatsoever because it had never needed any:

*I sent a tenth of everything I earned to your war for thirty-one
months.*

*Four hundred and twenty thousand ryō through a board at Kasado and a
broker called Chōsei. A hundred and forty people minimum. It is one per
cent of everything that moved on the mainland chains and I have the
figures and I can show you the dates.*

*I never told anybody. I have never told anybody. You are the only
person alive it would mean anything to.*

He stood four feet from the Fifth Mizukage of Kirigakure in a round
room at the top of a tower and did not say it.

He did not say it because the pooling at the second transfer point had
been deliberate, and correct, and standard, and because he had known
that at fifteen and had approved of it, and because he would have
insisted on it himself if anybody had asked him.

He did not say it because there was no record anywhere in the world
that could support one word of it, and a man who stands up in a
diplomatic meeting and claims to have funded the other side's
revolution, without evidence, is either a liar or a problem, and he had
been both of those in a file already.

And he did not say it — and this was the one that arrived last and
stayed longest — because saying it would have been **asking her for
something**, and there was nothing she could have given him, and he
would have watched her understand that in about a second and a half and
then have to decide what to do with a boy who had wanted to be thanked.

*My own system,* thought Sazare Ruri. *Working exactly the way I built
it. In the only room I was ever going to want it to fail in.*

"Item five," said Kōji, gently, after a moment.

Sazare went back to the wall.

He did not know — and did not find out for another eleven years, and
then only by accident, from a Kiri quartermaster at a technical
conference in a country neither of them was from — that Ao had watched
the whole of it from second-from-the-left with a Byakugan active, and
had made a note that evening, and that the note said only:

*The boy at the wall wanted to say something and stopped himself. Not
deception. Recommend no action.*
