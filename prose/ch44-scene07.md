# Chapter 44, Scene 7 — Four Per Cent

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,547 words.*

---

He did the arithmetic that night because there was nothing else left to
do with it.

The room was hot and had been hot since four and would be hot until
about two in the morning, and the persimmon outside was in full leaf and
making no sound at all, and he had the window all the way up and the
lamp turned down to almost nothing because the lamp made it worse.

He got out a clean sheet and did it properly, from the top, the way he
would have done it for the board.

*Statement of the finding, as given to me on a staircase on the
thirteenth by a person who has not read one line of my work:*

*It is not a property of the object. It is not a property of the
receiver. It is in the taking-up.*

*Statement of my own finding, from three hundred and eleven entries in
a quartermaster's ledger over fourteen months:*

*It is not a property of the instruction. It is not a property of the
medium. It is in the taking-up.*

*These are the same sentence. I am not going to argue about whether
they are the same sentence, because I have tried for three days to
break the correspondence and I cannot, and the reason I cannot is that
I did not construct either of them.*

He looked at that for a while and then went on, because stopping there
would have been the whole of the cowardice.

*Consequence, from the reject table.*

*The material that takes up a course fastest is the material that has
already held one.*

*Yard aggregate, twenty-two per cent reject. Granite, thirty-one. Site
rubble off the invasion streets, forty-four.*

*My own prior work, re-seeded: four.*

He put the pencil down and got up and stood at the window for a bit
with the whole village going quiet below him in the heat.

Then he came back and wrote it.

*Four per cent.*

*Not because it is better material. It is the same material. It is four
per cent because it has held an instruction before and the pathways are
already cut, and the thing goes in without argument, and it goes in
fast, and it goes in whether or not I have decided that it should.*

---

*So. Honestly, and then I am going to stop.*

*The question is not why her. The question is why anybody at all, and
the answer is that the route was cut a long time before either of them
was in it.*

*So when was it cut.*

He sat with that, because it was a dating problem and dating problems
were tractable, and he had eleven years of ledgers upstairs in a box
and could in principle go and get them.

He did not need to.

*It was not the hug and it was not the hand on the shoulder and it was
not any of the things a book would put in the ninth chapter.*

*It was the first time in my life that somebody took a thing I brought
them and made the conversation about something I had not brought.*

*I have been read accurately by a great many institutions. Four
countries have priced me. Kiri has a number on me in a book. Konoha has
a file with nine hundred pages in it and everything in that file is
correct.*

*Not one of them ever changed the subject.*

*Every exchange I have had since I was thirteen has been as large as the
object on the table and no larger, and I did not know that was a
description of a limit, because a man who has only ever had one kind of
conversation does not experience it as a kind.*

*And then somebody put a stack of paper on the floor.*

---

*Second consequence, and this is the one I have been walking round for
four days.*

*If it is in the taking-up, then it is not a fact about her and it is
not a compliment to her. It is a fact about the state of the material,
and the material is me, and the route was cut by somebody doing
something that anybody could in principle have done and nobody
happened to do.*

*Which means the correct conclusion is not "it is her."*

*It is: I am four per cent, to that one instruction, and I have been
four per cent since about the eleventh month of my seventeenth year,
and every other medium in the world now reads as twenty-two, or
thirty-one, or forty-four — not because they are worse, but because
they are asking me to cut a route I have already got.*

*K.S. is not forty-four per cent. She is not even twenty-two. She is
the best material I have ever been handed by anybody and I could put
that in writing in front of a board.*

*It makes no difference at all and I am not going to pretend, in my own
book, that it does.*

---

He stopped there for about twenty minutes, and did not write anything,
and at one point got up and moved the lamp to the other side of the
table for no reason and moved it back.

Then he wrote the last section, and headed it, because everything got a
heading.

*WHAT I AM GOING TO DO ABOUT IT*

*Nothing.*

*I am going to give three reasons and I want them in descending order
of how much I like saying them.*

*One. She is the Hokage of this village and I am a subordinate in a
building she runs, and every single thing I bring her becomes a review,
a signature, and a programme, and if I put one more thing on that pile
it will be a thing she has to rule on. That is real and it is
sufficient and I would stop there if I were being tidy.*

*Two. She is somewhere above forty years older than me and knows it to
the day and I do not know it at all, and the entire village would have
a view, and the view would land on her and not on me, and she has
already paid four separate times this year for letting me get away with
things a subordinate should not get away with. I have watched her pay
and she has never once mentioned the bill.*

*Three, and this is the true one and neither of the others is doing any
work.*

*I do not know what it is that I want.*

*I know that I want the fourth hour of the morning with the stack on
the floor. I know I want somebody who will stop me from writing a
sentence that would have run for a century, and who will tell me my
scheme is clever and sustain the objection anyway, and who will say
"say the end of that sentence out loud" and let me hear it. I want
that, unambiguously, more than I have ever wanted a technique or a
result or a name in a book.*

*And I do not know whether that is the same object as the other thing,
or whether they merely arrive in the same person, and I have no
experiment that separates them.*

*Every test I can construct destroys the thing being tested. There is no
version where I find out and still have the fourth hour.*

*So: nothing. Not out of nobility, which I want on the record, because
the honest half of this is that the reason I am not doing anything is
that I am not willing to risk the conversation.*

*I would rather have the conversation than know.*

---

He turned to the back of the book, where the page with no unit at the
head of the second column was, and looked at the eight items under it.

He had the pencil on the paper for the ninth.

Then he took it off again.

*Not entering this one.*

*Eight items in that column and every one of them is a thing I noticed
and could not name. This is a thing I have named, in four pages, with a
reject table, and putting it in that column would be filing a solved
problem in the drawer for unsolved ones because the drawer is more
comfortable.*

*It goes in the main book. Dated. Where I have to walk past it.*

He closed the notebook.

Then, because it was half past one and the room was still hot and there
was absolutely nothing in the yard or on the staircase or in his own
head that he could do anything about at half past one, he reached over
and pulled the other pile towards him — the one that had been sitting
under the window since the eighteenth, the six practitioners' document
and the four studies he had borrowed off Ochiai to see how other people
built a protocol.

He had taken them for the method. He had not read a word of the
content.

He got about nine pages into the second one, which was thirty years old
and about something else entirely, and stopped, and read a paragraph
again, and then a third time.

Then he sat up.
