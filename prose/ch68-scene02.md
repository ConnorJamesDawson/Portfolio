# Chapter 68, Scene 2 — What the No Was

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the north fields. Jiraiya, age fifty-four.
POV: Jiraiya. ~1170 words.*

---

Jiraiya had watched a lot of people fail to hit him.

It was, if he was honest — and he was, in the notebook nobody would
read until he was dead — one of the richer texts available in the
profession. Men failed to hit him out of fear, which was boring;
out of awe, which was worse; out of cunning, holding back to hide
their real speed, which at least gave you something to work with.
Boys failed out of hero worship and grown jōnin failed out of
politics. In fifty-four years he had assembled a complete taxonomy
of the pulled blow, and he had built the morning's first exercise
around it deliberately, because the fastest way to read a fighter's
soul was to order him to do a small violence and watch which excuse
arrived.

No excuse arrived.

The boy stood in the frost for four and a half minutes — Fukasaku
timed it; the old man timed everything — and conducted, in full
view of anybody with the eyes to watch it, a complete good-faith
engineering survey of his own interior, looking for the mechanism.
No theatre. No glance for permission. No drift in the feet — Sakai's
ghost held his stance the entire time, which told its own story
about the quality of dead swordsmen. Just a man going room to room
through his own house with a lamp, and coming back out, and
reporting the house's contents accurately.

*I cannot locate the decision.*

Jiraiya had stood in front of that sentence with his arms spread
and felt the morning get more interesting by an order of magnitude.

*Not "I can't." Not "I won't." Not the flinch, not the sermon, not
the please-don't-make-me. An inventory result, delivered in the
grading voice: went in along six approaches, component not found,
difference between untrained and absent flagged as material, full
implications priced. Eighteen years old. I have jōnin of twenty
years' service who have never once looked at that room in
themselves, because everybody who might have made them look was
too busy needing them to keep killing.*

---

Fukasaku came off the cloth at the noon bell, when the boy was away
up the lane, and stood beside him in the churned frost, and they
watched the gate a while in the manner of two old men at the end of
a first day of term.

"Well," said Fukasaku.

"Say it, then."

"Ma trained forty years of tadpoles," said Fukasaku. "I have seen
every species of boy there is. That one is not a boy who cannot
fight. That one is a boy nobody has ever *asked*. Every violent
thing in his file arrived as a side effect of protecting something
— the fixtures, the splint, the leg, the close. He has never once
been the subject of the sentence. Always the instrument of some
other clause." The old toad worked his jaw, thinking. "Your
academies would have fixed that by now. Fixed it right out of him."

"That's why he's out here with us and not in there with them."

"Mm. And the other thing."

"Say the other thing too."

"He said no to the Toad Sage of Mount Myōboku," said Fukasaku,
"at six minutes past nine in the morning, alone, in a field, with
nothing propping him but his own arithmetic, and his hands never
shook. Whatever you are planning to build on that boy—" the old
toad looked up at him, yellow-eyed, ancient, amused— "the
foundation's poured. You're just walling."

---

He did his own notebook at noon, on the wall by the gate, in the
shorthand he had used since before the war made it necessary.

*Day 1. Baseline better than advertised and worse than needed —
which is the correct starting shape; the other shapes lie.*

*The no: real, load-bearing, delivered under charisma at close
range. Do not fix. Repeat: DO NOT FIX. The no is the same muscle
as the yes. A man who can refuse me in a frozen field on day one
is a man whose yes, when I finally get it, will hold against
anything on the continent. Every trainer I have ever despised
would sand this. It is the best thing he owns and he thinks it is
his defect.*

*The body: eight years late and honest about it. Footwork takes
correction like wet clay — Sakai's stance survives under fatigue,
which means the legs learned from fear and fear is a thorough
teacher. Strength floor: adequate. Ceiling: irrelevant — his war
was never going to be arm wrestling. Conditioning: Kakashi's
numbers hold. A year gets him to the spec. The spec is not ANBU
muscle. The spec is: cannot be flipped inside two feet, cannot be
outlasted inside six minutes, cannot be surprised by his own
skeleton. Anything past that is vanity, mine or hers.*

*The creed: sighted, not engaged. He wrote something under it in
one of the four books — he didn't say so, but he arrived this
morning like a man who has already surveyed the ground he expects
me to dig up, which is exactly what he would do. Good. Let it
stand until the field earns the conversation. A creed revised in
week one is a slogan. A creed revised the morning after he
first chooses the yes and holds it — that one goes in the front of
books for the rest of his life. There will be a date. I'll know
the week when I see it. No earlier.*

*The window: unchanged. She was awake at dawn to see him off —
Shizune's diary says otherwise, and Shizune's diary has never once
lied, which is how I know it was personal. Target holds. That
woman does not stand at that window in spring. Everything out here
is in service of one sentence and the sentence is not about him.*

*Tomorrow: the old man's answer, and then the boy's rule comes off
that page. Fukasaku is pretending he hasn't noticed the rule.
Fukasaku noticed the rule before the boy sat down. Whatever's
under it, he's been not-looking at it with his whole face for
three hours, and I have read enough of his file to have a guess,
and if my guess is right—*

He looked at the last line for a while, out on the wall in the
cold, an old man with an orange notebook and the best student he
would ever be handed arriving at the rate of four mornings a week.

*—if my guess is right, then the thing I refused to say behind the
bathhouse is going to look like an extremely quaint concern by
midsummer, and four countries are eventually going to be very,
very interested in a quartermaster's yard, and I am going to have
to teach this one the real trade after all. The one where nobody
claps.*

*Later. First: footwork.*

He shut the book. Down the lane, precisely on the half hour,
because the boy did everything precisely, the second-floor window
of the tower opened to let the noon in.
