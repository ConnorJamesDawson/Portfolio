# Chapter 30, Scene 5 — In Her Own Language

*Act 4, part C. The Hokage's office. Sazare Ruri, age sixteen. POV:
Sazare. ~1,798 words.*

---

The interruption came at the twenty-eighth minute and it came through
the window.

Sazare did not see how. There was an ANBU in the room who had not been
in the room, mask on, one knee down, and a folded report held out at
arm's length, and Tsunade took it without any change in posture
whatsoever, read four lines of it, and said a word Sazare had heard
Nurse Yagi use exactly once, at the third hour of the invasion, over a
patient she had just lost.

"When?"

"Second hour this morning. Confirmed at eleven."

"How many?"

"Four. Two recoverable."

"Kanuma's team?"

"Yes."

Tsunade folded the report closed.

"Tell the mission office they are not to notify the families until I
have signed it, and if anybody tries to be kind and get ahead of me on
that, I will have their post. Go."

The ANBU went. The window was as it had been. The whole thing had taken
under twenty seconds, and Sazare, who had spent his professional life
reading what people did with their hands, watched the Fifth Hokage put
the report face-down on the corner of the desk, square it to the edge
with two fingers, and go back to the sheet in front of her without
saying one word about it.

"Where were we," she said.

"You were going to tell me what I'm allowed to do."

"I was going to tell you what I'm *writing down*, which is a
considerably different thing and I want you to hear the difference."

---

She did it fast, out loud, with Shizune taking it down.

"One. His hospital assignment is protected. Not *he may keep it* —
protected. Second-floor ward lead, medical crystal technician, under
Kaburagi's operational authority and Fujita's clinical authority.
Nobody moves him off it without my signature, and *nobody* means
mission office, personnel, the Council or anybody who arrives with an
emergency and a good story."

"Two. Every experimental application on a living person goes through
review before it happens. Not after. Fujita for eligibility, Yagi or
another medic-nin for the pattern, Kanzaki for the material, an
independent party for consent — not him, he doesn't take his own
consents, that's already in his own protocol and it's the single best
thing in this file. Shizune reviews the file after each case."

Shizune's pen did not pause. "Me?"

"You. You've read it twice already and you found the coat pocket."

"Three." Tsunade held up a finger without looking at Sazare. "Special
operations, technical debrief, Root, or any office that submits a
request with an office seal and no officer's name on it — refused at
the desk, referred to me, personally, in writing. I don't care what
authority they cite. There is one emergency in this village that
overrides that and it is me being dead."

Sazare said, before he had decided to: "There was a request in the
sixth week."

"I read it," said Tsunade. "It's on this desk. It's the reason clause
three is worded the way it is instead of politely." She turned the
sheet over. "Four. The combat evaluation stands. I'm not waiving it and
I wouldn't if he asked. The village does not deploy people it hasn't
measured, and more to the point, he doesn't know what he is either,
and that's a worse problem than the paperwork."

"Five. No deployment of any kind — none, including inside the walls,
including anything anybody wants to call an exercise — until the knee
is cleared by Fujita *and* the chakra pathways are cleared by a medic
of my choosing. Two separate clearances, two separate signatures, and
if the second one takes a year then it takes a year."

She stopped, and read the whole thing back off Shizune's shorthand with
her hand out for the page, which Sazare noticed and did not understand
the significance of until considerably later.

"Six. Accelerated medical competency examination. Anatomy, diagnostics,
pharmacology, infection, triage, medical chakra control. Full paper,
same as a medic-nin candidate, no allowances for the fact that he came
in sideways." She handed the page back. "He'll do extremely well in
control and wound management, adequately in systems, and badly in the
parts he taught himself, and the point of it is not the grade. The
point is that at the end of it, there will be a document in this
building saying exactly which parts of him are trained and which parts
are guesswork, and everybody who uses him will have to read it."

---

Sazare did the arithmetic while she was still talking, because he could
not not.

He had walked into that room a probationary genin with a ward, a
prohibition, and a device nobody had the authority to let him use
again. He was walking out with: a protected post, a reviewed research
programme, a route through which Root would have to come to the Hokage
personally and in writing, a mandatory examination, two separate
medical clearances standing between him and any deployment whatsoever,
and a training pathway with four hundred hours of anatomy in it.

More restrictions than he had arrived with. Considerably more.

And every single one of them was a wall built between him and somebody
else, rather than between him and the work.

"You've noticed," Tsunade said.

"That it's more rules."

"That it's more rules *and* you're safer, and that those aren't in
tension." She capped the pen. "Sarutobi protected you personally. He
read your file himself on Wednesdays because he did not trust the
system to do it, and he was right not to, and then he died and the
protection died with him inside a week. That's not a criticism of a
dead man. It's the design flaw in doing it that way, and I watched what
it cost you in the fortnight afterwards, because it's all in here."

"You're writing it down instead."

"I'm writing it down instead. Which means it survives me being busy, me
being wrong, and me being dead, and I intend all three of those to
happen eventually." She pushed the papers into a stack. "It also means
it can be read by people who don't like you, which is the part nobody
enjoys about doing it properly. A written protection is a written
description of exactly what you're worth to this village. You'll want
to think about that. Most people don't until it matters."

He thought about the Act 1 inventory, and a bingo book with a price
boxed at the bottom, and a quota, and a diplomatic clause that did not
yet exist and that he had no way to know was coming.

"I've thought about it," he said.

Something moved in Tsunade's face — not sympathy, which he would have
recognised and disliked. Something closer to recognition.

"Yes," she said. "I imagine you have."

---

Shizune began gathering the folders. The black pig, which had been
asleep on the side table throughout the entire hour and had at no
point been explained to Sazare, woke up, looked at him, and went back
to sleep.

Sazare stood, which took slightly longer than he wanted it to, and
picked up the tin of six devices and the notebook and Kanzaki's dish.

"Leave the dish," Tsunade said. "And the spent one."

"Kanzaki wants the dish back."

"Kanzaki can come and get it. Tell her I said so and watch her face."

He left the dish.

At the door, he stopped, because there was a thing he had been holding
since the twenty-eighth minute and had not found a place to put.

"The report," he said. "Kanuma's team."

Tsunade did not look up. "What about it."

"Two recoverable. If either of them is a bleed that keeps reopening
under transport, and they're more than four hours out, a stone won't
help — there's no medic at the far end to load a pattern and no time
to key it to them. I wanted you to have that from me rather than find
out you'd hoped."

Tsunade's pen stopped.

She looked up at him for a long moment across a desk covered in eleven
weeks of his own handwriting.

"That's the first useful thing anybody has said to me about a limit
today," she said. "Everybody else in this building tells me what their
thing can do."

"It's a very narrow tool."

"I know. That's why I want another one." She went back to the page.
"Thursday week. Second hour. Bring the degradation curve, or bring the
reason you haven't got one."

"Should I make an appointment with—"

"I've just made it. Shizune, put him in the book."

"He's in the book," said Shizune, who had written it down before either
of them said anything, and who did not look up either, and who was
therefore the only other person in the room who did not see Sazare
Ruri stop in a doorway for slightly longer than leaving required.

---

He walked back down the tower stairs and out past the memorial board
and down the length of the Nakadōri, and he was three streets from the
hospital before he identified what he was doing, which was composing.

Not a report. He had the report; he'd have it written before the sixth
hour and Ashida would file it in a tray.

He was composing the *telling*. Handa first, because Handa would ask
and would not stop asking. Then Yagi, who would want the three flaws
and would be pleased about the fibrin in a way that would be unbearable
for approximately two days. Kanzaki, who would want the balance back
and the dish and would say something short about a Hokage using a
quartermaster's instrument without a chit. Urushibara, who would want
the degradation question and would be annoyed at being right about a
week and unable to prove it.

He had six people to tell and he was ordering them by how much each of
them would enjoy it.

He stopped in the middle of the road with the hospital in sight and
stood there long enough that a woman with a handcart went round him
and said something uncomplimentary.

*I have never once in my life come out of a room and wanted to tell
somebody what happened in it.*

*I have four hundred hours of anatomy, an examination I will fail parts
of, two clearances I don't control, and a woman who found five things
wrong with my work in twenty minutes that I could not have found in
five years.*

*And I am standing in a road.*

He went to the hospital and did the second half of a shift he had not
been rostered for, and told Handa everything, and did not sleep again
that night either, and did not mind that either.
