# Chapter 57, Scene 6 — Things That Have Moved

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower roof. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1510 words.*

---

"Both of you," said Tsunade, "out. I've got ninety files and a
delegation and I am not doing either of them with you two in the room."

So they went up.

Jiraiya went up the outside of the building without discussing it, which
meant Sazare had to as well, and the roof of the tower at half past five
on the sixteenth of the eleventh month was cold and flat and had a low
parapet on the north side and a view of the whole of it.

The sky was doing the thing it did about forty minutes before it was
light.

Jiraiya sat down on the parapet with his back to the drop.

He still had the nineteen pages.

---

"Right," he said. "First. She gave me this. You didn't."

"I know."

"You've known since you came through the door."

"I knew from the landing," said Sazare. "I could see the string. It's
fabrication-room string, it's about a two-millimetre flax and there's
about four hundred metres of it in this village and all of it's mine,
and it was through the top corner where I put it in the tenth month."

"And you said nothing about it. You came in and gave her a lecture about
an undated amendment."

"That was the job she gave me."

"Kid," said Jiraiya, "that is not why."

Sazare stood on a roof and did not answer for a moment.

"No," he said. "It isn't."

---

"I want to be accurate about this, because I've had about eleven minutes
with it and it's got two halves and they don't agree, and I'd rather say
both than pick the one that sounds better.

"The first half is that I'm pleased.

"I gave her that paper because I did not want to be the only person
holding it. I wrote that in the note. Four lines, on the top, and the
third one says it. A man holding a document nobody knows about is
concealing something, and if I had kept it in a drawer in my own room
then every single day after that I would have been a person with a
hidden paper, and I would have known it, and it would have been true.

"So she has given it to a second reader. That's the paper moving in
exactly the direction I asked for it to be able to move.

"And she's given it to the one man in this village who can read the
middle of it. I hadn't got that. I have been thinking for four months
that there were about two people alive who could assess pages nine to
fifteen and both of them were four hundred miles away and neither of them
can read."

"And the second half?"

"She didn't ask me," said Sazare.

---

Jiraiya did not say anything.

"That's not an accusation and I want that clear. She had the right. It's
her building and it's her security judgement and I put the thing in her
hands on purpose and told her in writing that I wasn't asking her to do
anything with it, which means I gave her the whole of the discretion and
I knew I was doing it.

"So she has done nothing wrong and I have got no complaint that would
survive four minutes.

"And it's still the third time in five days."

"Third," said Jiraiya.

"Third. There have been three occasions since Wednesday morning where
somebody has stood between me and a procedure and decided on my behalf,
and every single one of them was correct, and every single one of them
was somebody who is on my side, and I said all right to all three.

"I'm not building a grievance. I'm counting."

"Why?"

"Because I have never had to count them before," said Sazare, "and now I
do, and the reason I do is that four months ago I was a fabricator and
nobody had to decide anything about me at all."

---

Jiraiya turned the pages over in his hands.

"You know about the letters," he said.

Sazare stopped.

"Which letters?"

"Don't."

"I'm not — I have two correspondences and one of them is Kanzaki."

"The Oto one," said Jiraiya. "Written arrangement with Morino Ibiki,
first exchange in the fifth month, second in the eleventh, third due,
fee agreed in advance and accepted by both parties, and the fee was
words and not money."

He said it exactly the way he had said *frame-worker*, which was as a
line out of a folder.

Sazare stood on the roof and did the arithmetic and it took about four
seconds and came out somewhere he did not want it.

"How long have you had that?"

"Since the seventh month."

"Who from?"

"Nobody. I read the traffic. It's what I do." Jiraiya shrugged. "It's
not a leak, kid, and nobody's betrayed you. A letter went out of this
village to a place I watch and came back, and it came back with a
different hand on the outside than the one that went, and that's a flag,
and I pulled it.

"Ibiki knows I know. He's known since the eighth month. We had about
four sentences about it in a corridor and neither of us has raised it
since, because there was nothing to raise."

---

*Right.*

*Say it in order.*

*On Wednesday morning at about ten past eight I stood in a cold kitchen
and told Shizune that somebody had to tell Ibiki, and I laid it out —
concealed relationship, live foreign correspondence, unreported
vulnerability with a name and a date on it — and I said it should be me
and it should be today.*

*And she said not yet, and I said all right, and it took two seconds,
and I have been carrying it since Wednesday as the first dishonest thing
I have ever done.*

*And the man in front of me has had the correspondence in a file since
the seventh month.*

*And Ibiki has known that he has it since the eighth.*

*So the part I have spent five days deciding not to report has been in
two files and one corridor conversation for four months, and I did not
know, and Shizune probably did not know, and the thing I agreed to sit on
was not mine to sit on.*

*It was never mine.*

He found that he had put a hand on the parapet.

"You've gone a funny colour," said Jiraiya.

"There's a thing I've been not doing since Wednesday," said Sazare, "and
I have just found out it was already done, by other people, four months
ago, and that I have spent five days feeling like a man carrying
something and I was carrying an empty box."

"That's the job."

"It is not my job."

"No," said Jiraiya. "It isn't. It's mine, and I'm telling you now because
in about four months somebody is going to try to use it on you, and I
would rather you found out on a roof off a man who likes you."

---

He let Sazare stand there for a bit.

Below them the village was starting: a cart on the north road, somebody
opening a shutter, the first of the smoke off the bathhouse.

"Can I ask you something," said Jiraiya.

"Yes."

"Have you got a bit of stone on you?"

Sazare looked at him.

"I've got a tin."

"Course you have. Get it out."

---

He got the tin out.

It was the ordinary tin — the flat one, off the bench, with the graded
grain in it that he carried in the coat because he had carried
*something* in that coat since he was sixteen and had stopped noticing.

"Do the thing," said Jiraiya.

"Which thing?"

"The smallest one you've got. The one you'd do without thinking about
it. Not a demonstration — I don't want a demonstration, I've read the
reject table, I know what you can do with four hundred hours and a
lamp."

"It's four seconds and it's not interesting."

"Do it anyway."

So he tipped one grain out onto the flat of his left palm and laid a
course.

It came up in four seconds. Three deep, fine damping coarse. No
hesitation and no effort and nothing in it that felt like anything other
than every other time since he was nine, and he held it for about two
seconds and let it down.

He looked up.

Jiraiya had not moved and was not looking at the crystal.

He was looking at Sazare's face, and he had been looking at Sazare's
face the entire time, and there was something happening behind his own
that had not been there thirty seconds earlier.

"Do it again," said Jiraiya.

He did it again.

"Right," said Jiraiya, very quietly. "Right."

He put the nineteen pages down on the parapet and put a stone on top of
them so they would not go, which took him about two seconds, and it was
the first thing he had done all morning that was not fast.

"Kid," he said. "Who taught you?"
