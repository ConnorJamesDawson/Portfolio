# Chapter 51, Scene 5 — Who Has Been Teaching You

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2,195 words.*

---

"I have," said Katsuyu.

She said it immediately and without any weight on it whatsoever, in the
register she used for *the fourth floor is holding*, and Sazare
understood about four seconds too late that there had been a moment in
which he could have said something first and that it would not have
helped.

Tsunade did not move.

"Since when."

"The seventeenth of the eighth month," said Katsuyu. "The morning you
put your coat on. I have been in this yard eleven evenings of nineteen
in the ninth month and nine of fourteen so far in the tenth."

"Doing what."

"At first, telling him when he closed. He had no report of it — he had
done it four hundred thousand times and never once been aware of it,
and I could see it perfectly well, and so I said *now* until the inside
of him caught up. That took two days and it is how his people teach
pulses, he tells me."

"And after."

"After, the take and the grip." Katsuyu settled. "He draws about a
third more than he uses, every time, and discards the remainder. And he
holds it at approximately twice the effort required. Both are eleven
years old. Both were invisible to him and are extremely visible to me,
and I assumed for some weeks that he knew, because I have been assuming
things about what people know and I have been wrong every time."

Tsunade said nothing for a moment.

"And since Saturday," said Sazare.

"I was going to get to that," said Katsuyu.

---

"Since Saturday," said Tsunade.

"Since Saturday I have been in it with him rather than beside it."

Tsunade's hands, on her knees, did not do anything at all.

"Explain that."

"I do not think I explain things well. I have found that out this
year." A pause. "Telling him was not working. Not badly — it worked,
and he has come a very long way on it, but I was describing a room to a
man standing outside it and shouting, and he was having to go in and
find the furniture by feel every time. He cannot take instruction. He
has no — I am told there is a notation, and that it is shut to him, and
that everything he has ever learned he learned by holding a finished
one until his agreed with it."

"So you gave him a finished one."

"There is no article to hold. It is the operation itself." Katsuyu
shifted. "So I do it alongside him. At the same time, in the same
place, with what I have, so that there is something for him to feel his
own against."

Tsunade stood up off the wall.

She did it in one movement and she went four paces to the end of the
bench and stood with her back to both of them, and Sazare, who had spent
two years and eleven months learning what she did with her hands, saw
her put them flat on the wood the way she had put them flat on the
table in a scrubbed room at half past five in the morning in the eighth
month.

"Katsuyu," she said. "When you are in it with him. What are you
*doing*."

"I am holding some of it."

"Say the rest."

"He cannot hold enough yet," said Katsuyu. "So I hold the rest. And as
he becomes able to hold more, I hold less, and at some point I shall be
holding none of it, and that will be the end of it and he will not
notice the evening it happens because there will be nothing to notice."

---

Nobody said anything for what he estimated afterwards at nine seconds.

Then Tsunade said, quietly, to the bench:

"That's senjutsu."

Sazare heard the word and did not know it.

That was the whole of his experience of the next four seconds and he
recorded it afterwards exactly as it had happened, because it was
important and because it did not reflect well on him: a word went past
in a cold yard, and he did not have it, and his first and only reaction
was that he wanted the definition.

"What's senjutsu?"

Tsunade turned round.

He had seen her face do the still thing perhaps six times in three
years. He had never once been able to read underneath it. What it did
now was not that — it moved, for about half a second, and he could not
name what across it either, and then it stopped moving and became the
other thing.

"You don't know," she said.

"No."

"You have been doing it since the seventeenth of the eighth month and
you don't know what it is."

"I don't know what it's called," said Sazare. "I asked. On the last
evening of the ninth month I asked Katsuyu what we were doing and
whether it had a name and she couldn't understand the question, and
then she said *it is how you hold it*, and told me slugs keep no
scrolls and never have and there is nothing to write down. I wrote that
on the back of a requisition form and I've got it in my coat."

He got it out and held it up, because that was what he did.

Tsunade looked at the requisition form.

"Of course you have," she said.

---

Then it came, and it came placed exactly where she could put it.

"You should have told me."

"I—"

"Not the wood. The wood you did correctly and I want that said before
anything else, because you sent me a slip with an operator section in
it and named the fifteenth of the eighth month and asked me to be
present and offered to wait, and there is not one thing wrong with any
of that." She had her arms folded. "The *training*, Sazare. Eight weeks.
Twenty evenings. A creature that belongs to my family teaching a
subordinate of mine a thing that has three living practitioners in the
world, in a quartermaster's yard, at eleven at night, for eight weeks —
and I found out about it thirty seconds ago because I asked you to hold
a sphere."

"Yes."

"Don't just agree with me."

"I'm not agreeing to close it," said Sazare. "I'm agreeing because
you're right. That is a command matter and it should have come to you
in the third week and it didn't, and there is no version where that is
somebody else's fault."

"Then why didn't it?"

---

And he said it, because it was true, and it took about nine seconds for
the whole of the yard to change shape around it.

"Because I thought she told you."

Tsunade went absolutely still.

"I assumed the channel existed," said Sazare. "She's your summon. It's
your contract. It's been your family's for a hundred years and the
arrangement is formal and it is written down somewhere in that tower,
and I assumed — without ever once auditing it, which is what I do, it
is the only thing I am reliably good at — that information runs along
it. That she goes back and it goes in a report. That you knew what she
had been doing on any given evening because *of course* you did."

"...Ruri."

"There was never a moment where I decided to conceal it. I have gone
over the eight weeks four times this evening while you were reading the
protocol and there is not one. It never came up, because in my head it
had already been said."

He stopped.

"Katsuyu," he said. "Do you report to her?"

---

"No," said Katsuyu.

She said it in exactly the same mild voice she said everything in.

"No — forgive me, that is too short and I can hear that it is too
short. I do not report to anybody. I am summoned, and I am instructed,
and I do the thing, and I am released, and there has never been any
part of that in which I go back and say what happened. It would not
occur to me. There is no shape in which it would go."

"Never," said Tsunade.

"Not once, Tsunade-sama. In forty-one years."

"You've never—"

"You have never asked me to," said Katsuyu, without any reproach in it
at all, which was the thing that made it unsurvivable. "I would have. I
would have done it from the first year and I would have done it every
time and I would not have found it a burden. It simply was not part of
it, and neither of us ever said so, and so it has never happened and I
have never thought about it until this minute."

---

Tsunade did not say anything for a long time.

Sazare sat on the yard stone and did not move and did not fill it.

He had it by then. He had all of it, or enough of it, and he had got
there in about the last four seconds and he was going to have to live
with the fact that he had got there by watching her.

*One. Nobody asked her. That is real and it is the one she has said out
loud and it is a command matter and she is entitled to every part of
it.*

*Two. It is her summon. Katsuyu has a life now — eleven evenings of
nineteen, and a bench, and a stool Kanzaki puts out — and it is not
with her.*

*Three. And I am not going to write the third one down tonight because
Katsuyu said it to me in the ninth month in this yard and it was not
said to be repeated.*

*Four.*

*She has had the same access for forty-one years.*

*Four hundred summonings. A creature that would have answered any
question she asked, standing next to her, in a hundred rooms, since
before I was born. And she never asked one, and I asked one at seven in
the morning between a delegation and a border argument because I did
not know there was a reason not to, and I have eight weeks of it and
she has none.*

*She is standing four feet away working that out for the second time in
two months and she is doing it in front of me.*

*Do not say it.*

*There is nothing in the world I could say about it that is not me
telling her a thing she has already got. She said please in a scrubbed
room in the eighth month and I left it standing and it was the hardest
thing I did that year, and this is the same and it is harder and it is
the same answer.*

He said nothing.

Tsunade looked at him, and he understood — from about four inches of
her jaw and the fact that she looked away first, which she did not do —
that she had watched him arrive at it, and had watched him decide, and
knew exactly which one he had not said.

---

"Right," she said, eventually.

She came back and sat down on the low wall again.

"I've spent about four minutes," she said, "wanting to forbid it, and
I'm going to say the whole of the reasoning out loud because you're
going to need it and because if I don't say it now I'll say it worse in
a fortnight."

"All right."

"I can't forbid it, and the reason isn't that you'd disobey me. You
wouldn't — you'd hate it and you'd write four pages about it and you'd
comply, because that is what you did with the paper trap and the study
and the letter and every other time I have put a hand on your work."

She looked at the three pieces of stem on the felt.

"I can't forbid it because it's the *safety measure*," she said.

"Yes."

"You filled up in the eighth month because you could not hold what came
in. She is teaching you to hold it. If I stop that tonight you are a
man who can produce Mokuton — which you can, I have watched it three
times in twenty minutes — with no capacity and no observer, and you are
not going to stop producing it, because you never stop, and the next
time it happens there will be nobody in the operation and it will be
the arm."

"That's how I'd have costed it."

"I know," said Tsunade. "That's the worst part of it. I have been
sitting on that wall running your arithmetic and getting your answer,
and I set the term myself in a room in my own building on no sleep —
*you don't do it alone* — and this is what that term looks like when
somebody actually keeps it."

She rubbed her eyes.

"So I can't stop it," she said. "And I can't be in it, because I've
nothing to be in it with. And I can't teach it. There is nothing
whatsoever for me to do here except stand in a yard at ten at night and
watch."
