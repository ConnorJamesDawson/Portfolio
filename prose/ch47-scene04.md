# Chapter 47, Scene 4 — A Man Called Tenzō

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, a room over a closed tea house. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,858 words.*

---

Word came on the Wednesday and it was four words long and in Kakashi's
hand, which Sazare had never seen and which turned out to be small,
upright and entirely legible, and not at all what a man who was late by
forty minutes as a unit of measurement ought to have.

*Thursday. Two. Directions over.*

The directions took him to a tea house on the north side that had been
shut since before the invasion, with the shutters up and the mats
gone, and a stair at the side that went up the outside of the building
to a room over the shop.

The room had a table, four cushions and a window with the paper
replaced in one panel and not the other three. There was nothing else
in it at all — no shelf, no brazier, no hook — and the floor by the
window had a patch worn paler in a rough oval about a yard across.

*Somebody stands in that window,* thought Sazare, coming up the stairs,
*and has done for years, and the room is kept empty on purpose.*

*He has used this before.*

---

The man at the table was somewhere around thirty and Sazare could not
have said within five years.

He had the standard jōnin uniform and it fitted him properly, which was
rarer than it should have been. Dark hair, short. A face that was
entirely pleasant and gave nothing whatsoever — not blank, which is a
thing people notice, but *mild*, which they do not. He had made tea and
had put a cup in front of the empty cushion before Sazare arrived,
which meant he had been there some time.

Sazare sat down and did what he did, and got almost nothing, and the
almost-nothing was the finding.

*Uniform: issue. Boots: issue, current pattern, worn evenly, no
preference in the heel. Belt: issue. Pouch: issue, and the flap seat is
even, which means he does not favour a side, which means either he has
trained it out or he has never had long enough with one pouch to wear
it in.*

*Wrist wrap: issue. Undershirt at the collar: issue.*

*Cup he is drinking out of: the tea house's.*

He went round it twice, and then a third time, more carefully, the way
he would go round a casting that had rung wrong.

*There is nothing on this man that he chose.*

*I have read eleven hundred people. Every one of them has four or five
objects on them that they picked — a strap replaced in the wrong
colour, a knife they like, a coat kept eleven years past its life, a
button off a different garment because it was to hand and they didn't
mind. That is where people are. The issue kit tells you the
institution; the four wrong objects tell you the man.*

*He has none.*

*That is not an absence. That is a policy, and it has been in force for
a very long time, and I have been sitting here ninety seconds and it
is the loudest thing in this room.*

Then he found the button.

---

It was the third one down on the left of the jacket and it was wood.

The other five were the issue horn, dark, stamped, identical. The third
one down was a disc of pale close-grained something, hand-cut, about a
sixteenth proud of the others because whoever made it had not thinned
it enough, with four holes drilled slightly off square and a chamfer
worked round the edge with a knife in about nine passes.

Sazare looked at it for slightly too long.

"You've found the button," said the man.

"Sorry."

"Don't be, Kakashi-senpai warned me." He did not smile exactly. "He
said you'd have the room done inside two minutes and me inside four,
and I've been counting, and you took a minute and fifty on the room."

"I got nothing on you."

"I know." He turned the cup. "That's the part he said you'd find
interesting."

"You made the button."

"I made the button."

"With a knife," said Sazare. "Nine or ten passes on the chamfer, drill
by hand, and you didn't thin the back enough so it sits proud and it'll
catch on things and you've left it like that for — " he looked at the
wear on the thread — "two years."

"About two years."

"You could have grown it."

The man looked at him for a moment.

"Yes," he said. "I could have grown it. It would have taken about a
second and it would have been better."

He did not explain, and Sazare, who had come up a set of outside stairs
with three conditions in his coat, did not ask.

---

"My name's Tenzō," he said. "That's all of it you get and it isn't
rudeness, it's the arrangement, and Kakashi-senpai will have told you
the same. He said you had a materials question and it would take an
afternoon and the Hokage knows. He also said — and he was very careful
to say it twice — that he'd told you I'd almost certainly say no."

"He did."

"Why do you think I didn't?"

Sazare thought about it properly, because it was plainly a real
question.

"I don't know," he said. "I've got four guesses and I don't like any of
them enough to say them out loud."

"That's the right answer." Tenzō put the cup down. "It's because he
built me a door. He came in and gave me an exit before he gave me the
question, and he has done that for me for about eighteen years, and I
have used it perhaps forty times, and this time I didn't want it." He
shrugged very slightly. "Ask your question."

---

Sazare put both hands flat on the table.

"Where does the wood come from," he said.

Tenzō blinked.

"From—"

"Not from you. I'm not asking you where *you* got it and I'm not going
to. I'm asking a materials question and I'll say the whole of it so you
can see the shape." He kept it flat and level, the camber register,
because it was the only delivery he had ever found that let a large
thing be received as an ordinary one. "When you make a tree, there's a
tree, and it weighs something. Say a young beech, four inches at the
butt — that's a hundred kilos of timber and about sixty of water, and
it exists, and a minute earlier it didn't."

"Yes."

"Where was it a minute earlier?"

Tenzō did not answer.

He sat with the cup between his hands and looked at the paper panel
that had been replaced, and Sazare watched a man who had been asked
four hundred questions arrive at one he had not been asked, and it took
about eleven seconds and none of them were comfortable.

"I don't know," he said.

"Have you ever weighed anything?"

"No."

"Have you ever lost weight doing it? A hard day — an engagement, a
barrier, something large. Are you lighter afterwards?"

"I'm *emptied*," said Tenzō. "I've never been on a scale about it. I've
never — " He stopped, and started again, and his voice had changed by
a degree that Sazare would not have caught six months earlier. "I have
been doing this since I was six years old and nobody has ever asked me
that, and I have never asked me that, and I have just this second
understood that I have been making matter out of nothing for
twenty-five years and I have thought about it *approximately never*."

"Nobody asks."

"Nobody asks."

"They write *bloodline* in the box," said Sazare, "and the box is
where an answer is supposed to go, and then everybody stops. The
Hokage told me last week she has three of the four in her family tree
and has never once wondered where the wood comes from, and she said it
in a yard at two in the morning and did not notice she'd said it."

---

"Then let me tell you what it's like," said Tenzō, "because that's the
part I do have, and I've never had anybody to give it to."

"Please."

"It isn't making." He turned his hands over on the table. "I know
people think it's making. It isn't like moulding a thing or forcing a
thing. It's — " He stopped, plainly hunting, and Sazare did not help
him, which took some doing. "It's nearer to asking. And being allowed.
And the amount I'm allowed isn't the same every time and I have never
worked out why, and I stopped trying when I was about twenty because
there was nobody to ask and it made no difference to the work."

Sazare's hands did not move.

"Say more about the amount."

"It varies. Not with how tired I am — that's separate, I know what
tired feels like. Some days it comes up like a full bucket and some
days I'm dragging it, and it's the same me and the same technique."

"Does it vary with *where*."

Tenzō looked up.

"...Yes," he said slowly. "Yes, it does. Old forest is easy. The deep
part of the Land of Fire, up past the river — it's like working
downhill, I can hold four times what I can hold in a training ground
and I've always put that down to being comfortable."

"And the worst?"

"Cities." He said it without hesitation, which told Sazare it had been
sitting there for years without a label. "Burnt ground. There was a
place in the third year of the war — I wasn't there for the war, I mean
a place that had been in it — and it was like pulling a cart. And I
have said that out loud twice in my life and both times to people who
said *well, no trees, is there*, and I let it go, because that
explanation is available and it fits and I had nothing better."

"It doesn't fit," said Sazare. "You're not using the trees. If you were
using the trees the trees would weigh less."

"...No. They wouldn't, would they."

---

Sazare got up and walked to the window, which he did not normally do,
and stood in the worn oval on the floor without noticing what he was
standing in.

*Yard aggregate, twenty-two per cent reject. Granite, thirty-one. Site
rubble off the invasion streets, forty-four. Own prior work, four.*

*Old forest: easy. Burnt ground: dragging.*

*Different substance. Different technique. Different man, who has never
read a line of my work and cannot read a line of my work and does not
know I have a reject table.*

*Same shape.*

He stood there for some time.

"Are you all right?" said Tenzō.

"No," said Sazare. "Can I ask you for the second thing? It wasn't in
what Kakashi put to you and you can say no and I'll go home with what
I've got, which is more than I came for."

"Ask."

"Make me something small," said Sazare, "and let me weigh it. And then
let me weigh you."
