# Chapter 73, Scene 7 — The Uncounted Hours

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, Tsunade's house. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~940 words.*

---

The second attempt was eleven minutes later and lasted long
enough for him to learn her.

That was the entry he would never write, and the reason he
would never write it was not modesty, it was that the material
would not survive the format — that a ledger is a device for
holding what can be held, and the hours between the lamp
burning and the lamp going out contained nothing that could be
held in a column.

He learned the sound she made when he got something right, and
that it was different from the sound she made when he got
something right *twice*. He learned that fifty-three years of
the finest medical hands alive were, in the dark, in a house
with the door locked, the most extraordinary instruments he
had ever been under. He learned that she said his name — his
given name, the stone-name, the one four people in the world
had ever used — in a register he had not known existed, and
that hearing it did something to him that no work in his life
had ever done.

He learned that he was allowed to be slow. That was the great
finding of the second hour, and it arrived the way all his
findings arrived, sideways, off an offhand remark: she told
him, laughing, breathless, to stop *surveying* — and he said
that he was not surveying, he was *learning*, and there was a
difference, and she went very quiet and said, "Learn, then,"
and after that he took his time, because taking his time was
the one skill he had brought into the room, eleven years of a
bench and a loupe and a hand that did not hurry, and it
turned out — this was the thing he would carry for the rest
of his life — it turned out that the entire craft transferred.

*Unhurried is the whole of the grammar.*

*She taught me that in a bathtub in January and I have spent
two months applying it to conversation.*

Somewhere in the third hour she cried, briefly, and told him
it was nothing, and then told him the truth, which was that
nobody had touched her in nine years and she had made her
peace with the arithmetic of that and the arithmetic had just
been publicly and comprehensively falsified, and he held her
and said nothing at all, because there was nothing to say
that was better than not saying it.

Somewhere in the fourth hour she taught him a thing that he
would think about at inopportune moments for approximately
the next four decades.

And somewhere near the end of it, with the lamp guttering and
the window going grey at its lower edge, she fell asleep on
his chest with her hair everywhere and one hand closed in the
fabric of nothing at all, because there was no shirt to grip,
and after a while her fingers found his ribs instead and
settled there.

He lay in the dark of a house that had been kept in case, and
did not sleep, and did the accounting that could be done.

*Item: the ledger gets nothing.*

*That is not sentiment. It is method. I have written down
every important hour of my life since I was fifteen years
old, because writing a thing down is how I hold it — and I
have just discovered the first material that the instrument
degrades. Anything I write about tonight will be smaller than
tonight. The count, the columns, the findings — the entire
apparatus is a way of surviving things at a distance, and
there is no distance in this room, and I do not want any.*

*So: no entry. The first night of my life that goes
unrecorded, on purpose, by a man who records everything.*

*Some things you do not keep. Some things you simply get to
have been in the room for. Katsuyu-sama told me that about a
market. She was talking about something else and she knew
exactly what she was talking about.*

*Item: I am eighteen years old and I have been in this
village for three years and two hundred and eleven days, and
every single thing in my life is a thing I built or was
handed by somebody who chose to hand it to me, and tonight
the woman who signed the order that let me stay wrote three
words on a piece of paper in a locked house without asking
me first and they were the same three words as mine.*

*Item: she is asleep. Her hand is on my ribs. Her hair is
in my mouth and I am not moving.*

*Item, last, and this is the one that will not fit in any
column and I am going to attempt it anyway, once, tonight,
and then never again:*

*I did not know it was possible to be this happy. Not as a
figure of speech. I had no data. There was no row in any
table I have ever kept that this belongs in, and I have
kept a table for everything since I was seven years old,
and if my mother could see this room she would put down the
wire and look at her for a long time and then go back to
work and say nothing at all, and I would know exactly what
she meant by it.*

*That is all.*

*The lamp is nearly out. Let it go out.*

He let it go out, and the grey came up slowly in the window
of a house in Konoha with two people in it, and somewhere
around six he slept.
