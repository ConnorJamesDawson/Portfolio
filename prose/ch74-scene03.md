# Chapter 74, Scene 3 — Through and Out

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, the second floor. Tsunade,
age fifty-three. POV: Tsunade. ~2,230 words.*

---

The noon tray came up at twelve exactly, morning blend, no items, per
the law — and she had been standing at the window since ten to,
which she noted about herself the way she noted a raised white cell
count: not alarming on its own; alarming in combination.

*Presentation,* she thought, watching the lane. *Female,
fifty-three. Standing at a window. Again. Chart it honestly or
don't chart at all.*

*Mitigating: it is not the pining window. The pining window faced
the yard and had a lamp in it. This is the north window, and I am
at it because a man walked out of this building at half five this
morning knowing that his teacher had been sharpening something for
nine weeks and was going to use it today, and he kissed me at the
door like a man going to work, and I have been useless since.*

*Aggravating: I signed the order. Twice. Once as the office and
once as the other thing.*

He came up the lane at four minutes past.

She had thirty years of reading gait off casualty lines and she had
three years of reading his in particular, and she had the whole of
it before he reached the forecourt: the walk was intact. That was
the first thing and it mattered. He was not limping and he was not
carrying anything wrong. Whatever had happened out there had not
happened to his body.

The second thing was that he had his coat buttoned to the throat, in
the third month, in mild weather, which he did when he was cold in
some way that had nothing to do with air.

*Right,* she thought, and went to the table. *So it was today.*

---

He came in and put the case down and sat, and said, "Good
afternoon," in the flat working voice, and picked up the chopsticks,
and ate.

The law held. That was the extraordinary thing and she made herself
sit inside it for a full four minutes and watch him do it. No items
at the tray. Nothing kept, nothing about a thing. He had written
that clause himself in an incident report on a February evening,
brought it to her in a folder as exhibit one, and he was going to
sit here and honour it with his hands shaking on a bowl rather than
spend her twenty minutes on the worst morning of his year.

*He'd do it too. He'd get all the way to the stairs.*

She watched him get about a third of the way down the bowl and stop
being able to, and put the chopsticks across the rim, correctly,
the way he did everything, and sit with his hands in his lap.

"Eat two more mouthfuls," said Tsunade.

"I'm—"

"Two. It's not a request from your — it's not a request. You've had
a hundred and ten grams of rice and no protein since five o'clock
and you have got a face on you I have seen on men after theatre.
Two mouthfuls and then I'm going to do something and you're going
to let me."

He ate two mouthfuls.

She set her own cup down.

"Noon law," she said, in the flat administrative voice, "amended, by
me, unilaterally, at twelve past twelve on the sixteenth of the
third month. Amendment reads: *the through-and-out clause is
suspended for the balance of this hour at the model's discretion,
once, without precedent, and the fabricator may not cite it.*
Minuted, dated, signed by the person who co-wrote the original and
therefore entitled to break it once and take the consequences."

She watched him look up.

"You built me a law so that the traffic couldn't get in here," she
said. "It was a good law and I have been grateful for it every day
for a month and I am telling you that it was written to keep the
*board* out. Not you. You are not traffic in this room. Now put it
down."

And she watched — this was the part she would keep — she watched
the whole architecture of him look for the clause that let him, and
fail to find one, and take the amendment instead, because a man who
cannot take an unspecified thing had just been handed a
specification.

"He struck the creed," said Sazare.

---

She got it in order, because he could not deliver anything except in
order, and she took it the way she took a casualty report, which was
without moving.

The audit of the sentence. The two ages of it. *It is a governor and
I did not know I had written one.* The trigger condition. Her own
hands, flat on the table, at *if I am never worth anything, nobody
comes.*

The argument he had made about his mother — he gave her that
verbatim, and the fact that he had fought properly was the only
thing in the entire account that made her want to stand up and go
and find Jiraiya and shake his hand.

The answer he had been given. *Value kills the unguarded.*

And then Fukasaku.

"On his mountain," said Sazare, "nobody is permitted near the oil
until they have stood in front of it and said what they intend to be
for. Out loud. Witnessed. At nine years old, and again whenever it
changes, on a stated day." He was looking at the table. "He says an
unsettled account is a grip, and a mind that grips is a place where
the world stops moving through and starts collecting.

"He then observed that I went into it at seventeen with no rite, no
witness, and a creed in the front of four books stating that I
intended to be nothing in particular, and that I came out of it with
a hand made of stone, and that he was not going to explain the
connection to me because I had read the physics."

Tsunade did not say anything for a moment.

*Six hours,* said the part of her that never stopped charting, in a
voice she rarely allowed out. *Fifteenth of the eighth. Six hours on
a thing I could not name, at a bench, with his hand going away under
my hands a centimetre at a time, and I got it back and I never once
found out why it happened.*

*And a toad has just told him it happened because a boy had nothing
to spend it on.*

"Say the rest," she said. "There's a Friday in this somewhere.
There's always a Friday."

"The replacement is due Friday. Six sharp, in my own hand, in all
four books, and I am to bring book one so that he can read it out of
the same object as the sentence it replaces." He turned his cup a
quarter, the way he did. "And he refused to let me write it this
morning. He said I would produce something beautiful inside twenty
minutes and it would be a slogan. He is correct. I had four drafts
by the bottom of the lane."

"Of course you did."

"I have thrown them away."

"Of course you have," said Tsunade, and heard her own voice do
something, and let it. "So for four days you're carrying four books
with a hole in the front of them."

"Yes."

"And a promise you broke this morning at eleven minutes past seven
that nobody living can release you from."

"...Yes."

---

*And here it is,* she thought. *The bill. Presented on a Monday over
a bowl of rice, four months after I signed for it.*

She had wanted this. That was the thing to be honest about and she
was going to be honest about it in her own head at her own table if
nowhere else. She had sat in this tower in the eleventh month and
told Jiraiya, out loud, over a cup, that the fifth thing — the one
she would give him if she could only give him one — was *he doesn't
want to be strong*. She had said it as a marvel and a warning in the
same breath. She had known even then which way it had to end,
because she had buried enough marvellous unguarded people to have a
policy about it, and the policy was: not this one.

So she had signed an order that put the best instrument in the
village into a field with the only man alive who could take the
brakes off him without ruining the mechanism, and the man had gone
and done exactly that, competently, on schedule, and this — a boy
with his coat buttoned to the throat who could not finish a bowl —
was the invoice, and it was not overpriced, and she was going to
pay it without one word of complaint, and she was never going to
tell either of them how much of it she had wanted.

*Do not fix it,* she wrote, in the register, later, at one in the
morning, in the hand she used for the true ones. *Do not soften it,
do not go down to that field, do not send Shizune with a memo about
the pace. He is doing the hardest four days of his interior life and
the only thing that would ruin it is me arriving with a bandage.*

*What you get to do is feed him. That's the whole permitted
intervention. Feed him and be here on Friday.*

---

"I'm going to ask you something," said Sazare, "and it is not
traffic, and you can decline it."

"Ask."

"What are you for?"

She had been reaching for the pot. Her hand carried on and did the
reaching, which was fifty years of practice, and the rest of her
stopped entirely.

*Oh,* she thought. *Of course. Of course that's the next thing. He
has been told to write a specification for himself by Friday and he
has gone straight to the one person whose answer he wants, because
that is what he does with every problem, and he does not have the
first idea what he has just picked up.*

Because she had the answer. She had written it out in ink four days
ago in a register nobody would ever read, at the end of an audit
about a boy in a gambling town — *the fifth face believes there will
be a sixth, and knows its name, and is keeping the estate for him* —
and it was, without any competition at all, the single most
load-bearing sentence in her life, and she had told it to nobody:
not Shizune, not Jiraiya, not the man sitting at this table who told
her everything.

*Not today. Not on a day when he'd take it and use it as a model and
write me into the front of four books by Friday.*

*It's mine for a while longer. He'll get it. He gets everything
eventually — I have never once managed to keep anything from that
man and I have stopped trying to on principle. But he gets it on a
day I choose, and today he needs an answer he can actually use, and
mine would flatten him.*

"I'll give you the one I've got at fifty-three," she said, and
poured, "and it's smaller than you want and it's true, so take it as
data and not as a template.

"When I came back to this village I was for one thing: making sure
this place stopped eating the people I could see. That's it. That's
the whole of it. Not the Land of Fire, not the office, not history —
the hospital roster, the genin lists, the eleven idiots on my floor.
It's not a creed, it's a *scope*. And the reason it works is that it
fits in a sentence and I can check it every single day against
things that actually happened."

He was writing. She let him. He would have written it on his own
forearm if she had taken the notebook off him.

"So that's the correction I'd make to your old man," said Tsunade,
"with respect to four hundred years of toads. Don't write down what
you *are*. You'll get it wrong; everybody does; it's a vanity
document by the second sentence. Write down what you're *for* — and
make it something you can be caught failing at."

The pen stopped.

"...That is a specification," said Sazare.

"That's what I said."

"No. I mean—" and he looked up, and something had come back into
his face that had not been there since he came through the door, and
it was the specific ugly light of a man who has been handed a
constraint that his hands know what to do with. "*Something you can
be caught failing at.* That is an acceptance test. He told me to
write him a specification and not a creed and I have spent four
hours failing to understand the difference and you have just given
it to me over a tray in one clause."

"Twelve past twelve," said Tsunade, "to twenty-nine minutes past.
Seventeen minutes, one amendment, one clause, and I want it in the
minutes that the law was broken by the person it was written for."
She put his chopsticks back in his hand. "Now finish the bowl,
fabricator. You've got a yard at two."
