# Chapter 32, Scene 1 — The Top Rung

*Act 4, part C. Konoha, training ground eleven. Sazare Ruri, age
seventeen. POV: Sazare. ~1,339 words.*

---

Kakashi stopped the observation training on a morning cold enough that
the puddles in the ruts had ice on them, and did it the way he did
everything, which was without any warning that a thing was about to
end.

"That's the last one," he said.

Sazare had just finished eleven minutes on a man mending the fence at
the far side of the ground — boots, gait, a knife carried in the boot
rather than a belt sheath, which meant he had spent time somewhere it
was unwise to be seen with one, and a wedding ring worn on a cord round
the neck instead of on the hand, which meant either he worked with
his hands in a way that would ruin it or he had stopped being able to
get it on, and Sazare had worked out from the state of his knuckles
that it was the second.

"That was rung four."

"That was rung four and it was good. That's not why I'm stopping."

The bench was cold. Kakashi had not sat down on it, which was itself
information; he had been sitting on that bench for eleven weeks.

"Two months ago you couldn't have told me a thing about a person that
wasn't hanging off them," he said. "Now you can read a stranger to
intention in about nine minutes and to something past it if you're not
paying attention to yourself. That's faster than it should have gone
and I want to say plainly that it isn't because I'm a good teacher.
It's because you were already three quarters of the way up and nobody
had put a name on the last bit."

"Then what's wrong with it?"

"Nothing's wrong with it." Kakashi put his hands in his pockets. "It's
finished. There isn't a rung six. The last one isn't a thing you look
at."

"You said rung five was what they're feeling."

"I did, and I was wrong, or at least I was lying by omission, which is
the same thing with more effort." He looked out across the ground.
"Rung five is what you *do* about it. That's the whole of it. And I
can't teach you that, and I've spent about a fortnight working out how
to say so."

---

Sazare waited, because he had learned over eleven weeks that Kakashi's
silences were load-bearing and interrupting them cost you the sentence
underneath.

"The evaluation office bench," Kakashi said. "Fourth of four. Boy of
thirteen with a new forehead protector and his mother."

"How do you know about that?"

"Because you wrote it in your notebook and Toriyama's clerk has the
same handwriting as my last three mission reports and this village
leaks like a colander." He said it entirely without apology. "You got
her. Not the boy — the boy was rung two, anybody could have done the
boy. You got *her*, right the way up, and you did it without going
through the steps, and then you wrote down that it felt like reading
somebody's post and you looked away."

"It did feel like that."

"I know. That's the correct instinct and it's why you're safe to teach
this to." Kakashi turned round. "Here's the part I've been chewing on.
A woman sat four feet from you being frightened for her son and
deciding he wasn't going to find out, and you saw the whole of it,
clearly, in about six seconds. And you did nothing whatsoever with it."

"What was I supposed to do?"

"Anything. Say the office takes eleven minutes. Say the man in the
cardigan is harmless. Say the deferrals list is normal and nobody's
ever had it held against them." Kakashi shrugged, and it was not a
casual shrug. "You said all of that to the boy, incidentally. Twenty
minutes later, on your way out. You gave the boy the whole thing and
you gave her nothing, and she was the one who needed it, and you knew
that at the time."

Sazare stood in the cold with no answer at all.

"That's the top rung," Kakashi said. "Not seeing it. Doing something
about it in the four seconds where it's still any use. And there is
now a gap between what you can see and what you'll say that did not
exist three months ago, because three months ago you couldn't see it
either, and I have made that gap by teaching you, and I don't
entirely feel good about it."

---

"Then teach me the rest of it."

"No."

"Why not?"

Kakashi was quiet for long enough that Sazare, three months into
learning how to read a person, read him, and immediately wished he
hadn't.

*Rung two: he hasn't moved. He's stood in one place for the whole
conversation and he sat down for every previous one.*

*Rung three: he's not comfortable and he's decided to have the
conversation anyway.*

*Rung four: he's going to tell me something true about himself and
he's already regretting it.*

"Because I'm not any good at it," Kakashi said.

"That isn't—"

"I'd like you to let me finish, because I've had a fortnight to get
this into four sentences and I'd rather not have to build it again."
He looked at the fence, not at Sazare. "I can see what a person's
feeling. I've been able to since I was about nine and it's the single
most useful thing I have, professionally, and it is worth roughly
nothing to anybody I've ever cared about, because seeing it and saying
something about it are different skills and I only ever bothered to
learn the one that keeps people alive."

"You do say things."

"I say accurate things, at the right moment, to people who need
information. That's not the same and you'd know it's not the same if
you'd ever been on the receiving end of the other thing." He put his
hands back in his pockets. "There are three people I should have said
something to. Not one of them is alive, and I had years, and I read all
of it correctly at the time and said nothing, every time, because there
was always going to be a better moment and the work was more urgent
and I am extremely good at deciding that the work is more urgent."

The ice in the ruts had begun to go grey at the edges where the sun was
reaching it.

"So no," Kakashi said. "I'm not teaching you the last rung. I'd teach
it to you wrong, and you'd learn it perfectly, because you learn
everything I give you perfectly, and in thirty years you'd be standing
in a field telling some poor sod exactly this."

---

He gave Sazare a task instead, which was the only thing he had ever
given him that was not a correction.

"Say one true thing to one person," he said. "Out loud. Not about their
equipment, not about their technique, not about anything they've made
or mended or done, and not about anything that's wrong with it. A thing
you noticed, that they'd be glad to hear, and that costs you something
to say."

"Costs me what?"

"That's the whole exercise. Work it out." Kakashi started walking.
"And Ruri — don't come and tell me when you've done it. That's not what
it's for. If you tell me, you've done it for me and not for them, and
you'll have got it exactly wrong in a way that'll take you another
year to see."

"How will you know if I've done it?"

"I won't," said Kakashi, over his shoulder, without stopping. "That's
the first honest transaction you and I have ever had. Enjoy it."

He went out through the gate at the far end and did not come back the
following Tuesday, or the Tuesday after that, and Sazare stood in
training ground eleven at nine o'clock on both of them before he
accepted that the arrangement had ended and that nobody was going to
send him a form about it.
