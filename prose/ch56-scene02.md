# Chapter 56, Scene 2 — The Fourth Hour

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1520 words.*

---

He was awake at three and he knew what he was going to do at about ten
past.

He lay on the floor of the room over the persimmon tree with his hands
behind his head and did not pretend otherwise for a single minute,
because pretending was not available to him and never had been.

*I am going to go up at the fourth hour.*

*And I am going to have to be honest with myself about what that is,
because there is going to be nobody else in the building to be honest
with.*

*I told her I knew. Twelfth of the eleventh — no. Eleventh. I stood in
that office and said* you come in at the fourth hour, four days in
five, and it's always the worst paper *and she did not say anything at
all, which at the time I filed as her not having a response and which I
now think was something else.*

*So if I walk into that building at the fourth hour tomorrow, she will
know exactly what I have done. There is no version where she thinks it
is a coincidence, because I removed that version myself, on purpose,
eleven days ago, for a different reason.*

*It is the first thing I have ever done to her deliberately with no
justification underneath it.*

*Kanzaki does not need me. Shizune's box does not need me for another
five hours. There is no article and no patient and no docket. There is
nothing on the table.*

*I am going because she will be there.*

He got up at ten to four and put his coat on.

---

The building at that hour was a different building.

He had known that in theory — he had built a whole item out of ink
drying at the edge of a sheet — and he had never stood in it, and it
was cold and entirely silent and there was one lamp on the second-floor
landing and the smell of a place where nobody had opened a door yet.

The door of the office was open about four inches.

He stood on the landing for a moment and did the only sensible thing,
which was to knock.

"It's open," said Tsunade.

---

She did not look up.

She was at the desk with the lamp pulled over and about nine inches of
paper on her left and four inches on her right, and she had her sleeves
pushed back and her hair not up, and the top sheet in front of her had
a family name on it.

He stood in the doorway.

"Sit down or go away, Ruri, don't hover."

He sat down.

She went on reading.

That was the whole of it for about eleven minutes: a woman reading a
compensation file at four in the morning with a pen she was not using,
and an eighteen-year-old sitting four feet away in a chair with his
hands on his knees, and the lamp, and the building being empty
underneath them.

He looked at her the entire time.

He was aware of doing it. He was aware of it as a continuous condition
rather than as an event, and he made two separate decisions to stop and
carried out neither of them, and at one point he got as far as looking
at the corner of the desk instead and was back inside about four
seconds without having noticed the transition.

*This is not sustainable.*

*I am going to have to find some way of being in a room.*

---

She turned the sheet over and put it on the right-hand pile and picked
up the next one, and then, without any change in her voice at all, she
said:

"You told me you knew about this."

"I did."

"And here you are."

"Yes."

"At the fourth hour."

"Yes."

She still had not looked up.

"Are you going to tell me why?"

"No," said Sazare.

Her pen stopped.

It was about a quarter of a second and then it went on, and she wrote
something in the margin of a compensation file at four twenty in the
morning, and he had four hundred pages on the way she held a pen and
nothing on that quarter of a second at all.

"Good," she said.

---

He got another nineteen minutes.

He spent them doing something he had never done in his life, which was
nothing whatsoever. He did not have a book and he did not have the
notebook, deliberately, because he had left it on the low table and had
stood looking at it for four seconds before he went out of the door;
and he sat in a chair in a cold room and listened to a pen.

At about twenty to five she pushed a thing across the desk at him.

He looked at it.

It was a docket. It was a Corps docket, four pages, with the
quartermaster's flimsy still pinned to the back of it, and it was not
his and had nothing to do with him and had come up from the annexe with
a request for a determination that only she could give.

"Tell me what's wrong with it," said Tsunade.

---

He read it.

It took him about two minutes and it was extremely easy and he did not
say so.

"The unit rate's right and the quantity's right and the total's right,"
he said. "The specification's wrong."

"Where?"

"Third page, clause four. They've asked for four hundred of a
splint-grade alloy at a hardness that doesn't exist in that alloy —
somebody's copied a figure across from a different table, probably from
the tool schedule, because the number they've written is the tool
number and it's out by a factor that's exactly the difference between
the two scales.

"So nobody can supply it. Kanzaki will bounce it, and the annexe will
resubmit with the same figure because the figure came off a printed
sheet, and it'll go round three times, and in about the fourth month
somebody in a ward will not have four hundred splints."

"What do I do about it?"

"Don't determine it," said Sazare. "Send it back with the tool schedule
attached and a line saying *compare clause four to item nine*, and
don't say what's wrong with it, because if you tell them they'll fix
that one and if you show them they'll find the other eleven."

Tsunade took the docket back.

"There are eleven?"

"There are eleven in the first box I opened yesterday," he said. "It's
the same printed sheet."

---

She sat back for the first time since he had come in.

"Right," she said. "You're going to write that down. Not for me — for
Kanzaki, and you're going to put your name on it, and I'm going to send
it to the Corps board over the head of a man who voted against your
post."

"That'll annoy him."

"Yes."

"It'll annoy him more if it's right."

"Yes," said Tsunade, and there was something in that one, and it was
gone before he could get anything on it.

She put the docket on the right-hand pile.

Then she pulled the nine inches back in front of her and picked up the
next compensation file, and that was it, that was the end of it, and
she did not say another word to him until Shizune came up at the fifth
hour and stopped dead in the doorway with a folder in her hand.

---

He walked down the stairs at about six with the docket problem in his
head and the whole of the rest of it underneath.

*She did not send me away.*

*That is the finding and I am going to have to be careful with it,
because it is a negative and negatives are the easiest thing in the
world to overweigh, and I have four hundred pages on people who did.*

*She did not say anything about last night and she is not going to. She
did not say anything about the morning and she is not going to. She
asked me why I was there and I said no and she said good.*

*And then she gave me a docket.*

*Which is the largest thing she could have given me, and I do not think
she picked it. I think it was on the desk and she pushed it across
because it was there and because eleven minutes of silence had gone on
long enough for both of us.*

*It was her work. It was not my work and it was not a favour and it was
not a test. She had a thing she had to determine and she gave it to an
eighteen-year-old to read.*

*I have never had that off anybody in my life.*

He stopped on the second-floor landing.

*And she is going to do it again tomorrow, and she is never going to
say anything about it, and that is what this is now.*

*Fine.*

*I can work with that.*

*I would like it recorded that I would work with considerably less.*
