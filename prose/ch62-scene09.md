# Chapter 62, Scene 9 — Let's Train

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the north store and the tower. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2420 words.*

---

Naruto came into the store on the Monday afternoon without knocking,
because the double doors were open onto the yard and because it had
apparently not occurred to him that a door could be a question.

"This is *massive*."

Sazare put the nib down.

*He has walked in.*

*Nobody has walked into a room of mine since I was seven years old. Not
one person. Yagi came to that door on Friday and stopped in it and did
not come in until I said something, and Kanzaki does not come past the
yard gate, and Sakura has been on a stair with me fifty-one times and has
never once been in a room I was in on her own.*

*And this one has come through a double door with a bag of something and
is now walking the length of my racking looking at things.*

*He is the first.*

*And it has taken him three days and he did not ask and it has not
occurred to him that there was anything to ask.*

"Don't touch the second shelf," said Sazare.

"I'm not touching anything." Naruto touched the bench. "What's this
one?"

"That's a bench."

"No, this—"

"That's the reject bin, and it's the aggregate that failed the grading,
and if you put your hand in it you will get about four cuts and none of
them will be interesting."

Naruto put his hand in it.

---

"Right," he said, about eleven minutes later, sitting on the step of the
cart door with two of the four cuts wrapped in something Sazare had had
in a drawer since the eleventh month. "So I came about a thing."

"Go on."

"Train with me."

Sazare looked at him.

"Not like — I don't mean *teach* me, everyone's doing that, Ero-sennin's
doing it and Kakashi-sensei's doing it and Sakura-chan's doing it and she
isn't even supposed to be." He wound the cloth round again. "I mean
train. Properly. Go at it.

"Because I've been back two months and I've been going at it with Sakura
and with Kakashi-sensei and with about nine people at the grounds, and
they're all — it's the *same*, right? Everyone in this village does the
same six things. Everyone. There's about six ways to fight in
Konohagakure and they're all good and I've seen all of them and I've been
seeing them since I was nine.

"And you don't do any of them."

"I don't do any of them because I can't do any of them," said Sazare.

"*Yeah*," said Naruto, with enormous enthusiasm, "and I want to see
what that's like."

---

*Right.*

*Say the true thing before he builds anything on the wrong one, because
he is going to build fast and he is going to build a lot.*

"I've never been in a fight," said Sazare.

Naruto stopped winding the cloth.

"...What?"

"Not one. Ever. In my life."

"You're a—"

"I'm a sergeant in the Medical Corps and I have never been deployed, and
I have never been in a training match, and I have never been hit by
another human being on purpose except by the Hokage twice in the last
fortnight and neither of those was a fight."

"But you—" Naruto gestured at all of him. "You've got the — everyone
says you're—"

"Everybody says I am above ninety-five per cent of the roster," said
Sazare, "and that is probably true and it is about *capacity*, and
capacity is not fighting. The slugs teach holding. That is the entire
tradition and Katsuyu has told me so and I have got about forty pages of
it and there is not one line in any of it about doing anything to
anybody.

"I can hold more natural energy than almost anybody alive and I have got
three operations and none of them is fast and one of them takes four
months.

"You have spent two years in the field with one of the three. You have
been fighting since you were twelve. You have got about forty techniques
and I have got three and one of mine is *the gather*, which you can do
without at the moment because you have not been taught it and it will
not matter to you for another year.

"If we go at it properly you will put me on the floor inside about
eleven seconds, and I will have found out something extremely valuable
and you will have found out nothing at all."

---

Naruto thought about that for a bit, which he did with his whole face.

"Yeah, but that's the interesting part though," he said.

"Which part?"

"The eleven seconds!" He got up off the step. "Because it's not going to
be eleven seconds, is it. It's going to be — look, I've done this. I did
this with Neji and everyone said it'd be four seconds and it was not four
seconds, and the reason it wasn't is that the four-seconds guess was
about *technique* and the fight is never about technique.

"So either you go down in eleven seconds and everyone was right and you
learn something, or you don't, and then everybody in this village finds
out something about you that nobody knows including you.

"And you *want* to know. Don't tell me you don't want to know, you've
got a room with four hundred impossible things nailed to the wall."

"It's a roll of paper and it's on a shelf."

"Same thing."

---

*He is right.*

*He is right and it took him about ninety seconds and I have been walking
round it since the eleventh of the eleventh month.*

*I am going to be deployed. Not this year, possibly, and possibly this
month — the box is failing, everybody can see it, she said so in a
kitchen and Jiraiya said it on a windowsill and there are fifteen people
on that council who have been waiting for a return on a post since the
eighth to seven.*

*And when it happens I will go out of that gate having never once found
out what I am.*

*I have got a capacity figure and a review from four elders that says*
six seconds and then a man standing in the open holding a handle*, and I
have got a governor with eleven clauses, and I have got no idea
whatsoever what happens when somebody comes at me and does not stop.*

*That is not a gap in my training. That is a hole in the middle of the
only unexamined thing left about me, and I have been able to see it since
the eleventh, and the reason I have not looked at it is that looking at
it requires another person and I have never had one.*

*And a sixteen-year-old has walked into my room with a bag of something,
put his hand in a reject bin, and offered.*

"Yes," said Sazare.

"...Really? I had about four more—"

"You can have the four more. I'd like to hear them and I'm going to say
yes at the end of them anyway, so it depends how much you want to say
them."

Naruto said all four of them.

---

It got out.

He was not entirely sure how and he had a fairly good idea, and the idea
was that Naruto Uzumaki had told Sakura on the Monday evening and that
Sakura had asked Shizune whether the second training ground was free on
the twentieth, and that Shizune had asked the Hokage, and that the Hokage
had asked whether anybody was providing medical cover.

By Wednesday morning Jiraiya was sitting on the windowsill of the
Hokage's office eating a persimmon.

"Twentieth," he said. "Ground two. Half past nine."

"So I'm told."

"And I want it on the record that I have had *nothing* to do with any of
this and that I am simply here."

"You've got a bet on."

"I've got a bet on," Jiraiya agreed.

---

"Which way?" said Sazare.

"Ah," said Jiraiya. "Now."

He put the persimmon down.

"I'm on the boy," he said. "Two years in the field, forty techniques,
has been hit by professionals about four hundred times and got up, and is
the single most difficult person to put down that I have ever trained,
and I have trained the Fourth. That is not sentiment. That is the sensible
money and any man in this village would take it and about nine have."

"That seems correct."

"It is correct."

"Then—"

"She's on you," said Jiraiya.

Sazare turned round.

Tsunade did not look up from the desk.

---

"Say the whole of it," she said.

"You know the whole of it."

"He doesn't."

Jiraiya sat back on the sill.

"Kid," he said, "how much do you know about her gambling?"

"She's not good at it."

"She is not *good* at it."

"That's what everybody says and I have never had a figure. I have got
about eleven references in four years and every single one of them is a
remark and none of them is data, and I have assumed the whole of it is a
village story that has grown in the telling."

"It has not grown in the telling," said Jiraiya. "It has *shrunk* in the
telling, because the real version is not funny. That woman has been
betting for forty years and she loses. Not usually. Not on balance.

"She picks a thing and the thing does not come in, and it has been so
consistent for so long that there are people in three countries who
follow her about at festivals and take the other side, and they do it
professionally, and it works, and she knows they do it and has never once
stopped."

"That's not—"

"Don't," said Jiraiya. "Do not say *that is not a mechanism* to me at
half nine in the morning. I know it is not a mechanism. I have known it
is not a mechanism for forty years and I have watched it happen about
four hundred times and I would still not take a bet she was on.

"And she has just put money on you," he said, "in front of me, on
Tuesday, out loud, at a table with three other people at it.

"Which means every single person who hears about it is going to know two
things by Friday.

"One: the smart money is on the boy.

"Two: she could not do it."

---

Sazare stood in the middle of the office.

*Right.*

*He has just said that out loud in this room in front of her.*

*And she has not moved and she has not argued with it and she has not
looked up, and she has been reading the same sheet since I turned round,
and I have watched that woman not read a sheet about four times and this
is the fourth.*

*She could not bet against me in public.*

*She has forty years of a record that says her backing a thing is the
worst thing that can happen to it, and she knows it better than anybody
alive, and on Tuesday at a table with three other people at it she was
asked which way and she said me.*

*Which is the single most irrational thing she has ever done and she is
the most rational person I know.*

*And it is going to be all over this village by Friday, and it will read
as a joke, and about four people are going to read it as a joke and then
stop.*

*Jiraiya has just told me that, in front of her, on purpose, this
morning, because he is not going to say it to her and he wants somebody
in the room to have heard it.*

He did not say any of it.

"There's a prize," said Tsunade, to the sheet.

---

"Sorry?"

"If you win."

"I'm not going to win."

"Then it will cost me nothing," said Tsunade, "and you can stop
arguing."

She put the sheet down.

"If you win, you get an IOU. From me. One. Unconditional — any time, any
purpose, no expiry, and I do not get to ask what it's for and I do not
get to refuse it."

The office was extremely quiet.

"That's—" Sazare stopped.

"Say it."

"That is not a prize," he said. "That is the most valuable object in the
Land of Fire.

"An unconditional undertaking from the Hokage of Konohagakure, held by a
Corps sergeant with a foreign name, a live correspondence with an agent
of Otogakure under written arrangement, and a price on him in two
countries.

"If anybody found out that existed it would be worth more than the
building we are standing in. It is a lever with your name on it and it
would be in my coat."

"Yes," said Tsunade.

"You've thought about that."

"I thought about it on Tuesday for about two hours before I said it in
front of anybody, and then I said it in front of Jiraiya so that there
would be a witness to the terms, and the terms are the ones I have just
given you and they are not going to move."

She picked the sheet back up.

"And you're not going to win," she said, "so it does not matter, and if
you *do* win then I will have found out something about you that is
worth considerably more than an IOU, and I would like you to sit with
that for the next four days."

---

He got to the door.

"Ruri," said Jiraiya.

"Yes."

"The sword."

Sazare stopped.

"There's no specification," he said.

"I know there's no—"

"There's a rule and it is in writing and it has got her name on it, and
the rule is that I do not draw except to a specification, and the panel
on the front of that instrument says *unknown* in my own hand and it has
said it since the eleventh of this month and it is on a shelf in a
dye-works where I can see it from the bench."

He put his hand on the door.

"So on the twentieth I am going to walk onto that ground with the best
thing I have ever made on my hip and no lawful reason to draw it," he
said, "and either I write a specification in the next four days or I
find out what I am without it.

"And I have got no idea which of those is the point of the exercise."
