# Chapter 62, Scene 8 — Please

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the hospital, the north store. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1890 words.*

---

He got the racking up on the Sunday and the box onto the second shelf
with a padlock through the double doors, and then he took the roll of
paper off the windowsill and unrolled the whole of it on the bench with a
weight at each end.

It was nine feet long.

*Four hundred and some. She said she stopped counting.*

*Four hands in it. Yagi's, which is the bulk of it and is small and
backward-sloping and unchanged in eleven years. Emiko's, which is round
and has too many strokes in it. A third one I do not know that stops in
about the fourth year.*

*And the fourth one, which is four pages, and which is at the top,
because whoever put this roll together put the oldest at the top.*

He found it at about the ninth item.

*A FRAME FOR A CRUSHED HAND. TO HOLD THE BONES AND LET THE FINGERS
WORK.*

*8–12 wks. Must adjust as it knits. Must not come off for cleaning.
Fingers must be used from wk 1 or they are gone by wk 6 and I have seen
it eleven times.*

*Nothing exists. I have asked four people.*

*Please.*

---

He stood at the bench for some time.

*Nobody puts* please *in a requisition.*

*It is not a form of address and it is not politeness — there is no
politeness in a requisition, there is a reference and a quantity and a
justification, and I have written about nine hundred of them and read
about four thousand and I have never once seen that word on one.*

*It is there because he had run out of the other kind of writing.*

*He had asked four people and got nothing and he was writing it down for
the fifth time, on his own, in an office, at the end of a day, knowing
perfectly well that there was no fifth person, and he put* please *at
the bottom because there was nobody to put it to.*

*And then he died in the ninth year and Yagi has carried the four pages
from three different offices and could not throw them out and told me
yesterday morning that she would rather I did not ask.*

He put a weight on the corner and got a fresh page.

*Right. Work it.*

---

It took him about four hours and he did not eat.

*Statement of the problem.*

*The bones want rigidity. That is not negotiable — a hand that is not
held knits crooked and it knits crooked in about eleven days and after
that it is a matter of breaking it again, and I have watched Yagi do that
twice and it is the worst thing on that floor.*

*The fingers want movement. Also not negotiable. He has written it and
he has written the evidence — from week one or they are gone by week six
and he has seen it eleven times, which means he watched eleven people
lose a hand that had already been saved.*

*These are in direct opposition and everybody has always known it and
that is why nothing exists.*

*Every frame ever made picks one. You hold the bones and the hand dies,
or you let the fingers work and the bones go crooked, and the whole of
the craft for four hundred years has been about picking which one you
lose and how much.*

He drew it out. He drew it out eleven times.

*Splitting it does not work. Two frames, one rigid on the metacarpals
and one articulated distal — that is the obvious answer and it is the
answer everybody gets to in about four minutes, and it fails, because
the load path goes through the joint you are trying to keep free and you
have made the problem smaller and not different.*

*A hinge does not work, and I want to write down why properly because it
is going to come back.*

*I do not make hinges. I have never made a hinge. Everything I have ever
made is one object — a course is one object, a splint is one object, the
instrument is one object with a thing drawn out of it and the drawn thing
is not a second object, it is the same object further along.*

*A hinge is two objects and a relationship, and I have got no operation
for a relationship, and I cannot hold two shapes at once because I hold
the entire shape by hand every single time and there is exactly one of
me.*

He put the pencil down at about the third hour.

---

*So the answer is not a mechanism.*

*The answer is that the frame has to be different in week four from what
it was in week one, and different again in week eight, and the change has
to happen on the patient without the thing coming off.*

*It has to change while it is on him.*

*Which means it has to be a thing that is still going.*

And he sat down on the step of the cart door, because he had got there,
and he had known he was going to get there since about the second hour.

*It has to be open.*

---

He wrote it out because writing it out was the only way he had of not
running away from it.

*A closed article is finished at the point of closing. It has what it has
and it runs out at six days and it does not change and it cannot change,
and that is the whole of the six-day problem and I have got a controlled
pair from the twenty-second and the twenty-seventh of the eleventh month
that says the close is the mechanism and order is not.*

*An open article does not run out and does not stop and can be altered
while it is running, because the route is still there.*

*I have got one on my hip. A hundred and seventy-one hours on the eighth
and four hundred and some now and no degradation of any kind, measured
twice a day and written on a sheet that goes to her on the first of the
month.*

*This is the second application and it is a medical one and it took me
four hours to get to and about nine seconds to know I was not going to do
it.*

He got the book out and wrote the reason down in the plainest words he
had.

*Eleventh of the eleventh month, in that office, on the record, in front
of a witness: an open article does not go near a patient until it is
one-way.*

*I agreed. I agreed inside about four minutes and I have never once
argued with it and I am not going to argue with it now because I want a
dead man's four pages to work.*

*The rose was open. I put a bare palm on an open drawing structure on
the fifteenth of the eighth month and eighteen minutes later the line was
at the mid-metacarpal, and the only reason I have got a hand is that a
woman worked on it for six hours without knowing what she was stopping.*

*I am safe with the blade because there are nineteen inches of graded
conductor between me and the route, and I worked that out on a Thursday
afternoon and it took two hundred and three hours of somebody's life to
build and the somebody did not know what he was building.*

*A man with a crushed hand has not got nineteen inches of graded
conductor.*

*He has got a crushed hand.*

*And it is in contact with the article for eight to twelve weeks, day and
night, and it cannot come off for cleaning, and he wrote that down
himself in the ninth year because he had watched it matter.*

*If the article draws through him then I will not find out for about
eleven days and I will find out from his fingertips.*

---

He put the pencil down and sat on the step in the dark for a while.

*So: the answer exists, it is the right answer, it is the only answer,
and I cannot use it.*

*Not cannot in the sense of cannot make. I could build the first one this
week. I have got the route and I have got the grading and I have got a
room with a bench in it and four hundred grams of reject and a budget
line three times the size of the one I costed.*

*Cannot in the sense that the thing between the article and the man does
not exist yet, and until it does the frame is a way of taking a hand off
somebody very slowly while telling him it is treatment.*

*And that is the whole of the work.*

*Not the frame. The thing in between.*

*One-way. It has been the term since the eleventh month of the year I was
seventeen and I have used it about four hundred times as a condition and
I have never once sat down and worked on it as an object.*

He wrote the last line of the entry and then sat back and read the whole
of it.

And then he noticed the thing that was actually in front of him and had
been all afternoon.

---

*I have written a specification.*

He went back to the top of the page.

*Load: the bones of a human hand, from a crush, at eight to twelve weeks,
with the fingers in use from the first week.*

*Circumstance: on the patient, continuously, day and night, not removed
for cleaning.*

*At whose instruction: a ward, on a doctor's order.*

*What happens if it fails: a hand at week six.*

*It is all there. It has been there since the ninth item on a nine-foot
roll of paper and I did not put one word of it together — it was already
written, in four pages, in a hand I do not know, by a man who died in the
ninth year.*

He looked at it for a long time.

*The instrument has got nothing at the front of it.*

*Two hundred and three hours and eleven sheets of Shikkotsu leaf and a
question I have been unable to answer since the twenty-fourth of the
eleventh month, and there is one word on the front of it and the word is*
unknown*, and it is on a shelf above this bench where I can see it and I
have not rolled it up.*

*And this one specified itself in about four hours.*

*Because there is a person at the front of it.*

He sat on the step of a dye-works at eight in the evening.

*Every single thing I have ever made that had somebody at the front of it
has told me what it was for.*

*The one thing I made with nobody at the front of it has not, and I have
been sitting in front of a blank line for three weeks asking the object,
and the object cannot know, because objects do not know anything.*

*It is not a question about the instrument at all.*

*They were not asking me what the article is for.*

*They were asking me who it is for.*
