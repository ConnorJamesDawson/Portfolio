# Chapter 55, Scene 5 — No Completion Condition

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's house. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1620 words.*

---

"I need to ask you some things," said Sazare, "and I'm going to be bad
at it, and I'd rather you let me be bad at it than help."

"Go on."

"I don't know what this is."

Shizune waited.

"That's not modesty and it isn't a way in to something," he said. "It's
a statement of my actual position. I have got nine pages out of a book
I borrowed when I was sixteen and a girl on a staircase, and that is
the whole of what I know about the subject, and I found out last night
in front of her that it's the whole of it, and I am going to have to
get the rest from somebody.

"You've been at her side since you were eleven. You're the only person
who's got both halves of it. So it's you."

"That's the worst possible reason to ask me and it's also completely
correct," said Shizune. "Ask."

---

"How often."

She blinked.

"How often *what*, Ruri?"

"That's — right. Yes." He put his hands flat on the table. "That's a
badly formed question and I can see it now that it's out. I'll do it
again.

"At what interval would two people in this situation be expected to see
one another outside of the ordinary business between them?"

"There's no interval."

"There has to be an interval."

"There isn't."

"Then how does anybody know whether they're doing it?"

Shizune put her cup down.

"Nobody knows whether they're doing it," she said. "That's not me being
clever. That is the true answer to the question you asked. There is no
interval and nobody sets one and if you set one you will have made a
rota, and she will spot it inside a fortnight, and it will be the
funniest and most upsetting thing that has ever happened to her."

"All right." He absorbed it. "Next.

"Who initiates."

"Either."

"How does the other one know it's happened?"

"They don't always."

"*What?*"

---

"Ruri," said Shizune, "how many of these have you got?"

"Eleven."

"Eleven."

"They're not all like the first two. Some of them are worse." He did not
smile, because he did not, but something in the room went down about
half a level. "Can I do them?"

"Do them."

"What's the first deliverable."

"There isn't one."

"What does it look like when it's working."

"You won't be able to tell from inside. I'll tell you, if you want, and
I'd probably be wrong."

"Who is allowed to know."

"That one's real and I'll answer it properly. Nobody, for now, and the
list is me, and it stays me until she says otherwise, and if it grows
it grows by her deciding and not by you working out that somebody's
guessed."

"What am I supposed to *do*," said Sazare.

Shizune sat back.

---

"Right," she said. "Stop. That's the one underneath all eleven and
you've got to it faster than most people ever do, so let's have it
properly.

"You want a specification."

"Yes."

"You want to know the acceptance criteria."

"Yes."

"You want to know what it's called when it's finished, so you can look
at it and know you built it right."

"Yes," he said. "That's exactly it. That is precisely what I want and I
can hear how it sounds and I don't have another way of wanting
anything."

"I know you don't." She said it kindly, which he had not been expecting
and did not have anywhere to put. "So here it is, and it's the only
useful thing I've got, and it's going to be worse than the eleven.

"There's no docket.

"Nobody raises one. There's no reference, there's no scope, there's no
schedule of works, and there's no line at the bottom for a name. Nobody
signs it off — not her, not you, not me, not a board.

"And there is no completion condition, Ruri. There isn't one. It does
not have a state called *finished*. It is not a thing you build and
hand over and then it exists whether you're there or not.

"It's a thing that only exists while both of you are doing it, and it
stops the day one of you stops, and there is no drawing of it anywhere
and nothing to hold up at the end and say *there*."

---

He sat very still.

He had expected the fear to come from a different direction. He had
gone into the kitchen braced for security, for the council, for the
fifteen, for a version of the next four years that he had costed at
about four in the morning on the tenth of the eleventh month; and he
had costed all of it, and none of it did what the last sentence did.

*No completion condition.*

*Everything I have ever done has had one.*

*A course either takes or it does not. A splint holds a load or it
fails. A book is finished when the column has no gaps. An exercise is
two and then it is nine and then it is fifteen, and there is a number,
and the number is the thing, and I have had a number since I was seven
years old.*

*There has never been one day of my life without a number in it.*

"I don't know how to do a thing with no end state," he said.

"No."

"I'm not being difficult. I'm telling you I have never once done it. I
don't have the — there's no procedure. There's nothing in me that
works without a—"

"I know."

"Then how do people—"

"Badly," said Shizune. "Everyone does it badly. All of them. Every
single person who has ever done it has done it badly and made it up as
they went and got most of it wrong, and that is not a failure of the
method, that *is* the method, and you are going to hate it more than
anybody I have ever met."

---

He had one left.

He had known he had one left since about the third question, and he had
been putting it at the end because it was the one he could not build a
sentence for, which had never happened to him before with a question he
actually wanted answered.

"There's a twelfth," he said.

"Go on."

"It's a physiological—"

He stopped.

He started again.

"Since about seven o'clock this morning there has been a — there is a
response. It's endocrine. I can name all of it. I could write you the
pathway and the timings and the half-lives and get full marks, and I
have known the whole of it since I was fifteen and I have taught two
sections of it to first-years.

"I've never had it before. Not once. I've had my hands inside four
people and I've been in a room with about nine hundred and there has
never been anything, and I wrote that down at sixteen as a thing that
might be wrong with me and then I wrote it down at seventeen as a
professional advantage and I've been fairly pleased about it.

"There's nothing wrong with me. It was never broken. It's been sitting
there in order for eighteen years with nothing pointed at it and now
there is something pointed at it and it does not care in the slightest
that I can draw it.

"I know the mechanism completely and I have no control over it at all,
and that has never happened to me before in my life, and it happened
twice this morning before eight and the other one was the seal.

"So the question is—"

"No," said Shizune.

---

He stopped.

"I haven't asked it."

"You don't need to. I know what it is and I'm not answering it, and
that's the only one this morning I'm going to refuse outright, and I'd
like you to work out why rather than me telling you."

He worked it.

It took him a while and she let it, and at one point she got up and put
more water on and sat back down, and he did not notice her do either.

*One: it is not hers. That is the same answer as the seal and it is
part of it and it is not the whole of it, because she gave me the name
of the seal and she has not given me anything at all here.*

*Two: I already know the answer. Everything I asked her in the first
eleven was a thing I did not know. This one I know completely. I said so
out loud in the middle of asking it — I said I could write the pathway
and get full marks.*

*So I was not asking for information.*

*Three—*

He got there and did not like it.

*I was asking to be told it was all right.*

*That is what that was. I dressed an absolution up as a clinical
question and brought it to the only adult in the building who is on my
side, and what I wanted was for somebody with authority to look at it
and say* that is normal and you are not doing anything wrong*, and she
will not say it.*

*Not because it is not true.*

*Because if she says it then she has issued it, and it becomes a thing
I have got a licence for, and there is exactly one person on this earth
who is entitled to have an opinion about it and she is upstairs putting
her hair up.*

"You want to say it," said Shizune.

"You won't give me a licence."

"No."

"Because it isn't yours to issue."

"No, Ruri," said Shizune. "Because you'd *use* it."
