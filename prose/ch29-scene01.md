# Chapter 29, Scene 1 — Eight Seconds Again

*Act 4, part C. Konoha General. Sazare Ruri, age sixteen. POV: Sazare.
~1,247 words.*

---

Nohara Daichi's graft lifted for the fourth time on a Tuesday, and the
fourth time was the one that put the problem in front of Sazare
properly, because he was the one holding the dressing tray when it
happened and there was no medic within call for the better part of two
minutes.

The burn ran from the back of his right hand to four inches above the
wrist, a clean split-thickness graft laid down in the second week
after the invasion, healing well by every measure the chart carried.
The trouble was the edge nearest the tendon, where the new skin had to
move every time the hand closed round anything, and every time it
moved past a certain angle it lifted a fraction along one seam and
began, quietly and without any particular drama, to bleed.

"It's the grip," Daichi said, watching Sazare work with the flat
patience of a man who had explained this four times already and
expected to explain it a fifth. "Not the wound. The wound's fine sat
still. It's the second I close my hand on anything — a cup, a door
handle, the strap on my own sling — the edge goes."

"How often."

"Depends what I'm gripping. Twice a day, most days. Worse if I've been
using the hand for anything that isn't holding a teacup."

"And each time."

"Somebody comes, presses it for however long it takes, tells me not to
grip things, and leaves. Then I hold a door open for somebody an hour
later without thinking about it, and we do the whole thing again."

Sazare pressed the pad against the seam and held it, counting under
his breath the way Yagi had taught him to count anything that needed a
fixed duration rather than a felt one. Eight seconds. The bleeding
slowed at six and stopped at eight, the same eight it had stopped at
the last three times, which told him nothing was actually wrong with
the graft and everything was wrong with how the hospital was spending
its attention on it.

"That's the fourth time this week," he said.

"Fifth. You weren't here for Sunday."

"Who came for Sunday."

"Yagi. She was already in the corridor for somebody else and heard me
swear." Daichi flexed the fingers experimentally, watched the seam
hold, and stopped. "It's not a big thing. I know it's not a big thing.
It just keeps being a thing at the exact moment somebody's needed
somewhere more urgent."

That was the whole of it, stated more plainly than the staffing
figures Kaburagi kept posting outside her office ever managed. The
hospital was not short of competent hands in any way that showed up on
a roster. It was short of competent hands being in the specific six
square inches of a specific forearm at the specific eight seconds a
specific vessel decided to reopen, and no roster in the world could
solve a problem that particular.

---

He raised it with Yagi that afternoon in the sluice, where she was
counting swabs into a tray with the automatic economy of somebody who
had counted the same tray several thousand times and no longer needed
to watch her own hands do it.

"Daichi's graft."

"I know Daichi's graft. I've been pressing it since Sunday."

"Eight seconds, every time. The same eight seconds."

"It's not complicated. The edge is under mechanical load it isn't
ready for yet. Give it another fortnight and the tissue will catch
up." She finished the tray and started another. "It's a minor
inconvenience, not a crisis."

"It's a minor inconvenience that costs a medic's attention six times a
week, at a time nobody can predict, for a task that takes the same
eight seconds every single time it happens."

Yagi stopped counting.

"Say the rest of that," she said. "You've got a face on."

"If the intervention is identical every time — same site, same
pressure, same duration, same outcome — then the only variable is
whether a person capable of delivering it happens to be within thirty
feet when it starts. That's not a medical problem. That's a
distribution problem."

"And you think you can distribute eight seconds of my hands."

"I think I can distribute eight seconds of what your hands already
did once, correctly, with nobody having to be in the room the second
time it's needed."

Yagi looked at him for a long moment with the specific stillness she
reserved for the times she was deciding whether an idea was
irresponsible or merely inconvenient, which he had learned, over four
months, were not always the same judgement.

"You built something in your room," she said. "Kanzaki mentioned a
chip that discharged on its own."

"A test. Not medical. It proved a store could wait for a trigger
instead of firing on contact or on a clock."

"And now you want to put that trigger on Daichi's arm."

"I want to know if it's possible before I decide whether I want it."

"That's a distinction nobody in this building is going to believe you
make honestly, including me, some days." She set the tray down and
folded her arms, and the sluice went quiet around them in the way a
room does when somebody in it has stopped performing patience and
started actually giving it. "Here's what happens if we do this
properly, and I want you to hear the *if* in that sentence before you
hear anything else. It gets built on a bench, on things that have
never been a person, until it either works or it kills a piece of raw
meat convincingly enough that nobody has to wonder what it would have
done to Daichi. And if it survives that, it does not go anywhere near
him until somebody who isn't you has explained to him, in words he
understands, exactly what it is and exactly what it might do wrong."

"Why not me."

"Because you invented it, you want it to work, and a person who wants
a thing to work is the last person who should be the one telling
somebody else it's safe to try." She said it without any particular
edge, the way she delivered most of the things that mattered most to
her, as a fact rather than an accusation. "You've spent your whole
life being told what you're worth by people who never asked you
first. I am not going to let you become the person who does that to
somebody else, even by accident, even with the best intentions
anybody in this hospital has ever had."

Sazare stood with that for a moment, turning it the way he turned
everything, and found that it did not need turning very far before it
settled.

"All right," he said.

"That was faster than I expected."

"You're not wrong. I checked."

"Good." Yagi picked the tray back up. "Then we start on the bench,
properly, with Kanzaki keeping the numbers and Urushibara keeping the
seal honest, and if it survives all of that, somebody who isn't
either of us goes and asks Daichi whether he wants to be the first
person in the Land of Fire to test it."

"Who."

"Emiko. She's watched more people decide things under pressure than
anybody else in this building, and she has no reason to want it to
work." Yagi carried the tray toward the door. "Which is exactly why
she's the only person whose yes would mean anything."
