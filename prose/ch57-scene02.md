# Chapter 57, Scene 2 — The File

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Tsunade, age fifty-three.
POV: Tsunade. ~1460 words.*

---

He got both feet on the floor and then he saw the chair.

She watched it happen. She had watched that man read rooms for forty
years and it was the single most underrated thing about him — people
saw the hair and the entrance and the books, and did not notice that he
had been standing in a doorway working out what had happened in a room
before he opened his mouth since he was eleven years old.

She gave it about two seconds.

It took him one.

*There it is.*

She saw his eyes go to the boy, and to the chair the boy was in, and to
the fact that the boy had sat down in it without being asked, and to
the clock on the wall behind her which said four minutes past four in
the morning, and then to her, once, for about a quarter of a second.

And then he grinned.

*Oh, no.*

---

"Well, *now*," said Jiraiya.

"Don't."

"I haven't said anything."

"You've said *now*," said Tsunade. "I've had forty years of *now*. Sit
down and shut up, you're bleeding on my floor."

"Am I?" He looked at his own arm. "So I am. That's four days old, that
is, and I want you to note that I have not asked you to do anything
about it."

"You're about to."

"I'm about to."

He came across the room, and then he did the thing she had known he was
going to do from the moment his eyes went to the chair, and there was
nothing whatsoever she could do to stop it.

He went straight past her desk and stuck his hand out.

"Sazare Ruri," said Jiraiya.

---

The boy stood up.

She watched him do it, and she watched him look at the hand, and she
watched about four separate calculations go across a face that had
never in its life been able to hold one, and then he took it.

"Yes," he said.

"I know it's yes. I'm saying it because I've wanted to say it out loud
to your face for about seven months and I have had to make do with
saying it to a folder."

"...I'm sorry?"

"Kirigakure, Hōzuki district, second son of Ruri Kaede, frame-worker.
Out through the eastern lines in the fourth month of the purge year at
seven and a half years of age with nobody. Gate at Konoha at fifteen
with a burn on the left forearm and no papers.

"Ward assistant, second floor, on Yagi's recommendation over three
objections. Fabrication room in the eleventh month of the year after —
which nobody authorised, incidentally, you just started doing it and it
took the Corps eight months to notice they had a department.

"Splint study, rejected. Splint study, resubmitted with the reject
table attached, which is the single funniest thing I have read in
eleven years. Surplus programme. Standing correspondence under a
written arrangement with T and I, second exchange due in the eleventh
month. Post created on the eleventh, eight to seven, and there is a
price on you in two countries at a figure that ought to embarrass you
because it is far too low."

Jiraiya let go of his hand.

"Have I got any of it wrong?"

---

The boy stood there.

*Say something, Ruri.*

*Say literally anything.*

"My mother worked the frames," he said. "She didn't own them. It's in
about half the files as *frame-maker* and it's wrong, and it matters,
because the ones that say maker have her on a different tax line and
that's how the compensation office lost her in the third month."

Jiraiya stopped.

Then he pulled the other chair out and sat down on it heavily and
looked up at him.

"That," he said, "is not what I expected you to correct."

"It's the only thing that's wrong."

"I know it is."

"Then—"

"Sit down, kid."

The boy sat down.

---

She poured him a drink. It was four in the morning and he had come
through a window with a four-day-old wound and he would not have taken
tea if she had made it at gunpoint, and she poured him a drink and did
not have one herself, which she noticed and did not examine.

"Naruto," she said.

"Back on the eleventh, in one piece, eating." Jiraiya took the cup.
"He's twice the size and about a third as stupid and he's been at that
girl's throat since the second day about something, and I have not
asked and I am not going to. He'll be up here inside the week wanting
something."

"He was up here on Saturday wanting something."

"Course he fucking was." He drank. "I went straight out again. Four
days, north, and it was a waste of everybody's time except that it
wasn't, and I'll tell you about that when the room's smaller."

She glanced at the chair.

"He's fine," said Jiraiya.

"He's an eighteen-year-old sergeant in the Medical Corps."

"He's a man with a written arrangement with Morino Ibiki and a price
on him in two countries," said Jiraiya, "and he has been sitting in
that chair for eleven minutes not asking me a single question, which
tells me more about him than the folder did."

---

*Right.*

*Here we go.*

"Ruri," she said, "there's a thing I want doing."

The boy looked up.

"The dispatch box that went to the annexe on Sunday. There's a return
in it from Kanzaki, third bundle, and the numbers on the second page
don't match the schedule, and I have looked at it twice and I cannot
see it and it is annoying me.

"Go down and find it. Take as long as it takes. Come back when you've
got it."

*And that is a real job, and he'll know it's a real job, because if I'd
made one up he'd have it apart before he got to the stairs.*

*And it's an hour.*

He stood up.

"It'll be the tare," he said.

"It will not be the tare."

"It's usually the tare."

"Ruri, get out of my office."

"Yes, Hime," said Sazare, and went.

---

The door shut.

She counted four steps on the landing and eleven on the stair and then
the door at the bottom, and then she sat back in the chair and put both
hands over her face for a second, and when she took them off Jiraiya
was looking at her with an expression she had spent forty years wanting
to take a chair to.

"*Hime*," said Jiraiya.

"Don't."

"He calls you *Hime*."

"About six hundred people call me Hime."

"About six hundred people call you Hime behind your back and about four
to your face, and three of them are me, Shizune and the daimyō's
brother-in-law who is frightened of you." He put the cup down. "That
boy said it like it was your name."

"It is my name."

"It is *not* your name, Tsunade, it's a bloody nickname off a summoning
contract, and I have watched you take the head off a jōnin commander
for using it in a briefing." He sat back. "Fourteenth year. Field
station. He went through a wall."

"Supporting wall."

"Supporting wall. Thank you."

---

She got up and went to the window because there was nowhere else to go
and she was not going to sit still for this.

"Say it, then."

"I haven't decided what it is yet."

"Bollocks you haven't. You had it before you got your second foot on
the carpet, you've had a grin on you like a man who's found money, and
you have been sitting there for a quarter of an hour being *pleasant*,
which you are not, which means you're building up to something."

"I am building up to something."

"Then have it out."

"No," said Jiraiya.

She turned round.

"No?"

"No, because you'll take a swing at me, and I've got a four-day-old
hole in my arm, and because—" He stopped, and for about a second and a
half he was not doing the voice. "Because I'd like to do it properly,
Hime, and not at four in the morning across a desk while you're on your
guard.

"So pour me another one, and tell me about the Suna delegation, and
then when you've stopped standing like that we'll talk about the kid."

She stood at the window for a moment.

Then she came back and picked up the bottle.

"Fine," she said. "But I'm not standing like anything."

"You're standing like a woman at a window."

"*Jiraiya.*"

"All right, all right." He held the cup out. "Suna. Go on. And start
with the fucking temple, because I heard about the temple in a bar in
Tanzaku and I want it from the source."
