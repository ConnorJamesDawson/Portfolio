# Chapter 69, Scene 3 — The Docket

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Tsunade, age fifty-three.
POV: Tsunade. ~1130 words.*

---

The docket arrived through the proper channel on the Wednesday,
because everything he did arrived through the proper channel, and
she read the title line twice, and then sat back in the chair with
the whole afternoon rearranging itself around the improvement.

*Proposal: load-supporting garment articles for the active roster.
Fabricator: Sazare Ruri. Category: medical device. Attachments:
eleven.*

She read all eleven. The rib case first — Tachibana, whom she
remembered, and whose intake notes now had a sentence in them that
was going to change forty years of issue standards, because the boy
had found the box the form never had. The ward survey: sixty-one
presentations in three years that said so out loud, and his flat,
furious little footnote — furious by his standards, which meant one
adjective — about the ones filed under *cause unstated*. The load
diagrams. The seal families, breathable and form-fitting, his first
compositions on a public docket, sitting there in the notation
Urushibara taught him like the estate paying its first dividend.
The line — she read it three times and put her hand over her mouth
the third time — *lace is a snag hazard.*

And underneath all of it, visible to exactly one reader on the
planet, the second document. The one not written down. A man who
had been asked *how does that wonderful chest of yours not hurt
your back*, and had been given the answer, and had then gone away
and — because he was who he was, because the care had never once
been for the dockets but the dockets were his native language —
built the answer a delivery mechanism for every woman who did not
have her chakra control and her fifty years.

*Both motives,* she wrote in the register that evening, *read in
about four seconds, and I want it recorded that they do not come
apart, because nothing of his comes apart, I've tested. He is
solving a real injury I told him about in my own bedroom, for
several hundred women who will never know where the idea came
from. And he has also just engineered himself a legitimate,
supervised, clipboard-armoured reason to spend professional hours
in close study of my chest, and he thinks I have not noticed the
theory underneath — occupy the instrument and it overloads slower
— because he thinks the theory is his.*

*The theory is mine. I have been running it on him since a towel
in a bathroom. He has independently reproduced my own methodology
and is aiming it back at me, and I have honestly never been
prouder of anything I did not give birth to.*

*The diary said the answer to his next ask was yes. The diary has
no idea what it signed.*

---

He came up at six to present it, formal, standing, and she made
him do the whole presentation because watching him present was
one of the standing pleasures of her office, and then she went to
the last page, where the proposal wanted a name for the pilot
line, and found the field blank, and one line of his hand below
it.

*Proposed: "Hime." Rationale on request.*

"No," she said, on pure reflex, with the December ruling already
half out of its holster. "There is a standing—"

"Rationale," said Sazare, "since it has been requested. One: it
is a word every woman in the five nations already knows how to
say, and it carries rank without carrying a clan, which matters,
because this line must never read as one family's product — ask
Kiri why. Two: it is what a garment like this is *for*. Every
woman on that roster gets treated like infantry from the neck
down. The name says otherwise. Three—"

He stopped, exactly where she would have stopped.

"Three," she said.

"Three is the rationale that is not on request," said Sazare, in
the working register, level as his own spirit gauge. "It is
between the fabricator and the name."

The office was quiet. Below the window the village went about its
January business, in which the Hokage of the Land of Fire did not
sit at her own desk feeling her whole chest go warm over a
trademark filing.

*The word is Jiraiya's for me in every bar in the country. It is
half the continent's word for any woman with rank. It points
nowhere. It is perfectly, administratively innocent — and it will
be on the waistband tape of half the village by summer, and only
two people alive will ever know that every single one of them is
a dedication.*

*He has hidden a love letter in plain sight, in my own filing
system, under a category heading, with the rationale withheld,
and there is not one thing in the correspondence rules I can do
about it, because I wrote the correspondence rules.*

"Approved," she said, and signed it, and if the signature came
out a half-degree warmer than regulation, the docket would keep
that between the three of them. "The pilot is the ward cohort
plus volunteers from the roster, Shizune builds the protocol, and
the medical oversight is one form — *one*, Sazare, I've seen your
forms — documenting any side effects of prolonged crystal
contact, of which there will be none, because I've read four
years of your reject tables and the material is cleaner than the
bandages. And the board." She tapped the structure page. "You've
put yourself down as fabricator only. Design authority: a
standing board of women. Chairs: to be appointed."

"The users hold the specification," said Sazare. "That is not
modesty. It is the whole method. I build to spec. The spec
belongs to the people who fight in the result — I have never
once been thrown by my own chest and I am not going to pretend
eleven diagrams fix that."

"'I have never once been thrown by my own chest,'" said Tsunade,
writing it down with enormous care. "That's going in the minutes
verbatim. Get out, brief Shizune, and Sazare—" she let him get
to the door, because timing was a medical skill— "the lead
model position. I assume that's mine."

He turned in the doorway, and she watched him take the shot the
way he took everything, level, unhurried, both hands.

"The appointment letter is already drafted," he said. "It was
attachment twelve. I held it back because the terms are
non-negotiable and I am told one does not open with those."

And he was gone down the stairs before she could get the folder
open, and attachment twelve was one page, in the small exact
evening hand, and it said what it said, and she read it and
put her head back and laughed alone in her own office like a
woman being informed of the terms of her surrender.
