# Chapter 72, Scene 2 — Page Sixty-One

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, Naruto's apartment. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1370 words.*

---

The hotpot was from the hospital cookbook, page sixty-one, which
he recognised at the second spoonful because he had certified
the kitchen trial of that recipe two years ago, and he said so,
and watched the information land on Naruto like a decoration.

"You're kidding. You KNOW page sixty-one?"

"I audited it. The ward kitchen wanted the stock cheaper and
the dietician wanted the egg later, and I ran the trial that
settled it. The egg goes in at the end. You have put the egg
in at the end."

"The book SAYS the end—"

"The book says the end because of the trial. You are eating
the recipe as amended," said Sazare, "and you have doubled the
pork, which the dietician would oppose and I am declining to
report," and Naruto pointed at him with the ladle in the
manner of a man conferring knighthood, and said the guest gets
the good egg, and the evening found its pace there, at the
table with one chair, in the steam off a shared pot.

The flow of it was the finding of the night.

He had eaten with many people now — the noons, the Fridays,
the board lunches, a bathhouse of one — and every one of those
tables had a grammar, a set of channels the talk ran in. This
table had none and needed none. Naruto talked the way water
found a slope: the seven weeks, the itch inside the bone, the
genin on his old team roster who had made chūnin while he was
horizontal and how that was GREAT and also completely
unacceptable; a question about the sword, mid-sentence,
answered and absorbed; a story about Iruka and a flak jacket;
the pot refilled without asking, the good bits redistributed
by ladle-fiat, guest first, always, enforced.

*He conducts a meal the way Katsuyu conducts a Friday,* Sazare
recorded later. *As care, disguised as procedure. The
procedure is: everybody eats, nobody asks. I have been
studying hospitality for three months out of a small orange
book, and the standing master of the discipline turns out to
be a sixteen-year-old with one chair, and he does not know it
is a discipline. Sixth documented instance of him arriving at
the true thing by not looking. The instances are becoming a
canon.*

The rematch came up over the second round, as scheduled — as
*Naruto* scheduled, which was to say head-first.

"So. Spring's basically here. When am I getting my fight?"

"Not soon," said Sazare, and laid it out plainly, because this
table's grammar was plainness: the mornings were Jiraiya's,
four a week, a year deep; the afternoons were — he chose the
public words with their true weight — a production line and a
research program, both with boards attached; and the honest
arithmetic underneath, which Naruto had earned at the original
fight: "Also: nothing meaningful has changed in seven weeks.
You have healed and I have drilled footwork. If we fight now
we re-run December with better cardio. The version of me
worth your rematch is the one Jiraiya-sama is building, and
he is perhaps a third built. Ask me at midsummer."

Naruto considered this with the pot steaming between them.

"That's so *boring* and so *right*," he said, aggrieved. "Fine.
Midsummer. Put it in the— you've got the thing, the book with
the days in it—"

"A diary."

"Put it in the diary." He shuddered, visibly and sincerely.
"A *diary*. You know what my schedule is? It's a guy telling
me things and me showing up. It's a great system. It's never
once failed— okay it's failed a bunch of times. Put me in the
diary, fabricator."

He put him in the diary. *Midsummer: N., the rematch, terms
per December.* And underneath, not read aloud: *he watched me
write his name in it, and something crossed his face — filed,
pending — the specific expression of a man seeing his name
scheduled into somebody's future on purpose.*

---

The chair matter he raised at the end, over the tea, because
he had been sitting in the only chair in the apartment for two
hours while his host sat on the window sill beside a plant,
entirely content, jacket on against the draught, and the
arrangement had been sitting in Sazare's chest at the same
angle as a bag of soup carried on crutches.

"You gave me the chair."

"You're the guest," said Naruto, in the tone other men used
for load-bearing walls. "Guest gets the chair. That's just
manners."

"And when Iruka-san comes?"

"Sill's fine. He likes the sill. You can see the monument."

"And when you are the guest, at mine — you took the bench
without asking, both times, and put me on the stool by the
scale, which is the good seat." He set the tea down. "So
state the rule as a rule. I want it in the record properly."

Naruto looked at him for a second, and then out the window
at the monument, four faces in the last light and a fifth
he'd fought a war of attrition to see up there someday, and
shrugged the shrug of somebody handing over a thing that had
never once occurred to him to consider valuable.

"People who come get the good spot," he said. "Because
they came. That's the whole rule. Why get a chair if no one
comes over — but that's not a sad thing, everybody makes it
a sad thing, Sakura-chan got all quiet about it once — it's
just TRUE. A chair's a promise, right? You buy the chair
when there's someone the chair is FOR. Otherwise it's just
— furniture pretending. I hate furniture pretending. The
apartment's honest. Everything in it is for something real."

*A chair is a promise,* wrote Sazare, in his head, in ink,
in the permanent collection. *He has just, between two
mouthfuls of tea, produced the load-bearing principle
underneath eleven years of my own inventory — everything I
own does a job — and extended it into a register I never
reached: the job a thing does can be a PERSON. The furniture
is honest. The room over the persimmon tree was honest too.
Two honest rooms, ten years apart, and neither of us ever
once bought the promise-chair, because a promise requires a
counterparty, and neither of us had—*

He stopped the entry. He looked at his host on the window
sill, sixteen, cleared, stamped, redistributing the last of
the good bits to the guest by standing decree.

"Tomorrow is Sunday," said Sazare. "There is a dinner. At
the tower, the second floor, six o'clock. Jiraiya-sama will
be there, and the Hokage, and Shizune-san, and the food is
beyond anything either of us can produce. It is a standing
monthly arrangement and tomorrow is the first one." He said
the next part in the working register, level, so it could
be received as what it was, which was a specification: "I
am inviting you. As thanks for this meal, which is the
first meal anybody has cooked me in their own home since I
was thirteen years old. Come to dinner, Naruto. There is a
chair, and it is for somebody, and I am telling you that
the somebody is you."

Naruto stared at him.

"That's—" he started, and stopped, and Sazare watched the
whole campaign of it move through him at Naruto-speed —
the reflex no, the *dinners are a thing other people have*,
the recount, the arithmetic of an evening actually already
spent proving the arithmetic wrong — and then the grin
arrived, the full unbanked one, the one the village would
follow anywhere, and he said:

"Is there gonna be enough food, though? Because Ero-sennin
eats like a punishment, and I eat like TWO punishments, and
Baa-chan— wait." The grin went sideways into pure delight.
"Wait. Does Baa-chan know you're inviting me?"

"She will by the time you knock."

"Oh, this is going to be GREAT," said Uzumaki Naruto, and
came off the sill to shake on it, one pump, ladle-firm, the
first formal contract of his social life. "Six o'clock.
I'll bring— what do you bring? You're the manners guy.
What does a guy bring?"

"Yourself," said Sazare, "and the appetite. It does a job.
Everything at that table is for something real."
