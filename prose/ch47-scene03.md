# Chapter 47, Scene 3 — The Gate

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,682 words.*

---

Kakashi came to the yard, which he had never done.

He arrived at about four on the third, through the gate rather than
over the wall, with the book out, and stood under the awning looking at
the sacking Sazare had rigged over the far bench on the twenty-ninth of
the previous month at the Hokage's instruction — four uprights, a
tarpaulin, and a second layer of hessian on the west side that had
taken an evening and worked perfectly.

"That's a lot of sacking," he said.

"It was that or explain it."

"Mm." Kakashi put the book away. "Go on then. The better version."

---

Sazare had had a night with it and had written four openings and burnt
none of them, and what he said in the end was not any of the four.

"I'm going to tell you the whole shape first," he said, "because if I
ask the question cold you'll answer the question, and the question
isn't the thing I need you to decide about."

"That's already better. Go."

"Crystal comes from nowhere. I can put a number on it — seven point one
one grams a draw, eleven hundred and forty kilos over three years by
Kanzaki's books, and nothing in that yard weighs less afterwards
including me. I've tested air, ground, operator and seed and eliminated
all four." He kept it flat. "Which means I'm not making it. I'm
collecting something that's already there and giving it a shape, and I
don't know what it is or where it is or whether it runs out."

Kakashi did not say anything.

"The Hokage has seen the working. Her instruction is one question, one
person, no paper. And the question is whether the same thing is true of
Wood Release — where does the timber come from, and does anything weigh
less afterwards — because if a man tells me it comes off a seed, or out
of the ground, or that he's four kilos lighter at the end of an
afternoon, then the whole of it collapses in about nine seconds and I'll
have spent a fortnight."

"And if it doesn't collapse."

"Then I've got two unrelated bloodlines drawing on the same
unaccounted source, and that's a different kind of thing, and I'm not
going to say the rest of that sentence in a yard." Sazare stopped.
"That's the shape. Here's the part I'm actually asking you to decide.

"I think you know one. I think the Hokage knows one and can't tell me,
because I watched her not answer a question for four seconds and she
told me to come to you and said it was yours to give and hers to hold.
And I think that if I'm right, then the reason it's held is that it
costs the man something, and I'd rather you told me it costs him too
much than told me he doesn't exist."

---

Kakashi looked at the sacking for a while.

"Sit on the wall," he said.

They sat on the wall.

"Yes," said Kakashi. "There's one. He's alive, he's in this village
about a third of the year, and I've known him since he was a boy."

Sazare kept his hands still.

"Now the rest of it, and I want you to hear all of it before you say
anything at all." Kakashi had his forearms on his knees. "It costs him.
Not a little. The subject is classified at a level that would end this
conversation if the wrong person were standing behind that gate, and
the reason it's classified is not a security reason, it's that a great
many people did a great many things to a great many children about
twenty-five years ago and the paperwork is embarrassing to institutions
that still exist."

"Then—"

"I said all of it." Kakashi did not raise his voice. "He is the only
one. Not the only one in this village — the only one anywhere, out of
a number I know and am not going to say, and he is the only one because
everybody else it was done to died. He was a small child. He has spent
his entire adult life being the one who came out, and being useful to
people because of it, and the only thing anybody has ever wanted him
for is the thing that was put into him without asking."

Sazare said nothing.

"So when you sit down in front of him and ask him a question about how
it works," said Kakashi, "you will be the four hundredth person to want
him for it, and he will know that inside four seconds, because he has
had four hundred of you."

---

"Then I withdraw it," said Sazare.

"No you don't."

"I—"

"You don't, and I'd like you to notice how fast that came out, because
that was reflex and not judgement and I've watched you do the reflex
version of the decent thing about four times this year." Kakashi turned
his head. "Sit there and do the actual arithmetic. Out loud, since you
can't do it any other way."

Sazare sat there.

"He's the only living instance," he said, slowly. "Which means there is
no version of this question that doesn't cost him, because there's
nobody else to ask. If I withdraw it, the question doesn't go away, it
just goes unanswered — and it goes unanswered for the rest of my life,
because he's it."

"Go on."

"And I'd be withdrawing it to feel better, which is the cheapest
possible reason, and I'd have done it in about two seconds without
consulting the one person it's actually about." He looked at his hands.
"Which is exactly what four hundred people have already done to him in
the other direction. They decided what he was for without asking. I'd
be deciding what he can't be asked without asking."

"There it is," said Kakashi.

---

"So this is what happens," he said. "I'll put it to him. Not you — me,
and not today. And I am going to put it to him *badly*, on purpose, and
I want you to know that in advance so you don't hear about it later and
think I sold you."

"Badly how."

"I'm going to give him the easiest exit I can build. I'll tell him
there's a young fabricator with a technical question about material
provenance, that it will take an afternoon, that the Hokage knows, that
I think it's a real question and not a curiosity — and then I am going
to tell him that I have already told the young fabricator that he'll
almost certainly say no, and that saying no costs him nothing with me
and nothing with anybody." Kakashi shrugged. "If he wants out, he
steps out, and it's clean, and neither of you ever meets."

"And if he says yes?"

"Then he'll have said yes with a door open behind him and both hands
free, and you'll know that, and it matters." He stood up. "Three
conditions and they're not negotiable.

"One. One afternoon. You do not go back for a second.

"Two. No notebook on the table. You can write it up afterwards and I
know you will, and I'd rather you did it in a room he isn't in.

"Three." Kakashi looked down at him. "Don't ask him where he got it."

Sazare frowned.

"That's my question," he said.

"No it isn't. Your question is where the *material* comes from — the
timber, the mass, the substance. That's a materials question and it's
about the world." Kakashi's voice went entirely level. "*Where did you
get it* is a different question with the same words in it, and it is
about him, and the answer involves a laboratory and a number and
fifty-nine other children, and he has been asked it by four hundred
people who thought they were being sympathetic."

"...Yes. All right."

"You'll be tempted, because the two questions sit about nine seconds
apart in any honest conversation, and you're the most curious man I've
ever met and you have never once left a wrong number standing." Kakashi
got the book back out. "Leave that one standing. If he wants to tell
you, he'll tell you. He won't."

---

He was at the gate before Sazare said the other thing.

"Kakashi."

"Mm."

"What's it costing you."

Kakashi stopped, with one hand on the gate post.

"Say why you're asking."

"Because you came here," said Sazare. "You've never come here. In six
months you have never once come to a place I work — I go to the ground,
that's the arrangement, and you walk me back the long way afterwards
and you leave me at the bottom of the hill. And today you came through a
gate at four in the afternoon and stood under the awning and looked at
the sacking for eleven seconds before you said anything."

"That's rung one."

"That's rung two," said Sazare. "You're the one who taught me the
difference and you told me not to skip to the top and I'm not skipping.
I'm asking."

Kakashi was quiet for a moment.

"He's my subordinate," he said. "He was my subordinate when he was
fourteen and I was not much older and I was worse at it than I am now,
and there is a specific list of things I did not ask him at the time
because I did not want to know the answers, and about a third of the
way through that list is the reason he trusts me."

He opened the gate.

"So what it costs me," he said, "is that I'm about to walk into a room
and ask him to talk about the one thing I have spent twenty years
demonstrating I would never make him talk about. That's the whole of
it, and it's mine, and I've already decided, and you asked correctly so
you got the answer."

He went out.

"Thursday," he said, from the lane. "Or never. I'll send word either
way and I won't dress up the never."
