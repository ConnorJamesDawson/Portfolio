# Chapter 50, Scene 3 — Four Days in the Seventh Month

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,309 words.*

---

Kanzaki needed a sealwright on the fourth.

"Batch of forty," she said, not looking up. "Third and fourth quarter
medical, and Kaburagi wants them signed off before the year turns
because Kaburagi is a woman who has been let down by a supplier once
and has organised the remaining nineteen years of her life around it.
Four days' work. I'll want a name on the request by Friday."

"There's a form."

"There is always a form and you have filled in nine hundred of them and
you know exactly which one it is, so I am going to assume that was not
a question."

He went and got the log.

He told himself he was checking the last certification's figures, which
was true — the reject count and the seventh-application fault and
whether the second batch had come in under the first — and he was four
pages back before he stopped pretending.

*Seventh month. The eleventh to the fourteenth.*

---

There were four entries and they were his and they were perfect.

*11/7. Certification, second batch, day 1. Kurihara S., 09:00–17:00.
Forty stones, rows one to three. Eleven corrections, all mine, all
accepted. Retaining figure now clean on the seventh — no recurrence
since the fifth month.*

*12/7. Day 2. Rows four and five. Nine corrections. Discussed the new
requisition form (worse in four respects, see over).*

*13/7. Day 3. Rows six to eight. Six corrections. She has changed the
order in which she lays the ground; asked; the answer is that it is
faster and not better and she is aware of that and does it anyway on
work of this class. Noted for my own practice.*

*14/7. Day 4. Final row and sign-off. Four corrections. Batch closed
16:40. Paid out of the seal office allocation.*

He read all four twice.

Then he sat on the stool at bench three in the ninth month with a log
open in front of him and did the thing he had spent eleven years being
better at than almost anybody, which was to look at a document and find
what was not in it.

*Thirty corrections in four days.*

*Every one of them recorded. Every one accepted. The eleven on the
first day is high and the four on the last is low and the curve between
them is the ordinary curve of a person getting used to a hand again,
and I have the numbers because I always have the numbers.*

*Eight hours a day, four days. Thirty-two hours.*

*Four entries. Two hundred and eleven words.*

*And there is no person in any of them.*

---

He made himself go through it properly, because a fault report was
worth nothing if you would not stand in it.

*What is in the log: the work. All of it, accurately, in a form
somebody could reconstruct the batch from in forty years.*

*What is not in the log:*

*Whether she brought her own lens. (She did. I can see it on the bench
in my head, the brass triple in the fitted case with the worn strap, at
the far end under the sky, in full sun, with a taper going.)*

*What time she left. (Five. All four days. Not seven.)*

*Whether she used my name. (No.)*

*Whether any of the thirty corrections was unsoftened. (No. Thirty out
of thirty had a sorry-but on the front of it. In the fourth month she
corrected me forty-one times and not one of them was softened and I
wrote that down at the time as a virtue.)*

*Whether there was any subject in four days that was larger than the
object on the table.*

He stopped there and sat with it.

*No. There was not. Thirty-two hours and the largest subject was a
requisition form.*

---

And then he found the gap.

It was on the third day and it was not in the entry at all, because
there was nothing to put in an entry — she had said something at about
four in the afternoon, standing at the far bench with her back to him,
in an ordinary voice, and he had said something back.

He could not remember what either of them was.

He sat at bench three and went at it for eleven minutes and got
nothing. He could reconstruct the light — late, gold, coming in low
under the awning the way it did in the seventh month. He could
reconstruct the smell of the tar going soft on the office roof, and the
pigeons, and the fact that his left hand had been aching because the
weather was about to turn and it had been doing that since the invasion.

He could not get the sentence.

*I have not lost a piece of a working day since I was nine years old.*

*I can tell you what Urushibara said in the eleventh month of last year
in a cold room with the fire not lit, and I can tell you the order he
said it in, and I have it in a notebook with my reading in brackets
kept separate from his words.*

*I can tell you what a fishmonger said to the woman behind me in a
market in the third month.*

*Somebody spoke to me for four days and I have thirty corrections and a
requisition form and a gap at four in the afternoon on the thirteenth.*

*I was not there.*

*I was at that bench for thirty-two hours and I was not in the yard for
any of it, and there is no version of that where the fault is hers.*

---

He filled in the form that evening.

It was a straightforward form and he had filled in nine hundred of
them. Establishment, batch, class, hours estimated, allocation, and
about two-thirds of the way down a line that read *Personnel requested
(leave blank if immaterial)*.

He looked at the line for some time.

*Leave blank if immaterial.*

*That is what the form says and that is what the office prefers, because
a blank line means the sealing office sends whoever is free and nobody
has to arrange anything, and I have left that line blank on every
requisition I have ever filed in my life, including the four in the
fifth month and the two in the seventh, one of which she then worked.*

*I have never once put a name on a form.*

*Because a name on that line is a request. It is a thing on paper, in
my hand, in an office, saying I would like this person and not another
one, and it goes to a clerk and gets filed and stays filed, and
anybody who ever looks will see that on the fourth of the ninth month
I asked.*

He wrote *Kurihara S. (sealwright, ext.)* on the line.

Then he read the whole form back once, the way he read everything, and
did not change it.

---

*Requisition 40-Q3/4, filed 4/9. Personnel line completed rather than
left blank.*

*This is the smallest possible act. It is four words on a form that a
clerk will read for nine seconds. It obliges nobody, it says nothing,
it can be declined by the sealing office without anybody being told
why, and she may not even see it — the office may simply assign her
because she is the person who does this batch.*

*I want that on the page because I can already feel myself preparing to
treat it as something, and it is not something.*

*What it is, is that for four months the correct move has been eleven
seconds of true thing said out loud in a yard, and I have not done it,
and I am not going to do it on a form, and I know that.*

*But I have stopped leaving the line blank.*
