# Chapter 54, Scene 3 — Armour

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's office. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1160 words.*

---

He counted it, because he could not help counting it.

Nineteen seconds.

Her hand was up at about the height of her sternum with the fingers
half closed, which was not a seal and was not anything, and it was the
only part of her that was moving, and it was moving the way a hand
moves when it is being held still by somebody.

*I have got this wrong.*

*I have got something wrong and I don't know which thing.*

He went through it while he sat there, because that was what he had
instead of a face.

*One: she is deciding whether to punish me, and this is deliberation.
That is what I assumed and it does not fit, because deliberation is
not this and I have watched her deliberate about nine hundred times.*

*Two: she cannot do it in front of me specifically.*

*Three—*

Her hand came down.

Not fast. It came down the way a thing comes down when the arm has
stopped being told anything, and it went to the edge of the desk and
took hold of it, and she looked at the wall over his left shoulder.

"No," said Tsunade.

---

He did not say anything.

That was the single most difficult thing he did all night and he did
it, and he did it because there was a version of him from about four
months earlier who would have said *why* in a helpful voice.

"No," she said again.

"All right."

"Don't *all right* me."

"All right."

She let go of the desk and picked the cup up and drank what was in it,
and poured, and the neck of the bottle went on the rim twice, which was
new.

"Thirty years," she said, to the wall. "Thirty years I've put that up.
You know what it's for?"

"No."

"Neither did I."

She sat down.

She had not sat down all night, and she did it now, heavily, in the
Hokage's chair, with the drink in her hand, and it made the whole room
different in a way he could not get at.

"Everyone thinks it's vanity," she said. "That's the version. Tsunade
of the Sannin walks about looking twenty-five because she can't stand
the sight of it. And I've let that run for thirty years because it's
convenient and because it's rude enough that nobody follows it up."

"Is it not that?"

"It's a bit that." She drank. "There's a bit of that in it, and I'd be
lying if I sat here and told you a woman doesn't—" She stopped. "It's
a bit that. It's not mainly that."

"What's it mainly?"

"You just found out," said Tsunade.

---

He sat with it.

*She put it up as a threat and she could not carry the threat out.*

*A threat you cannot carry out is not a threat. And a thing that is
only ever pointed at other people and never used is not a weapon
either.*

*It only works while nobody calls it.*

And then it went one further, and arrived, and he wished it hadn't.

*It has never been for other people.*

*She has been holding it up at everybody for thirty years so that
nobody ever gets close enough for her to have to look at it herself,
and it has worked perfectly, and the reason it stopped working tonight
is not that I was brave.*

*It's that I asked politely.*

"I'm sorry," he said.

"What for?"

"I didn't know that's what it was. I'd have—"

"You'd have what?"

He opened his mouth to say *I'd have found another way in*, and heard
it in advance, and did not say it.

"I don't know," he said instead.

Tsunade looked at him properly for the first time in about ten minutes.

"That's the first true thing you've said since you sat down," she said.

---

"That isn't fair."

"No," she agreed. "It isn't. Have some anyway."

"I don't drink."

"I know you don't drink. I'm not offering." She turned the cup round on
the desk. "I'm telling you that you've been in this room for
three-quarters of an hour and you've answered four objections and
you've been *right* four times and I am no closer to anything at all,
and I want you to sit there for a minute and ask yourself why that is."

"Because being right about the objections isn't—"

"Don't answer it, work it."

He worked it.

It took him longer than it should have done, and the reason it took him
longer was that the answer was in a place he did not look, which was
not the objections and not her and not the arithmetic.

"Because they're not why," he said slowly.

"Go on."

"They're — you gave me the rank and the village and the years, and they
are all real, and I took all three of them apart, and none of them was
ever the load-bearing one." He put his hands flat on his knees. "You
gave me the three you could defend. In order. Most defensible first.
That's how you'd brief a room you didn't want asking a follow-up."

"And?"

"And I dismantled all three and you haven't moved an inch, which means
the thing holding the structure up isn't in the structure."

Tsunade closed her eyes for a second.

"There it is," she said.

---

"So what's the fourth one?"

"No."

"Hime—"

"*No.*" She put the cup down. "And here's what I'm actually going to
say to you, and then I want you to think about it for a long time,
because it's the only useful sentence anybody's going to give you this
year."

He waited.

"You're doing the thing where you solve it."

He did not understand for a moment.

"You have been doing it since the second you sat down. Somebody hands
you a problem, you take it away, you find the constraint, you tell them
the constraint is a good constraint, and then you tell them it doesn't
bind. It's the finest bit of you and it's the reason there are people
walking about this village on legs, and it does not work here, and you
cannot see that it doesn't work because it has *never not worked*."

"I'm not trying to—"

"Ruri. I did not hand you a thing to fix."

And that landed, and he felt it land, and it did not go in anywhere
clever.

*She did not bring me a problem.*

*She asked me a question.*

*And I have spent forty minutes taking apart the reasons for the
question instead of answering it, and I did that because taking things
apart is the only thing I have ever been able to do in a room with
another person in it.*

*And she has been watching me do it since I was fifteen and she has
never said so until tonight.*

He looked at the desk.

Then he did the hardest thing available, which was nothing.

He stopped constructing.
