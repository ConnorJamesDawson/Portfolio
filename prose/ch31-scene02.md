# Chapter 31, Scene 2 — What You Didn't See

*Act 4, part C. Konoha, training ground eleven. Sazare Ruri, age
sixteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,715 words.*

---

"Describe the man who just left," Kakashi said.

They had been at the fundamentals for six weeks by then, in a corner of
training ground eleven that had a wall on two sides and a bench and
nothing else, and Sazare had been expecting to spend the morning
falling over, because the previous four Tuesdays had been spent falling
over.

A groundskeeper had come through about ninety seconds earlier with a
barrow, emptied a bin, and gone out the far gate.

"He's about fifty," Sazare said. "Left-handed — the bin was on his
right and he swung it across himself rather than turning. His boots are
resoled, both, badly, by the same person twice, and the left one's
worn through at the outside heel, which means he stands on it more than
the other one, which means something's wrong with his right hip or his
right knee. His jacket's village issue from at least three intakes ago;
the collar's turned. His barrow's got a new axle pin and an old
handle and somebody's whipped the handle with cord where it split. He
smokes — the first two fingers of the left hand. And he's been doing
this job long enough that he emptied that bin without looking at it."

Kakashi did not say anything.

"That's what I've got."

"That's a very good answer," Kakashi said, "to a question I didn't
ask."

He was sitting on the bench with one ankle across the opposite knee and
the book unopened beside him, which had happened perhaps twice in six
weeks. His flak jacket was undone. He wore the kunai pouch on the right
thigh, strapped high and very tight, and Sazare had noticed in the
first week that the flap on it was worn pale in a single narrow band
about two fingers wide and nowhere else, which meant that in however
many thousand draws, the hand had gone to exactly the same place every
time.

"I asked you to describe a man," Kakashi said. "You described his
equipment, his footwear, his employment history, his gait, and the
cord on his barrow handle. All of it correct, most of it useful, and I
could not pick that man out of a room of four using it."

"That's not—"

"Was he in a hurry?"

Sazare opened his mouth.

"Was he angry? Had he been crying? Did he like his job? Was he
frightened of anyone? Had he been told something bad this morning?"
Kakashi let each one land. "Was he going to steal that barrow?"

"...I don't know."

"You don't know. And you had ninety seconds, at eleven feet, in good
light, with nothing else happening." Kakashi uncrossed his ankle. "You
have the best untrained eye I've worked with in about nine years. It is
pointed entirely at things that don't have opinions."

---

"I read people," Sazare said, and heard how it sounded.

"You read *rooms*. You read what people leave behind them and what they
carry and what they've done to it, which is a genuine and rare skill,
and it works because objects hold still and tell the truth." Kakashi
stood. "A person doesn't hold still and doesn't tell the truth, and
about half of what you need to know about anybody is in the half-second
before they arrange their face."

"Then how is it teachable?"

"It's teachable because you're already three quarters of the way up the
ladder and nobody's ever told you there was a ladder."

He picked a stone up off the ground, turned it over, and put it back
down, which Sazare eventually understood was not a demonstration of
anything and simply what the man did with his hands.

"Rung one. The object. You're better at that than I am and I'm not
being generous, I mean it as a fact — I could not have told you that
barrow had a new axle pin. Rung two is the object *in use*. Not the
pouch: how the pouch sits. Mine is high and tight. What does that tell
you?"

Sazare looked at it properly.

"That you draw from it constantly, and that you can't afford it to move
when you do." He thought further. "And that you've never had to reach
it with the other hand, or you'd wear it lower and more central."

"Correct, and both of those are facts about my *habits*, which is rung
three. Habits are the first thing about a person that's actually about
the person. From a habit you get a preference. From a preference you
get an intention, which is rung four. And rung five is what he's
feeling, and you will not get there directly for about two years, so
stop trying to and stop being ashamed of not managing it."

"You want me to work upward."

"I want you to work upward, one rung, out loud, every time, until it
stops being a ladder and starts being a look." Kakashi nodded at the
far gate. "Go again. Same man. Start at the boots and get me one rung
past where you stopped."

---

It took four days to get one rung.

He did it on a woman selling roasted chestnuts at the corner of the
Nakadōri, whom he watched for eleven minutes from a bench with his leg
straight out in front of him and a bag of chestnuts he did not want.

She was fifty or so, broad through the shoulders, in three layers with
the outer one a man's padded jacket that had been made for somebody a
head taller — the cuffs turned back twice and stitched to stay turned,
which is not a repair anybody does for a garment they intend to give
back. She stood with her weight even, which was rare in a person who
stood all day, and she turned from the hips rather than stepping, which
kept her feet in one place for hours. Her right sleeve was scorched
along the forearm in a line. She had a scoop in her right hand and
never put it down, not once, not even when she took money.

*Habit: she never puts the scoop down.*

*Preference: she wants her right hand full.*

And he sat with that for four minutes and could not get past it, and
then a boy of about nine came up to the stall and she gave him a bag
without taking money, and did it with her left hand, and the scoop
never moved.

*She keeps her right hand on the thing she'd have to pick up again.*

*She's not guarding the scoop. She's saving the four seconds it takes
to find it. She is running that stall alone and she has been running it
alone long enough to have removed every unnecessary movement from her
day, and the reason she gives that boy a bag for nothing is not
generosity, or not only — it's that arguing about it would cost her
more than the chestnuts.*

He went back to Kakashi with it on the Friday and got through about
half before Kakashi held a hand up.

"Stop there. That's rung four and you got to it from a scoop." He
picked up the book, which meant the lesson was ending. "Now the part
you'll like less. What was she feeling?"

"I don't know."

"Guess."

"I don't guess."

"I know you don't. That's the problem, and I want you to notice that
it's a *choice* you made a long time ago and not a fact about your
head." Kakashi found his page. "You're precise because precision kept
you alive, and a guess is a thing that can be wrong, and being wrong in
front of somebody has cost you more than it costs most people. So you
built a rule that says *I don't guess*, and it's a good rule for
crystal and a terrible rule for people, because with people the choice
isn't between a guess and certainty. It's between a guess and nothing."

---

The last thing he said, that Friday, was the thing Sazare wrote down
that night.

"There's a version of this I should have said six weeks ago and
didn't, because I wanted you to find the ladder yourself first."
Kakashi had the book open and was not reading it. "Three headbands
from one die, on a market row, in the fifth month."

Sazare went still.

"Ibiki's already had that conversation with me."

"Ibiki had a conversation with you about *reporting*, which is his job
and which he was right about." Kakashi turned a page he hadn't read.
"I'm having a different one. You saw three protectors from one die and
you concluded *procurement*. That was correct. That was an excellent
piece of work off almost nothing, and every single part of it was rung
one — the metal, the stamp, the cut, the wear."

"And?"

"And there were three men attached to them, and you cannot tell me one
thing about any of them. Not how they stood. Not whether they were
comfortable. Not whether the man in the middle deferred to the man on
his left, which he would have, because somebody in a group of three is
always in charge and it shows in about four seconds if you know to
look." He closed the book. "You got the objects and missed the people,
and the objects gave you *procurement*, and the people would have given
you *purpose*."

The training ground was very quiet. Somewhere beyond the wall a
practice range was running, the same four sounds over and over.

"Would it have changed anything," Sazare said.

"Probably not. Ibiki's right about that, and I'm not going to hand you
a bigger stone to carry than the one you've already got." Kakashi stood
and dusted the back of his trousers off. "I'm telling you because
you're about to be evaluated by people who will put you in a position
with an enemy in front of it, and you will read the ground perfectly,
and then a person will do something the ground didn't predict."

"And I'll miss it."

"You'll miss it," Kakashi agreed, "and then you'll survive missing it,
because that's what the ground is for. But I'd rather you knew what you
were compensating for. Nine o'clock Monday. Bring the leg."
