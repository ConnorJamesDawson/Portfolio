# Chapter 65, Scene 1 — What Travels

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the north store and the hospital. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1930 words.*

---

He started collecting them on the twenty-second because he could not help
it and because by the twenty-second there were four.

*Version one. Kanzaki, in the yard, on the evening of the twenty-first,
who had it off a man in the annexe who had it off a nurse.*

*"You put the Uzumaki boy through a wall."*

*There was no wall. There were two walls and he went round both of them
and I was inside the second one at the time.*

*Version two. Emiko, in the corridor, twenty-second, who did not tell me
where she had it from and who asked whether I was all right in a way that
suggested somebody had told her I was not.*

*"They said the ground grew."*

*That one is nearly right and it is the only one that is and she does not
know that it is.*

*Version three. A clerk on the records stair, twenty-second, who did not
know I was the subject and was telling somebody else.*

*"He turned him to stone."*

*Version four. Sakura, twenty-second, in the ward, who had heard it in a
tea shop from two chūnin who were arguing about it and who reported it to
me with her jaw set.*

*"The Hokage's crystal user nearly killed the jinchūriki."*

---

He stopped writing at the fourth one.

*She said a word.*

*She said a word I have not got and she said it as though it were an
ordinary noun, and she has read the room since I was fifteen and she has
never once been careless in front of me.*

*So it is a word people use.*

*And I have got a boy on the second floor with a spiral tibia and
something in his abdomen that is not tissue, and a woman who went the
colour of the underside of a leaf, and a man who looked at the abdomen
and not at the head.*

*It is not a wall.*

He put the pencil down.

*I know where the wall is. I said so in a corridor on the twentieth and I
thanked her for showing me where it was and I asked for it recorded that
I knew what I was agreeing to.*

*Forty-eight hours.*

He drew a line under version four and did not write the word down, and
went back to the store.

---

*Fifth version, twenty-third, which is the one that matters and which I
got from the fishmonger's boy who is eleven and who did not know he was
telling me anything.*

*"He made the crystal come alive."*

He sat down on the step of the cart door with that one for a while.

*That is not wrong.*

*Every other version so far has been wrong in a direction — bigger,
worse, more deliberate — and I have got four and they have all grown in
the telling and I had assumed that was the whole of the mechanism.*

*This one has not grown. It has got *smaller*, and it has got smaller in
exactly the right way, and it is the shortest true sentence about the
twentieth of the twelfth month that anybody has produced including me.*

*An eleven-year-old with a basket.*

*Right. So the thing that travels is not the leg and it is not the mode
and it is not the ninety seconds where I did nothing.*

*It is one clause and it is accurate and it is going to be in four
countries inside a month.*

---

He did the arithmetic properly that evening, at the bench, with the lamp
on and nothing else going, and it was the worst hour he had spent since
the eleventh.

*Why did I win?*

*Not: what did I do. I have got what I did and it took me four hours and
eleven minutes to write and it is nine pages and it is honest.*

*Why did it work?*

*Because at four minutes fifty I came out of the back of my own box at a
run and left about eleven pieces of it lying on the packed earth in a
twelve-foot circle.*

*And they lay there for a minute and a half.*

*And he stepped over them, and round them, and past them, about nine
times, and on the fourth step of a charge at five minutes fifty-one he
put his foot down on one of them.*

*He never looked at the ground.*

*Not once. I have gone back through the whole of it and there is not one
instant anywhere in six minutes where that boy looked at the floor.*

He put both hands flat on the bench.

*Why would he?*

*Nobody looks at the floor. There has never been a reason to look at the
floor. In the entire history of fighting in four countries, the debris a
man leaves behind him is *debris* — it is spent, it is over, it is the
wreckage of the thing he tried four minutes ago and it is worth exactly
nothing, and every single person alive has been trained since they were
nine to ignore it and be right to.*

*I won because Uzumaki Naruto did not consider that my rubbish was still
mine.*

*And nobody was ever going to consider it, because until the twentieth of
the twelfth month it was not true of anybody.*

---

*So say the finding.*

He wrote it out in the ordinary notation and did not soften it.

*23/12. Finding, and it is the only one that matters out of the whole of
the twentieth.*

*My advantage in that fight was that nobody knew what I was.*

*Not surprise. Not tactics. Not capacity and not the mode. It was that a
sixteen-year-old with two years in the field and forty techniques had
built a completely reasonable model of me off four Corps papers and a
reputation, and the model did not have* the floor is alive *in it,
because nothing on this earth has ever had that in it.*

*It was an unknown. Not a secret — I did not conceal it, I did not know
it myself until about five minutes fifty on the day.*

*And an unknown is worth an enormous amount and it can be spent exactly
once.*

*I have spent it.*

*In front of six witnesses, two of whom are not human and two of whom
went home to a mountain, and in a story that an eleven-year-old with a
basket had in a shortened and accurate form by the twenty-third.*

*It is gone. It is not coming back and there is no version of the next
forty years in which anybody I fight has not heard it.*

---

And then he found the second half, at about half past nine, and put the
pencil down and did not pick it up for eleven minutes.

*And the rest of me has been public for four years.*

He got the box out.

*Splint study, rejected seventh month of last year, resubmitted ninth
month with the reject table attached. Four hundred rows. Method section
of about eleven pages.*

*Four Corps papers with my name on them, all of them with method
sections, all of them in the annexe, all of them requestable by anybody
with a card and by about nine hundred people in four countries who do not
need a card.*

*The surplus reports.*

*The amendment rule, which went in under her name and not mine and which
is the only thing I have ever published that does not have me in it.*

*And a man in the Land of Sound has been reading the method sections
since the fifth month and got to the six-day problem off one question
about a splint cohort, three months behind me, and told me so in a
letter on the eleventh of this month without meaning to.*

He sat on the step in the dark.

*I have spent four years writing down exactly how I work and putting it
in a public archive.*

*Every single thing about me is legible except the one thing that was
never true until the twentieth.*

*And it is not a mistake and I would do all of it again — that is the
whole of what I am for, it is why the reject table went through and it is
why there is a post and it is why there is a room the size of a barn
behind this hospital.*

*But I would like it written down that on the twenty-third of the twelfth
month, at about half past nine at night, I understood that I have
published my own manual, and that the only thing that saved me on the
twentieth was that my opponent had not read it.*

*And that everybody else will.*

---

He went up to the ward at ten because he had said he would look at the
leg at ten.

Naruto was awake and had been awake for four days and had reached the
stage of a healthy sixteen-year-old confined to a bed, which was that he
had rearranged everything within reach twice and had started on the
window.

"They're saying I got put through a wall," he said.

"I know."

"There wasn't a wall!"

"There were two walls," said Sazare, "and you went round both of them and
I was inside the second one, and that is going to be in the version by
about the fourth month and there is nothing either of us can do about it."

"Well, that's *rubbish*."

"It's not rubbish. It's better than the truth."

Naruto stopped fiddling with the window catch.

"How is it better?"

"Because a man who puts somebody through a wall is a man with a
technique," said Sazare, "and people prepare against techniques. They
learn the counter and they read the paper and they turn up ready.

"A man who makes the ground come alive is not a technique. It is a thing
that nobody has got a counter to because there has never been one, and
about nine-tenths of the people who hear it are going to build a version
of it in their heads that is considerably worse than what I can actually
do.

"And I am not going to correct any of them."

---

Naruto looked at him.

"That's the second time you've done that," he said.

"Done what?"

"The thing where you find out something horrible about yourself and then
about four seconds later you work out how to *use* it." He shifted the
leg, badly. "You did it in the lane with the sword. You went all weird
and then you told me you'd put it on your belt so people would ask about
it instead of your face.

"And now you've gone all quiet about the wall thing and then you've come
out with — that."

"Yes."

"Doesn't it bother you?"

"It bothered me for about eleven minutes at half past nine tonight," said
Sazare, "and then I found the useful half, and the useful half does not
make the first half not true.

"I have spent four years telling four countries exactly how I work. That
is a fact and it is not going to change and I am going to have to fight
somebody informed at some point in the next year.

"And the story going round this village is bigger than I am and it is
going to keep getting bigger, and for about eighteen months that is going
to be worth more to me than anything I can actually do.

"Both of those are true. I would rather have both than one."
