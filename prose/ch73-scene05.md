# Chapter 73, Scene 5 — Both Papers

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the garden of Tsunade's house. Tsunade, age
fifty-three. POV: Tsunade. ~1060 words.*

---

She picked up her own first, because her own was the coward's
order and she had earned one act of cowardice in a garden she
had just won a fight in.

*So open his, then,* said the part of her that had never once
let her get away with anything. *Victor opens both. You wrote
the clause.*

She opened his.

Three words, in the small exact hand, in the middle of the
paper with the ruled fold lines squaring them like a diagram:

*The deep end.*

She stood very still with it.

Then she opened her own, which she had sealed on Thursday night
on the edge of a made bed in a lamplit room, and held them side
by side in the failing light of the fourteenth of the third
month, two pieces of paper written two days apart in two
different buildings by two people who had not discussed it, and
they said, in two hands:

*The deep end.*

*The deep end.*

"Oh, you *idiot*," said Tsunade, and her voice came out
wrecked.

---

He did not ask what hers said. That was the thing that got
her, standing there — that he stood four feet away in a
destroyed garden with grass in his hair and did not ask,
because the victor opened both and the terms had been agreed
and he would sooner have cut his own hand off than take a
piece of information that had not been offered.

She turned the papers around and held them up so he could see
them together.

She watched him read them.

She watched — because she had spent three years learning this
face, and she had never once had a better seat than this one —
the whole thing arrive: the four decimal places of him going
still, and then the recalculation, the enormous silent
rearranging of a man discovering that a thing he had been
carrying alone had been carried in parallel the entire time by
somebody who had never said so either.

"We wrote the same words," he said.

"We wrote the same words."

"Two days apart. In two buildings."

"Yes."

"Neither of us asked."

"Neither of us *can* ask," said Tsunade, "which is the whole
disease, fabricator. You can't take a thing that isn't
specified and I can't say a thing that isn't a document, and
between us we have built the most ridiculous, watertight,
mutually-assured silence in the history of two people who
have been in love since November." She was aware that her
hands were not entirely steady and that she did not care.
"So we did the only thing either of us knows how to do. We
built a format, and we put the truth inside it, and we let
the format say it."

*And it said the same thing,* said the register, at the very
back of her, in a small voice she had not heard from herself
in about thirty-five years. *He wrote it Wednesday or
Thursday, sitting at that desk, with no idea what was in
mine, and he sealed it and carried it into a garden and
fought me for the right to have somebody else read it.*

"Say it out loud," she said. "Once. Not the paper — the
paper's a document. I want it in your voice, and then we're
going inside."

She watched him pay the toll. It cost him what it always
cost him — the copper coming up his throat and into his face
in the last of the light, the whole physiological bill
arriving in full — and the voice, when it came, was the
level one, the one that had carried a governor and a
confession and a treaty and every large true thing he had
ever put in front of her.

"I want the deep end," said Sazare Ruri. "With you. Tonight,
if tonight is what you want — and I have wanted it since
before I had any standing to want anything, and I have never
once been able to say so, because you have never specified
it and I do not take what is not specified. So specify it,
Hime. Please. Because I am eighteen years old and I have
been standing at the edge of this water for four months
waiting to be allowed in."

The bird over the wall had stopped. The garden was
absolutely silent, and every article in it was hers, and
every one of them had been made by him.

"Specified," said Tsunade.

And then, because she was who she was, and because the two
of them had built the whole of this out of formats and there
was no earthly reason to stop now, she folded both papers
together, squared them the way he squared everything, and
put them in her sleeve, and said, in the flat
administrative voice, with her heart going like a hammer:

"Prize awarded. Both parties in agreement, terms witnessed
by nobody, no file, no reference number." She held out her
hand. "Come inside, fabricator. It's cold, you're covered
in my garden, and I have had a lamp on that nightstand
since Thursday."

He took her hand.

*Since Thursday,* she thought, halfway to the door, with his
fingers wrapped in hers and the dark coming down over the
wall. *He'll have that. He'll have worked out the lamp
inside a minute of seeing the room, because he reads rooms,
because he has read every room he has ever stood in since he
was a boy in a shop over the water.*

*Let him. Let him read the whole thing. The made bed and the
new lamp and the four rooms with nothing in them and the
glass on the nightstand with three weeks of dust on it, and
the woman who has lived out of a tower for eleven years
because the alternative was expecting somebody back.*

*Read all of it, and then look at me anyway. That's the
whole of what I'm frightened of, and it's the only thing in
fifty-three years I've never once had to be frightened of
with him, and I am still frightened of it, and we are going
in anyway.*

*The deep end.*

*Both of us wrote it. Neither of us has any idea what we're
doing.*

*In.*

She opened the door of her own house, and took the boy she
loved inside it, and closed it behind them both.
