# Chapter 5, Scene 1 — Second Best

*Act 1, beat 12. Sazare Ruri, age ten to eleven. POV: Sazare. ~2,600 words.*

---

In his last year the unit stopped being a unit.

There was no announcement about it. There had been four sessions in the fifth
month of his ninth year and four more in his tenth, and then in the eleventh year
there were no sessions at all, and Sazare noticed this with relief for about a
fortnight before he worked out what had actually happened, which was that it had
gone into everything else.

It was in the history now. The Second War had four men in a room, and Mizuho still
did all four of them, but now there was a paragraph before it about the clan
levies and how they had been raised and what had happened when two of them
refused. It was in the geography: the eastern hills got a line about
*concentrations of inherited technique in isolated settlement*, which had not been
there the year before, because Sazare had copied the eastern hills out twice.

It was even in the tactics, which was a new subject that year and which everybody
loved, because it was mostly about ambushes. There was a section on *irregular
capability* and how you planned around it. It had a worked example. The worked
example was three paragraphs long and did not name anybody and was, quite
obviously, the Kaguya.

Nobody in the room said so. Thirty-one children — they were thirty-one now — sat
and copied out a worked example about a night two years before that every single
one of them had lain awake through, and put their pens down, and went to lunch.

---

Mizuho had got older.

Sazare could not have dated it. She had always been past forty and she was still
past forty, and she still sat on the front desk with one boot up, and she still
did the voices. But she had begun, that year, to do something with her hands when
she was reading out of a book — a slow turning of one thumb against the other,
over and over, which she did not appear to know about.

She only did it with certain books.

He watched her do it through the whole of the eastern hills, and through the
worked example, and through a session on *village cohesion* that was so dull that
two boys at the back went to sleep, and then she closed the book and said "right,
bowls," and the thumbs stopped.

---

He was, by then, extremely good at the bowls.

He had done nine minutes in the seventh month, which was three more than anybody
else in the year had ever done, and Mizuho had watched the whole nine minutes
without saying anything and then said "again" and he had done eight and a half.

It was not a thing you could be admired for. That was what he liked about it. The
boys who were good at throwing got admired; the girls who were good at the
climbing wall got admired; nobody had ever once been admired for sitting
absolutely still in front of a bowl of water for a quarter of an hour, and Jirō —
who could not do two minutes and had reached a state of philosophical acceptance
about it — described the entire discipline as *the most boring thing that has ever
happened to anybody*.

Sazare did it in the evenings as well. He did not tell anyone that.

He had worked out, some time in the eighth month, that the bowl and the frame were
the same thing. Nobody had told him. His mother had never once said *this is like
that*. But you sat, and you put your hands either side of a thing, and you found
where it was moving, and you did not do anything about it — and the difference
between four hours and nine minutes was only how long you could keep from
interfering, and he had a suspicion, which he did not test, that his mother could
have done the bowl until the lamps went out.

---

The graduation streams got read out in the ninth month.

It was not a decision, Mizuho said. It was a *note*. It went in your file and the
examiners looked at it and then made their own minds up, and it was not a
decision, and she said this four separate times in the space of a morning, which
Sazare found reassuring at the time.

Combat. Reconnaissance. Support. Sealing and administration, which everybody
called *the desk* and which was where you went if you were clever and slow.

She read the list out. Jirō got reconnaissance and was so pleased he could not
speak for about a minute.

Sazare got support.

"That's not a decision," said Mizuho.

"You said."

"I did say. I'll say it again in a minute." She was collecting the bowls. "Do you
know what support is?"

"It's medics and supply."

"It's medics and supply, and messages, and anything anybody hasn't got a word for.
It's about a third of a village and it's the third that doesn't get talked about."
She stacked. "There's worse places to be put."

"Jirō got reconnaissance."

"Jirō," said Mizuho, "can run for eleven hours and cannot sit down for nine
minutes, and if I had put him in support he would have died of it before the
first winter." She put the stack on the shelf. "Sit down a minute."

---

He sat down.

She did not sit down. She leaned against the front desk with her arms folded and
looked at the door, which was open, and then went and shut it, and came back, and
leaned on the desk again in the same position.

"How long can you do the water?"

"Nine minutes."

"How long can you *actually* do the water?"

Sazare hesitated.

"Fourteen," he said.

"Fourteen." Mizuho did not look surprised, which was somehow worse than if she
had. "And how long are you going to do in front of the examiners in the spring?"

"...Fourteen?"

"No," said Mizuho.

He waited.

"You're going to do about seven," she said. "Seven's very good. Seven's better
than anyone else in that room will manage and it'll get you through and it'll get
you a decent note in your file, and nobody who reads it will ever think about you
again, and that's exactly what you want."

"But if I can do fourteen—"

"Sazare."

"It doesn't *make sense*. Why would I do worse on purpose—"

"Because you don't show people your best," said Mizuho. "Not ever. Not once. You
show them your second best, because your second best is enough for almost
everything, and your best is *yours*, and the day you hand it over you don't get
it back."

She said it in an entirely level voice, the way she said everything, the way she
had read out the four categories.

Sazare sat with his hands on his knees and worked at it and could not get it to
come out to anything except unfairness, which at ten and a half was the only shape
he had for it.

"Is that what you did?" he said.

Mizuho laughed — one short breath through the nose, not really a laugh at all.

"I'm a schoolteacher," she said. "What do you think?"

---

He told Jirō about the streams on the way down the hill, and did not tell him the
rest.

"*Support?*"

"It's a third of a village."

"It's *bandages*."

"It's not just bandages, it's supply as well, and messages—"

"It's *bandages* and *carrying things*," said Jirō, who had been given
reconnaissance forty minutes ago and had been an authority on the entire shinobi
profession for approximately thirty-nine of them. Then, seeing Sazare's face, with
the immediate and total loyalty that was the best thing about him: "It's not
though. My mum's cousin's in support. He's got a house."

"Has he?"

"He's got a *house*, Sazare."

They thought about this.

"Are you going to be all right?" said Jirō.

"What?"

"In support." Jirō had gone slightly pink again. "Because — right, so. My brother
says the streams don't matter for the first bit but they matter later, because
later they put you where your note says, and if your note says support then you're
where they can find you." He shrugged, and looked at the water. "And he says
that's better. He says being where they can find you is better. So."

"So what?"

"So it's good," said Jirō. "That's all. It's good. Shut up."

---

At home he told them at dinner and Iwao said "*support*" in a tone that could have
stripped paint, and Ren said "that's the one with the medics, isn't it, that's good,
that's steady," and his mother said nothing whatsoever until much later, when he
was doing the small lattices on the step and she came out and sat down and did the
big ones.

"Support," she said.

"Mizuho says it's a third of a village."

"It's more than that. They just don't count it properly." She turned a lattice
over. "Your grandmother was support. Nobody ever mentions it because there's
nothing to mention. She did forty-one years and came home every night of them."

"Did she like it?"

"I never asked her," said Hisui, in a slightly odd voice, and then, after a
moment: "She'd have said it was steady. She said everything was steady. It was the
only word she had for anything that hadn't caught fire."

They did the lattices.

"Mum."

"Mm."

"How long can you do the water?"

His mother's hands stopped for about half a second.

"I've never done the water," she said. "There weren't bowls when I was at the
Academy. There was a stone and you had to keep a leaf on it in a wind."

"How long could you do that?"

"Long enough that they stopped asking me to," said Hisui, and put the lattice down
and picked up the next one, and Sazare — who was ten years old and had been given
two separate pieces of the same instruction that day by two different women and
had not noticed that either of them was an instruction — went on doing the
corners, properly, because she would check.
