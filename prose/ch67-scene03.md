# Chapter 67, Scene 3 — Chapter Twelve

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the upper room. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~840 words.*

---

He conducted the study on the Friday night, at the bench in the upper
room, with the door shut and the lamp trimmed and the small orange
volume propped on the stand he used for grading tables, because if a
thing was going to be a syllabus it was going to be treated like one.

The fourteen notes were still in the back in his hand, numbered, the
anatomy and the arithmetic and the three that were not either. He had
made them in December as an editor, in the same register he would
audit a splint method, and the register had held perfectly for
fourteen notes and two readings.

*So the material is known,* he wrote. *The material has been read
twice, cold, without incident. Tonight's variable is not the
material. Tonight's variable is the application.*

The distinction had arrived on the ferry and had been growing since.
Reading the book as a document about other people was one operation.
Reading it as a manual — running each scene forward with two actual
persons substituted into it, one of whom was himself, the other of
whom was upstairs signing correspondence with her hair down — was, it
turned out, an entirely different operation, conducted by entirely
different equipment.

He established this at chapter twelve.

Chapter twelve was the bath chapter. He had noted it in December —
note nine, on the plumbing, a nine-hundred-ryō repair narrated as
though it were nothing, which in December had been the chapter's most
interesting feature. The couple share a mixed bath at a mountain inn.
There is steam, and a misunderstanding about towels, and then the
twist arrives — the twist being the thing the woman does at the
midpoint of the pool, which the author spends a page and a half on,
and which Sazare had read in December with the detached interest of a
man confirming that human shoulders do not, in fact, articulate that
way without training.

He substituted the persons and began the run.

He got four paragraphs.

*Interruption one,* he logged, with a hand that was not entirely
level, which he noted, because the hand was data too. *Pulse
elevated, estimate ninety and climbing. Focus loss at the fourth
paragraph — the page continued and comprehension did not. Heat,
peripheral vasodilation, and a specific systemic clamour that I am
declining to itemise but which made three years of medical vocabulary
unavailable simultaneously. Recovery: four minutes, kettle, the
reject table recited to row forty.*

He went back in.

He got two paragraphs.

*Interruption two. Worse, not better. The material is compounding —
the second attempt began from the first attempt's state, not from
baseline. Note: this is exactly the loading curve of capsaicin, which
I have understood since I was nine years old and a cousin bet me
a copper I could not eat the red one. Tolerance to heat is real,
trainable, and famously not built by eating the hottest thing in the
jar twice. It is built in graduations.*

He put the book down, capped it with the grading weight, and stood at
the window for a while, watching the snow come down through the lamp
spill, letting the equipment settle, thinking with the part of him
that had survived every other deep end.

*So. The finding, honestly stated: I cannot read chapter twelve as an
application. A man who cannot read the scene is some distance from
conducting it. And the objective—*

He went back to the bench and wrote the objective, because an
objective unwritten is a wish.

*Study: tolerance. Subject: self. Stimulus class: cf. II-12.
Baseline established tonight: read incomplete, twice; compounding
observed. Objective: exposure without system failure. Method: graded,
increments to be determined. Note: the subject has been sleeping
beside the stimulus for two months, which the literature would call
habituation and which has produced no measurable habituation
whatsoever, an anomaly the study does not currently explain.*

He looked at the entry. The encoding was sound — *II-12* meant
nothing to anybody but him, the volume itself lived in the inner
pocket of a coat nobody searched, and the word *stimulus* was doing
work it would answer for later.

*Step one,* he wrote, *is finding the limit.*

And then, because his honesty had a bench of its own and worked
nights: *The limit is not going to be where the protocol thinks it
is. The limit has never once been where the protocol thinks it is.
On every deep end of my life — the chain, the ditch, the wood — I
planned graduations and the world declined them. There is no reason
to expect the world has changed policy.*

*Proceed anyway. A protocol that expects to be overtaken is still a
protocol. And the alternative is another eight weeks of receiving.*

He shut the notebook, and banked the lamp, and went up to bed beside
the stimulus, who took hold of his shirt at some point in the small
hours with both hands, as documented, as always, and thereby reset
whatever tolerance the evening's four paragraphs had built, back to
zero, where it apparently lived.
