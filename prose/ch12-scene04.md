# Chapter 12, Scene 4 — Alive Only

*Act 2, part C. Sazare Ruri, age fifteen. POV: Sazare. ~2,300 words.*

---

The bounty men came up the valley in the mud month, two of them, off the
coast road, and stayed at the cheap inn and soaked at the old bathhouse,
and were, by the standards of everything Sazare had spent three years
bracing for, disappointingly ordinary.

They were not hunting him. He established that within the hour, from the
edges of their talk in the water, the way he established everything: they
were after a grain factor from down the coast who had run with a season's
advances, and they were following the man's brother-in-law's boasting up
the spring towns one inn at a time, and they were tired, and their feet
hurt, and the younger one wanted to be home for his sister's wedding.

Trade gossip came off them like steam. Work was strange on the coast this
year, said the older one to a quarry foreman who'd asked; the debt lists
were short and the *retrieval* lists were long. "Retrieval?" said the
foreman, and the older one said, "Water's buying its strays back. Paying
proper rates, too, cash on the border, no questions," and the bath
settled a fraction, the way baths did in that town when the war put its
head round the door, and somebody changed the subject to the wedding.

Sazare sat in the steam with his eyes half shut and his sleeves — he was
the only person in a bathhouse with a cloth on his arm, burn-dressing,
Tōan's suggestion from two years back, renewed without comment ever
since — and finished the arithmetic. *Buying its strays back. Cash on the
border.* Then he went home and said nothing to anyone and lay awake until
the third hour, listening to the river.

The book was the next morning, and it was not bravery, and he never
pretended it had been. The men had gone up to the quarry office to show
their papers around. Their gear stayed racked in the inn's entry the way
road gear did, and the book was there in it — he had seen the older man
carry it, oilcloth packet, breast of the coat, the size of a prayer-book
and handled like one — and the entry hall was empty, and the innkeeper's
girl was hanging wash in the yard, and Sazare stood in the doorway with
his heart going and every instruction of four years standing in a row
telling him to walk on.

Curiosity went past them the way it had gone past them at every door of
his entire life, without looking.

Ninety seconds. He had counted the girl's round at the wash-line; he was
a boy waiting out of the rain; his hands did not hurry. The packet
unwrapped to a fat, sweat-swollen, much-thumbed ledger of the dead and
the priced, tabbed by country along the fore-edge in a clerk's neat
ink — and there was a tab for the Land of Water, and it was the thickest
tab in the book, and he went down its pages with the flat sliding
attention of the chip game, not reading, *recognising*, until his own
name came up at him off the paper like a hand.

**RURI, SAZARE.**

He read it the way he had read the sequestration notice on the gate,
three times, the words arriving out of order and assembling late.

*Male. Kirigakure, lower district. Age at listing 13; current 15–16.
Kekkei genkai: Shōton (crystal). Sole surviving holder.* Then, in the
dense clerk's shorthand of the trade, the square: *killing of a state
officer, public sentencing, crystal projection at approx. ten yards.
Prior trade assessment (district record: ornamental grade,
non-combatant) VOID — see incident. District annex notes anomalous
aptitude, interrupted-cycle growth, recorded age 10.*

The clerk's kindness, five years down the road, filed as a caveat. *He'll
get looked at properly one of these days*, the man had told his wife,
pleased with the wording, and here was the look, and here was the
properly: one line of warm handwriting in a district annex, boiled down
by some intelligence clerk into the single sentence that told every
hunter with a book never to trust the earrings story again.

*Identifying: heavy scarring, right forearm and palm, characteristic
(exit-pattern). Slight build, undersized for age (at listing).
Approach: subject presents as non-combatant tradesman. Do not rely.*

And at the bottom, boxed, in the space where the money lived:

*ALIVE ONLY. Standard rate plus retrieval premium, paid at any Water
mission or border post. No proof-of-death fee. Damage to hands or
forearms voids premium.*

He stood in the entry of a country inn with rain on the step behind him
and read the last line four times.

*Damage to hands voids premium.*

Somewhere east, under the fog, an office had thought about him. Not
hated him — he'd have understood hated; hate was the square, hate was
comprehensible. An office had sat down with what the file held —
Schedule B, the annex, four names in block capitals, a shelf grown over
two nights by a ten-year-old — and had weighed him, and priced him, and
specified his packaging, and what the office had concluded was that
Sazare Ruri, murderer of a state officer in front of four hundred
witnesses, was to be brought back *undamaged*, *with particular care
taken of the hands*, because the hands were the asset, because dead he
was a closed file and alive he was — Anzu's voice, flat, through a wall
of planks: *say what he is* —

Stock. Breeding stock, working stock; the last frame of a
four-hundred-year line, wanted back at the trestles.

He rewrapped the book in its oilcloth with the fold-lines matched, and
racked it exactly, and walked out past the wash-line into the rain, and
was sick, quietly and thoroughly, behind the woodpile of an inn he never
in his life went into again.

---

He told Tōan that evening, because an assistant who might bring hunters
up a valley was a fact with medical standing, and because the house's
laws had one unwritten rider he had learned by now: *how you came to be
here* was nobody's business, but *what might come here after you* was
the household's.

Tōan heard the whole of it — the entry, the void, the annex line, the
boxed words at the bottom — with his hands flat on the bench, and was
quiet for a while.

"Two years listed," he said at last. "And this is the first book through
this town, and it came hunting a grain factor. Books don't hunt, boy.
Men hunt, and men go where the money's fresh, and yours is two years
stale and priced for specialists — *alive, undamaged, premium* is a
job for a team, not for two footsore lads chasing a debtor. You've years
yet before that price finds feet. I'd not pack tonight."

"But it finds them."

"Prices like that always find feet," said Tōan, evenly. "Yes."

Sazare looked at the shutters, and the lamp, and the good scales, and
the whole small mended world of the last two years, and asked the
question underneath the question, the one he had been carrying up the
hill all day like a stone in the boot:

"When it does — what happens to the town it finds me in?"

And Tōan, who did not lie, and whose not-lying was the whole
architecture of him, the way thinness had been the architecture of
Kohaku's people —

said nothing at all.

The stove ticked. Up the gorge the sawmill had shut down for the night,
and the river ran warm under the floor, carrying its heat down the
valley to people who had never once asked him a question he couldn't
afford.

"Right," said Sazare.

"Not tonight," said Tōan. "Not this season, and likely not this year.
You'll go when there's a reason, not when there's a fright — a man who
moves on frights arrives everywhere out of breath and leaves a trail of
relieved landlords, and that's a *description*, boy, that's the easiest
description in the world to sell to a man with a book. You'll go slow,
you'll go planned, and you'll go — " and here the dosage voice cracked,
one degree, the width of a hair, for the first time in two years —
"you'll go *taught*. There's a difference between a boy who ran and a
man who left. We've time enough yet to finish making the second one."

He took the kettle off, and poured, and the household's third law held
the silence after that the way the lattice held the sand.

But that night Sazare heard the scales chime and the spoon tap on the
jar rim into the small hours, orders for nobody, measure after measure
after measure; and up in the dark of his room, fifteen years old,
priced, undamaged, and two years from the road, he began — quietly, in
his head, in the only ledger nobody could read — the count of what he
owed this valley, entry by entry, against the day the bill came due.
