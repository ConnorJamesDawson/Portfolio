# Chapter 71, Scene 6 — The Laxness Audit

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the second floor. Tsunade, age
fifty-three. POV: Tsunade. ~1000 words.*

---

He reported it the same evening, before the hair, because he
reported everything and because this one had a clock on it.

He gave it to her the way he gave all findings — in order,
verbatim where verbatim mattered — and she listened the whole
way through with the stillness she kept for casualty reports,
and when he reached *the coat has stopped fitting* she closed
her eyes, and when he reached *she protected my mother once,
when I was small* she kept them closed a moment longer than
the sentence strictly required, because she remembered the
night in question, and the frightened girl on the engawa, and
had not known until this evening that the girl had been old
enough to keep it.

"Hyūga Hinata," she said, at last. "Of every set of eyes in
that clan. Do you understand the odds we drew? If it had been
the father — if it had been any elder of the main house — that
information is on a council agenda by spring, dressed as
concern. Instead it lands in the one Hyūga alive who treats
seeing as a debt she owes the seen." She opened her eyes.
"We got the luck my cards never give me. Which means we don't
get it twice. Audit. Now. Both of us, out loud, and nobody
defends anything."

They ran it together, sitting on the end of the bed, like the
professionals they had apparently stopped being for a
fortnight: every instance either could recall, dated,
itemised, assessed. The hair off the shoulder with the runner
at the gate. *Good, fabricator* in the wrong register in an
open corridor. The doorway, the half-second, the small of a
back. Her hand on his shoulder in a formal meeting with the
heir of the most observant clan on the continent sitting
eight feet away with her attention professionally deployed.

"Eleven instances," said Sazare, "that we can reconstruct.
The true count is higher — carelessness is under-logged by
its nature; that is what makes it carelessness. Finding: the
war made us rich, and the rich stop counting. The village's
inattention held because the village has a story, and
stories suppress data collection. It failed the first time
we performed in front of a professional, and it failed in
one pass, at noon, while both of us believed ourselves to
be conducting clan business."

"Concur," said Tsunade, in board voice, and then, not in
board voice, rubbing the bridge of her nose: "Gods. I took
the salute off your shoulder in front of the *Hyūga heir*.
Twenty years of statecraft. You've ruined me, fabricator,
I want it minuted."

"Countermanded. You were ruined by the fourteenth of the
eleventh at the latest; I have the documentation." He let
her have the laugh — forty-nine — and then put the page
between them, because the page was the point. "Remediation.
Proposed: the protocol does not tighten inside these walls.
Not one degree. What we have on the second floor we keep at
full amplitude — I will not engineer a flinch into either
of us at home; a flinch trained at home deploys in public
on its own schedule, which is worse. Instead: a boundary
with a name. Inside the door, everything. Outside it, the
working register, entire, no leakage — treated not as
denial but as *craft*, the way the misdirection was craft.
We were good at this in December. We were artists at it.
We got rich and stopped practising. So: we practise."

"And the clock?" She said it quietly, because it was the
real item, and it had been sitting under the audit the
whole time like a stone under cloth. "You heard what she
was actually telling you. It isn't 'be more careful.' It's
'the coat has stopped fitting.' Secrets age, Sazare. This
one is getting big for its room — it's got a *product line*,
it's got Thursdays, it's got a Hyūga holding a corner of
it out of grace. One day — not this spring, maybe not this
year — the arithmetic flips, and the secret costs more than
the telling would. I've watched it happen to better-kept
ones than ours."

The room was quiet.

"I know," said Sazare. "I have known since the appointment
letter went in your filing system. So: standing entry,
tonight, both our hands." He wrote it, and she watched him
write it, and it went into the book that kept everything:
*The cover is a bridge, not a house. One day we will walk
off the far end of it, on purpose, together, in daylight —
the day the office can afford it and not one day sooner.
Until then: craft, at full grade, and no flinches, and we
keep the door.*

She read it twice. Then she took the pen out of his hand
and signed it — both names, the office's and the other one,
the second larger for once — and put the pen down, and sat
looking at the page with an expression he could not
entirely cost.

"'On purpose, together, in daylight,'" she quoted, at
last. "You've just written the first document in the
village that promises me a future in your own hand, do you
know that? Filed under *security remediation*." The laugh
that came was small and complicated and worth double, and
she leaned her shoulder into his and let the audit end
there, two professionals on the end of a bed, the page
drying between them. "One day I'm going to hold you to it,
fabricator. Daylight."

"It is minuted," said Sazare. "It is the most minuted
thing I have ever written."

"Good. Now do my hair. Fifty passes. And tomorrow—" she
stood, and stretched, and the register changed, and he
heard the war resume the way you hear weather turn, "—
tomorrow is Thursday, and there is a bath with your name
on it, and I believe you were taught this week that the
Hyūga see a great deal. Well. So do I, fabricator. So do
I. Bring your composure. I have plans for it."
