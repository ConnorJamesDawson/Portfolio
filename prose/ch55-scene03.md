# Chapter 55, Scene 3 — Pleasant Sleep

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's house. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1440 words.*

---

Shizune was standing at the bottom of the stairs.

She had a cup of tea in one hand and she was not drinking it, and she
had a coat on that she had clearly had on for some hours, and she was
looking up at him with her head very slightly on one side.

"Pleasant sleep, I hope," said Shizune.

---

He got to the bottom of the stairs and stopped and did not say
anything, because there were four available responses and every single
one of them made it worse, and he had learned that much from her, about
her, in a corridor outside the records annexe two years ago.

"That's not fair," he said eventually.

"No," said Shizune. "It isn't. Sit down. There's tea."

"I don't—"

"There's tea, Ruri."

He sat down.

---

The kitchen was as unused as the rest of it.

He noted that and put it away with everything else. There was one pot,
which Shizune had clearly brought, and a bag of something on the side
that had come out of her coat, and a window with the shutter still
across it because nobody in that house had opened it for three weeks.

She poured him one.

"You've been here all night," he said.

"Yes."

"How long?"

"I came at about twenty past one." She sat down opposite him. "You'd
been in about ten minutes. I stood in the lane for a bit and then I let
myself in and sat down here, and I have been sitting here since, and at
about four I made a pot of tea and drank the whole of it on my own in
the dark."

"Why?"

Shizune looked at him.

"Ask a better question," she said.

---

He put the cup down.

*Right.*

*She did not follow us here, because she cannot move that fast and she
was not on the roofs. She came here because she knew where we were
going, and she knew where we were going because she knew we had left,
and she knew we had left because she was already handling it.*

*And the guard.*

"You sent the guard home," he said.

"I did."

"When?"

"About twenty past eleven."

He worked it.

*Twenty past eleven is the middle of it. That is somewhere in the six —
that is her going through the list, or just before it.*

"You were on the stair," said Sazare.

"I was on the stair from about ten to eleven until you came out with
her, which is three hours and forty minutes, and no, I did not hear all
of it, and no, I am not going to tell you what I did hear."

"That's fair."

"I know it's fair." She turned her cup round. "I heard tone. That's
mostly what you get through that door — you get tone and you get about
one word in six, and the one word in six is whichever one somebody puts
weight on."

"So you got the loud ones."

"I got the loud ones."

---

"There's a thing I want to say and I want to say it before we do
anything else," said Shizune, "because it's the only part of this I
actually understand and everything after it is going to be me guessing.

"She told you to fuck off."

"Yes."

"I was three seconds from that door."

He looked up.

"I want you to understand what that means," she said, "because you
won't, and you'll take it as a nice thing about her, and it isn't a
nice thing about her, it's the most frightening thing that happened
last night and I've had six hours to sit in the dark with it.

"I have been at that woman's side since I was eleven years old. I have
been in the room for — I'm not going to give you a number, I've never
counted, and I could." She stopped for a second. "In twenty years I
have watched three people push her to that exact point. Not close to
it. That point, that word, that voice.

"One of them was a jōnin commander in a field station in the second
month of the war and he went through a supporting wall and it was two
days before anybody could tell him what had happened.

"One of them was a man in Tanzaku who put a hand on her arm, and I had
to pay for the room.

"And one of them was me. I was nineteen. I said something about Dan
that I had every right to say and should not have said in that room at
that hour, and she did not touch me, and she picked up the table.

"Three for three, Ruri. Twenty years. It is not a temper, it is a
*mechanism* — somebody gets in past the front of it and the arm moves
before she does, and it has never once failed."

She put both hands round the cup.

"You said something to her at about half past eleven last night that
made her say that word, and I got up, and I was three seconds from that
door.

"And nothing happened."

---

"So she likes me," said Sazare.

Shizune closed her eyes.

"No," she said.

"Then—"

"*No*, Ruri. That's the nice version and I knew you'd take it and it's
wrong and I need you to have the right one." She opened her eyes.
"She *couldn't*.

"The mechanism didn't fail. It didn't fire. Something got in past the
front of it and the arm didn't move, and I have never seen that, and I
sat outside that door and I have been sitting in this kitchen since
half past one in the morning working out what it means, and what it
means is that whatever you are, you are not on the outside of her any
more.

"You are in the part where the guard doesn't work.

"And she does not know that. She will not have noticed. She'll have
gone to bed — she'll have been *carried* to bed — thinking she had a
hard conversation with somebody and shouted at them, and she has no
idea that the thing that has kept her alive for forty years just didn't
come out."

---

He sat with that.

The tea was going cold in front of him and he had not touched it, and
he had, for the first time since he had woken up, entirely forgotten
about his pulse.

"You're not congratulating me," he said.

"No."

"You're warning me."

"Yes."

"Why?"

Shizune said, "Because you can hurt her now."

And she said it plainly and without any weight on it at all, in the
voice she used to read out a bed count, and it went through him and
came out the other side and left a hole.

"I don't want to hurt her."

"I know you don't. That's not the problem. Ruri —" She leaned forward.
"There is a version of this conversation where I tell you to be careful
because she's fragile, and it would be easier for both of us and it
would be a lie. She isn't fragile. She is the strongest person in the
Land of Fire and she has spent forty years being *unreachable*, which
is a completely different thing, and it has worked, and it is why she's
still here when everyone else isn't.

"You have got past it. I don't know how and I don't think you do
either. And the danger isn't that you're going to hurt her.

"It's that you *can*, now, and you can't yet tell the difference
between the two sentences, and until you can, every single thing you
say to that woman is loaded and you will not be able to feel the
weight of it in your own mouth."

---

He did not answer for some time.

*She is right.*

*She is right and she has said it better than I could have said it and
she has done the thing Hime does, which is take the object off the
table and ask for the rest, except she has done it to a thing that
happened rather than a thing I made.*

*And there is a second half to it that she has not said and I do not
think she is going to.*

*If the mechanism did not fire, then it did not fire in either
direction.*

*I have been assuming all morning that the risk in this room is what I
might do to her.*

*Both of us are in the part where the guard doesn't work.*

"Shizune," he said.

"Mm."

"What's on her forehead?"
