# Chapter 72, Scene 5 — Live Load

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1030 words.*

---

The Thursday cohort review was eleven minutes old when the
system failure occurred, and Sazare had the whole event at
survey grade, from four feet, with a clipboard already in hand,
which is how the finest documentation of his career came to be
taken by accident.

The yard was at working strength: the second-run trays racked,
Tenzō at his eight feet, the review table set with the cohort
returns, and Hyūga Hinata at the table's end on double business —
the clan's maintenance-licence sign-off, and beneath it,
unwritten, the third session of the consultation, which had
progressed to *formats under mild load*: she had that afternoon
delivered a four-minute licensing summary to a table of three
without one stall, and he had written *bridge holding; increase
load gradually* in the margin of his copy, and then the gate
went, wood on wood, two knocks and a lean.

"YARD TOUR," announced Uzumaki Naruto, arriving at full
amplitude with Sakura behind him. "I brought my notebook. Where
does the fuss start? Start me at the beginning of the fuss—"

And Sazare, turning to manage the intake, had one half-second's
view of the review table, and on it the finest read of his
reading life, wasted on an audience of one:

Hyūga Hinata — clan negotiator, treaty author, holder of a
Hokage's secret at perfect pressure for three weeks, a woman he
had personally watched conduct doctrine at full fluency —
ceased, between one heartbeat and the next, to function.

It was not a blush. He had a taxonomy of blushes now,
field-collected, extensively cross-referenced against his own.
This was a *cascade*: the colour arriving in one wave from
collar to hairline; the licensing summary in her hands
acquiring a tremor at four hertz; the breath going high and
short; the eyes performing a triple transit — Naruto, escape
route, Naruto — and the voice, when Naruto bore down on the
table with his notebook out and said, cheerfully, "oh hey,
Hinata! You're in the fuss too?", producing:

"N-Naruto-kun. I. Yes. The — the clan is. I am here for the.
The licences. Which are. I have to check the — kilns."

There were no kilns requiring checking. She went to check
them. She checked, at speed, with her summary clutched to her
chest like a shield-issue, a bank of kilns that his own log
would confirm she had signed off forty minutes earlier, and
stood with her back to the yard performing an inspection of
sealed brickwork while her ears burned visibly at thirty
feet.

"Is she okay?" said Naruto, watching this with mild concern.
"She goes red a lot. Like — a LOT a lot. I hope she's not
coming down with something. Hey, maybe she should see
Sakura-chan—"

"She's FINE," said Sakura, in the voice of a woman carrying
two conversations, one of them internal and violent. "Kilns
are — kilns are very important. Come and see the load
articles, Naruto. Now. This way. NOW."

The tour proceeded. Sazare conducted it on professional
autopilot — the ovals, the seal families, Naruto asking, on
schedule, the question the brief was hiding (*"wait, if the
crystal drinks charge off her hair, what's it doing with it?"*
— flagged, deferred, filed under the boy's terrifying nose
for load-bearing walls) — and throughout it, at the margin of
every sightline, the heir of the Hyūga completed the longest
kiln inspection in the history of ceramics, recovered by
degrees, returned to the table, and got through her licence
sign-off in the working register, voice level, hands steady,
provided one stood between her and the gate so that no orange
jacket existed in her field of view.

*Entry, Thursday, consultation file, written that evening
with the care the finding deserved:*

*The street has a name.*

*I did not ask and she did not tell me, and I had — I
confirm this against my own record — genuinely not deduced
it: the file said "eleven years" and "a street" and I had
assessed the probability across her graduating class
without weighting, because she gave me nothing to weight,
because for three weeks she has held that name with the
same discipline she holds mine. Today the live load found
her, and I watched the finest natural composure I have
surveyed in this village fail in the identical cascade
mine failed in November — the same wave, the same four-hertz
tremor, the same flight to a procedural task that did not
exist. I have seen this failure mode before. In a mirror.
The kilns were her reject table.*

*And the name is Naruto.*

*Findings, in order. One: the consultation's difficulty
rating is hereby revised upward by an order of magnitude.
The format did not fail today — the format never deployed;
the load arrived without warning and took the whole
structure before one course was laid. Training must
therefore include the no-warning case, which is the case
that matters, because HE is the no-warning case, perpetually,
for everybody, on every axis.*

*Two: the object of eleven years of the finest-guarded
devotion in Konoha spent this afternoon four feet from her,
worried she might have a fever, and recommended a medic.
The instrument that reads everybody read nothing. This is
not his failure. It is the load case: she has spent eleven
years engineering exactly this invisibility, and the
engineering WORKS, and she is now inside her own perimeter
and cannot signal out. I know that architecture. I lived in
it for three years. Somebody had to walk into mine without
knocking before it opened, and nobody walks into hers,
because everybody knocks around a Hyūga.*

*Three, filed with care, because the boundary matters: none
of this is mine to arrange. The consultation's contract is
a format for HER sentence, to be carried on HER schedule,
across HER street. I am her engineer, not her broker. The
finding changes the training plan. It does not change the
contract.*

*Four. Tenzō watched the entire event from eight feet and
his face did nothing at all, which for Tenzō is a standing
ovation. His pattern file gained an entry today. Mine
gained a mirror. The yard is becoming a museum of people
not saying things, and I am, apparently, its keeper, and
its one graduate.*
