# Chapter 46, Scene 3 — Ten, and Then Forty

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard, after hours. Sazare
Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,726 words.*

---

He did it on the eleventh of the seventh month and he wrote the
protocol out first, on a clean sheet, in the ordinary way, because a
thing done without a protocol was a thing you could not report.

*Question. Is four seconds a property of the technique or a property of
the articles I have been making?*

*Prediction. Property of the articles. If the seat carries and the far
end does not have to carry itself, then the far end is a load and not a
conductor, and there is no reason for it to burn out, and the limiting
factor becomes the supply.*

*Method. Draw a short blade off the seat end. Do not lay it slowly —
lay it as fast as I would lay a wall in a corridor, because the point
is that the far end is cheap. Hold it. Count.*

*Timing. Counted, out loud, which is unreliable by up to a fifth over
long intervals and which I have no way of improving at one in the
morning. The tower gives me the hour and nothing between.*

*Safety. Nobody in the yard. Gate shut. Water bucket, which will not
help with anything I can think of and which I have put there anyway.*

*Stopping rule. I stop at any of: pain, the note going wrong, or a
hundred.*

He read it back and added a line, because a stopping rule you hold
yourself is not a stopping rule and he had learned that in a treatment
room in the fifth month from a man called Nemoto with his arms folded.

*Amendment. I have nobody to hold it, so I am writing the number down
in advance and I am not going past it. A hundred.*

---

He put his palm over the seat.

The note came up — a tone and a half above the tower's middle, four
voices, two of them beating against each other about twice a second,
utterly familiar now after two weeks.

Then he drew.

It came out of the seat end the way water goes up a wick, which was not
a thing he had ever seen crystal do, because crystal had always come
*out of a medium* and this came out of an article. One foot six of edge
in about a second and a half, laid at corridor speed, rough, the
proportions off a nine-hundred-year-old book Ochiai had found him in a
fortnight.

Pale. Translucent all the way through, with the lamp on the far side of
it going into the body of the thing and coming out somewhere it had no
business coming out of.

The note changed.

Not louder. It picked up a fifth voice — lower, a little rough, the
sound of a run that was badly made and knew it — and the fifth voice
was the blade, which was rough and badly made, because he had laid it
in a second and a half on purpose.

Sazare stood in a wet yard at one in the morning holding it and
counting out loud.

"Four," he said.

Nothing happened.

"Five. Six."

---

Six was where a seeded article went.

He knew the feel of it the way he knew the feel of a stair with a
missing tread — the small give, the moment the store stopped being a
store, four seconds unseeded and six seeded, nine hundred times since
he was nine years old, the last hundred of them with a Hyūga shouting
at him in a corridor.

Six went past.

He heard himself say seven and it did not sound like his voice.

"Eight. Nine. Ten."

The fifth voice was still there and had not changed. The four under it
had not changed. The blade in his hand had not changed by any measure
he could take while holding it, and the lamp still went into it and
came out wrong, and there was nothing in the whole of it that felt like
the give.

"Eleven. Twelve."

He got to about twenty and stopped counting, because counting had
become a thing his mouth was doing while the rest of him did
arithmetic.

*It is not going.*

*The seat is carrying it. The blade is not carrying anything — it is
what the carrying is FOR — and a thing that is not a conductor cannot
burn out, and I have known that in principle since the fifth month and
have this instant found out what it means, which is a different verb.*

He fed it harder, because that was the next question and he was not
going to have another night like this one.

---

The core lit.

It was the first thing anybody else would have noticed and it was the
last thing he noticed, and he wrote that down afterwards with some care
because it was not modesty, it was simply the order in which it had
happened.

The rod in his hand went from pale to something with a light in it,
white-blue, deepest along the axis and washing out towards the surface,
and the light moved when he moved it, and the four branches showed as
brighter lines inside the body of the thing like the veins in a leaf
held to a window.

Underneath that, the note came down a tone.

*That is the reading,* he thought, and had to stop himself saying it out
loud in an empty yard. *The light is a by-product. The note is the
reading. The ways are opening under load and coming back and the pitch
is telling me by how much and there is a curve in this that I could
have in a fortnight.*

He fed it harder still.

The light went up. The note came down another half and picked up
something under it, a graininess in the second voice, and he backed off
by about a third immediately, on reflex, without deciding to — and then
stood there having done it and worked out four seconds later what had
happened.

*I heard a fault start.*

*Second branch. Not failed. Starting. And my hand came off it before I
had processed the sound, which is the same reflex that reads a fracture
in a casting off a tap and it is the only skill I own that has
transferred to this entire business unchanged.*

---

He got to forty-one and his arm gave out.

Not the technique. The arm — the left one, the one Kakashi had been
making him land on since the fourth month, and the shoulder Tsunade had
put two fingers into in an office in the fifth, and it went at about
forty and he got one more count out of it and then set the whole thing
down on the felt and sat on the wet stone.

The blade went as soon as his palm came off the seat. No collapse and
no shatter — it simply stopped being there, the way the note stopped
with no tail on it, and there was nothing on the felt but a rod four
inches long.

He sat in the yard for a while.

Then he wrote it up on his knees, in the wet, because he was not going
to walk home first.

*Forty-one. Stopping rule not reached. Terminated by operator, left
deltoid and shoulder, not by article.*

*Six seconds is gone.*

*I want to be precise about what that sentence means, because there is
a version of it that is a boast and it took me about four minutes on
this stone to find the version that is true.*

*It does not mean I have got stronger. I have not. I have exactly the
output I had on the tenth of the seventh month and it is the same
output I had at sixteen, and the arm that failed at forty-one is the
arm that has always failed.*

*It means the four seconds was never a measurement of me. It was a
measurement of what I was making, and I have been making it badly for
eleven years because I learned this trade in rooms where somebody was
waiting, and I have spent all of those eleven years reading a materials
failure as the ceiling of my bloodline and writing it in ledgers as a
fact about myself.*

*Two hundred and nine dishes said so. I looked at all of them in the
fifth month with a borrowed lens.*

He stopped, and read that back, and then wrote the last part.

*And here is the thing I did not expect and am still sitting in.*

*The limit tonight was my arm.*

*I have never in my life had a limit that was mine. Every ceiling I
have ever hit has been in the material, or in a border, or in a file
somebody else wrote about me, or in what I was allowed to be — and I
have got extremely good at going round those, because going round is
the only move available against a ceiling somebody else built.*

*You cannot go round a shoulder.*

*You can only train it, and I have been doing that badly and
resentfully on a training ground for four months under a man who told
me the boring part is the whole of it, and I have privately regarded it
as the price of being allowed to do the interesting thing.*

*Exercise 1: two.*

*That number is not an embarrassment any more. It is the frontier. It
is, as of about one o'clock this morning, the most important number I
have got, and it has been sitting at the bottom of every page in this
book for six weeks while I wrote "nothing to report" above it.*

---

He got home at four and did not sleep and did not mind.

The last line of the night, under the hours and the count and the
protocol and the four minutes on the wet stone, was three words long
and he looked at it for some time before he let it stand, because it
was the kind of sentence that could tip.

*I am the bottleneck.*

Then, underneath, because the ledger got both halves or it got neither,
and because this one was — for the first time in about eleven years —
the flattering half and the honest half at once:

*Good.*
