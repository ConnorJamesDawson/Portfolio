# Chapter 68, Scene 4 — Increment

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the second floor. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~940 words.*

---

The noons established themselves inside two days, the way anything
she put in the diary established itself, which is to say
geologically.

The format was fixed by Tuesday: the tray for two in the small room
off her office, the bruise inspection conducted over his objections
that there was nothing to inspect, her running commentary on
Jiraiya's methods — professionally scandalised, personally
approving, the ratio never quite stable — and twenty minutes of the
kind of talk the ferry had invented, through and out, nothing kept.

It was, he recorded, the best standing appointment of his life,
displacing the previous holder, which had been Thursday tea with a
dying man, and he noted the succession in the book with the
seriousness successions deserved.

The evenings, meanwhile, had begun to change policy without
consultation.

The arrangement, since the tower, had been the landing: she changed,
he absented himself to the top of the outside stair with whatever he
was reading, and the door's particular click recalled him. It was
not a rule anybody had made. It had precipitated, the way their
arrangements precipitated, out of one evening's geometry, and had
run seven weeks without amendment.

On the Wednesday, day three of the field, he was at the desk in the
upper room with the splint correspondence when she came up from the
office, pulled the sticks out of her hair, said "don't stop on my
account" —

— and changed.

There. Mid-room. With the unhurried thoroughness of a woman at the
end of a long day in her own home, which was what she was, and the
shirt went up and over and away, and his systems, which had survived
a towel by eighteen inches of bathwater, performed the by-now
documented sequence — white noise, vocabulary loss, a heat like
standing too near the annealing lamp — with one wholly new feature,
which was that his heart executed four beats in the space budgeted
for two and then attempted, briefly, none at all.

*Cardiac event,* some surviving clerk in him logged. *Minor.
Probably minor. The literature says the heart does not actually
stop from this. The literature has not met her.*

"Breathe," said Tsunade, muffled, from inside the evening shirt,
not even looking, and he discovered he had not been, and resumed.

And it was there — mid-resumption, all filters down, the clerk
unconscious, the department of consequences dark — that his mouth,
unsupervised for the first time in perhaps four years, asked the
question his eyes had been filing under *structural anomalies,
unresolved* since approximately the twelfth of November.

"Hime, how does that wonderful chest of yours not hurt your back?"

The room went very quiet.

She turned around, shirt settled, sticks in hand, and looked at him,
and he watched her run the assessment — his face doing whatever it
was doing, which at a guess was the copper patches again, his
posture that of a man listening to a landslide he had personally
started — and he watched her arrive, with visible relish, at the
correct diagnosis, which was that the question had escaped rather
than been sent, and was, for all that, *an actual question*.

"That," said Tsunade, sitting down on the end of the bed to take
her hair down properly, "is the first honest medical consult
anybody has brought me in a month, so I'm going to answer it
properly. Sit up straight, this is anatomy.

"Yes, it should. The load case is real — you're the engineer,
you've priced cantilevers. An ordinary woman built like this pays
for it in the thoracic spine and the shoulder grooves, every day,
for decades, and it is not a small tax, and I have treated
thousands of backs that nobody ever took seriously because the
cause embarrassed people. The answer in my case is that the same
reinforcement that puts my fist through a wall runs a trickle
through the paraspinals all day at no cost I can feel. Fifty years
of chakra-supported posture — I could carry a market stall on my
spine before I noticed. That, plus core discipline older than your
village rank, plus—" she pulled the last pin and pointed it at him
— "engineering in the underpinnings that is a military secret
considerably better kept than anything in your Corps file. Written
answer available on request. Diagrams extra."

"Understood," he said, at about half size.

"Any further questions from the floor?"

"Not at this time."

"Pity," said Tsunade, and the smile she was biting got away from
her at one corner, and she let it, and went back to her hair, and
the room resumed rotation at its ordinary speed.

*Entry,* he wrote, much later, when the clerk had been revived and
sworn back in. *The changing arrangement has been amended
unilaterally. No graduation was offered. The study's authors note,
with what dignity remains available to them, that this is now the
third consecutive increment introduced at maximum amplitude
without warning, and that the pattern is no longer attributable
to chance.*

*She is dosing me. She has been dosing me since the towel — since
before the towel; the towel was itself a dose. Matter-of-fact is
the delivery mechanism. There is a protocol running in this tower
and I did not write it, and its increments arrive exactly as fast
as I survive the last one, which means the metering is being done
from outside, by an observer, off my own readouts.*

*A teacher's hand on the boy the whole time, feeling what he
cannot.*

*Fukasaku described her method this morning, in a field, about a
different substance entirely.*

*Filed without further comment. The comment would only be the
copper patches, in prose.*
