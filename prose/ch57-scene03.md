# Chapter 57, Scene 3 — So He's the One

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Tsunade, age fifty-three.
POV: Tsunade. ~1830 words.*

---

They did Suna and they did the temple and they did about nine minutes
of the northern thing he was not going to tell her properly, and by
half past four she had stopped standing like anything and had put her
feet on the open bottom drawer, which she had not done in front of
another person since about the sixth year.

Then he put the cup down.

"So he's the one you chose, eh?"

"Here we go."

"I didn't put you down for a toy boy, Hime."

"He's not a toy boy."

"He's eighteen and he's got a face like a plank and he calls you
*Hime*."

"He's a *sergeant in the Medical Corps* who has produced about eleven
hundred articles that are keeping people upright in four countries," said
Tsunade, "and if you say toy boy again I am going to put you through
that window you came in by and I do not care about your arm."

Jiraiya laughed.

He laughed properly, out loud, in a tower at half past four in the
morning, and put his head back, and she found that she was not as
annoyed as she had been eleven seconds earlier and was extremely
annoyed about that.

---

"Right," he said. "I'll behave. And before you get going — I want one
thing on the table and then it's done and I'll never bring it up
again."

"Don't."

"I'm going to."

"Jiraiya—"

"I asked you when we were twelve," said Jiraiya, "and you told me to
get stuffed, and I asked you again at nineteen and you told me to get
stuffed in a considerably more anatomical way, and I have had
forty-one years to make my peace with it and I made it somewhere
around the second war.

"I shot my shot a long time ago. It's not a wound. I'd tell you if it
was, because you'd know anyway and you'd be unbearable about it."

He picked the cup back up.

"So there's no hard feeling in this room and there isn't going to be.
I want that established, because in about ten minutes I'm going to say
something you don't like, and I don't want you sitting there thinking
it's coming out of forty years ago."

She looked at him.

"All right," she said.

"All right?"

"All right, you absolute bastard. Yes. Noted."

"Good." He drank. "Now. Congratulations, by the way."

"On what?"

"On getting caught," said Jiraiya. "*He* got round all that armour.
That's the bit I want to sit with. Forty years of the best plate work
I have ever seen on a human being, and an eighteen-year-old walked
through it, and I would very much like to know how."

---

*Armour.*

She had a moment about that and did not show it.

*That's the second one. Shizune said it in a kitchen on Thursday
morning and this one has just said it off his own bat at four
thirty-five in the morning having been in the room a quarter of an hour,
and they have not compared notes and they are not going to.*

*And they are both right, and I have never once used the word about
myself, and I am not going to start at half four.*

"There's no how," she said. "That's the honest answer and you're not
going to like it. There wasn't a manoeuvre. He didn't do anything."

"Nobody gets through that by not doing anything."

"He got through it," said Tsunade, "by looking at me for three years
and writing it down."

Jiraiya went quiet.

"Go on."

"No."

"Hime—"

"You asked me what makes him so special," she said, "and I'll give you
that, because you've earned it and because you're going to need it in
about ten minutes for whatever it is you're building up to. But I'm not
giving you the other thing and I'm not going to pretend I might."

"Fair enough."

"Is it?"

"It's fair enough," said Jiraiya. "Give me the first list."

---

She thought about it properly.

It was not difficult and that was the strange part. She had expected to
have to reach, the way you reach when somebody asks you what you like
about a person and you find you have four adjectives and no evidence.

It came out in order and it came out fast, and she understood about
halfway through the second item that she had done this before, on her
own, more than once, and had never admitted it.

"One," she said. "The reject table.

"The splint study got knocked back in the seventh month of last year —
properly knocked back, on method, and the method objection was
reasonable. And he resubmitted it in the ninth with a table attached of
every single material he had tried and every single one that had failed
and by how much.

"Nobody does that. Nobody in the *history* of this building has ever
done that. You bury the failures, that's the whole craft of a
resubmission — you go back and you put the good number at the front and
you hope the man reading it is tired.

"He led with the failures. Four hundred rows of them. And the study
went through, and it went through *because* of the table, and he did not
understand why and had to have it explained to him twice."

"I've read that table," said Jiraiya.

"Everybody's read that table."

---

"Two. He came and told me he'd looked at me."

Jiraiya raised an eyebrow.

"On the eleventh," said Tsunade. "Stood in this office and told me he'd
written a four-clause entry about me in his notebook on the fifteenth of
the eighth month at five past eleven at night with his hand going, and
ruled a line through it the next morning, and then not read the top of
that page for eleven weeks.

"And he told me *because he'd rather I had it from him than worked it
out*. Unprompted. In advance. Of his own free will, at some cost, out
loud, standing up.

"Do you know what men do with that information, Jiraiya? They spend
thirty years making sure you never get it. Or they hand it over as a
weapon. He brought me the worst thing about himself in a bracket
because he thought I was owed it."

"That is a boy with no sense of self-preservation whatsoever."

"That's three," said Tsunade.

---

"Three. He has never asked me for anything.

"Three years. Not a posting. Not a budget line. Not a word to Yagi, not
an introduction, not a favour, not one single thing — and I am the
*Hokage*, and everybody who comes through that door wants something and
most of them are honest about it and the honest ones are the good ones.

"He has come up that hill about four hundred times and he has never once
come up it to ask.

"He asked me for something on Thursday morning. First time in three
years."

"What was it?"

"None of your fucking business."

"Fair enough," said Jiraiya, entirely equably.

---

"Four. He's wrong in public.

"Not gracious about it — he doesn't do gracious. He says *that's wrong*
and he says it flat, in front of whoever's there, and then he starts
again out loud from the beginning. I have watched him do it to Kanzaki
and to Yagi and to me and to a council committee, and the first time you
see it you think he's being got at and the fourth time you realise he
does not have any of the machinery the rest of us have got for not
being seen to be wrong.

"He hasn't got a face, Jiraiya. That's not a figure of speech, he
genuinely hasn't got one, and it means every correction happens in the
open where everybody can watch it.

"There are men on that council who have been wrong in the same direction
for nineteen years because being seen to change would cost them
something."

"There are men on that council I could name."

"So could I. I sat through eight of them on the eleventh."

---

"And five," she said.

She stopped for a second.

"Five's the one I'd give you if I could only give you one.

"He doesn't want to be strong."

Jiraiya put the cup down.

"He's got it written in the front of four books," said Tsunade. "In
ink, at fifteen, and he's never revised it. *I am not going to get
stronger. Find out how it works.*

"He means it. That's the bit. I've had it tested — I have put things in
front of that boy that any shinobi alive would have reached for, and
he doesn't reach, he takes it apart to see why it holds and then he
gives it to somebody who needs it.

"And the joke, and it is a joke, and it is not funny — is that he has
become one of the most dangerous people in this village anyway. As a
*side effect*. Because he found out how it works.

"He'd never have got there by chasing it. Nobody who chases it ever
gets there. And in about six months everybody is going to look at him
and see a man who went after strength, and it will be the exact
opposite of the truth, and there is not one thing I can do about it."

---

Jiraiya sat and looked at the cup for a long moment.

"Right," he said.

"Right?"

"Right, that's the thing I was building up to, and you have just walked
straight into the middle of it, so I'll say it now and we can be
finished.

"He's going to get killed."

Tsunade did not move.

"I'm not being dramatic and I'm not warning you off him," said Jiraiya.
"I'd have been about a week too late for either. I'm telling you what
I do for a living.

"There are four countries with a file on that boy and I have read two
of them. The Kiri one says *do not approach*, which is not about danger,
it's about *value*. The Oto one — and there is one, and I have not read
it, and I know the shape of it from what people have stopped asking
about — has him as an acquisition.

"And up to about the eleventh of this month he was a hospital fabricator
with an odd name, and that was survivable, because the worst thing
anybody could do with him was steal him, and stealing a man who won't
work for you is expensive and stupid.

"He is not that now."

"No," she said.

"He is a strategic asset who comes up this hill at four in the morning,"
said Jiraiya, "and every single week that goes past, more people work
out that the fastest route to the Hokage of Konohagakure is an
eighteen-year-old with no face.

"That's not a lever on him, Hime.

"That's a lever on *you*, and he is the handle."
