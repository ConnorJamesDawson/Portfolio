# Chapter 31, Scene 5 — Hold, and Leave

*Act 4, part C. Konoha, the exercise ground. Sazare Ruri, age sixteen.
POV: Sazare. ~2,089 words.*

---

They gave him a culvert, a road, two casualties who could not move,
twenty minutes, and four people.

"Objective is the culvert mouth," Toriyama said. "Casualties are inside
it. They don't walk, they don't crawl, and if either of them takes a
marked hit the exercise is over and you've failed it. Opposing team is
six and they've been told to take the ground. You hold it for twenty-five
minutes or until they stop trying."

"What's my authority over the four?"

"Total, inside the exercise."

"Then I'd like it said out loud in front of them, because otherwise
I'll spend nine minutes finding out."

Toriyama said it out loud in front of them.

They were: a chūnin of about twenty-two named Sagara with a wrestler's
neck and a pouch worn dead centre at the back of the belt, which
Sazare could not read at all and filed to ask about later; two genin,
a boy and a girl of about fourteen, both of whom stood the way people
stand when they have been told they are being observed; and Nohara
Daichi, whose burned right hand was down to two fingers in a
half-glove and who had arrived without explanation and had not been on
any list Sazare had seen.

"You're not cleared," Sazare said.

"I'm cleared for exercise, non-contact, and I'm here as an evaluator's
prop," Daichi said. "Which means I'm one of your four and I'm also
going to write down everything you do, and Toriyama thinks that'll
bother you."

"It will."

"Good. That's the point of it."

---

He spent eleven of the twenty minutes on the ground and it was the
easiest eleven minutes of the entire evaluation.

The road came down to the culvert on a falling grade with a bank on the
left and open ground on the right, and it had been resurfaced at some
point with a coarse aggregate that answered fast, and the culvert mouth
itself was old dressed stone with open joints — which answered slower
and better, and which he read in about four seconds and did not have to
think about again.

He did not build a wall. He built six short faces on the fall of the
road, staggered, none of them higher than a shin, each one behind and
inboard of the last, and a seventh at the culvert mouth on the right
where the open ground gave the only approach that did not require
making a decision.

He put nothing at all on the bank.

"That's the obvious way in," Sagara said.

"Yes."

"You've left it open."

"I've left it open and I've made everything else expensive, which
means they'll use it, which means I know where they'll be." He was
already moving. "You'll be at the top of it. Not fighting. You put
one hand on somebody and stop them, and then let go and let them past
you, and then stop the next one."

"Let them *past*?"

"Onto the road. Where the faces are."

Sagara looked at him for a second and then laughed, once, and went and
stood on the bank.

That part was fine. It was the next part that went wrong.

---

He did not use the two genin at all for the first nine minutes of the
exercise and he did not notice that he had not.

He put them at the culvert mouth — sensible, defensible, the correct
place for two frightened fourteen-year-olds — and then he built his
ground and watched his lines and read the road, and the two of them
stood at the culvert mouth doing nothing, because he had given them a
position and no job, and it did not once occur to him that those were
different things.

The opposing team came at eleven minutes and came, as predicted, up
the bank.

Sagara stopped the first one and let him past. The faces on the road
did what faces do: four short decisions in four seconds and the man
put a foot wrong on the third and Daichi marked him. The second came
through and stalled at the fourth face and went back. The third and
fourth came together, split, and one of them broke right into the
open ground toward the culvert mouth —

— and the boy genin moved to meet him, and the girl did not, and they
got in each other's way, and for about a second and a half the
casualty on the left side of the culvert had nothing between him and a
man with a marker but a fourteen-year-old who had not been told which
of them was supposed to move.

Daichi's voice, flat, from the side: "*Mark.*"

"No mark," the girl said, "I got him," and she had, barely, and the
exercise did not end.

Sazare stood on a road he had built perfectly and understood, with
total clarity, that he had nearly lost it in the one place he had not
built anything.

---

"Ruri." Daichi, again, not moving. "Nine minutes. What have those two
been doing?"

"Holding the mouth."

"Say what you told them to do. The words."

"...I put them at the mouth."

"You put them at the mouth." Daichi's two fingers made a note on the
board without his eyes leaving the field. "You gave four people a
position each and three jobs. You've been running that road like it's
got four hands and one of them's yours."

Sazare turned and looked at the two of them properly for the first
time since the exercise started.

The boy was breathing too fast and had his weight on his front foot,
which was wrong, and had a kunai out that he did not need out. The girl
had a scrape down her forearm and had put herself half a pace forward
of the boy without appearing to know it. Her pouch was on the right,
too low, the strap new — issued in the last month. His was on the
right too, identical, the strap creased in three places from being
adjusted three times.

*Rung two. He's moved his pouch three times this month. She hasn't
touched hers.*

*Rung three. He's still deciding what kind of shinobi he is. She's
decided.*

*Rung four—*

"Names," Sazare said.

"Ito," said the boy. "Ito Kenta."

"Mori Suzu."

"Mori, you're the mouth. Nothing else. Anything that gets to the open
ground on the right is yours, and if I put crystal there it's for you
to stand behind and not for you to fight from. Ito, you're not at the
mouth, you're four feet behind Mori and one to her left, and your job
is that when she commits you call it — out loud, so I hear it, in one
word — and then you go where she isn't."

"That's all?" said Ito.

"That's the whole thing. She'll commit twice more before this is over
and I can't see the open ground from where I have to stand." He was
already turning back to the road. "You're my eye. If you do that
properly the two of you cover a gap I can't build across, and if you
don't, I lose the exercise on the right hand side and it will not be
your fault."

He heard the second half of that sentence come out of his own mouth
and did not have time to examine it.

---

They came four more times and the third one nearly worked.

They had understood the faces by then and started breaking them
deliberately, which was the correct response and which Sazare had been
waiting eleven minutes to see, because a broken face is stock and stock
is his. He grew from the fragments twice and the second time it stopped
two men in the same second at different ends of the road.

Ito called Mori's commit twice. Both times it was one word — "*left*"
— and both times it was in time.

And at nineteen minutes, Sazare read something he did not have a name
for and acted on it anyway, which was the first time in his life he
had done that.

The opposing team had come to the top of the bank and stopped. There
were four of them left unmarked and they were standing in a loose
group about forty feet out, and their leader — a woman, thirties,
kunai pouch high on the left, jacket that fitted — was talking.

Nothing about the ground had changed. Nothing about their footing told
him anything. But one of the four had shifted his weight back onto his
heels while she was still speaking, and the man beside him had looked
not at the road but at the sky, which is a thing people do when they
have already stopped thinking about the problem in front of them, and
the woman herself had put a hand on her hip in a way that was not
about balance.

*Rung two. Heels back. Nobody puts their weight back before a rush.*

*Rung three. He's done. He decided before she finished talking.*

*Rung four — they're not planning the next attempt. They're pricing
it.*

Sazare did not build anything else.

That was the whole of it, and it was entirely counter-instinctive, and
he stood on his road for a full ninety seconds with six seeded
fragments in reach and did not put up a single face, because a new
wall going up in front of a group of people doing arithmetic is a new
number in the sum, and the number they had was already high enough.

At twenty-two minutes the woman said something and the four of them
went back down the bank.

Toriyama blew a whistle at twenty-five.

---

The second scenario was after lunch and Sazare nearly failed it inside
the first four minutes.

The brief was almost identical and one thing was different: the
casualties were mobile, the objective was a road junction, and the
sheet said *hold pending relief*, and at seven minutes a runner came
in with the words *relief is not coming, extract*.

Everything in him wanted to build.

He had the junction. He had it *properly* — he had spent six minutes
reading it and it was better ground than the culvert had been, with a
sunken lane on one side that would have cost an attacking team four
men to force, and he had two fragments already placed and a third
growing under his hand when the runner said the words.

He kept growing it.

He was four seconds into a face he had no further use for before he
understood what he was doing, and the understanding did not arrive as
a thought. It arrived as Ubukata's voice saying *do you understand the
difference between a ceiling and a challenge*, and Kakashi saying *a
weapon you can't safely de-escalate*, and underneath both of them, much
older, the flat certainty of a boy under a floor at Tomari who had
learned before he was eight that the way you survive a room is to make
it expensive.

*I am building because building is what I do when I am frightened.*

He stopped the growth. The half-face slumped under his palm.

"Withdrawing," he said. "Mori, Ito, casualties, now, sunken lane, you
go first and I follow. Sagara, you're last and you don't fight anyone,
you just leave. Daichi—"

"I'm a prop."

"You're a prop who's going to walk in front of me at my pace so nobody
has to notice that I've got a bad leg."

They got out in under four minutes and lost nobody, and the whole way
down the sunken lane Sazare had to actively stop himself seeding the
ground behind them, because a lane behind a withdrawal is the single
most tempting piece of terrain a fortifier will ever be offered and
crystal left in a lane the relief might later come up is crystal in
the way of your own people.

He counted it afterwards. He had wanted to do it eleven separate
times.

Toriyama's note, which Sazare read six days later when the file was
opened to him, said:

*Withdrew on order. Delay of approx. 4 sec at the point of decision,
during which candidate continued to fabricate. Candidate stopped
without prompting. Recommend this be recorded as a pass with the delay
noted, on the grounds that the delay is the honest measurement and the
recovery is the relevant one.*

And underneath, in a different hand, in pencil:

*Agreed. Watch it anyway. — N.*
