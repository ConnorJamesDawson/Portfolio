# Chapter 55, Scene 2 — Pervert

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's house. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1890 words.*

---

He went back to the checks, because the checks were legitimate and he
needed something that was.

*Right side. She came down on that shoulder twice yesterday and once
was on a desk edge.*

He looked, properly, the way he would have looked at anybody, starting
at the clavicle and working down: the set of the shoulder, the line of
the ribs under the shirt on the in-breath and whether it was even, the
hand he could see, the colour of the nail beds, the ankle above the
foot she had not got under the covers.

*Ribs even. No guarding. Nail beds good. No oedema at the ankle, which
I would expect after nine measures and I do not have, which is the
liver doing something I would like to write a paper about and never
will.*

*Shoulder—*

"Pervert," said Tsunade.

---

Her eyes were open.

He had no information at all about how long they had been open. That
was the part that stayed with him: he had spent three years being the
man who noticed when somebody's breathing changed at nine feet, and she
had come up out of sleep beside him at four inches and he had missed
the whole of it.

Her hands came out of his shirt.

She did it as she said the word, both at once, and it was not a
snatching-back — it was a letting go, done deliberately, the way you
put a tool down when you have finished with it, and she flexed the
fingers of the right one twice afterwards because they had been shut
for seven hours.

Neither of them said anything about it.

They never did. Not that morning and not in the eleven years that came
after it, and it stayed in the room, and it was the largest thing either
of them owned.

"Good morning, Hime," said Sazare.

---

"You were looking at my chest."

"I was checking your ribs."

"*Ruri.*"

"On the right side," he said. "You went into a desk edge on the
fourteenth and you came down on that shoulder twice yesterday and you
have been drinking on it, and if there's a costal fracture it presents
about now and it presents as guarding on the in-breath, and there
isn't, and I'm glad."

Tsunade looked at him for a long moment.

"That's true, isn't it," she said.

"Yes."

"That's completely true and it is the worst defence I have ever heard
in my life."

"I know."

"You could have said *sorry*."

"It wouldn't have been true."

"No," she said, and something happened at the corner of her mouth that
she did not let get any further. "No, it wouldn't."

He accepted the charge.

He did it silently and without any expression and it took about a
second, and what he accepted it on was a technicality, and he knew
exactly what the technicality was and what it was covering.

*She has got me for two seconds of an ordinary examination.*

*She has not got me for the ninety seconds where I laid her face for a
bust, and she has not got me for the four minutes on the seal, which is
the actual offence and is not a small one.*

*I am going to be called a pervert for the only innocent thing I did in
this bed.*

*And I am going to take it, because the alternative is telling her what
the other two were.*

---

She sat up.

It was an ordinary movement. She got an elbow under her and came up
against the headboard with the covers going where covers go, and she
was in the shirt she had been in since yesterday morning and there was
nothing in it that anybody could have written down.

His pulse went to a hundred and twelve.

*Right.*

He knew it instantly, in full, with the reference. He could have named
the two hormones and the gland and the four seconds of latency and the
half-life, and he did name them, at speed, internally, the way you say
a name to make a thing smaller.

It did not make it smaller.

*One hundred and twelve. Cause identified. No effect on the finding.*

*I have been in a room with an unclothed adult approximately nine
hundred times. I have had my hands inside four of them. I have never
once had any response to any of it that I could measure, and I have
written that down twice as a professional advantage and once, at
sixteen, at two in the morning, as a thing that might be wrong with me.*

*There is nothing wrong with me.*

*The apparatus is intact. It was never broken. It has been sitting
there in perfect order for eighteen years with nothing pointed at it.*

*And now it has got a target, and it does not care in the slightest
that I can draw it.*

He lay very still and looked at the ceiling and understood, with the
same completeness that he understood everything, and with none of the
usual comfort that came with understanding a thing, that this was going
to be a permanent condition of the rest of his life.

"Ruri," said Tsunade.

"Yes."

"Close your eyes. I need to get dressed."

---

"What," he said, "I can't stay and watch?"

He heard it leave and had a quarter of a second of complete horror.

*That is a joke.*

*I have made a joke. I have never made a joke in my life, I have made
four remarks that people took as jokes and I have written down every
single one of them because I could not reproduce the conditions—*

Tsunade laughed.

It came out of her properly, once, and then she got hold of it, and it
was the first noise she had made all morning that was not on purpose.

"No, Ruri," she said. "That was the polite way of saying get the fuck
out of my bedroom."

He shut his eyes and got off the bed.

---

He got his boots from the end of the frame where he had put them and he
did not look round, and he was aware of the room behind him in a way he
had never been aware of a room in his life and would have to find some
way of not being, eventually, presumably, at some point.

And he stood there with a boot in each hand and did the arithmetic on
one word.

*Bedroom.*

*She said bedroom.*

*She could have said house. House was available; house is the correct
word for the thing she wanted, which is me out of the building and down
the stairs and gone before somebody comes; house is what she would have
said to anybody at all, and she did not say it.*

*She has drawn a line round eleven feet of floor and left the rest of
the building alone.*

*I do not think she noticed doing that.*

*I am not going to mention it. I am going to go downstairs.*

He went to the door.

---

The shelf was by the door.

He had seen it at one in the morning in the dark with a woman in his
arms, and he had counted four things on it and registered that none of
them were photographs and gone on to the bed, because that was all
there had been light for.

There was light now.

He got about two feet past it before the shape stopped him, and he did
not turn his head, because his head was not required. He had it in the
edge of his vision and the edge of his vision was enough, and he stood
in the doorway of the Hokage's bedroom with a boot in each hand and did
not move.

*Nine inches. Two leaf pairs. Four thorns.*

*Third one crooked.*

---

*It went on the twenty-fifth of the eighth month. I did not see it go.
I was not asked to see it go and I did not ask.*

*It is opaque now, which is what they do — the order comes out and the
light stops going through and it looks like chalk, and it will hold its
own shape sitting still on a flat surface indefinitely and it will not
survive being picked up.*

*It has been picked up.*

He could read it the way he could read a joint.

*The outer edge of the top leaf pair is gone. Not broken — worn, on the
underside, which is a thumb; that is where a thumb goes if you take a
stem between finger and thumb about an inch below the head.*

*The bud has lost about a third of its face and it has lost it evenly,
which is not one drop. That is repetition.*

*I would put it at forty. I would put it at forty times, and I would
put it at more than twice a week, and I would not be prepared to defend
either figure and I am not going to have to, because nobody is ever
going to ask me.*

And then he saw the dish.

It was a saucer. It was an ordinary white saucer off an ordinary tea
set and it was under the rose, and there was a fine grey dust in it
about a third of the way up the inside curve, and it had been emptied
at some point, because there was a tide line above the level.

*She put a dish under it.*

*She watched a thing shed and she went and got a saucer and put it
underneath so that what came off it did not go on the shelf.*

*Nobody does that for a dead flower.*

*You do that for a thing you have decided to keep and cannot keep, and
the dish does not stop it, and she will have known the dish does not
stop it inside about a week, and the dish is still there.*

---

He stood in the doorway for perhaps four seconds in total.

*She carried it here.*

*She did not leave it on the desk, where forty people would have asked
her about it and she would have had to produce a sentence. She did not
throw it out. She carried it the length of the village to a house she
does not live in and put it on a shelf in a room nobody goes in, which
is the only place in Konoha where she does not have to explain it and
does not have to look at it.*

*And it went on the twenty-fifth, and I last came up this hill on the
twenty-fourth, and there are thirty-six days on the other side of that
which I have written in a month-end and turned over.*

*I have always filed those two as separate.*

*I am not going to be able to file them as separate any more and I am
not going to be able to ask, and I am going to have to walk down these
stairs now with a face on.*

He did not have a face on.

He had never had one, and he was not going to acquire one in a doorway,
and behind him a woman he could not read was putting on a coat.

He went down.
