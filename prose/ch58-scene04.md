# Chapter 58, Scene 4 — The Chain

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1800 words.*

---

They went round it for about forty minutes and got nowhere, and it was
the best forty minutes he had spent on a problem in two years.

*State it properly. Three walls.*

*One: I have no notation. I cannot write it down, there is nothing to
write, and if there were I could not read it back.*

*Two: my method is to match a finished article, and he cannot match a
crystal article because he cannot make crystal, and if he could make
crystal we would not be having the conversation.*

*Three, and this is the one that actually kills it: what I would be
transmitting is not a technique. It is the absence of one. It is a
negative, and you cannot demonstrate a negative, and every single
attempt to teach a negative in the history of instruction has produced a
man doing something else very carefully.*

"That's it," said Jiraiya, at about twenty to five. "That's the whole
board. I've taught for thirty years and I have got nothing."

"Neither have I."

"Then we're done."

"We are not done," said Sazare.

---

"Kid—"

"I want to say why, because it isn't optimism." He had got up off the
floor at some point and was standing by the end of the desk, which was
where he stood when he was working. "It's that we've been asking the
wrong question for forty minutes.

"We've been asking *how do I teach it*. And the answer to that is no,
and it's been no for forty minutes and it's going to stay no.

"So stop asking it. Ask the other one.

"*Has it ever been done?*"

Jiraiya frowned.

"Has what been done?"

"Has anybody ever successfully transmitted a non-notational method to a
person who cannot read notation," said Sazare. "Ever. Once. Anywhere."

There was a pause.

"You," said Tsunade, from the desk.

---

She had put the pen down some time ago. He had not noticed when.

"Me," said Sazare. "Yes. That's one. There is exactly one recorded case
in the world of somebody teaching me anything, and it happened
seventy-two times over nineteen evenings between the eighth month and
the eleventh, and it worked completely.

"Katsuyu has no notation. Slugs don't write and they don't use seals and
they have never had a word for any of it — she has never once given me an
instruction. She looks at what my hands are doing and then she does the
thing correctly next to me until I can see the difference.

"That is not a teaching method that exists among humans. It cannot be
scaled and it cannot be published and there is no book at the end of it,
and it is the only thing that has ever worked on me."

"That's one end," said Jiraiya slowly.

"That's one end. What's the other end?"

"...Me."

---

"Thirty years," said Sazare. "You said it on the roof. Everything you
have got in senjutsu came off toads, and you did not learn it out of a
scroll, because there isn't one, because they haven't got one either.

"So there are two proven cases. A slug successfully taught a human who
cannot read notation. Toads successfully taught a human for thirty
years.

"And what we have been calling one impossible gap is actually three
steps, and two of them are already solved."

He put his hand flat on the corner of the desk.

"Me to Katsuyu. Proven, seventy-two times, in a yard.

"Katsuyu to a toad. Unknown. Nobody has ever tried it and I do not know
of a reason it should fail — they are both senjutsu traditions and
neither of them uses a notation, and whatever it is they hand each other
it is not writing.

"Toad to you. Proven. Thirty years.

"One unknown out of three. That is not the same problem we had at
twenty to five."

---

Jiraiya sat on the windowsill and did not say anything for about eight
seconds.

"That is insane," he said.

"Probably."

"That's a four-link chain with three translations in it, and every
translation is a place where a thing gets bent, and by the time it comes
out the other end—"

"Yes," said Sazare. "That's the second half and I'd got it before you.
That's the reason it needs me in the room."

"Go on."

"Every step, in the same room, at the same time, with me present.

"Not to teach. I've established I can't. To *stop deviation* — because I
am the only one who can look at what comes out of any link and say
*that's not it, go back*. Katsuyu can tell me what my hands are doing.
Nobody but me can tell her what the article is supposed to be.

"So the author sits in the room and the thing moves along the chain in
front of him and every link gets checked at the point of transfer."

---

"It'd be slow," said Tsunade.

She had come round the desk.

"It'd be *appallingly* slow," said Sazare. "Weeks. Possibly months. Every
transfer has to be verified before the next one starts and there is no
way to run two of them at once."

"Good."

He looked at her.

"That's the part I like," said Tsunade. "That's the part I like more than
any other part of this, and I'll tell you why in front of both of you
because it's about him and he should hear it.

"Everything that frightens people about that boy is *speed*. No seals, no
interval, nothing between the thought and the world. Hatake wrote it in
eleven words and I have had it read back to me by two people on the
council in language considerably less kind.

"And the only way anything he knows can be transmitted is a four-link
chain that runs at the speed of a slug explaining something to a toad."

She looked at Jiraiya.

"There is no fast version. There is no version where somebody takes it
off him. There is no version where it goes out of a building in an
afternoon.

"It is the slowest transmission medium that has ever existed and it is
the *only* one, and that is not a limitation, that is the best security
property I have been handed since I took this post."

---

"There's a diplomatic problem," said Jiraiya.

"There's an enormous diplomatic problem," said Tsunade. "Two summons
from two mountains in the same room in my tower. I don't think that's
happened since there were records. I don't know that it's happened at
all."

"Fukasaku'd come."

"Would he?"

"He'd come out of curiosity if I asked him properly and out of spite if I
asked him badly, and either way he'd come." Jiraiya rubbed the back of
his neck. "Ma'd want to come as well and I'd rather she didn't, and I'm
going to have that argument and lose it."

"And the slug's not the problem you think it is either," said Tsunade.

"No?"

"Katsuyu's on my contract. She'd come if I called her." She stopped.
"And I'm not going to."

---

Both of them looked at her.

"He asks her," said Tsunade. "That's how it works and that's how it's
going to go on working. There's no contract between them — he decided
against one, deliberately, for a reason I would not have thought of and
have not stopped thinking about since he told me — and she comes on
Fridays because she has said yes eleven times and he has never once
assumed the twelfth.

"If I summon her for this then she is here because I called her, and the
entire thing they have got becomes a thing I did.

"So he asks her. On Friday. Like always."

Sazare stood at the end of the desk and did not say anything for a
moment.

*She has just refused to use a summoning contract in front of two other
people at ten past five in the morning in order to preserve an
arrangement between an eighteen-year-old and a slug.*

*I am not going to be able to say anything about that today.*

"I'll ask her on Friday," he said.

---

"Right," said Jiraiya. "How do we know if it's worked?"

"You make one."

"Make one what?"

"A crystal," said Sazare. "A course. The smallest thing there is — the
thing I did on the roof, three deep, fine damping coarse. If the chain
works, that comes out of your hand and not mine."

"And if it doesn't?"

"Then nothing comes out of your hand and we've learned something worth
knowing, and it isn't nothing, because at the moment there is no
recorded instance in the history of the world of a kekkei genkai
appearing in an adult with no line and there would then be a documented
failed attempt, which is data."

He stopped.

"I want to be honest about the expected result," he said, "because I'd
rather say it now than after."

"Go on."

"If it works, what comes out of your hand is going to be terrible."

---

"Terrible."

"Worse than the first thing I ever made," said Sazare. "And I made that
at nine, in a room, on a floor, with a bit of gravel, and it lasted about
four seconds and had no order in it at all and I have got it written
down as *failed*.

"Yours will be worse than that. It'll be shapeless and it will not sing
and it will come apart while you're looking at it and it will probably
take you an hour to produce four seconds of it.

"And that is the finding."

"That's not much of a finding."

"That is the *entire* finding," said Sazare, and he heard his own voice
go up, which it did about twice a year. "That's the whole thing. Nobody
cares what it looks like. It doesn't matter if it's the worst article
ever produced in the Land of Fire.

"It has to *exist*, and it has to come out of a man with no clan and no
line and no bloodline of any kind, in a room, in front of witnesses, and
then it has to be picked up and put on a scale.

"Because at the moment what I've got is a slug's word, and that is
worth nothing, and I wrote that on page fourteen myself.

"And what I would have instead is an object."

Behind him, quite quietly, Tsunade said, "That's it, then."

"That's it."

"That's what I asked you for on Monday morning," she said to Jiraiya,
"and he's just built it in front of us in forty minutes at five o'clock
in the morning, and I want somebody in this room other than me to say
out loud that that is what happened."

Jiraiya was already standing up.

"I'll write to Myōboku today," he said.
