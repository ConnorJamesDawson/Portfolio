# Chapter 48, Scene 5 — What She Did Not Write

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1,827 words.*

---

Katsuyu went at about seven, having taken rather more than a tenth and
having said so, and having also said — from the table, to nobody in
particular, in the tone of a creature stating the weather — that she
would come if she were called, including for things that were not
instructions, and that she had said this before and would say it again
if required.

Tsunade did not answer her.

She sat at the scrubbed table with her forearms across it and her head
down for about four minutes after the slug had gone, and Sazare sat on
his stool and looked at a patch on his palm the size of a large coin
and did not say anything at all, because there is a species of silence
that has a person in it and you do not put things into it.

Then she sat up.

"Right," she said. "The next part is worse than the last part and I'd
like to get it done before either of us has slept, because we will both
be more sensible now and less honest later."

"The report."

"The report."

---

"I'll write it," said Sazare.

"No."

"It's mine. I ran an unauthorised procedure on myself, I wrote a
protocol with a risk line that said none identified and I underlined it,
and I put myself in this building at eleven at night and cost the
Hokage a night's work and the medical corps a hand." He had it in
order; he had had it in order since about four. "I've written worse
about myself. I wrote a slip for Ibiki in the fourth month that took me
four minutes and was the worst document I've ever produced and it was
the most useful thing I've ever done."

"I know. He told me."

"Then let me—"

"*No*," said Tsunade, "and I am going to explain why, and I need you to
follow the whole of it and not just the first half, because the first
half is going to sound like I'm protecting you and I am not."

She put one finger on the table.

"The moment anybody writes this down," she said, "there are two words
in a file, next to each other, and the words are *natural energy* and
*Sazare Ruri.*"

He stopped.

"That file goes to the Division, because an incident involving an
unknown mechanism and an injured shinobi is a Division matter and I
cannot make it not one. From the Division it goes into a summary. The
summary goes to a council of people, four of whom I trust and two of
whom I do not, and one of whom has spent thirty years collecting
exactly this sort of paper."

"And the paper leaves the building," said Sazare.

"The paper leaves the building." Tsunade sat back. "You have spent five
months in a correspondence with a man who reads Konoha's hospital
schedules, and you sent him eleven hundred words in the fifth month
under an arrangement I signed off on, and we do not know who he is, and
the notice on those department boards has stopped the ninety per cent
case and has not stopped him because we have no evidence either way."

"So a report is the leak."

"A report is *a* leak, with a delay on it, and I would be building it
myself." She rubbed her eyes. "And here's the second half, which isn't
about you at all. It isn't the incident that matters. A boy hurt his
hand — that's four lines and nobody cares."

"Then—"

"It's the *product*, Ruri." She said it flatly. "There is an inch of
green wood in a slip of paper on that table, produced by a Shōton user,
in four minutes, by copying. That is not an incident report. That is
the first piece of physical evidence anybody has ever had that a
kekkei genkai is a technique, and it is not explicable without the
hypothesis, and the first competent officer who reads the file asks the
obvious question and the honest answer is the sentence I made you say
out loud in my office on the first of this month."

"You want me to destroy it."

"No." She looked at him. "God, no. It's the only one in the world and
you'd never forgive either of us. I want it in a box, and I want the
box in this building, and I want to be the person who knows which
drawer — and I am aware, before you say it, that what I have just
described is exactly what a man collecting this sort of paper for
thirty years would also want, and the only difference between us is
which of the two of us gets to be wrong about themselves."

---

Sazare sat with it.

"Then you're covering," he said.

"Yes."

"That's—"

"I know exactly what it is." She said it without any heat. "I have been
in this job two years and I have done it four times and every one of
them was smaller than this, and I want you to understand something
about the arithmetic, because you're going to carry this and I'd rather
you carried an accurate version."

"Go on."

"If this ever comes out — not the hand, the *deciding* — then it is not
a scandal about you. Nobody will care what an eighteen-year-old
fabricator did in his own room. It will be a Hokage who concealed an
unexplained mechanism affecting a shinobi under her command, for a
personal reason, and there is no version of that sentence in which the
personal reason is not the first thing anybody says out loud."

"Then don't do it."

"I've done it," said Tsunade. "I did it at about two o'clock this
morning somewhere between the third unload and the fourth, and I did
the arithmetic properly and it comes out the same however I run it, and
I would rather you knew what it costs than have you find out in four
years from somebody else."

She stood up and went to the window, which was fully light now.

"And the last part," she said, "is that I don't get to be noble about
it, because there is a version of me that would have made this decision
for the wrong reasons and arrived at the same place, and I have no way
of proving to myself which one of them made it."

---

"There's a guard," said Sazare.

"There's a guard, and there's the man who relieved him at midnight, and
there's Shizune, who found me at seven and worked out the whole of it in
about nine seconds and has said nothing and will say nothing." Tsunade
had her arms folded at the window. "The guard is fine. The guard saw a
hospital fabricator come to the head of the medical corps at eleven at
night with a hand injury, which is *true*, and which is boring, and I
am going to have him write exactly that and I am not going to ask him
for anything else."

"And the other thing."

"What other thing."

"You came down the stair at a run," said Sazare, "in your shirt, with
your hair down, past a man who has been on that post for two years."

Tsunade did not turn round.

"Yes," she said. "That'll go round the second watch by Thursday and the
laundry by Friday and it will not be about a hand."

"Do you want me to—"

"There is nothing you can do about it that does not make it four times
worse, and I want you to notice that you were about to offer and stop
offering." She turned round. "It's mine. I ran, I knew I was running,
and I have been not thinking about why for about eight hours and I am
going to go on not thinking about it, and you are going to let me,
because you have been extremely good at letting things stand tonight
and I noticed it twice and I am mentioning one of them."

---

"Terms," she said.

"All right."

"I'm not going to tell you to stop." She came back to the table.
"That's the first thing and I want you to hear how deliberate it is. I
thought about it at eleven last night and I decided against it inside
about a minute, because I would be issuing an instruction I know you
cannot keep, and I did that at a gate three weeks ago and you were in
this building four hours later having broken it, and it was my fault
and I said so."

"So what."

"So: *you don't do it alone.*"

She put one finger down on the table.

"Not the crystal. The crystal's yours and it's been yours since you
were seven and I'm not going anywhere near it. I mean this — the other
thing, whatever it turns out to be called. Anything where you are
putting your own hands on a substance that we now know can fill a
person up. Not in a yard at midnight and not in your room with a
protocol that has no subject on it. You come and get me, and if I'm at
the capital you wait, and if you can't wait you tell Yagi you can't
wait and she'll get me."

"Yagi doesn't know any of it."

"Yagi doesn't need to know any of it to hear you say *I can't wait,*"
said Tsunade. "She's known you three years. She'd be up that hill in
four minutes."

Sazare looked at the table.

"That's not a term," he said. "That's you putting yourself in it."

"Yes."

"You've just spent four minutes telling me what it costs you to conceal
one night, and now you're—"

"Ruri." She said it quite quietly. "I have had one night of holding
your arm and putting it into a wall, and in about six hours you are
going to wake up and start thinking, and the thing you are going to
think about is *it's a medium*, and I would very much rather be in the
room for that than find out about it in the ninth month."

She sat down opposite him again.

"That's the term," she said. "It's a bad one and it's expensive and I
have not thought it through and I have never in my life made a
condition like it to anybody, including two people I would have died
for. Take it or don't, but take it now, because I've been awake for
twenty-six hours and by this afternoon I'll be sensible."

Sazare put his good hand flat on the table next to the other one.

"Yes," he said.

"That's it? Yes?"

"You told me once that I do better when the terms are stated." He
looked at her. "You've stated them. There isn't a second thing I need
to do to a stated term except keep it."
