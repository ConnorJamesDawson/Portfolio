# Chapter 39, Scene 2 — One Invitation

*Act 4, part D. Konoha General, the sluice. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1,141 words.*

---

He designed it for four days, which he understood by the third day was
itself the problem and did not stop him.

*Requirement: one invitation. Chapter six, page forty-one — "the reader
is advised that the marking of an occasion is very rarely the difficult
part. The difficulty is the asking, and the asking is a skill in its
own right, and it is best practised on something small before it is
attempted on something large."*

*Something small.*

He wrote out four options and struck three of them, and the one that
survived was not the one he expected.

*Option 4: ask a person to go somewhere for a drink.*

He looked at that on the page for some considerable time before he
understood what he had done, and when he understood it he put the
pencil down and sat back and was, briefly, appalled at himself.

*I have not designed anything.*

*I have copied it. This is Thursday. This is exactly Thursday — a
person, a place, a fixed evening, no stated purpose, nothing required
of either party except turning up. I have taken the only working
example in my possession and reproduced it at one to one and I did not
notice I was doing it until the fourth day.*

*I have one case. I have been running my entire social method off a
single case for fourteen months and I did not know it was a method.*

He thought about crossing it out.

*Alternatively: it is the only case and it works and I have no others,
and a man who throws away his one working example because he is
embarrassed about where he got it is a fool.*

He left it in.

---

He asked Yagi in the sluice on the Tuesday, at the end of a shift,
because the sluice at the end of a shift was where the two of them
conducted everything.

"Are you doing anything on the ninth?"

Yagi's hands did not stop on the tray, which he registered, and then
did stop, about half a second later than they should have, which he
also registered and could not read.

"The ninth."

"Of this month. It's a Friday. You're off from the fourth hour and
you're not on again until the Sunday, I looked at the roster, which I
am aware is—"

"You looked at the roster."

"I looked at the roster before I asked so that you would not have to
manufacture a reason, because I have worked out that people manufacture
reasons and that it costs them something, and it seemed better to
remove the need." He heard how it sounded. "That was probably worse."

"It was a bit worse."

"Right."

Yagi put the tray down.

"Ask the question properly."

Sazare took four seconds, which he had by then learned to expect and had
stopped attempting to shorten.

"Would you come and have a drink with me," he said.

---

There was a pause of a length he did not like at all.

He had modelled about nine outcomes over four days and had assigned
rough probabilities to each of them, and the highest of them, at about
forty per cent, had been a straightforward yes, and he had another at
twenty-five per cent that was a kind yes with an obvious excuse
attached, and he had one at about fifteen that he had made himself
write down which was *no*.

What he had not modelled was two full seconds of Nurse Yagi looking at
a tray.

"Not the ninth," she said.

*There it is.*

"Right. That's—"

"Ruri, *stop*." She had her hand up before he had finished the word,
which she had never done. "You have gone somewhere in the last second
and a half and I can see you doing it and I want you to come back and
listen to the second half of the sentence, because you have never once
in two years waited for the second half of anybody's sentence when the
first half was a no."

He stopped.

"Not the ninth," said Yagi, "because I am doing something on the ninth
that I cannot move and that I am not going to tell you about, and if
that sounds evasive it is because it is evasive and I would rather be
plainly evasive than lie to you. The tenth. Or any day the following
week. Or both."

"...Both."

"Both would be considerably better than one and I would like it noted
that I have now asked you for something, which by my count makes it the
first time." She picked the tray back up. "Tenth. Where?"

"There's a place off the west end of the market row. Six tables. No
sign."

Yagi's face did something extremely small and extremely fast.

"I know it," she said.

---

He wrote it up that night because he wrote everything up, and the entry
took him two attempts.

*Attempt one, unposted:*

*Asked. Refused. Alternative offered and accepted. Net result
acceptable.*

He read that back and struck the whole of it through, because it was
the version he would have written a year ago and it was wrong in
precisely the way that mattered.

*Attempt two.*

*Asked one person to go somewhere for a drink. Took four days to
design, which was three and a half days too many, and I copied it
wholesale off Thursday without noticing until the fourth day.*

*She could not do the ninth. She said so and then she stopped me,
because I had already begun the thing I do, which is to hear a no and
start dismantling the request behind my own eyes so that it can be
withdrawn before anybody has to refuse it twice.*

*She saw me do it. In about a second and a half. She has been watching
me for two years and she knew what the silence was and she reached into
it and stopped my hand.*

*And the second half of the sentence was two dates instead of one.*

He looked at that.

*Finding: I have spent nine years treating a no as a complete sentence.*

*It is not a complete sentence. It is very frequently the first half of
one and the second half is usually better than the thing I asked for,
and I would not know this, because in nine years I have never once
stayed in the room long enough to hear it.*

Then, underneath, in the second column where there was no unit:

*She was evasive about the ninth and said so rather than lying, which
is the single most Yagi thing that has ever happened, and I have
decided not to work out what she is doing, and I want it recorded that
deciding not to work something out is not a thing I have ever
previously been able to do.*
