# Chapter 65, Scene 6 — Notes

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, behind the east bathhouse. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2080 words.*

---

He read it in two evenings and made notes, because a man who hands you a
document wants it checked.

That was not a decision either. Kanzaki handed him a docket and he
checked it. Yagi handed him a splint and he checked it. Shikkotsu had sent
back eleven sheets of leaf with six numbered answers on it and he had
written up the actions arising in order of size, and had been grateful,
and had said so.

Jiraiya had put a book in his hand and said *you'll need this*.

So he read it properly and he wrote the notes properly and he took them
back on the thirtieth.

---

"Right," said Jiraiya. "Verdict."

"I've got fourteen."

"Fourteen what?"

"Notes," said Sazare, and got the sheet out.

Jiraiya looked at the sheet.

It was a full page in the ordinary notation, numbered, with the chapter
in the left margin and the page reference next to it, and there were two
more folded underneath it.

"...There's three pages."

"There's two and a bit. Some of them are one line."

---

"Chapter four, page thirty-one," said Sazare.

"Kid—"

"You have given the man a dimension and it is thirteen inches, and it is
in the text twice so it is not a slip.

"A woman cannot accommodate that in the manner described on page
thirty-two. I do not mean it would be uncomfortable. The mandible has a
range and the range is not a matter of enthusiasm — you would be looking
at a subluxation of the temporomandibular joint at about nine inches on
most adults and a full dislocation past that, and she would be at the
hospital and she would not be able to shut her mouth to tell anybody
about it.

"And the airway is behind the tongue. There is a limit on what can be in
the pharynx and it is a hard limit, it is not about willingness, and past
that limit what you have got is a woman who cannot breathe.

"So the scene as written ends with an emergency and not with the other
thing."

Jiraiya had gone a colour.

"It's a *novel*."

"Yes. It's a novel with a figure in it and the figure is wrong," said
Sazare, "and I have got no view about the rest of it. If you take the
thirteen out and do not replace it with anything, the whole of the scene
works and nobody in four countries would ever notice.

"It is the only number on that page and it is the only thing on it that
is checkable."

---

"Chapter six, page fifty-eight," said Sazare. "You have got a typo."

"I have not got a—"

"You have written *cervic*. It is *cervix*. It occurs once and it is
spelled correctly on page sixty-one, so it is the printer or it is a slip
and either way it is fixable.

"And while I am on it: what happens on page fifty-eight does not happen.

"Not in the sense that people do not do it. In the sense that if that
occurred, at that force, at that angle, the woman on page fifty-nine
would not be doing any of the things she does on page fifty-nine.

"She would be in a great deal of pain of a very specific character, she
would probably be bleeding, and it is a thing I have admitted to a ward
about four times in four years and all four of them arrived at two in the
morning with somebody who was extremely apologetic.

"You have written it as pleasurable. It is an injury."

---

"Chapter nine," said Sazare.

"*No*."

"Chapter nine is the one I would fix first if you only fixed one."

"I am not—"

"There is no lubrication anywhere in that chapter," said Sazare, "and the
act described requires it, and it is not a preference and it is not
squeamishness on my part, it is that the tissue there does not produce
its own and it does not stretch the way you have written it stretching.

"What is on page ninety-one is a fissure at minimum and a partial tear at
the figure you have given, and the woman is four foot eleven and the man
is the thirteen inches from chapter four, and I have costed it and it is
about a nine-hundred-ryō repair and a fortnight.

"It would be a hospital admission. It would be an admission with a
history that the patient would lie about, which is the part I would
actually mind if I were writing it, because you have got two people on
page ninety-two who are pleased with themselves and one of them would be
on my floor by Thursday."

Jiraiya put both hands over his face.

"How long have you been doing this?" he said.

"Two evenings."

"Two *evenings*."

"I read it on the twenty-eighth and I wrote it up on the twenty-ninth and
I have got eleven more," said Sazare, "and four of them are about
duration."

---

"Duration," said Jiraiya, from behind his hands.

"The man in chapter four is forty-one. You give his age on page nine.

"Between page thirty and page forty-four he is described as performing
five times in a period which the text puts at *a single night* and which
I have worked out as about seven hours from the light references, which
are consistent and which I checked.

"A man of forty-one does not do that.

"There is a refractory period and it is physiological and it lengthens
with age and it is not a matter of character. At twenty you might see
four in seven hours in an exceptional individual. At forty-one you are
looking at two, and the second one is going to be considerably later than
you have got it.

"It is the single most common error in the genre and I have read nine
pages of a hundred-and-forty-year-old book that made the same one."

There was a long silence behind the hands.

"You've read another one," said Jiraiya.

"I have read nine pages of another one and I have had them since I was
sixteen and they are about the etiquette of the approach and they are
useless," said Sazare. "I said so in an office on the twenty-first of the
eleventh month.

"Yours is considerably better. I want that said and I want it said before
I do the rest of the sheet, because I have given you four criticisms in a
row and you are going to weight them wrongly.

"It is a *much* better book than the nine pages. It is better in the
places that matter and worse in the places that are checkable, and
checkable is what I have got."

---

Jiraiya took his hands off his face.

"Say that again," he said.

"Which part?"

"*Better in the places that matter.*"

"I have got fourteen notes and eleven of them are anatomical or
arithmetical," said Sazare, "and every one of the eleven is correct and
every one of them is worth about nothing, because nobody who reads that
book is reading it for the figures and I have known that since about page
four.

"And I have got three that are not.

"Do you want those?"

"...Go on."

---

"Chapter eleven, page one hundred and nine.

"The woman says the same thing twice."

Jiraiya stopped moving.

"She says it on one hundred and nine and she says it again at the bottom
of one hundred and ten, and it is the same sentence with the word order
changed, and the text presents it as her meaning it more the second time.

"That is wrong and it is the only thing in the whole book that made me
put it down.

"A person who means a thing says it once.

"Saying it twice is what people do when they do not think the first one
held — it is a repair, it is a man going back over a stroke because he
does not trust the ink — and I have got that in my own hand about a
sentence I said in a room on the twenty-first of the eleventh month, and
I did not say it twice for exactly that reason and it was the hardest
thing I did that night.

"So when she says it again on one hundred and ten, what the reader gets
is not a woman meaning it more.

"It is a woman who has looked at his face and decided the first one did
not land.

"Which is a completely different scene and it is a better one and I do
not think you meant to write it."

---

Jiraiya did not say anything for about eleven seconds.

Then he got the notebook out of his coat, the ninety-page with the
binding gone at the top, and opened it about two-thirds through, and
wrote for about forty seconds.

Sazare sat on the wall and did not look at what he was writing, because
he had spent three years learning exactly how much of a thing he was
entitled to see.

"Say the other two," said Jiraiya.

"Chapter two, the whole of it. He apologises four times in nine pages and
she forgives him four times, and by the fourth one it is not a
relationship, it is a *procedure*, and I have got a book about that as
well and mine is worse.

"And chapter fourteen. She never asks him for anything.

"Not once, in two hundred and eleven pages. He gives her about nine
things and she takes all nine and she is grateful and it is written as
generosity on his part, and it is not, it is that you have written a
woman who cannot ask.

"And you have not noticed, because everybody who reads it is looking at
him."

---

Jiraiya wrote for considerably longer that time.

Then he shut the notebook and put it away and looked out at the steam
coming over the fence for a while.

"Kid," he said.

"Yes."

"Two things and then I want you to go away and let me sit here.

"The first is that eleven of your fourteen are correct and I am going to
fix about four of them and ignore the rest, and I want you to understand
that the ones I ignore are not being ignored because you are wrong.

"They are being ignored because the man in chapter four is thirteen
inches for a reason that has got nothing to do with anatomy, and a man in
a bathhouse in the Land of Rice does not want a *correct* figure, he
wants a figure that means something, and I have been doing this for
twenty-two years and I know exactly what I am doing and I do it on
purpose."

"That's fair," said Sazare. "I'd have said the same thing about a splint
that was over-specified for a ward that could not store it."

"...That is the strangest agreement anybody has ever given me."

"It's the same problem."

"It is *not* the same—" Jiraiya stopped. "It is exactly the same problem.
Kami's sake."

---

"And the second thing?"

"The second thing is that page one hundred and nine goes in the next
edition," said Jiraiya, "and it is going in the way you said it, and
there are about four people alive who could have told me that and three
of them are dead.

"And you are not going to be credited for it, ever, anywhere, because I
am not putting *technical assistance: S. Ruri, Medical Corps* in the
front of an Icha Icha."

"I don't want to be credited."

"I know you don't. I am telling you so that in about eleven years, when
you are sitting somewhere with somebody, you do not mention it."

Sazare put the sheet back in his coat.

"I'm going to mention it," he said.

"Kid—"

"Not on purpose. I have got no filter and I have never had one and
everybody in this village has told me so including you, and at some point
in the next decade somebody is going to say something about that book in
a room and I am going to say *I fixed page one hundred and nine* before
I have finished deciding whether to.

"I would like it recorded now that you were warned on the thirtieth of
the twelfth month."

Jiraiya put his head in his hands again.

"Go away," he said.
