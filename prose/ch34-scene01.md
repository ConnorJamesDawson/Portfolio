# Chapter 34, Scene 1 — A Hole in the Position

*Act 4, part C. Konoha General. Sazare Ruri, age seventeen. POV:
Sazare. ~1,389 words.*

---

Five weeks went past and he spent every one of them assuming he had
years.

That was the error, and it was not an unreasonable one. Kudō had said
two to three years to a first determination, probably three with an
appeal. Hazama had said a fortnight to never. Every single person who
had touched any part of it had handed him a number with a *year* in it,
and he had rebuilt his whole internal calendar on the assumption that
nothing about the Land of Water was going to move again inside his
lifetime.

The five weeks themselves were good ones.

Ubukata came again and wrote a larger number on the form than the last
one, which was the first time in Sazare's life that anybody had raised
a ceiling of his rather than defended one. The medical stone programme
went from five patients to eleven under Shizune's review, with a failure
in the eighth that went into the ledger and also into a letter of
apology Sazare wrote himself and delivered by hand. He re-sat the
infection paper and got seventy-eight, which Tsunade described in a
margin as *adequate, and about forty points of it purchased with
panic.*

Kakashi did not come back to training ground eleven, and did, twice,
appear at the hospital gate at hours that made no sense, and on the
second occasion stood there for eleven minutes talking about nothing
whatsoever before leaving, and Sazare understood about a fortnight
afterwards what that had been.

The reparations query stayed open on Kudō's desk, and he asked about it
once, and the answer was that nothing had moved, and he was on balance
relieved.

Then, on the ninth day of the following month, Sarutobi Kōji came to
the hospital.

---

He came to the ward rather than summoning him, which Sazare had learned
by then was a thing Kōji did deliberately and which had cost him at
least one argument with somebody above him.

He was greyer. The eleven months had gone into him the way they had
gone into everybody who had been holding the administration together
since the invasion — not as tiredness but as a kind of thinning, so
that the same man occupied slightly less space. His coat was the same
coat. It had been mended at the elbow, badly, by somebody who had used
a thread two shades off.

*Rung two: he's had that done rather than replace it, and whoever did
it wasn't paid.*

"Nine minutes," said Kōji. "Where can we sit that isn't a ward?"

They sat in the records store behind admissions, at the table with the
scorch mark.

"Normalisation talks with Kirigakure. A delegation arrives in three
weeks."

"I'd heard."

"Everybody's heard, the whole village has heard, my cook has opinions
about it." Kōji put a folder on the table and did not open it. "Two
weeks ago the normalisation office did what normalisation offices do,
which is run our own establishment roll against every list the other
side has sent us, in case there's anything on it that would be
embarrassing to discover across a table."

"And there was."

"There was you."

Kōji opened the folder then, and turned it round, and it was the
photographic copy Sazare had signed off in Serizawa's office eleven
months before — the grainy page, the print running uphill toward the
gutter, his own four-year-old face taken off a district register when
he was twelve.

*STATUS AMENDED. Subject confirmed serving, Konohagakure establishment,
medical corps auxiliary. Retrieval standing: MAINTAINED.*

"I signed that," said Sazare.

"You signed it as an accurate description of what they hold, which it
was, and which is why it went into a cabinet and stayed there, because
a threat-review file is a threat-review file and nobody in the
normalisation office has ever had cause to open one." Kōji tapped the
appended line. "Until a clerk with a cross-check list did, three weeks
ago, and had what I am told was a very bad afternoon."

---

"What does it actually mean," said Sazare, "for you."

"It means Kirigakure is presently maintaining a standing order for the
retrieval of a serving Konoha shinobi." Kōji sat back. "Alive,
undamaged, at a price. And they have known he was ours for at least a
year, because they updated the entry to say so, and they left the order
underneath it."

"That's a threat."

"It is almost certainly *not* a threat, and that's the part I want you
to understand before you spend three weeks constructing something worse
than the truth." Kōji held up two fingers. "Option one: it's
deliberate, and it's leverage they're carrying into the talks. Option
two: an office in Kiri that maintains lists updated a list, because
that is what it does, and the office that would have to withdraw the
order has never been told the talks are happening, because it's a
hunter-nin section and nobody tells them anything."

"You think it's two."

"I think it's overwhelmingly two, and I have thought so from the
first afternoon, and it makes absolutely no difference whatsoever."

"Why not?"

"Because I cannot walk into a room with the Fifth Mizukage and say
*we assume your standing order to abduct one of our people is
administrative.*" Kōji closed the folder. "It has to come off the list.
And the fact that it is on the list is now the single most useful thing
Konoha is holding, because it is the one item on that agenda where they
are unambiguously in the wrong and they will know it in about four
seconds."

Sazare sat with that.

"So I'm an agenda item."

"You're item four."

"Because it's inconvenient for you."

Kōji did not answer immediately, and Sazare had spent eleven months
getting better at reading the shape of a silence and knew, this time,
what was in it.

"Yes," said Kōji.

"Not because of the retrieval order."

"The retrieval order has been standing for six years and this village
has known about it since the day you walked up to that gate, and in
six years nobody has done one single thing about it, including me,
including the man in the hat." He said it flatly, without any attempt
to arrange it. "It became actionable when it became embarrassing. I am
not going to dress that up for you, because you'd see through it, and
because you have a habit of forgiving institutions faster than they
deserve."

"I don't forgive institutions."

"You extend them the benefit of the doubt on the grounds that they're
made of clerks, which is a species of forgiveness and is more generous
than most of them have earned." Kōji stood and picked up the folder.
"Three weeks. You'll be present. You'll stand, you will not speak
unless spoken to, and I am going to spend some of those three weeks
making sure that when they do speak to you it is on our terms and not
theirs."

"Am I allowed to refuse to be there?"

Kōji stopped.

"Yes," he said, after a moment. "Genuinely. It would make my job
considerably harder and I would not hold it against you, and if you
want that in writing I'll put it in writing this afternoon."

Sazare thought about a schedule with *Cl. ext.* in the status column,
and about a boy of thirteen who had gone out of a country on a boat in
the fog answering the only question anybody asked him with *I make
earrings*.

"I'll be there," he said.

"I thought you might." Kōji paused at the door. "One more thing, and
it's not administrative, so you can disregard it. There's a second
schedule coming with the delegation. Reparations, fourth tranche,
non-monetary."

"Non-monetary."

"Property. Objects. Things that were taken out of houses and turned up
in collections, and that somebody has spent two years matching against
a register." He was not quite looking at him. "I don't have the list.
I've been told there's an item against your family's name and I have
not been told what it is, and I could have not mentioned this to you at
all and let you find out in the room, and I decided about four days ago
that I wasn't going to do that."

He left.

Sazare sat in a records store behind an admissions desk in a foreign
country for some considerable time, and then went and did the four
o'clock observations, and got them all right, and could not afterwards
remember doing a single one of them.
