# Chapter 41, Scene 2 — What the Receiver Contributes

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,251 words.*

---

Kanzaki let him have the ledgers on a Thursday and made him work in the
yard office with the door open, which he understood to be a compromise
between the audit and the fact that she wanted to see what happened.

Three years. Three hundred and eleven entries. Six columns.

*Date · Item · Method · Source medium · Mass · Disposition.*

He went down the source column first and sorted it, which took him two
hours and produced four categories, because it turned out that in
three years he had grown crystal from precisely four things and had
never noticed that either.

*Yard aggregate (river, washed) — 214 entries.*
*Granite chip, quartermaster stock — 61 entries.*
*Site material, unspecified (field, invasion, exercises) — 29 entries.*
*Own prior work (seed, fragment) — 7 entries.*

Then he went down the reject column and sorted that against it.

It took him about eleven minutes to see it and then he sat perfectly
still for four.

*Yard aggregate: reject rate 22%, three-year mean, stable.*

*Granite: 31%.*

*Site material: 44%, but the variance is enormous and the conditions
were terrible and I would throw this category out of any argument.*

*Own prior work: 4%.*

He checked the last one three times, because seven entries is not a
sample and he knew it was not a sample, and because four per cent
against twenty-two is not a small difference, it is the difference
between a good year and a bad one, and he had been staring past it for
three years.

"You've gone a colour," said Kanzaki, from the doorway.

---

"The reject rate is a function of the medium."

"Yes," said Kanzaki.

Sazare turned round.

"You knew."

"I have known since about the fourteenth month, which is when there
were enough entries to say anything at all." She came in and looked at
his sorting, upside down, and adjusted one line of it with a fingernail
without comment. "I have not known what it *meant*, which is a
different thing, and I am a quartermaster and not a bloodline theorist
and it is not my job to tell you what your own work means."

"You could have told me the number."

"I could have told you the number and you would have optimised against
it inside a fortnight," said Kanzaki Uta. "You would have moved
everything onto seeded stock, your rejects would have gone to about six
per cent, your monthly output would have gone up eleven per cent, and
the Thursday ledger would have stopped being a record of anything
because every entry in it would have been the same entry."

She tapped the four categories.

"That column is the only reason you can ask the question you are about
to ask me. If I had handed you the answer in the fourteenth month you
would have destroyed the data getting to it."

---

He took it home and worked it for three nights and on the third night
he had it, and he had it because of a letter from a man with no name.

*It is the finding, not the confound.*

He had read that sentence about forty times in five months and had
solved a piece of it in a sluice in nine seconds and had then not gone
back to it, because he had been busy with birthdays.

He went back to it.

*Medical stone. Yagi loads the pattern; the device works on Daichi at
full effect. A different medic loads an identical pattern, same wound,
same dose, same key, and it works at seventy per cent, consistently,
across four trials.*

*Tsunade's reading: the tissue learned Yagi's hands over six
repetitions. The stone is not delivering healing. It is delivering a
pattern the body already recognises, and the recognition lives in the
patient.*

*Therefore the effect is not in the device. It is distributed between
the device and the receiver, and about thirty per cent of it lives in
the receiver, and that is why an unfamiliar medic loses exactly that
much.*

He wrote that out and then sat looking at it and let the other half
arrive, which took about nine seconds and which he had been avoiding
for five months without knowing he was avoiding it.

*If a living receiver contributes, then so does a dead one.*

*Aggregate. Granite. Site rubble. My own prior work.*

*Twenty-two, thirty-one, forty-four, and four.*

*I have been treating mineral as a passive medium since I was seven
years old. Every single thing I know about this begins with the
assumption that the stone does what it is told and that all of the
instruction comes from me.*

*It does not. The material contributes and it contributes a measurable
amount, and the amount depends on what the material already is, and the
whole of my reject rate is the record of how much I have been asking
different substances to do without ever once asking whether they could
do it.*

---

He tested it for eleven days at the fabrication bench with a chit and
Kanzaki's clock and it held.

*Test 1. Identical bar, identical method, four media, ten repetitions
each. Rejects: aggregate 5, granite 7, site rubble 9, seeded stock 1.*

*Test 2. Same, but with the growth deliberately slowed by a factor of
about four. Rejects: aggregate 2, granite 3, site rubble 5, seeded
stock 0.*

*Slowing it helps everything and helps the poor media most, which is
the opposite of what I would have predicted a fortnight ago and is
exactly what the model requires: time is not for me. Time is for the
material.*

*Test 3 — and this is the one.*

*A bar grown at ordinary speed on seeded stock, and a bar grown at
one-quarter speed on granite. Matched for mass to within four per cent.*

*Vice test. They fail within two pounds of each other.*

He looked at that result for a long time.

*Four seconds of my attention on a medium that is already ordered is
worth sixteen seconds of my attention on one that is not.*

*The medium is not the thing I work on. The medium is the thing I work
* with.

*I have had a collaborator for eleven years and I have been giving it
orders.*

---

The last entry of that fortnight was four lines and he wrote it
standing up.

*Consequence for the courses.*

*I have been describing courses for two years as layers I lay down —
one course for fast work, four for slow, fine damping coarse. That
description is correct in every particular and completely wrong about
what is happening.*

*A course is not a thing I apply. A course is the material taking up
part of the instruction. Slow work does not lay more courses because I
have more time to work; it lays more courses because the stone has more
time to* join in.

*Which means the number of courses is not a measure of my skill.*

*It is a measure of how much of the work I managed to hand over.*

He put the pencil down.

*And a master lays many courses fast, which I have been unable to
account for since the second one of these I ever wrote — because I have
been imagining somebody working faster, and there is no such thing.*

*A master is somebody who can get the material to take up the
instruction quickly.*

*The skill is not in the hands. It is in the handing over.*
