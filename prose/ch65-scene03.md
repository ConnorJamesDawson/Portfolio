# Chapter 65, Scene 3 — The Bathhouse

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, behind the east bathhouse. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2710 words.*

---

He was told to be behind the east bathhouse at four on the
twenty-seventh, in a note pushed under the door of the north store that
said *4 o'clock, behind the bathhouse, bring nothing* and was not signed.

Jiraiya was sitting on the wall of the drying yard with a notebook.

"You're early."

"Four minutes."

"Sit down and don't say anything for about a quarter of an hour."

Sazare sat down on the wall.

---

*Right.*

*He is working.*

*That is not a joke and it is not a euphemism and I have been standing
here for about eleven seconds and I have got it entirely: that man is
working, in the way that I work, and I have watched about four hundred
people work and I know what it looks like from nine feet.*

*He has got the notebook on his knee and it is a ninety-page and it is
about two-thirds full and the binding has gone at the top, and he is
holding it the way Kanzaki holds a docket.*

*He has written eleven times in four minutes.*

*He is not looking over the fence. He looked over the fence twice at the
start and then he stopped, and he has been looking at the *steam* for
about three minutes, which comes over the top of that fence in a
particular way at four in the afternoon in the twelfth month because the
air is cold and the water is not, and it goes about eleven feet and then
it stops.*

*He is not researching what everybody says he is researching.*

*He is looking at how a thing looks in cold air.*

---

At about twenty past he shut the notebook.

"Right," he said. "Two things and then a third thing that is going to
take an hour. Have you eaten?"

"Yes."

"You have not eaten."

"I have not eaten since about six this morning and I was going to eat at
seven."

"That's what I said." Jiraiya threw him something out of a paper bag,
which turned out to be a rice ball and which was extremely good. "Eat
that. First thing."

He got a book out of his coat.

It was small and it was orange and it had been read a great many times by
somebody and the spine had gone soft, and he held it out.

"Take it."

Sazare took it.

"You'll need it," said Jiraiya. "I hear you're a bad kisser."

---

He did not say anything for about two seconds.

*Right.*

*One source.*

*There is exactly one person alive who has that information and she has
got it from about eleven instances, and she said the word* terrible *out
loud on the second of the twelfth month at two in the morning in a long
room and then put her forehead on my shoulder.*

*And she has told him.*

*Not deliberately. She would not — she has spent five weeks arranging so
that nothing is said, and I have watched her do it, and she has not put
one false word anywhere.*

*She has said it as a complaint. To the one man she complains to. At some
point in the last three weeks, with a drink, in a room, in about eleven
words, and it will not have occurred to her for one second that it was
information.*

He looked at the book.

"Hime," he said.

"I'm not going to tell you where I got it."

"You don't have to. There's one place it can have come from and it has
not come from her on purpose, and I would like it on the record that I
am not going to raise it with her, because if I raise it she will work
out that she said it and she will be extremely annoyed about a thing that
does not matter."

"That's decent of you."

"It's not decent," said Sazare. "It's arithmetic. She has got about nine
things to be annoyed about this fortnight and I am not adding one for
free."

---

"Are you going to read it?" said Jiraiya.

"Yes."

"...Just like that?"

"I have got nine pages of a hundred-and-forty-year-old book about the
etiquette of the approach and a thing a girl said to me on a staircase
when I was fifteen," said Sazare. "That is the whole of my corpus. I
said so out loud in an office on the twenty-first of the eleventh month
and it was the most useful thing I said all night, because the woman I
said it to could not argue with it.

"You have just handed me a third source and it is longer than the other
two put together, and it is written by somebody who has done the
research, and I would be a lunatic not to."

Jiraiya opened his mouth.

Then he shut it.

"Kid," he said, "there is going to be a conversation about this at some
point and I do not think either of us is going to enjoy it."

"Probably."

"Second thing," said Jiraiya, and got off the subject with visible
speed.

---

"You spent it."

"I know."

"Say it back to me so I know we've got the same thing."

Sazare put the book in his coat.

"On the twentieth I won because nobody knew what I was," he said. "Not
surprise and not tactics — an unknown. He built a model of me off four
Corps papers and a reputation and the model did not have *the ground is
alive* in it, because until about five minutes fifty on the day it was
not true of anybody in the world.

"An unknown is worth an enormous amount and it can be spent once.

"I have spent it in front of six witnesses in a story that an
eleven-year-old had in a shortened and accurate form by the
twenty-third.

"And I have spent four years publishing the rest of me. Four Corps papers
with method sections. Four hundred rows of a reject table. A splint study
with eleven pages of method. There is a man in the Land of Sound who got
to my six-day problem off one question about a cohort because he reads
method sections properly.

"Everything about me is legible except the thing that was not true until
last Friday, and now that is legible too."

"That's the whole of it," said Jiraiya.

"That's the whole of it."

"Right. Now the part you have not got, and it is the reason I made you
come and sit behind a bathhouse instead of doing this in her office."

---

"You are not going to win another one."

Sazare did not say anything.

"I want that plainly and I want it now, four days after the best morning
of your life, because in about a fortnight you are going to start
believing what a village is saying about you and I would rather you had
this first.

"You beat Naruto because he did not look at the floor.

"That is the whole of it. He is sixteen and he has been trained since he
was nine by people who taught him — correctly — that debris is over, and
he stepped over your rubble about nine times and put his fourth step on
it because there is no reason on this earth for a man to look at the
ground behind him.

"Nobody is ever going to do that again.

"Not one person. Anybody who has heard that story is going to walk onto a
ground with you and the first thing they do — the *first* thing, before
they look at your hands — is look at the floor. And then they are going
to fight you on ground they have chosen, at a range you cannot afford,
and they are going to keep moving, and they are going to make sure that
nothing of yours is ever left lying anywhere near their feet.

"And you have got no answer to that at all."

---

"No," said Sazare.

"Say it properly."

"An informed opponent beats me," he said, "and I have known that since
about half past nine on the twenty-third and I have not been able to find
a way round it.

"Not because I am slow. I can put a wall anywhere in a split second and
the twentieth proved it. Because I am *expensive*. On ground I have had
time on, my articles actuate for about a fortieth and the day is long.
On ground I have not, everything is full price, and at full price my
whole war is about six minutes deep, and my range is a price curve that
goes to nothing at sixty feet. An informed opponent does not have to
beat me. He has to decline my ground and stay patient, and the
arithmetic does the rest.

"And I have read exactly one fighter in my life, for six minutes, and
he is the least deceptive human being either of us will ever meet.
Against a liar the instrument is untested.

"The one thing that made the twentieth work was that nobody knew, and
now everybody knows.

"And the mode is not an answer, because I cannot enter it *on purpose*.
I have done it once, by standing in a ditch while somebody tried to
take my head off, and I have got no procedure, no trigger, and no
account of the conditions, and the only creature who could teach me has
never taught anybody anything. And if I could enter it at will I would
still be a man who has to stand still to do it, and standing still
against somebody who knows I need to stand still is a way of getting
killed."

"That is a considerably better statement of the problem than I could have
given you," said Jiraiya.

"It is not a solution."

"No," said Jiraiya. "It isn't. But you are the only person I have ever
met who gets *worse* when somebody tells him something and better when he
works it out, so I would rather you had the problem than the answer."

---

"So what's the third thing?"

Jiraiya put the notebook away.

"The third thing is that from the second week of January I am going to
train you," he said, "and it is going to be four mornings a week and it
is going to go on for about a year, and I have already had it out with
her and she said yes on the twenty-second before I had finished the
sentence."

Sazare looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because you asked me a question on a roof on the sixteenth of last month
and I have been unable to put it down."

"I asked you about the fifth voice."

"You asked me whether it would work," said Jiraiya. "The chain. Me to you
and you to a slug and a slug to a toad and a toad to me, four links and
three translations, and I said it was insane and you said *probably* and
we did it anyway and it produced nought point one one grams of the worst
article in the Land of Fire.

"And I have had that in my chest for three weeks.

"Because if a thing that everybody has always called blood can be moved
along a chain into a man whose father sold eels — then everything I have
believed about what can and cannot be taught is a *rule of thumb*, and I
am fifty-four, and I have been teaching for thirty-one years."

He looked at the steam coming over the fence.

"I have got no idea whether you can be trained to fight," he said. "None.
You are the strangest set of capabilities anybody has put in front of me
and about half of them are in the wrong order.

"And I am not going to find out by standing on a roof."

---

"Three things I want to fix," said Jiraiya. "In order, and the order
matters more than the list.

"One. The hesitation.

"Not the fault the slug found — that is a real fault and it is eleven
years old and I have got no quarrel with her description, and she is
wrong that it is the first thing.

"The first thing is that you have never decided to hurt anybody and you
do not know whether you can. And until you know that, everything else is
theory, and you will get to the moment and find out on the day with
somebody's life in it.

"So we are going to find out in a field with me, where the answer is
allowed to be no.

"Two. Ground you have not had.

"Everything you own assumes preparation. You are the finest siege
engineer I have ever seen and you have got no army and no walls and
nobody has ever put you on a road.

"Three. Somebody who knows.

"And I am going to be that person, because I have read the report and I
have watched you for six minutes and I know exactly how I would kill you,
and I am going to do it to you about four hundred times until you stop
being killable that way and then I am going to find another one."

---

"And at the end of the year," said Sazare, "what am I? Because there is
no word for it. I have looked."

"No, there isn't," said Jiraiya, "because nobody has ever been one
before. Which means I am not training you *at* a style, I have got to
build the style, and I have already started, on a roof, three weeks ago,
the way you start everything — by writing down what the parts do." He
counted it off on his fingers, folding the thumb down last. "Stone.
Seals. That sword. Your own two feet, which have never been taught one
thing in their life and are about to be. And a fifth, under the thumb,
that I am not going to say tonight — because you get worse when you are
told, and this one you are going to want to be told *badly*."

"That is four fingers and a thumb held down."

"It is, isn't it," said Jiraiya, and ate the last rice ball.

---

Sazare sat on the wall of a drying yard with a small orange book in his
coat and a rice ball in his hand.

*Right.*

*Say the thing that has been in my chest since he said* four mornings a
week*, because if I do not say it now I am going to say it in the second
week of January in a field and it will be worse.*

"What's the target?" he said.

"Say the question properly."

"What does the end of this look like?" said Sazare. "Not the year. The
end. Because I have got a creed and it is in the front of four books and
it says *I am not going to get stronger, find out how it works*, and I
wrote it at fifteen and I have never revised it and I meant it.

"And you have just described a year of somebody making me harder to
kill."

Jiraiya was quiet for a moment.

"The target," he said, "is that at some point in the next year that woman
stops doing the thing she has been doing for six weeks, which is going to
the window every time somebody says the word *deployment* in front of
her.

"That's it. That's the whole of the target and it is not about you.

"It is not that you become dangerous. You are dangerous. It is that you
become *survivable*, so that when they finally send you out of that gate
— and they will, in about four months, and neither of us is going to be
able to stop it — she does not have to stand at a window."

Sazare sat on the wall.

"That's not the creed," he said.

"No," said Jiraiya. "It is not. And you are going to have to do it
anyway, and I am not going to pretend that is comfortable, and if you
want it in your book you can write down that I said so on the
twenty-seventh of the twelfth month behind a bathhouse with a mouthful of
rice."
