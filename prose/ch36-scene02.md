# Chapter 36, Scene 2 — A Number, Not a Wall

*Act 4, part C. Konoha, the administration building. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,169 words.*

---

He did not go in to ask about travel.

He went in about a stationery requisition, which was genuine — the ward
had run out of the ruled observation stock and had been issued
something with the columns in the wrong order — and the travel question
came out sideways at the end of it, in the doorway, in the manner of
somebody who had rehearsed it for nine days and had decided at the last
moment to make it sound like an afterthought.

"How does leave work?"

Ashida looked up from the requisition.

"Leave?"

"Not leave. Travel. Outside the village."

"Ah." Ashida put the pen down. "You mean *out of the country*."

"...Yes."

"Right." He did not make anything of it, which Sazare registered as a
deliberate kindness. "I'm the wrong person by about three departments,
but I know who isn't, and it's Amagai, and she's on the second floor
and she likes being asked things. Come on."

---

Amagai heard the whole question without interrupting, which took about
ninety seconds because he had constructed it badly.

She was in a smaller office than she had been in for the delegation and
had, Sazare noticed, exactly one personal object on the desk, which was
a chipped enamel mug with a stem of something dried in it, and which
was positioned where a person's left hand would go.

*Rung two: she's right-handed, so it's not for use, it's for looking
at.*

"Right," she said, when he had finished. "I'm going to answer the
question you asked and then the one you meant, and you can tell me if I
get the second one wrong."

"All right."

"The one you asked. You're establishment personnel with an operational
classification, so foreign travel needs a movement authority, and a
movement authority needs a purpose, a duration, and your commander's
sign-off. That's Kaburagi for the hospital side and personnel
administration for the other, and neither of them has ever refused one
that I'm aware of."

"That's it?"

"That's the *ordinary* part and it applies to about four hundred people
and nobody thinks about it twice." She got a folder out. "Now the one
you meant. You are not an ordinary case, and the reason is not
security and it is not your file and it is not the Land of Water. It's
this."

She put the fabrication schedule on the desk.

"Four years. Monthly delivery. Eleven per cent increment in the fourth
quarter." She tapped it. "You are the single point of production. There
is no second fabricator, there is no stockpile, and there is no version
of this schedule that survives you being out of the village for eleven
days."

---

Sazare looked at it.

"So the answer's no."

"The answer is *arithmetic*," said Amagai, "and I want to be extremely
clear about the difference, because you have come in here braced for a
refusal and you're about to leave with a calculation, and those feel
similar for about a day and then they stop feeling similar at all."

"Explain it."

"You're not restricted. You're *committed*. There is no clause anywhere
in that instrument that says Sazare Ruri may not leave the Land of
Fire, and I have checked, because I read it out loud to myself on the
morning after the delegation and I thought of you at clause nine." She
turned the schedule round. "What it says is that four kilos a month
goes to a coast. If four kilos a month is going to a coast, nobody in
any office in either country has the faintest interest in where you
personally are standing."

"So I'd need to be ahead."

"You'd need to be ahead by however long you want to be gone, plus a
margin, because a cart can lose a fortnight to a bridge." She sat back.
"That's it. That's the whole mechanism. Build a surplus, file a
movement authority, go."

"How much surplus?"

"How long do you want?"

He had not, he realised, ever allowed himself to answer that question
in a form specific enough to be answered.

"Eleven days there," he said. "Say a fortnight, with the crossing.
Eleven days back. Four days at the other end."

"Five weeks and change. Call it six." Amagai did the sum on the corner
of the sheet in a fast untidy hand. "Six weeks of schedule is about
twenty-four kilos, plus a margin — say thirty. On top of quota."

"Thirty kilos."

"Thirty kilos on top of a monthly quota that Kanzaki has already told
the fourth quarter is optimistic." She turned the sum round so he
could read it. "That's the number. It's not a wall and it's not a
favour and nobody in this building will ever have to make a decision
about it. It is thirty kilos of stone and a form."

---

He did the second calculation on the stairs and did not get to the
bottom of them before he had it.

Thirty kilos of surplus, at his current rate of usable output, at the
hours he actually had, allowing for a quota that was going up eleven
per cent in the fourth quarter and for the two nights a week he had
started giving to a bench that produced nothing anybody wanted —

Sixteen months.

He stopped on the third landing.

Fourteen if he gave up the bench nights. Twelve if he gave up the bench
nights and the library and the two evenings a week that had become,
without either of them ever calling it anything, a standing table in a
place with six tables and no sign.

Sixteen months, keeping all of it.

He stood on a landing in an administration building with a stationery
requisition in one hand and worked out, with total clarity, that this
was the first time in his entire life that a thing he wanted had a
price on it that he could actually pay.

Not *nobody sells an unaffiliated boy an education in chakra*. Not
*there is no route to the second door from outside*. Not a bounty, not
a residence order, not a broker in Tsuzura shaking his head over a
counter.

Thirty kilos. Sixteen months. A form with three signatures on it, none
of which anybody would refuse.

---

Ashida caught him up on the second landing, out of breath, holding the
requisition he had left on Amagai's desk.

"You forgot the—"

"Sixteen months," said Sazare.

"...Right."

"Thirty kilos of surplus. Sixteen months at current rate keeping
everything I'm doing, twelve if I strip it back, and I'm not going to
strip it back."

Ashida looked at him for a moment.

"That's the first time," he said, "in three years, that you've told me
a number without me asking for it."

"Is it?"

"It's the first time you've told me *anything* without me asking for
it." He handed over the requisition. "Sixteen months is a long time."

"Sixteen months is a schedule," said Sazare. "I've never had one that
ended anywhere I chose."
