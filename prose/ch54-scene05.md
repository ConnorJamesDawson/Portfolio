# Chapter 54, Scene 5 — The Ledger

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's office. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1610 words.*

---

"Fuck off," said Tsunade.

He had heard the word before. He had heard it in a yard in Kiri at
seven and in a corridor outside theatre two about four hundred times
and he had heard it come out of a man on a table with a femur in three
pieces, and he had never once heard it in that room and he had never
heard it out of her.

"Hime—"

"No. No, you've had your go. Sit down. *Sit down*, Ruri, I'm not
finished."

He sat down.

She was drunk.

He had been watching it come and he had it timed to about the last
fifteen minutes, and it was not the falling-about kind; it was the kind
where the working goes on exactly as it was and the door comes off.

"You want to know why me," she said. "You want to know what the fourth
one is. Fine. Here's a better question. Here's the question I've
actually got, and you can put it in your bloody notebook."

She came away from the window with the bottle in one hand.

"Why does any man want me?"

---

"There's a list," said Tsunade. "There's a short list, and it's the
same list, and I've had forty years of it, and I could recite it in my
sleep. Do you want it?"

"No," said Sazare.

"You're getting it."

"I know."

"One." She put the bottle down on the desk and did not let go of it.
"The name. Senju. Granddaughter of the First, grandniece of the Second,
last of a line that people write books about, and there is a specific
kind of man — and there's more of them than you'd think, and two of
them are on that council — for whom the entire content of the thing is
that he would be the man who *had* that. Not me. The line. He'd never
say it. He'd never know he was doing it. But he'd tell it at a table in
eleven years' time and the way he'd say it would be *Senju*."

"Yes."

"Don't you dare *yes* me on this one."

"I'm not agreeing," he said. "I'm telling you I've met him. He came to
the ward in the fifth month about a supply contract and he asked me
four questions about you and none of them were about you."

She stopped for half a second.

"Two," she said. "The story. Not the name — the *story*. The Slug
Princess, the legend, the drunk, the debts, the gambling, the woman who
walked out on the village and got dragged back. There's men who'd
rather have a story than a person, and I'm a very good story. And the
thing about them is that they're *nice*. That's the trick of it. They
buy the drinks and they laugh in the right places and they've read up,
and what they're doing is standing next to a fire so people can see
them by the light of it."

"Three," she said, and her hand went up, and it was the same hand and
about the same height and it did not do anything this time either.

"Three's this," said Tsunade. "This. What you're looking at."

"That isn't you."

"*It's a twenty-five-year-old woman, Ruri.* Of course it's not me,
that's the entire point, that's the point I made an hour ago and you
sat there and said *go ahead* like a bloody schoolboy volunteering for
something. Three's the men who want the henge. They want a shape. And
they know it's a shape, and they don't care, and some of them like it
*better* because a shape doesn't have anything behind it that can be
disappointed in them."

"Four. What I can do. And this is the one that isn't even dishonest,
because there is a class of man in this business who is going to die of
something stupid at forty-one and he knows it, and the woman who can
put a hand on him and stop it is not a woman, she's an *insurance
policy* with a face on. And I have never once blamed anybody for that
one. It's the most reasonable thing on the list and it's the one that
makes me want to throw up."

"Five's the mothering, and I'm not going to explain five, and it's the
oldest one, and it started when I was twenty-two."

"And six," she said, "is what I do for a living now, which is why the
first thing you did tonight was a very intelligent little speech about
what a signature means."

She stopped.

She had run out of hand, and she had run out of breath about a line and
a half before she ran out of list, and she stood there over him and
finished it anyway.

---

He was not looking at her.

He had his eyes somewhere around the leg of the desk and he was not
saying anything, and the reason for both of those things was not what
she thought.

*She has numbered them.*

*She did not do that tonight.*

*Nobody produces six of a thing in order, at speed, drunk, with the
weak one flagged and the ugly one left unexplained, unless the order
was already there. That is not a person losing their temper. That is a
person reading.*

*She has a book.*

*It is not on paper and it is not in a drawer and she has been keeping
it since she was about thirteen, and every man she has ever met has
gone into it, and it has been revised, because item four has a note on
it — she has written* the most reasonable thing on the list *next to
it, which is a marginal, and you do not write a marginal on the first
pass.*

*I have four hundred books of exactly that.*

*I know what it costs to keep one.*

*I know what it costs to keep one on people.*

*And I built mine because nobody was ever going to tell me anything and
it was the only way to find out how the world worked, and I have
thought for four years that it was the worst thing about me and the
thing I'd hand back if anybody offered.*

*She has got one too.*

*She has had one for forty years and she has never once been able to
close it, because every single time she puts it down somebody walks in
and gives her another entry, and the reason she can recite it drunk at
half past eleven at night is that she has checked it more times than I
have ever checked anything.*

He found, without any warning at all, that he could not see very well.

---

"Nothing to say?" said Tsunade.

"No."

"That'll be the first time."

"It's not that."

"Then what?"

He got it out and it was not what she was expecting and it was not what
he had intended either.

"You've kept a list," he said.

She went very still.

"You keep the list," he said. "You keep it in order and you've had it
forty years and you've revised it, and you put a note on four, and you
won't explain five, and you know exactly which one goes at the top."

"Ruri—"

"That's what I've got. I haven't got anything about the men. I don't
care about the men. What I've got is that somebody has spent forty
years maintaining a record of every reason a person might want to be
near her and *keeping it current*, and I know how much that costs
because I do it, and I do it with rocks."

The bottle came down on the desk harder than it needed to.

"Don't," she said.

"You asked me what I had."

"Don't do that. Don't you turn it round and be *sad about me*, don't
you—"

"I'm not sad about you."

"Then what the hell are you doing?"

"I don't know," said Sazare. "I've been in this room for an hour and a
half and I've got no idea what I'm doing and I haven't had that since
the tenth month of last year."

---

She looked at him.

And whatever was going to happen next, he thought afterwards, was
sitting right there and could have gone about four ways, and the one it
went was the one where she picked the cup back up.

"You cannot possibly love me," said Tsunade.

She said it quite quietly.

That was the thing about it. Everything for ten minutes had been at
volume and this came out flat and level and slightly bored, the way she
read a casualty figure, and it was not an accusation and it was not
cruel and it was not even aimed at him.

"You're eighteen," she said. "You've never had a friend until you were
seventeen. The first person who was ever kind to you was a slug. You
don't know what the word means, Ruri. You've got a very large number of
observations about a woman who is the only adult who's ever asked you a
second question, and you've done what you do with observations, and
you've built a thing out of them, and it's a beautiful thing and it's
*wrong*, and in six years you'll look at it and you'll know exactly
what it was."

She drank.

"So no," she said. "That's the fourth one. There you are. It's not the
rank and it's not the village and it's not the years."

"It's that you're a boy who's mistaken the only decent thing that's
ever happened to him for something else, and I am not going to be the
woman who let you."
