# Chapter 74, Scene 5 — The Question

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, Naruto's apartment. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~2,740 words.*

---

The chair had been in the apartment eight days and had already been
tipped onto two legs, by his count, four hundred times, which was
approximately the rate Handa Rokusuke had forecast to within an
order that would have embarrassed most engineers.

"It creaks now," said Naruto, tipping it, delighted. "Listen. It
does a thing."

"It has bedded in. The tenons have taken a set. That is the joint
telling you it has stopped being four pieces of wood and started
being a chair."

"That's the best thing anybody's ever said about my furniture."

"It is the only thing anybody has ever said about your furniture."

"Yeah, well." He came forward onto four legs and went back to the
pot. "Low bar. Eggs?"

"Eggs at the end. There was a trial."

"There was a *trial*," agreed Naruto, with enormous satisfaction, and
the second Tuesday of the standing arrangement got under way.

It was, Sazare thought — and he thought it deliberately, and made
himself sit inside it for a minute, because it had been the worst
Monday of his year and he had spent the whole of Tuesday being a
professional at people — the best-conducted hospitality in the
village. There was no grammar to it at all. Nobody asked whether
anything was wanted; things simply arrived. The good pieces came out
of the pot and went into the guest's bowl by ladle-fiat and the fact
that the guest was no longer classified as a guest had made no
difference whatsoever to the ladle.

*Item, filed: I came up those stairs today with the front of four
notebooks empty and a promise broken thirty-two hours ago, and I have
not thought about either since the door opened.*

*He does not know he does that. He would be embarrassed if I told
him, and he would then do it more.*

The notebook came out over the second round.

It was, by now, an object with a history: brutally creased, corners
gone soft, a ring on the cover where a cup had lived. He had watched
it acquire that ring across five weeks with something he could only
identify as professional pride, which was absurd, because he had not
made it.

"Okay," said Naruto. "Homework."

"...Homework."

"I've been working on the thing. The yard thing. My question." He
found the page with his thumb, without looking, which was a small
detail Sazare filed instantly and did not comment on: the boy had
been in that page often enough to find it blind. "So I asked you the
thing about the crystal drinking, right, and you did the — the
face."

"I do not have a face."

"You have a face! It's tiny! It's like—" he demonstrated something
with his eyebrows that was, Sazare had to concede, not entirely
inaccurate— "and then you said 'flagged, deferred' in the voice, and
then you moved us on to the ovals really fast, and Sakura-chan
didn't notice but I did, because when somebody moves you on really
fast that's where the thing is." He looked up, cheerfully. "That's
just true. That's like the first thing you learn."

*Noted,* thought Sazare, with the specific chill of a man watching a
load reach a member he had not calculated. *He learned it the way
he learns everything. Not from a book. From being moved on quickly,
by adults, for twelve years.*

"So I've been doing it in here," said Naruto. "And I got somewhere
and I want to know if I got it right, because if I got it right I'm
gonna be really annoying about it."

"Read it out."

Naruto read it out. He read it the way he read anything, at speed,
with the emphasis in unexpected places, and his handwriting was
apparently a challenge even to him in two places, and none of that
mattered at all.

"Right. One. The crystal *drinks*. Off the air. You said that in the
brief — it's not you feeding it, it takes it in on its own, that's
why the sword's been open since December and hasn't gone bad.

"Two. It doesn't fill up. It's been drinking for — what is it now,
three months? Four? — and you said the mass hasn't changed and the
order got *better*, which I wrote down in caps because that's
mental.

"Three. So it's either leaking it out somewhere, or it's putting it
somewhere.

"And it's not leaking, 'cause you'd have noticed, 'cause you notice
everything, and also 'cause you carry it against your back and
you're not dead."

He turned the page.

"So it's putting it somewhere. It's *keeping* it. Which means it's
not a sword, it's a — I didn't have the word so I wrote 'jar.' It's
a jar. And you don't make a jar unless you're gonna—"

He stopped.

He had stopped, Sazare saw, not because he had run out of page.

"Huh," said Naruto.

"Say it."

"Nah, it's dumb, it's not — it's not a shinobi thing, it's just—" He
turned the cup. Outside, the old quarter went about its evening.
"It's just that when you say it like that. A thing that takes stuff
in and doesn't let go of it and doesn't ever get full and doesn't
run out, and it just — sits there, being fine, and everyone looks
at it and sees a sword."

He shrugged, and his voice was perfectly ordinary, and he did not
look up from the cup.

"I know what that's like from the inside, is all. So I noticed it
faster than a normal person would've. That's all I meant."

---

Sazare sat in the chair he had commissioned for exactly this table
and understood, with total clarity, that he had four seconds.

*He has it. Not the mechanism — the shape. He has arrived at the
shape from the only direction nobody on the Friday circle
anticipated, because Fukasaku-sama assessed the threat as four
powers and a laboratory and none of us costed a sixteen-year-old
with a thing in his stomach recognising the silhouette of his own
life on a bench in a yard.*

*And in about one more exchange he will ask the direct question, and
I cannot lie.*

*I have never been able to lie. It is on file in two countries. And
the one time in my life it would be useful, it is not a virtue and
it is not a choice; it is a missing component, exactly like the
other one, and I do not get to decide.*

*So the only thing available is the truth about the shape of the
truth. Say it before he asks, so that it is a disclosure and not a
refusal extracted under questioning. He has had a lifetime of the
second kind.*

"Naruto," he said.

"Yeah?"

"Put the pen down for a second, because I am about to do something
to you that I have thought about for eleven days and I would like
you to have your hands free."

Naruto put the pen down.

"You are right," said Sazare. "About all four steps. Step three is
the one that matters and you got there in a fortnight from a
forty-minute tour, and I want that stated first and I want it
stated plainly, because what comes next is going to be difficult and
I am not going to soften it by pretending you were wrong.

"There is an answer to your question. I have it. I have had it since
the fourteenth of the first month.

"And I am not going to give it to you."

The apartment was very quiet.

"It is not mine alone," said Sazare. "That is not a form of words.
There are three people who hold it and two of them are not human,
and one of the terms — stated in advance, before I understood what
I was agreeing to, and I agreed to it in a frozen field and it was
the right term — is that it is not said outside that circle. Not to
the Corps. Not to a board. Not in writing anywhere that I do not
personally burn.

"So I am telling you that there is a thing and that I am keeping it
from you, because the alternative is lying to you about the yard,
and I cannot lie, and if I could I would not do it to you
specifically, and I think you know why."

---

He got the four seconds.

Naruto sat entirely still for about three of them, and then he stood
up out of the chair so fast that it went over backwards onto the
floor with a crack that the whole building would have heard, and he
did not pick it up.

"That's—" His voice had gone somewhere Sazare had never heard it go,
and it was not loud, which was worse. "You know that's the *exact
thing*, right? You know that's the thing. 'There's a thing, kid,
it's not yours, don't ask.' You know how many times I've had that
one? Do you want the *number*? 'Cause I've got it, I've actually got
it, I counted it after. I found out when I was twelve — somebody
screamed it at me in a forest to see what I would do — and I went
home and sat on the floor and I *counted*, and it was everybody.
It was every single grown-up I ever met, all of them, for twelve
years, standing in front of me being nice and knowing something
about my own stomach that I didn't—"

He stopped, breathing hard.

"And you're the guy who tells the truth," he said. "That's your
whole — everybody says it. 'Sazare doesn't lie.' It's like your
*thing*. And I liked it. I liked it a lot, actually, and I'm not
gonna say the rest of that sentence out loud 'cause I'll sound like
an idiot."

*Take it,* thought Sazare, and did not move, and did not defend, and
did not deploy one single word of the four excellent formats
available to him. *He is right about the category. Do not argue with
the category. He has waited twelve years to say this to somebody
who would stand still for it and I am the first one who has, and
that is the entire reason he is saying it to me and not to
somebody who deserved it.*

"I know," he said.

"You *know*—"

"I know, and I am not going to tell you it is different, because
from where you are standing it is the same, and it is only different
from where I am standing, and standing where I am is a privilege I
have and you do not, which is precisely what you are objecting to."
His voice held. It held the way it had held through worse. "The one
distinction I will make, and then I will stop, and you may do
whatever you like with it: nobody ever told you there was one."

Naruto stopped.

"They stood in front of you being nice," said Sazare, "and let you
believe there was nothing there. That is what you counted. That is
the number. And I am telling you, at eight o'clock on a Tuesday, in
your own kitchen, with the eggs in: *there is one*. It exists, it is
about the yard, it is large, and you are not being told. You now
know the exact shape and size of what is being kept from you, which
means you can be angry at the accurate thing instead of a fog. I
would rather be hated for the true version."

---

It took him about nine seconds.

Sazare watched the whole of it go through him at Naruto pace, and it
was, he thought afterwards, the single most impressive interior
process he had ever been permitted to watch, because there was no
technique in it anywhere. The boy simply went where the argument
went, at speed, with nothing held back, and arrived.

"...Okay, that's different," said Naruto.

"It is a small difference."

"No it's not." He picked the chair up. He did it absently, the way
you right a thing in your own house. "No, that's — that's actually
the whole thing, that's the *whole* thing, 'cause the bit that
messes you up isn't not knowing. I don't care about not knowing. I
don't know how the water tower works either. It's the — it's finding
out that everybody was *standing there*, doing a face, and you were
the only one in the room who didn't—"

He sat down, hard.

"Yeah. Okay. That's different." He rubbed his face with the heel of
his hand. "I'm still mad."

"Correct."

"Don't do the voice."

"It is the only voice I have."

"I *know*, that's the annoying part—" and then he laughed, once,
wetly, entirely against his own will, and the fight went out of the
room the way weather goes out of a valley.

"Two things," said Naruto, "and then eggs, and then I want to hear
about whatever happened to you yesterday, 'cause you came in here
looking like somebody hit you with a shovel and I've been polite for
an hour and I'm out.

"One. When the three of you decide — you come and tell me. Either
way. Even if it's no. *Especially* if it's no, 'cause a no I get
told is a normal thing that happens and a no I find out about is
the other thing."

"Agreed. Without conditions."

"Two." He pointed. "In the book. The one with the days in it. Write
it down, so it's a real thing and not a guy saying stuff."

So Sazare took out the diary and wrote it in, at the table, with the
pot going, under the date, in the small exact hand:

*Undertaking to N., given 17/3, unconditional and undated: the
question of the yard to be put to the circle, and the circle's
ruling brought to this table in person, either way, in full, and
whatever the ruling is he hears it from me first. No lie in the
interval. If asked directly again I will decline again and say I am
declining.*

He turned it round. Naruto read it twice, moving his lips slightly,
and then nodded once and pushed it back and got up for the eggs, and
that was the end of it, and it had cost — Sazare priced it on the
walk home and found the figure high and would have paid it again —
about eleven minutes and one chair, repairable.

*Findings, Tuesday, filed at the bench, late,* he wrote that night,
under a heading he did not enjoy writing.

*One. I am now a man who keeps something from Uzumaki Naruto. That
is a position occupied, at one time or another, by every adult in
this village, and every one of them thought they had a good reason,
and most of them did. The reason is not the point. The position is
the point. I am in it and I put myself in it and there is no
version of the next months in which I get out of it by being
careful.*

*Two, and this one is for the Friday: our cover has no expiry on
it. That is the defect. Three keepers agreed in January that the
thing is not said outside the circle and nobody wrote down what
would ever change that, and a cover with no expiry stops being a
cover and becomes a house, and I have signed a document in another
part of my life that says exactly this about exactly this failure
mode and I did not think to apply it here for nine weeks.*

*So the circle must rule in advance and not in arrears. Not "shall
we tell him" asked in a panic in the eleventh month, when the boy
has worked it out and is standing in the yard with the whole of it
and a face. In advance. With a condition written on it that a
reasonable person could check.*

*Three. He got there in a fortnight, from the inside, because the
silhouette was familiar. Nobody costed that. I did not cost it and
I have spent three months being told I am the finest instrument on
the continent.*

*The most dangerous reader of the spring project is not a foreign
power. It is a sixteen-year-old who has spent his life being the
thing nobody explains.*
