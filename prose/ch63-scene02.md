# Chapter 63, Scene 2 — I Cannot Teach You To Fight

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the north store. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1670 words.*

---

She said it on the Tuesday morning, at the start, before they had done
anything at all, which he understood afterwards was deliberate and was
the single most useful thing she did in four days.

"I cannot teach you to fight."

"I know."

"You do not know. You have decided that I will be able to give you
something and that we will find out what it is by trying, and that is
what you have been doing since the eighth month and it has worked
seventy-two times, and I want you to stop before we begin.

"There is nothing.

"I do not mean that I am not permitted. I mean that Shikkotsu has no
tradition of it, has never had one, and could not produce one if
everybody on that mountain sat down and tried for a year."

---

"Why not?" said Sazare.

"That is the correct question and nobody has ever asked it," said
Katsuyu, "and I have thought about it since Monday because I knew you
would.

"We cannot lose."

He stopped with the tin half open.

"Say that properly."

"I divide," said Katsuyu. "That is the whole of it and it is not a
technique, it is what we are. There is no part of me that is the
important part. If a thing takes a portion of me, the portion is gone and
I am not, and I go on, and in some months there is as much of me as there
was.

"I have been divided by weapons, by water, by a mountainside coming down
on me in the second war, and by a man who was extremely determined about
it for about four hours, and I have never once been in danger.

"So there has never been a stake.

"Every art of fighting that anybody has ever built was built by
creatures who could be *ended*, and every part of it — the guard, the
distance, the deception, the whole of it — is about the fact that there is
one of you and it can stop.

"There is no version of that on my mountain. We have four hundred years
of medicine and four hundred years of holding and we have nothing at all
about being killed, because it does not come up."

---

Sazare sat on the bench.

*Right.*

*That is the most alien thing anybody has ever said to me and I have been
in a room with four elders and a toad.*

*And it explains the entire curriculum.*

*Nineteen evenings and not one line about power. I have had that since
the twentieth of the eleventh month and I have had it as a deliberate
withholding — she was teaching me capacity because capacity was the
warning and I logged it in a column.*

*It was not withheld. There was nothing there.*

*She has taught me everything Shikkotsu has got that is applicable to a
human being with one body, and it is holding and it is healing, and the
reason there is no third thing is that the third thing has never been
needed by anybody she has ever met.*

"I've got one of me," he said.

"Yes."

"And you've never had to think about that."

"I have thought about it since the eighth month," said Katsuyu, "because
I have been sitting on a bench watching a boy with one body do things
that would take a portion off me, and it is the only genuinely new
thought I have had in a considerable time.

"But I did not have it before, and I have got no craft to hand you that
came out of it, and four days is not going to produce one."

---

"So what have we got?"

"Two things, and I want to say both of them badly first and then say them
properly, because you will take the bad version and build on it.

"The bad version is: I can teach you to hold, and I can watch your hands.

"You have just decided that is nothing."

"...Yes," said Sazare.

"Say why."

"Because holding is not a weapon and watching my hands is not a weapon,
and on Friday there is going to be a sixteen-year-old with forty
techniques coming at me and neither of those things will be in the room."

"Now the proper version," said Katsuyu.

---

"On the first: I have taught you to hold for eleven weeks and you have
been putting the number in a column and calling it an exercise.

"It is not an exercise. It is the only *quantity* you have got.

"You do not need material. You have never needed material. You have got
one limit and it is how much you can carry and how long you can carry
it, and everything you can do on that ground on Friday comes out of that
one figure and nothing else.

"You have been treating your capacity as a thing you are practising and
it is a thing you are *spending*, and if you go onto that ground still
thinking of it as a score you will spend the whole of it in ninety
seconds on the first four things that frighten you."

He did not say anything.

"And you are at six," said Katsuyu.

"Yes."

"You were at fifteen on the eleventh of last month."

"Yes."

"I told you on the twentieth to be bad at it for some weeks and that the
watching would wear out, and I was right and it is wearing out and it is
going to take some weeks.

"And you have got three days."

---

"So I go in at six."

"You go in at six or at whatever you have on the morning," said Katsuyu.
"And that is the first thing I am going to make you do, and you are not
going to like it: **we are going to find out what six is worth.**

"Not what fifteen is worth. Six.

"You have never once measured the exercise against anything. You have
been holding a sphere on a felt for eleven weeks and putting a number at
the bottom of a page, and at no point has anybody said *and how many
articles is that*.

"By tomorrow evening you are going to know, to about a tenth, what one
minute of hold buys you in volume and in reinforcement, and you are going
to have it in a table, and you are going to have it for the rest of your
life."

*Right.*

*I have got a reject table for materials and a conductor-grade table and
about four hundred rows of what things cost in mass, and I have not got
one line anywhere in fourteen books converting my own capacity into
output.*

*Eleven weeks.*

*I have been running the most important quantity in my life as a score.*

"That's the fault," he said.

"That is one of them," said Katsuyu. "There is a larger one and I have
had it since Monday evening and I am not going to give it to you until
tomorrow, because you will want to fix it and it cannot be fixed in three
days and you would spend all three trying."

---

"And the second thing," he said. "Watching my hands."

"Yes."

"That's not a weapon either."

"It is the only thing that has ever worked on you," said Katsuyu.

She was on the corner of the bench, at nine inches, where she had been
for seventy-two evenings.

"I have found eleven years of faults in you by watching and telling you
what I saw, and every single one of them was invisible to you and would
have stayed invisible for the rest of your life, and there is not one of
them that you would have found on your own — you have said so yourself
about four times and written it down twice.

"And you have never once done anything under pressure.

"Every fault I have ever found in you was found at a bench, in a yard, at
night, with nobody in the room and nothing at stake and all the time in
the world.

"You do not know what your hands do when somebody is coming at you.

"Nobody does. There is not one person alive who knows what you do in
that condition, including you, because it has never happened."

She paused.

"So that is what the three days are for. Not to teach you. To find out
what you *are* before Friday finds out for you in front of the Hokage of
Konohagakure and about eleven other people."

---

He got up off the bench and stood in the middle of thirty feet by
eighteen with the north light coming in.

*She is right and it took her about four minutes.*

*Everything I know about myself was established in a quiet room.*

*Four hundred pages. Fourteen books. Nine hundred articles. Every single
observation in the whole of it was taken at a bench with a lamp on and
nobody in the room, and I have built the entire model of myself out of a
sample that has one condition in it.*

*I have got about nine thousand data points and every one of them is from
the same experiment.*

*And on Friday morning the condition changes for the first time in
eleven years and I have no idea whether any of it transfers.*

"How do we do it?" he said.

"Badly," said Katsuyu. "And with somebody in the room."

"You can't hit me."

"No."

"So who—"

And then he worked it out, and stopped, and put his hand over his eyes
for a second.

"Oh, no," he said.

"She has already agreed," said Katsuyu.

"She's the Hokage."

"She has already agreed," said Katsuyu, "and she agreed on Monday night,
before I asked, and she said one thing about it, which I was asked to
pass on, and it was: *tell him I will not be gentle and tell him I said
it in advance.*"
