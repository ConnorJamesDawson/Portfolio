# Chapter 63, Scene 7 — Believe In Me

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1900 words.*

---

She came down the corridor at about ten past twelve and he was awake, and
she knew he was awake before she got the lamp down, because she said
"don't pretend" from about four feet away and he had not been pretending
and had not had time to work out how you would.

"I wasn't."

"No, you weren't. You've been lying there for two hours."

"An hour and forty."

"Doing what?"

"The table," said Sazare. "And the range curve, and then the thing about
placed articles for about the fourth time, and then something else."

"What else?"

"That's the something else," he said.

---

She got in and did not say anything for a bit.

He lay on his back and looked at a ceiling he had now looked at
twenty-three times and did the last of the arithmetic, and then stopped
doing arithmetic, which took some effort and which he was getting
marginally better at.

Then he turned over and kissed her.

He did it carefully, which was the whole of what he had learned in
eighteen days, and he did not correct anything, which was the second
thing, and he came out of it after about two seconds because two seconds
was the figure he had worked out on about the ninth occasion and had not
revised.

*Better.*

*Not good. Better. No correction, no adjustment, angle acceptable, hand
in a place that was not actively unhelpful.*

*I have got about eleven of these now and I have written down four of
them and the four are not in any of the fourteen books and never will
be.*

"That was better," said Tsunade.

"I know."

"You've been *working* on it."

"There's no procedure. I asked."

"You *asked*—"

"Not you. I asked chapter two of a book I wrote, which does not have a
section on it, and then I stopped asking anything and did it about eleven
times."

Tsunade put her hand over her face in the dark.

"Kami," she said.

---

He lay there for about a minute.

"There's a thing I want to ask you," he said, "and I've been building it
since about five o'clock this afternoon and I stopped twice at a door and
once on a step, and I am going to say it badly because if I build it any
further it will have four clauses in it and a release at the end."

"Then say it badly."

"I know you think I'm going to lose."

She did not answer.

"I want to be accurate about how I know, because it is not a guess and I
would rather have it on the table than have you wondering which of the
four hundred things gave it away.

"You have not said one word about Friday since Monday night. Not one. You
have said about nine hundred words about a conversion table and about
sixty about a range curve and you have been extremely good about both,
and you have not asked me one single question about the twentieth.

"You walked at me nine times this morning and you did not say anything
afterwards and you went and stood at the end of the yard with your arms
folded.

"You were at that window when I came in at half nine and you had been at
it long enough for the lamp to be cold.

"And Jiraiya was on the bathhouse roof for three days and came through
that window at half eleven and went out of it at midnight, and I heard
neither of those and I know both of them, because there is a tile up there
that has been out of line since before I got to this village and it is in
line now and I go past it four times a week."

He lay on his back.

"So you have had something since about eleven o'clock this morning and
you have decided not to say it, and I have gone through what it might be
about six times and I have got two candidates and I am not going to ask
you which."

"Why not?"

"Because you decided not to tell me," said Sazare, "and you will have had
a reason, and I have watched you decide things about me for three years
and the reasons have been good every single time including the ones I
lost arguments about.

"And because if I make you say it tonight then whatever it is, I will go
onto that ground on Friday morning having decided to correct it, and I
would be doing it in thirty-six hours, and I have got a fairly good idea
of how that goes."

---

Tsunade was quiet for some seconds.

"That's the reason," she said.

"I know."

"You *worked out my reason*."

"It wasn't hard. It's the reason I'd have had." He turned his head.
"That's not the ask."

"Then what's the ask?"

*Right. Say it.*

"I know you think I'm going to lose," said Sazare, "and I think you're
probably right, and I've got the table and the curve and about two-fifths
of a capacity and a fault that's eleven years old, and I have run it and
I do not like the answer either.

"I'm not asking you to think I'll win.

"I'd like you to believe in me anyway."

---

The room was extremely quiet.

*That is the second thing I have ever asked her for.*

*Twelfth of the eleventh month, in a kitchen, at about half past seven in
the morning, and it was four words and it was about a kiss and she did
not answer it and went to work.*

*And this is the second, and it is thirty-nine days later, and it is
worse, because the first one she could give me by doing a thing and this
one has got nothing in it that anybody can do.*

*I am asking a woman who has looked at the evidence and reached the
correct conclusion to hold a position that the evidence does not support.*

*Which is the single most unreasonable request I have ever made of
anybody and I am aware of it and I am making it anyway, because I have
been lying here for an hour and forty and it is the only thing I want,
and I have not got a construction for why.*

He did not add a release.

He got about four seconds into building one and then stopped, deliberately,
with the whole of it assembled and ready, and did not say it.

---

"Right," said Tsunade.

She turned over onto her back.

"On Tuesday afternoon," she said, "at about four o'clock, in a room with
Jiraiya in it and three other people, I was asked which way I was going on
the twentieth.

"And I said your name. Out loud. In front of witnesses.

"Now. Do you know what that means in this village?"

"Jiraiya explained it to me on Wednesday morning in your office in front
of you, at some length, and appeared to enjoy it."

"He did enjoy it. He has been enjoying it since Tuesday and he will be
enjoying it at my funeral." She put her hands behind her head. "So you
know the record."

"Forty years. You pick a thing and the thing does not come in, and there
are people in three countries who follow you about and take the other
side and do it professionally and it works."

"That's the record."

"Yes."

"Then I would like you to consider," said Tsunade, "that on Tuesday
afternoon I looked at the sensible money, which was a boy with two years
in the field and forty techniques and about four hundred fights, and I
looked at a hospital fabricator who has never been hit on purpose, and I
put my own name behind the fabricator in front of the one man alive who
can read me.

"Which is, in my case, a documented curse. Actuarially. There is a
*table*.

"So you can have belief. It is on the record, it was witnessed, it is
being repeated in a bathhouse as we speak, and by Sunday there will not be
one person in this village who does not know that the Hokage of
Konohagakure looked at those two boys and picked the wrong one.

"I have not just believed in you, Ruri. I have gone on record believing in
you at odds, in public, against my own judgement and my own history and
about nine people's money.

"I'd say the ask is answered and I would like it noted that I answered it
three days before you got round to making it."

---

He lay in the dark.

*Right.*

*That is a deflection and it is a very good one and it took her about six
seconds.*

*She has answered a thing she cannot say by pointing at a thing she has
already done, and every single word of it is true, and it is funny, and
it is the funniest available version and she has picked it deliberately
so that neither of us has to sit here at midnight while she says a
sentence.*

*And it is not a deflection.*

*That is the part I want down, and I am not going to be able to write it
anywhere and it does not matter.*

*She was asked, at four o'clock on a Tuesday, in a room, with about a
second and a half, and she had the evidence and the evidence said the
boy, and she said my name.*

*She could not do it.*

*Jiraiya said so in front of her on Wednesday morning on purpose because
he was not going to say it to her, and she did not argue with him, and
she has not argued with it now.*

*She has just told me, at ten past twelve at night, in a joke, with a
table in it, that she believes in me against her own judgement.*

*Which is exactly the thing I asked for and I did not know it was
available and I did not know it was the shape of it.*

"That's the ask answered," he said.

"Good."

"I'd like it recorded that you did it with a joke."

"That is how it is going to be recorded," said Tsunade, "and it is how it
is going to stay recorded, and if you write it down any other way in any
book that exists I will find the book."

"They're in a dye-works."

"I know where they are. I signed the docket."

---

He lay there for a bit longer and then said the last one, because it was
going to be either now or never and he had had it since the yard.

"Hime."

"Mm."

"You didn't tell me the thing."

"No."

"That's fine. I'd like you to know I'm not going to ask again and I'm not
going to work on it and I'm not going to take it apart on the walk down
in the morning.

"You'll tell me when you tell me."

"Ruri—"

"That's the whole of it," said Sazare. "Go to sleep."

And she made a noise that was not a word, and after about a minute she
found his hand on top of the covers between them and took hold of it, and
she did not say anything else at all.

He went out in under two minutes.
