# Chapter 62, Scene 7 — The Room

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1150 words.*

---

He went up at about half past three to do the last walk of it, which was
a thing you did, apparently, and which he had never done before because
he had never left anywhere on purpose.

The room was empty.

It took about nine seconds to establish that. There was a futon rolled
against the wall for the landlord and a low table under the window and
nothing else at all, and the light was doing what the light did at half
past three in the twelfth month, which was come in flat and orange and
lie along the floor as far as the middle board.

He stood in the middle of it and waited.

Nothing happened.

---

*Right.*

*I have been putting this off for seventeen days and I have come up here
nine times and stood in the middle of it for between four and eleven
minutes and gone away again, and I have written down that the reason was
that I did not want it handled.*

*And it is a room.*

*It is eleven feet by fourteen with a west window and a floor that goes
at the third board and a hasp with no staple in it, and there is nothing
in here.*

*I have got four hundred pages written in this room and about six hundred
articles made on that table and I did the two columns on this floor at
sixteen and found out that they did not balance, and I sat against that
wall on the sixteenth of the eighth month at four in the afternoon and
laid a course with an eighty-five per cent hand to find out whether I
still had a trade.*

*And none of that is in here.*

*It is in the box, and the box is in a dye-works behind a hospital with
a padlock on it, and I put it there myself this morning with a
sixteen-year-old on the other end of a cart.*

He looked at the middle board where the light was.

*So what was I putting off?*

---

It took him about four minutes and he got it standing up, which was
unusual.

*I was not putting off the room.*

*I have been coming up here nine times in seventeen days for a nib and a
tin and a coat, and every single one of them was true and every single
one of them was an errand, and the errand is not the reason.*

*Her door is four feet from the bottom of that stair.*

*I have come up past it nine times and gone down past it nine times and
the kettle was going every single time, and there was no version of
knocking on that door where I did not have to write the sentence.*

*I was not putting off the room. There is nothing in the room. I have
been putting off a table with a slate on it.*

*And I have just spent an hour and a half at it and it was the best hour
and a half I have had this month, and she has had a man since 1958 who
could not write and took two years to teach and had a terrible hand, and
I have known her for two years and three months and I found that out
this afternoon because I was leaving.*

*Which is the whole of it, and it is on the second column, and it does
not balance and nobody in this street has ever been keeping a total.*

---

He went and stood at the window for a bit.

The persimmon had three leaves on it now, which was one fewer than this
morning, and the rest of them were on the bathhouse roof where they had
been since the eleventh month, and O-Shige had said in the tenth month
of last year that somebody ought to get up there and Nemoto had said that
somebody had, once, in about the ninth year, and it had not helped.

*I am going to be able to see this street from nowhere.*

*The tower is on the hill and the window in that room faces the wrong
way and looks at the roof of the records annexe, and I will be able to
see the hospital and the annexe and a corner of the bathhouse and about
four hundred roofs, and one of them will be this one and I will not be
able to tell which.*

*And that is the thing I have not thought about at any point in
seventeen days.*

*Not the room. Not the bench. The street.*

*Eleven doors. A shutter, four windows, eleven knives, a trestle leg, a
bathhouse door closer, the stair-nosing on the second flight, a mangle,
three chairs, a kettle handle, a tap twice, and a window catch four
times because she opens it with her foot.*

*I have got an entire column of a village down there and I have been
treating it as a place where I sleep.*

---

He went out and shut the door and stood on the landing.

Then he opened the tool roll.

The staple had been in the third pocket since the third month of the
year, because he had bought it with four other things and had never
fitted it and had written down twice that he would.

He fitted it.

It took about eleven minutes because the frame had gone at the top and he
had to sink it into sound wood two inches lower and then plug the old
holes so that the landlord would not take it out of somebody's deposit,
and he did it properly, with the good chisel, kneeling on a landing at
four o'clock in the afternoon in a building he did not live in any more.

Then he tried it.

The hasp came over and the staple took it and the door held.

*Right.*

*Nobody is going to know that is there.*

*The landlord will put somebody in here in about a fortnight and they
will come up that stair with whatever they have got and they will put a
lock through that staple on the first night without once thinking about
it, and they will never find out that for two years and three months
there was no staple in that frame and a man kept fourteen books in the
room behind it.*

He put the chisel away and rolled the tool roll.

*And that is the correct outcome and I would not change it.*

*And it goes in the first column, and the first column has never once
balanced against the second one, and nobody on this street has ever been
keeping a total, and I have been keeping one since I was nine.*

He went down the stair.

The kettle was going.

He did not knock, because it was Saturday and they wrote on Tuesdays.
