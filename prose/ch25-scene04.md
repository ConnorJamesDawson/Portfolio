# Chapter 25, Scene 4 — The Approach

*Act 4, part B. Konoha, the hospital approach. Sazare Ruri, age
sixteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,696 words.*

---

They lost the gate at nine forty-three and took it back at nine forty-seven.

Sazare knew the times because the clock above admissions remained visible
through the broken windows. Everything else became positions.

The forecourt was thirty-four feet wide at the hospital steps and narrowed to
twenty-one at the gate. A low wall enclosed the western side. The eastern side
belonged to a pharmacy whose shutters had been pulled down from inside; its
projecting sign made the roof above it unusable to anybody taller than a
child. Across the road stood the dumpling stall, a stationer's shop and a
house with a tiled wall high enough to hide behind and too low to stop a
shinobi.

The road itself fell towards the stadium. Its centre had been resurfaced the
previous year and contained fine river aggregate. The gutters were older, pale
granite blocks with open joints where weeds grew after rain. Crystal answered
the gutters better. The new road answered faster.

Sazare told Daichi both things.

"Which one kills people?" Daichi asked.

"Either."

"Which one stops them?"

"The gutter, if they don't see it."

Daichi put the genin off the gutter and did not ask for the rest of the
explanation.

The burned chūnin was Nohara Daichi. He put one genin on the wall, one at the
steps, Ashida on the eastern flank and himself wherever the line was failing.
He put Sazare three yards behind the gate.

"You don't chase," he said.

"I wasn't going to."

"Nobody is going to. I'm saying it to you because you're looking at the road
like you intend to rebuild it."

"The camber is useful."

"Good. Use it here."

The first push came under smoke. Sazare could not see the bend, then could not
see the gate, then saw a sandal enter the clear air at knee height. He grew a
low rib from the drainage joint. The runner caught it, turned the fall into a
roll and came up inside the perimeter. Daichi met him. A second attacker
cleared the rib and landed on the wall. The genin there lasted two exchanges
before Ashida struck the attacker from behind with a length of broken gate
hinge.

None of it was clean. People collided. A Konoha kunai struck Sazare's plate
and glanced into the steps. Sazare put a crystal brace where Daichi meant to
retreat and nearly trapped his ankle. Somebody hit Sazare in the mouth. He
never saw who.

The taste of blood made him think he had lost a tooth. He checked with his
tongue while pushing chakra into the road and found all of them present. A
resonant burst went through the low wall and turned the top course into
fragments. One struck him above the eyebrow. Another cut the genin at the
steps. Ashida shouted that the eastern roof was occupied. Daichi shouted
something else at the same time. Sazare obeyed both by doing neither: he
crouched behind the remaining wall until the projectiles passed.

When he stood, the attacker on the roof had changed position and the genin had
killed the person at the steps. Sazare had missed enough of the exchange that
there was no honest way to reconstruct it.

This was what combat training was for. Not technique. Knowing which voice
mattered while three people were shouting and the road was coming apart.

He spent four grains in ninety seconds.

The first made a breast-high baffle across half the gate. Sound broke it. Its
pieces remained.

The second anchored the loose gate leaf long enough for two casualties to
pass.

The third became a sloped plate in the road. An Oto shinobi broke it
deliberately, then stepped among its pieces. Sazare grew from the piece behind
his heel and put a spike through the back of his knee. Daichi finished him.

The fourth Sazare pressed into the plate on his arm. The plate thickened, took
another resonant strike and lost its outer course without opening.

"Hers breaks!" somebody shouted from the smoke.

That was the second time.

The smoke cleared in strips. There were seven attackers in the road and more
on the roofs. One wore a Sand forehead protector and was fighting a Sound
shinobi over access to the same wall, which answered any remaining question
about how organised the invasion was.

The Sand shinobi won the wall, saw the hospital sign and threw two paper bombs
at the windows. The Sound shinobi he had displaced caught his ankle from
below. Both disappeared over the far side before the bombs landed.

Sazare grew a sloped face from the forecourt paving. The first bomb struck and
slid. The second adhered to the crystal long enough for Daichi to kick the
whole thin plate away from the building. It detonated above the pharmacy. The
projecting sign vanished. Burning wood came down in pieces.

The hospital windows behind them remained open black rectangles. No one
appeared in them. A moment later a white carry sheet moved past the men's end,
held at four corners, and Sazare knew they had begun consolidating the second
floor away from the street-facing wall.

They could not evacuate. They could at least make the windows less useful to
an attacker.

The hospital was not being attacked by one unit. It was a fact in the village,
and everybody who reached the street made a decision about it.

Two Konoha shinobi came from the north, one carrying the other. Daichi opened
the line to admit them. The Sound group pushed at the same moment. The split
gate came off its lower hinge.

Sazare put both hands on the paving.

His right hand found the fine new aggregate. His left, armoured and clumsy,
reached the granite edge. He tried to grow from both at once and discovered
that the body did not care how sensible the idea was. Chakra went first to the
hand he had used most often for nine years. The gutter answered; the road did
not. He forced the second pathway open and the first one narrowed.

The result rose unevenly. The eastern blocks were dense and slow. The centre
pair came up too quickly, thin at their bases. One cracked under its own
weight before an attacker touched it.

Sazare corrected while people were running over the work. He thickened one
heel, abandoned another and pushed a seed into the centre block with his thumb
as it grew. A sandal landed two inches from his fingers. Daichi's blade passed
above his head. Hot blood struck the back of his hand and he did not know
whose it was.

He had fabricated under pressure before: for Ruri assessments, for customers
waiting at a counter, for men who would reduce the payment if he appeared to
need the money. None of them had tried to stand on the work before it existed.

Crystal rose in staggered blocks rather than a wall: six short faces, each
behind the next, each forcing a foot to land somewhere particular. The nearest
attacker changed direction twice, found no direct path and went high. Daichi
had been waiting for high. The second came through the left-hand gap and found
Ashida there. The third landed where Sazare had left bare stone.

Sazare touched the fragment beside his knee.

The bare stone was not bare any more.

When the gate was theirs again, five bodies lay between the gutter and the
steps. Two were Oto, one Sand, one Konoha, and one had no forehead protector
at all. Sazare had killed two of them and helped kill another. His left knee
had begun to move sideways every time he turned.

He tightened a stretcher strap around it and went on.

At nine fifty-one, Nurse Emiko appeared behind the broken window.

"We need the entrance clear."

"It isn't," Daichi said.

"I can see that. We have twelve more coming."

"From where?"

"Everywhere."

Daichi looked at the road, the bodies and the fragments scattered among them.

"Then we move the line to the bend."

It was the correct decision. The bend narrowed the approach. The shopfronts
provided cover. Holding there would keep projectiles away from the hospital
windows.

It also meant leaving the gate.

Sazare followed because the hospital needed the road. He followed with
fourteen grains in his pocket, a five-pound plate on one arm and a knee held
together by canvas webbing. By the time they reached the dumpling stall, the
village beyond it had disappeared behind smoke.

The owner had left one shutter down and one up. Bowls remained on the counter.
A pot of broth had boiled low enough that the smell of scorched miso persisted
beneath the smoke. Daichi sent the uninjured genin through the serving gap to
check the rear room. She returned with an old woman, a boy and a kitchen knife
long enough to count as a short sword.

"Cellar?" Daichi asked.

"Rice pit," the old woman said.

"Use it."

"My shop—"

"Will be here or it won't. Get down."

Sazare recognised the old woman from the market row. O-Shige had told him once
that her dumplings contained more cabbage than meat and that this was either
fraud or good business depending on which side of the counter one occupied.
The old woman looked at the crystal on his arm, then at his face, and
recognised him in return.

"Hospital boy," she said.

"Yes."

"Don't put that through my floor."

"The road is better."

"Good."

She pulled the boy through the serving gap and vanished into the rear room.

Daichi overturned the two outside tables. Sazare laid a seeded plate beneath
each, growing just enough crystal to bind the legs and leave a thin face
towards the road. The structures looked like cover. Their better use was as
material in places an attacker would approach without looking down.

"How long?" Daichi asked.

"Six seconds under load. Longer if nobody touches them."

"And after they break?"

"Possibly longer."

"That isn't how numbers work."

"I noticed this nine minutes ago."

Daichi wiped blood out of one eye with the back of his burned forearm. "Then
you've had nine minutes more instruction than any of them."

He pointed downhill.

Movement crossed the smoke.

"Teach."
