# Chapter 72, Scene 6 — The Specification Question

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1210 words.*

---

The tour ended at the kettle, because everything in the yard
ended at the kettle, and Sakura took her leave at five with a
last standing threat regarding yawns that had never been
deployed, and then it was the two of them and the brazier and
the early March dark coming down, and Naruto turned his cup the
way people turned things at that bench, and said, out of
nothing, at the pace of a man arriving at a thing sideways:

"Hey. Can I ask you a relationship question? You're like — you
give good advice. Sakura-chan says your advice is scary. That's
a compliment, in Sakura."

"Ask."

"How'd you know? With your — you've got somebody. Everyone
knows you've got somebody; nobody knows anything else; it's the
village's favourite thing. How'd you know she was the one you—"
he gestured, cup and all, at the entire concept— "you know.
The one you'd do the whole thing for."

*The village's favourite thing.* Filed, flagged for the
Wednesday audit, set aside, because the boy across the brazier
was asking in earnest and earnest got the register.

"I ran the account," said Sazare, "and found it never went to
deficit. Everything else in my life costs. The work costs. The
training costs — I pay it gladly, but I pay. She is the one
entry that only ever compounds. That is not the poetry
anybody wants and it is the true answer: you know, because
you audit, and one account refuses to behave like the
others." He set his cup down. "Now the question you are
actually circling. Ask it."

Naruto laughed — caught, unbothered — and asked it.

"Do you think Sakura-chan's ever gonna — me and her. Honest
read. You read everything, right? Read that."

The brazier ticked. Sazare gave it the four seconds it
deserved.

"Give me the file first," he said. "Not the version for the
village. The whole of it. How it started, what was said, and
what you are holding that you have never put down."

And Naruto — because it was that bench, at that hour, and
because nobody had ever asked him for the file in his life —
gave it. All of it, at Naruto pace, into the dark: the
Academy, the loud lonely kid and the girl with the temper who
was the smartest person he'd ever met; the years of being her
punchline and taking it as attention, because attention was
attention and he'd had a childhood where you took what came;
Sasuke, always Sasuke, the whole geometry of the three of
them, one girl facing one boy facing something in the dark
and himself facing the girl; and the promise — he slowed down
there, and the volume came off it, and Sazare noted the
change the way he noted a density gradient — the promise of a
lifetime, *I'll bring him back, believe it*, made to her tears
at the gate at thirteen, renewed since, carried like a rank.

"So that's the file," Naruto finished. "And I'm not stupid,
before you say it. I know she loves him. Knew it before she
did, probably. But people change, right? And I keep — I'm
gonna bring him back anyway, because I promised, and somewhere
in my head there's this — this thing, where I come through
the gate with Sasuke over my shoulder and she finally—" he
stopped, and turned the cup, and the honesty arrived the way
it always arrived with him, at the exact moment it hurt:
"—she finally looks at me first. There. That's the thing.
You wanted the whole file. That's the last page."

Sazare sat with the last page for a while.

"Findings," he said, at last, in the register the boy had
earned. "You will not like the middle one, and I am going to
give you all three anyway, because this bench does not sell
the soft version.

"One. The promise is the finest thing in the file. Keep it —
but hear its terms as written: you promised to bring him
back. You did not promise that bringing him back would buy
anything. The day you let the promise become an invoice, you
have ruined the finest thing in the file. A gift with a
price hidden in it is not a gift. I have it on the best
authority in the village that the paper only stays clean if
the two halves never touch.

"Two. The specification question, and here is where you
brace. You have described a woman by her direction, and her
direction — by your own file, held since the Academy — is
away. So audit yourself the way you made me audit myself at
the fourth step: *what do you actually want?* Her — Sakura,
the person, the artillery, as she is, pointed where she
points? Or do you want the moment at the gate — the finally-
looking-first, the being chosen at last by the one who
never chose you? Because those are different articles,
Naruto. One is a love. The other is a wound wearing a love's
clothing, and a man can carry the second one for eleven
years believing it is the first, and the tell — the single
diagnostic — is whether the dream is about HER, or about
being SEEN. Yours, as reported, four minutes ago, in your
own words: is about being seen.

"Three." He let the silence carry the second finding to the
bottom, and then finished, more quietly, because the third
one cost him. "I am not the referee of what you feel, and my
sample size is one. But I will state my one credential in
full, so you can weight the advice: I have loved once. It
has nearly killed me twice — I am not being figurative;
there were medical events; one required intervention — and
I would pay it again without auditing the invoice. THAT is
the article, when it is real: it does not feel like waiting
to be chosen. It feels like already having chosen, so
completely that the cost column stops mattering. When you
find the person who makes your ledger do that — whoever it
is, whenever it is — you will not need me to read anything.
The instrument reads itself."

The dark had come all the way down. Naruto sat with his cup,
quiet longer than the file said he could be, and Sazare let
the quiet run, because the kettle held the room and some
articles need to cool in the mould.

"'A wound wearing a love's clothing,'" said Naruto, finally.
"Man. Sakura-chan was right. Your advice IS scary." He
drained the cup, and stood, and rolled his shoulders the way
he did coming off a hard drill, and the grin that came up
was quieter than standard issue, and none the worse for it.
"I'm not gonna pretend I got all that sorted tonight. But
the promise-and-invoice thing — that one goes in the
notebook. And the — the audit question. That one too." He
pointed the empty cup at Sazare, ceremonial. "Nearly killed
you TWICE, though. And you'd pay it again. That's so
stupid. That's the least stupid thing I ever heard. How is
it both?"

"I have been asking that since November," said Sazare,
"and the current finding is: both is the whole point.
Goodnight, Naruto. Midsummer stands. Bring the notebook."
