# Chapter 52, Scene 3 — The Brackets

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,618 words.*

---

*I don't know* was not an answer and he had not been able to leave it
standing past about the second stair on the way home.

So he did what he did, which was to go and get the data.

He got the box down on the twenty-fifth. Fourteen books, three years
and a bit, the whole of it since the gate at sixteen — the yard log,
the bench log, the ledger proper and the four smaller ones he had kept
for things that did not fit anywhere — and he laid them out on the low
table and the floor and the sill, and started at the beginning.

*Question: what is the evidence, in my own records, of women behaving
towards me in a manner I have failed to interpret?*

*Method: read everything. Three years. I do not know what I am looking
for, which means I cannot search for it, which means I have to read.*

*Time estimate: two evenings.*

It took him five.

---

He tried counting first and it did not work.

*Raw mentions are useless. Kanzaki is in about nine hundred entries
because she is the quartermaster and I file to her. Yagi is in six
hundred because she is my superior. Emiko is in four hundred and every
single one is a bed state or a handover, and she has asked me whether I
have eaten every working day for three years and it is not in this book
once, because it is not work.*

*Counting names measures how much business I have with somebody. It
does not measure anything else and I knew that before I started and
did it anyway because counting is what I reach for.*

He sat back on the second evening and looked at fourteen books.

*So: what in this record is not business?*

And then he found it, and it was his own notation, and it had been
there the entire time.

---

*The bracket.*

*I keep my reading separate from the thing observed. It is the first
discipline I ever gave myself and I gave it to myself at about nine
years old and I have never once broken it: the fact goes in plain and
my interpretation goes in a bracket underneath, so that a reader can
have the evidence without having me.*

*I use it for materials constantly.*

*I use it for people almost never.*

He went back through and counted brackets.

It took four hours and he did it properly, and the result was on one
sheet, and he sat and looked at it for some considerable time.

*Brackets on a person, three years, fourteen books:*

*Urushibara Gen — nineteen. (Slow this evening. Sat down twice. Second
time mid-sentence and finished the sentence sitting, which he has not
done before. Room cold — fire not lit, wood in.)*

*The Hokage — one hundred and forty-one.*

*Kurihara Saeko — thirty-eight.*

*Everybody else, combined — four.*

He read the last line twice.

*Four.*

*Kanzaki, three, all of them about her hands and all of them in the
first year.*

*Yagi, one, in the eleventh month of my sixteenth year, about the ear.*

*Nobody else. Not Ashida, who I have known three years and whose flak
jacket has been buckled wrong across one loop since the spring, which
is in this book as a fact and has never once had a bracket under it.
Not Handa. Not Emiko, who asks me every day whether I have eaten.
Not Sakura, and I have fifty-one conversations with Sakura in here and
forty-four scores and not one bracket.*

*Three people in three years.*

*One of them is dead. One of them is the Hokage of this village. And
one of them is a sealwright who cleared her diary for two months in the
fifth month so that she would not have to be in a room with me except
by contract.*

---

He went and got the fifth month.

He knew what was in it. He had written it and he had read it since, and
he could have recited most of it, and he went and got it anyway and
read the whole of the fifth month in order like a document produced by
somebody else, which was the only way he had ever been able to see
anything.

*2/5. Nine visits in twenty days, none booked, none paid, none
requested by either party, no work order, no equipment need, no route
of convenience.*

*The certification closed on the fourteenth.*

*I have three hundred and eleven entries on what a medium does and I
have one entry type in this book with no unit at the head of it, and
this is the eighth item to go under it, and it is the first one that is
about a living person doing something on purpose.*

He put his hand flat on the page.

*I found it. That is the part I want recorded properly, because there
is a version of the last five months in which I was oblivious and that
version is a kindness and it is not true.*

*I did the arithmetic. I eliminated outstanding work, equipment need,
Kanzaki's booking, and route of convenience, in order, on paper, and I
got to the entries that had no procedure and I stopped, and I wrote
"the first one that is about a living person doing something on
purpose" and I went to bed.*

*I had all of it on the second of the fifth month.*

*I had it and I could not read it, in the same way I filled in a
source-medium column three hundred and eleven times without once
reading the column, and in the same way I copied out a paragraph on
natural energy at seventeen for an examination and forgot it for a
year.*

*Fourth time. And this one is a person.*

---

He read the rest of the fifth month and it got worse, in the ordinary
way that documents get worse when you finally know what they are
about.

The nineteenth. Four questions about retaining figures, three
corrections accepted, and a bracket — *(she has changed the order in
which she lays the ground; asked; the answer is that it is faster and
not better and she is aware of that and does it anyway on work of this
class. Noted for my own practice.)*

The twenty-second, and the comparison table, and the sheet he had
turned over and written on the back of rather than tear out.

The twenty-ninth. *She brought her own lens.*

And the four lines at the end of it, which he had written at about one
in the morning and had thought at the time were the honest half:

*I could have said eleven seconds of true thing at any point in four
hours and I said none of it, and she has arranged the next two months
so that she does not have to be in a room with me except by contract.*

*She did that in seven days. It is extremely good work and I would
admire it enormously if it had been done to somebody else.*

He sat on the floor with his back against the wall.

*That is not the honest half. That was as far as I could get in the
fifth month and I was pleased with it and it stops one line short.*

*The line is: she did it because I told her nothing was wrong, twice,
having been asked directly, at cost, after she crossed nine feet of a
yard in the heat to ask.*

*And the eleven seconds are still not said. It is the twenty-fifth of
the tenth month. It has been five months and three days.*

---

He wrote it up at about two.

*Findings, five evenings, fourteen books, three years.*

*1. I keep brackets on three people. One is dead. The other two are
the Hokage and Kurihara Saeko, at a hundred and forty-one and
thirty-eight, against four for everybody else I have ever met.*

*2. I do not need to interpret this. My own notation has done it. I
built the bracket at nine years old to keep my reading separate from
the evidence, and it turns out to be an instrument for detecting which
people I have been reading, and I have been feeding it for three years
and never once looked at what it collected.*

*3. On the second of the fifth month I established, on paper, by
elimination, that nine visits had no procedural cause. I had the whole
of it. I could not read it.*

*4. The evidence was never missing. It has been in my handwriting, in a
box under my bed, in order, dated, with the conditions recorded and my
readings kept separate underneath in the ordinary way.*

He stopped there.

Then he wrote the thing the slug had said, which had been sitting in
him for a day and a half and which he had not been able to put anywhere
until the fourteen books were on the floor.

*Ame: the grandchildren are better at it because the grandmother was in
the room.*

*Nobody has been in the room.*

*Not for this. There has never been anybody standing behind me on any
of it — not the crystal, which I have now had corrected by a creature
who could see, and not this, which nobody has ever been able to see and
which I have therefore been doing entirely by myself since I was
thirteen with no idea that there was anything to be bad at.*

*And I have three people in brackets in three years and one of them is
in the ground.*
