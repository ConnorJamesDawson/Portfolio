# Chapter 73, Scene 3 — Terms on the Ground

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the garden of Tsunade's house. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1090 words.*

---

The garden was bigger than the house deserved and had been let
go for years, which he read off it in the first eleven seconds
the way he read everything: a wall on three sides, old and
sound, one course of coping missing at the north-west corner
where a branch had come down in some winter nobody had been
here for. A stone lantern with moss up its north face. Four
mature trees — plum, two maples, and something he could not
name, which was rare enough to note. Grass to the shin. A
gravel path with the gravel gone into the grass at the edges.

*Twenty-two yards by fourteen. Wall height nine feet, which
gives privacy from everything except the tower's own upper
floors, and the tower's upper floors are hers.*

*Sightlines: none. This garden is the most private ground
inside these walls that is not underground, and she has owned
it for eleven years and never once used it.*

*Ground: soft, deep, unworked. Roots at three of the four
trees at the surface. Gravel on the path only. It has rained
for two days.*

*And she chose it, and she knows what I do to prepared
ground, and she has given me a walled box with four trees and
a soft floor.*

*She is not handicapping herself. She is handing me the
conditions and then beating me in them, because that is the
only assessment she considers honest.*

She came out of the house in her working clothes — no robes,
no jacket, hair up in the two lacquered sticks — and stood in
the middle of her own overgrown garden in the grey afternoon
light of the fourteenth of the third month, and the sight of
her there did something to his chest that he elected not to
audit until later.

"Terms," she said.

"Terms."

"Touches. First to three." She counted them off on her
fingers, brisk, the tone she used for briefing surgical
teams, which he understood by now was armour. "A touch is
open hand or flat of a blade, anywhere, controlled. No
strikes with intent. Nothing to the head. You can use
everything you've got — everything, fabricator, I'll be
extremely insulted if you hold back one article — and I am
going to fight you at a genuinely unpleasant fraction of
what I am, because an assessment at a fraction you can
survive is the only assessment that tells us anything."

"Define the fraction."

"No," said Tsunade. "That's part of it. Jiraiya won't tell
you either. The world doesn't publish."

"Agreed. My terms." He set the case down on the step, and
opened it, and took out the sword, and the garden's whole
quality of light changed by the small amount it always
changed by. "The blade stays sheathed unless a range reset
is the correct answer, and if it comes out it is the flat,
and the governor is live the entire time — eleven clauses,
including clause eleven. I want that stated aloud in front
of the only witness present, which is you, because it has
never once been tested against a person who might actually
manage it."

Something moved behind her eyes.

"Including if somebody takes it off your body," she quoted.

"Including that."

"Nobody has ever taken that thing off you."

"Nobody has ever tried," said Sazare. "You are the only
person alive who could. And if it is going to fail, I would
rather it failed today, in a garden, against you, than in
eleven months on a road against somebody who is not you."

She looked at him for a long moment, and then nodded once,
and something in her had gone from *briefing* to *working*,
which he had learned to recognise across three years as the
sound of Tsunade taking a thing seriously.

"Two more," she said. "One: this is not official and it is
not for any file, and I mean that — nothing in your book
tomorrow morning except the findings you'd write for
Jiraiya. The rest of today doesn't go in a ledger." She let
that sit. "Two: the prizes."

She took the sealed paper out of her sleeve and set it on
the step. He took his out of his coat and set it beside
hers, and they lay there on the stone, two small folded
documents, the least official and most binding pieces of
paper either of them had ever produced.

"Victor opens both," said Tsunade.

"Both," he agreed. "Which is either extremely fair or the
single most frightening clause anybody has ever put in
front of me."

"It's both. It's supposed to be both." She rolled her
shoulders, and the last of the office went out of her, and
what was standing in the garden then was not the Hokage of
the Land of Fire; it was the woman who had crawled the
length of a bathtub for the pleasure of watching an
engineer fail. "One more thing, fabricator, and then we
start. It's the only piece of coaching you get and I'm
giving it to you because I am not interested in a polite
result."

"Say it."

"You have never once tried to hit me," said Tsunade.
"Not on a roof, not in the yard, not in three years. And
Jiraiya's had you eight weeks and the thing he's written
in that report of his is that you cannot find the decision
cold. So." She raised her chin. "You want the prize on
that step? Find it. In my garden. On me. Where the answer
is not just allowed to be yes — where it's the only way
you get to open that piece of paper."

*Oh,* thought Sazare, with the whole of the last eight
weeks rearranging itself around a single sentence.

*She has read Jiraiya-sama's report. Of course she has read
it; she signs the order. She knows exactly what is missing
in me and she has built the assessment around it, and the
prize is on the step so that the finding has a reason
strong enough to move a man who does not move.*

*She is not testing whether I can fight her.*

*She is testing whether I can want something enough to
take it.*

"Understood," he said.

"Then we'll see. Ready?"

He stepped out onto the soft ground of the most private
garden in Konoha, with a sword he had grown himself and a
governor with eleven clauses and eight weeks of a man's
hands on his footwork, and looked at the strongest woman
alive across fourteen yards of wet grass.

"Ready," said Sazare Ruri.
