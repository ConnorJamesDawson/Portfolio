# Chapter 53, Scene 1 — Twenty-One Inches

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2,244 words.*

---

It finished on the fourth of the eleventh month at about one in the
morning and he did not notice for some minutes.

That was the thing he wrote down afterwards, because he had expected to
notice. He had costed four to six months in the fifth month and it had
come in at five and one week, and he had two hundred and fifty-one
hours in a column and a core at twenty and three-quarter inches on the
Tuesday, and he had gone at the last quarter-inch on the Wednesday
evening the way he had gone at every quarter-inch since the sixth of the
sixth month.

And then there was nothing left to widen.

He put the graver down and sat and looked at it and there was no
sensation attached to any part of it at all.

*Twenty-one inches. 251 hours over 22 weeks, five nights a week where
the evenings held.*

*It is finished. I have gone over it four times and there is not one
run in it that is not at the width I said, and the fourth branch has
come down two full tones since the seventh month, and the two middle
voices converged in the ninth and have stayed converged.*

*Five voices. The fifth arrived somewhere in the tenth month and I do
not know which run it belongs to and cannot find it under a lens, and I
have written that down four times and got no further with it.*

*I do not feel anything about this and I have been sitting here for
eleven minutes waiting to.*

---

He drew from it at about two.

He had drawn from the core perhaps forty times in five months — short,
diagnostic, four seconds and off — and this was the first time on a
finished article, and he did it standing, with the sacking down and the
gate shut and nobody in the yard.

It came out of the seat like water up a wick.

One foot six of edge in about a second and a half, laid at corridor
speed, rough, because the blade was cheap and always would be and that
was the entire point of the arrangement. Pale. Translucent all the way
through. The lamp on the far side of it went into the body of the thing
and came out somewhere it had no business coming out of, and the core
in his hand lit white-blue along the axis with five branches showing
inside it like the veins in a leaf held to a window.

The note came up and did not stop.

He held it for a count of about forty and let it go, and the blade
stopped being there, and there was a rod on a felt.

*Held at full course, no fault anywhere, terminated by me and not by
anything else. I did not push it. There was no reason to push it and I
have four hundred hours of not knowing what the ceiling is and I have
decided I am not going to find out alone.*

He wrapped it and put it on the bench.

Then he got the guard out of the drawer where it had been since the
third night of the sixth month, and looked at it under the lamp, and
turned it over.

The panel on the inside face was two and a half inches long, flat and
unornamented, on the side that would sit against the hand.

*Still nothing.*

*I have had five months and I have thought about it perhaps a hundred
times and I have got no further than I got on the third night, which
was that I have never in my life put a mark on a maker's face and that
I have apparently prepared one anyway.*

*It is not going on tonight.*

---

Katsuyu came back on the sixth.

She had been at Shikkotsu four days and she arrived on the corner of
bench three at about eight in the evening as though she had never been
anywhere, and the first thing she said was:

"They have it."

"How was it received?"

"I am not going to be able to answer that in a way that will satisfy
you," said Katsuyu, "and I have been thinking about it on the way and I
have decided to say so first rather than after four attempts." A slow
settling. "It is in the dry place. Ame carried it the last part herself,
which she did not have to do and which I did not ask her to. A great
many of them have come to look at it and a great many more will, and I
have said the six chapters aloud eleven times in four days and I shall
be saying them for some considerable time."

"And the book itself—"

"Is untouched, and will be untouched in four hundred years, and I have
stopped finding that odd since about the second day and I think you
will find it odd for the rest of your life."

"Probably."

"They would like to see your work."

---

He stopped with the file in his hand.

"Say the whole of that."

"There is not a great deal of it. They have heard what I have been
doing here — I have not concealed it and there was no reason to — and
some of them have questions, and Ame has told a number of them about
the yard, and the yard has become a thing that is spoken about." Her
mantle shifted. "And the substance of it is that if you came, you would
be welcome, and a considerable number of them would come and look at
what you make, and one or two of them would have things to say about it
that I cannot say."

"That's an invitation."

"I do not know," said Katsuyu, honestly. "I do not know whether we have
those. Nobody said the words *he should come*. It is more that the
possibility was in about nine conversations and nobody thought it was
strange."

Sazare put the file down.

He sat on the yard stone in the cold with a finished article on the
bench nine inches from his elbow and thought about it for slightly over
a minute, which was long for him.

"No," he said.

---

"May I ask why?"

"Yes. And you should, because the reason isn't the obvious one and if I
don't say it you'll assume the obvious one and it's not true." He
looked up. "It isn't caution. It isn't that I'm frightened of the
place, or that I think it's dangerous, or that I've got too much on.
I'd go tomorrow morning and I'd walk it if I had to."

"Then—"

"It's her family's forest."

Katsuyu did not say anything.

"Four hundred miles away there is a place that belongs to a line that
goes down through her grandfather and her, and the door to it is a
creature that is on her contract, and I have been in that yard for
three months getting taught something by that creature without one word
of it ever going up that hill.

"I assumed a channel existed. I have never audited a channel in my life
and I audit everything, and I did not audit that one, because it looked
formal and I took formality for a mechanism — and on the fourteenth of
last month I found out in this yard, in front of her, that there has
never been a channel at all, and it went into her like something with
an edge on it and I watched it happen."

He turned his hand over on his knee.

"So I'm not walking into that forest and finding out afterwards what it
cost. Not twice. Not the same fault in the same year on the same
person."

"You will ask her first."

"I'll ask her first."

There was a pause.

"You have not spoken to her in seventeen days," said Katsuyu.

"No."

"And you are not going to be able to ask her until you do."

"No," said Sazare. "That's the situation."

---

He worked at the request for two days and it took him about four hours,
which was longer than it should have taken.

*I am not sending an article to be admired. I want that clear on the
face of it before anybody reads a word, because if I send this without
saying so it will be received as a display and it will be my fault.*

*I am sending it for review.*

*I have run one study in my life and it was a bad document until six
practitioners took it off me and rewrote it in a treatment room, and
the whole of what I learned that evening was that a man who arrives
with a finished object and asks people to admire it has already lost.*

*So: numbered, and honest about the gaps.*

He wrote nine questions and struck out three and kept six.

*1. It is an instrument. I built it to measure conduction and it has
turned out to be a weapon, and I did not intend that and I am not able
to stop it being one. I would like to know what you think it is.*

*2. Under load it does not fail. I have taken it to a count of forty
and stopped because I did not want to find the ceiling alone. Should I
find the ceiling? Is a ceiling a thing this class of article has?*

*3. I know nothing whatsoever about the offensive use of natural
energy. I am not asking to be taught it. I am asking what somebody who
does know would see in this that I cannot.*

*4. The fifth voice arrived in the tenth month. I do not know which run
it belongs to and I cannot find it under a lens. If any of you can hear
what I cannot, I would rather be told.*

*5. The blade is cheap on purpose and the seat is not. Is that the
right way round?*

*6. What am I not asking?*

He read it back and added one line at the bottom, in a different hand,
because it did not belong with the others.

*I would rather have it back with faults in the margin than back with
nothing written on it at all.*

---

Katsuyu took it on the eighth.

She took the whole article — the core wrapped in felt and the guard
separately in a cloth, because the guard was ordinary stock and not
part of what he was asking about, and he sent it anyway.

"They will want to know how long," she said.

"They can have it as long as they want it."

"That is not an answer that will help them."

"No." He thought about it. "Tell them there's no date on it. Tell them
I would rather wait and have the sixth question answered than get it
back in a fortnight with the first five done."

"That will be understood."

She was most of the way to going when he said the other thing, which he
had not planned and which came out flat.

"Katsuyu — I've just given away the only thing I had."

"I do not follow."

"It's the best article I've ever made and it's a new class and there is
nobody in this village who's seen it." He looked at the empty bench.
"It's also the single best reason to go up that hill that I am ever
going to have, and any sane man would have taken it under his arm, and
I've just put it in a cloth and handed it to somebody going four
hundred miles in the wrong direction."

"Would you like it back?"

"No," said Sazare. "That's the point. If I take it up there I'll be
taking an object, and it will be on a table between us, and I will
spend an hour being extremely interesting about conduction."

---

He went down to the gate on the ninth with the month's returns because
Ashida had asked him to bring them personally, which meant Ashida
wanted twenty minutes.

The brazier was lit for the first time since the spring and the shelter
smelled of hot creosote and tea nobody wanted.

"You'll have heard," said Ashida, sorting.

"Heard what?"

"The Toad Sage. Coming back in, some time before the year turns —
Nemura had it off the west road detail and Nemura's an idiot, but Ōta
had it as well and Ōta's had one thing wrong in eleven years." He
turned a sheet round without looking at it. "And there's a boy with
him, apparently. Been out two and a half years."

"Right."

"Big to-do, if it's true. Half the second watch has a shift they want
moving."

Sazare signed the sheet and did not look up, and thought about it for
perhaps four seconds, and put it away.

He had two hundred and fifty-one hours in a column, an empty bench, a
count of fifteen at the bottom of a page, and a fault report with
nothing in the second column, and there was one person in the village
he could take any of it to and he had not been up that hill in
twenty-one days.

*Tomorrow,* he thought.

And then, because he wrote everything down:

*I have said tomorrow four times this week.*
