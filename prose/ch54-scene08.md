# Chapter 54, Scene 8 — Over the Roofs

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's house. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1680 words.*

---

He picked her up the way he had picked up about two hundred people,
which was under the shoulders and under the knees with the head brought
in against him first, and she weighed exactly what he had estimated,
which was the only part of the whole night that went to plan.

She did not wake.

He got the lamp out with an elbow and got the door with a foot and the
stair was empty all the way down, and the door at the bottom of the
stair was on the latch and not locked, which it never was and which he
had never once thought about before.

He did not go out of the front.

He went up.

---

The village at half past midnight in the second week of the twelfth
month was cold and dry and almost entirely dark, and he went over it on
the roofs, north-east, the long way round the back of the records
floor and the merchant quarter, because the short way went across four
streets that had lamps on them and one that had a noodle stand that
stayed open.

It took eleven minutes. The direct route on the ground was six.

He was careful in a way he had never been careful about anything that
was not on a bench. He did not run the ridge lines, because a man on a
ridge line is a silhouette; he ran the flats and the shadowed sides,
and he took the drops slowly with his knees, because a drop taken fast
puts a load through the arms and the arms had a person in them.

*Chin. Chin is down and the airway is fine and I've checked it three
times, and checking it a fourth time is not about her.*

*Twelve. Even. No catch.*

Her head was against his shoulder. Somewhere around the seventh minute
her right hand came up off her lap, without her waking, and took hold
of the front of his shirt.

He noticed it and did not do anything about it. There was nothing to do
about it.

---

He knew he was being watched from about the fourth minute.

He could not have said how. There was nothing to hear and nothing to
see and he had no aptitude at all for that class of thing and never
would; what he had was that the shape of the quiet on his left was
wrong, in the way a room is wrong when a door that was open is shut,
and it stayed wrong at about the same distance for four roofs and then
it was not there any more.

He did not look.

That was deliberate and it took him about a second and a half to
decide.

*If I look, then I have seen them, and they have to know I have seen
them, and then it is an incident.*

*If I don't look, it's a man carrying a drunk Hokage home over the
roofs so she isn't seen being carried home, and that is a completely
ordinary thing that happens in this village and has happened before and
will happen again, and it goes in a log as four words if it goes
anywhere at all.*

*And that version is true.*

*Every part of that version is true. I am not concealing anything. I
am walking home in a way that produces the least damage, which is what
I would do if the last two hours had never happened, and there is
nothing in what they are looking at that is false.*

*They can watch as long as they like.*

He went on north-east and did not look, and the wrongness on his left
went away, and he had no idea for the rest of his life whether it had
ever been there.

---

Her house was small.

He had not been inside it. He had been past it perhaps forty times and
had noted the roof pitch and the state of the gutter and nothing else,
because he did not look at the houses people lived in, and he stood on
the roof of it now with the Hokage in his arms and understood that he
was about to go in through a window and that there was no version of
this that did not look precisely like a crime.

The upstairs window on the north side was unlatched.

He got it open with two fingers and got them both through it without
touching the frame, and he stood in a dark room in a stranger's house
and let his eyes come round.

Bed. Chair. Chest of drawers with nothing on it. A screen with a coat
over it, and one shelf, and on the shelf four things and none of them
photographs.

No mess. He had expected mess.

There was a glass of water on the floor by the bed with about an inch
in it, which had been there long enough to have dust on the surface,
and he looked at that for slightly longer than he meant to and then
stopped looking at it.

---

He went to put her down.

He did it properly, the way he had done it two hundred times, which was
knees first and then the shoulders and then the head last with a hand
behind it, and he got the knees down and got her weight onto the bed
and started to take his arm out from under her shoulders.

Her hands were in his shirt.

Both of them.

He had not registered the second one arriving. The right had come up on
the roofs at about the seventh minute and he had noted it and let it
go, and the left was there too now, closed, at about the height of his
sternum, and the two of them together had a considerable amount of the
front of his shirt in them.

He tried to open the right one.

He got two fingers on it, gently, the way you take a hand off a rail,
and it did not come off. It was not resistance; there was no strength
being applied against him. It was that the hand was closed and had been
closed for some time and was not going to be anything else, and when he
put pressure on the outside of it the whole arm came with it and her
head came off the pillow, and he stopped immediately.

He knelt there on the floor beside the bed with his arm under her and
his shirt in her fists and worked the problem, which took about four
seconds and had two entries and one of them was not really available.

*One: wake her.*

*Two: don't.*

---

He got up onto the bed.

It was the single most improper thing he had ever done and he did it
because the alternative was to stand a woman up out of her own sleep at
one in the morning after the evening she had just had in order to
retrieve a shirt.

He did it as carefully as he could, on top of the covers, still
dressed, boots off at the edge of the frame and put together on the
floor where he would find them. He got his arm out from under her
shoulders and got it round instead, and got the weight distributed so
that nothing of him was on any part of her, and she came with him
without waking and ended up against his chest with her head under his
chin and both hands still shut in the front of his shirt.

He lay on his back in the Hokage's bed in the Hokage's house and looked
at the ceiling.

He was not tired. That was the strangest part. He had been up since the
fourth hour and had been in that office for three hours and had done
the most difficult thing he had ever done twice in one evening, and he
was not remotely tired, and he lay there for a long time being not
tired.

*I should have a plan for the morning.*

*I have got no plan for the morning and I am not going to be able to
build one, because every version of it starts in this room and there is
no honest account of this room.*

*Shizune comes up at the fifth hour.*

*Shizune comes up to the office at the fifth hour.*

*She will not find us. She will find an empty office and a bottle and
one cup, and she will work the rest of it out inside about four
seconds, because she has been working things out about the two of us
for a year and has never once said so.*

He turned his head very slightly.

Her face was about six inches away and had nothing on it at all. Not
peace; he did not think it was peace and he had seen enough sleeping
people to know that peace is mostly a thing observers put there. It was
just off. All of it, the whole apparatus, forty years of being
extremely good at this, switched off and doing nothing.

And her hands were shut in his shirt.

That was the thing he had.

That was the only thing out of the entire evening that he came away
with — not what she had said and not what he had said, not the six or
the eight, not the hand that went up twice and came down twice. Two
fists in the front of a shirt at one in the morning, closed, and not
loosening.

*I don't know what that is.*

*I have got no reading for it at all.*

*I'm not going to have one by the morning either.*

He looked at the ceiling of a room he had no business being in, with
the whole of the next day arriving somewhere off to the east, and got
about as far as the shape of it before he went under.

*And tomorrow is going to be just as bad.*
