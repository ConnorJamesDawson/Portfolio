# Chapter 70, Scene 9 — The Collision

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the second floor. Tsunade, age
fifty-three. POV: Tsunade. ~1260 words.*

---

She rehearsed it on the Monday evening, which was itself a
humiliation of a specific and novel grade, because she had not
rehearsed saying anything to anybody since the year she took the
hat, and she had never — not once, not at fourteen — rehearsed
saying *this*.

She did it pacing the second floor with the border digest held
open as an alibi against nobody, running drafts the way she now
knew he ran drafts, because his methods were contagious and she
had caught all of them.

*Draft one: "Your schedule is unsustainable." Rejected. Hides
the want inside a clinical finding. He'd take it as a directive
and re-engineer his sleep, the idiot, and I'd have used the
medic to do the woman's job, which is the same trap as using
the office, wearing scrubs.*

*Draft two: "I've been thinking we should review the evenings."
Rejected with prejudice. REVIEW. Thirty-nine years out of
practice and the first thing I reach for is committee language.
Shizune would revoke my licence.*

*Draft three is the true one and I know it's the true one
because I can't get it out of my own throat in an EMPTY ROOM.
Three words, four if I'm honest, five with his name on it. I
have told a war council to hold a wall with less trouble than
this. He asked for a bath with a nosebleed pending. He asked
for squid with the whole column empty. The going rate for
asking, in this house, was set by a boy who paid it shaking.
Pay it, you coward. Tonight. When he comes up — before the
brush, because after the brush he has four sentences in him
and I am not spending my confession into a fog—*

The stair door went.

He came in early — an hour early, the light still on in the
yard behind him by her window's account, which meant he had
walked out on his own bench mid-task, which had never once
happened — and he was carrying a folder, and he had the face
on, the working face, the one he wore into boards and
incident reviews, and her first thought, absurd, clinical,
was *he's found something wrong in the line* and her second
thought was nothing at all, because he had stopped in the
middle of the room, squared the folder like a man presenting
findings to a hostile committee, and said:

"I have run an audit and I am here to confess a deficit."

And the Hokage of the Land of Fire, three words loaded and
four days of rehearsal behind her, shut her mouth and let
him go first, because some collisions you see coming and
step into anyway.

He gave her all of it, in order, in the incident format,
folder open, exhibits tabbed: the flat count — *forty-one,
last entry the second, eleven days, the best data series I
own* — the coda log, the sixteen-day arrears, the noons
colonized at four in five, the second-floor lamp in eleven
days of peripheral logs *never once read by the man logging
it*. His voice held the register the whole way, level, at
grading pitch, and the register holding was somehow the
most terrible and most him part of all of it, right up
until the finding, where it didn't.

"The finding is that I have been embezzled by my own
projects, and the deficit landed on you, and you covered
it. Eleven days. In silence. Out of the account you have
been keeping since November that I only found on Friday —
and I know why you covered it in silence, I have read your
three clauses off your conduct, they are correct, every
one, you built a trap out of your own integrity and stood
in it so that my hours could stay clean." The register
came back, barely, hands flat on the folder. "So. Exhibit
one: the noons, re-armoured — the through-and-out law in
writing, no items at the tray, the board rerouted. Exhibit
two, countersigned by the programme architect this
morning: Thursdays. All of them. No field, no yard, no
board, no entries — a day with nothing in it, weekly,
structural. Shizune wrote one into a diary once and called
it three years' waiting. I am asking for it to be
standing." He turned the folder around and slid it across.
"Exhibit three we do at the bench together or not at all,
so it stays sealed tonight. And the finding of findings,
stated plainly, because a man who confesses a deficit in
exhibits is hiding in his own format and you deserve the
unformatted version—"

"Stop," said Tsunade.

He stopped.

"You magnificent, ledgered, impossible man," she said, and
heard her own voice fray on the middle word and let it.
"You've stolen my confession. I had a *draft*. I have been
rehearsing since Monday like a genin outside an academy
dance, I sent back a TRAY over tea leaves, I have a
collection of fourteen uncertified statements under lock in
a register you will never find, and tonight was the night,
and you walked in here with a FOLDER and did it first, in
*exhibits*—" and the laugh arrived in the middle of it, the
real one, the forty-second entry, eleven days late and
worth the whole flat stretch, and she crossed the distance
between them the way she had crossed the cedar tub, on
principle, and took his face in both hands. "Say the
unformatted version. Then I'll say mine. Shortest first —
that's the shop rule, isn't it? Smallest pins first."

"I miss you," said Sazare. "I have been in the building the
entire time, and I miss you, and every hour of the missing
was spent on things I would choose again, and I am not
willing to run the two accounts against each other for one
more day, and Thursday is my opening position."

"I miss you too," said the Hokage of the Land of Fire, four
words, five with the name, paid in full and shaking
slightly, exactly at the going rate. "Sazare. I've been
pining like a *teenager*. It's been three weeks. It's
mortifying. I diagnosed myself in writing."

"I know," he said. "I costed the lamp oil."

"You did NOT—"

"Exhibit four," said Sazare, and did not reach for the
folder, and the corner of his mouth did the thing it had
learned in January, and she understood, with the outrage
arriving hand in hand with something enormously warmer,
that she was being *teased*, in her own tower, at the exact
apex of the most emotionally exposed minute of her decade,
by a man she had personally taught to do it.

"Fifty passes," she said, when she could speak. "And then,
fabricator, you are going to sit up in that bed and stay
awake for one entire conversation if I have to prop you
against the headboard like a splint. I have fourteen
statements requiring certification, and we are doing them
*in order*."

They got through six.

He fell asleep between the sixth and the seventh, mid-
certification, hand in her hair, and she lay in the dark
doing the arithmetic of the day and found, for the first
time in three weeks, no deficit anywhere on the page —
and the last thing she filed, in the register nobody would
ever read, before sleep took her too:

*Thursday. He gave me all the Thursdays.*

*The collection stands at fourteen. Six certified. He
certifies them better awake.*

*Chart closed. Properly this time. Patient discharged into
the care of a man with a folder.*
