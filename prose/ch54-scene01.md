# Chapter 54, Scene 1 — The Wrong Question

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's office. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1390 words.*

---

Two words, and he took them apart wrong, and the whole of the rest of
the night came out of that.

*Why me.*

He had it inside a second, because it was a selection question and he
had been answering selection questions since he was fourteen. Why this
aggregate and not the other one. Why the yard stock at nine per cent
and not the granite at eleven. Why me is a comparison against a field,
and a comparison against a field has an answer, and the answer is the
property that the field does not have.

He did not notice that she had not asked that.

"Because you put the object on the floor," he said.

Tsunade was standing with one hand on the back of her own chair. She
had not sat down since he stopped talking, and she had been at the
window before that, and she was somewhere between the two of them now
with the whole desk in the way.

"I told you," he said. "It's the third one. It's the only thing that's
ever—"

"Don't."

He stopped.

"Don't recite it at me again."

"All right."

"You've said it once. It was —" She stopped, and something went across
her face and did not get out. "It was a good four minutes. Don't do it
twice."

"All right."

"Sit down."

"I'd rather stand."

"*Sit down*, Ruri."

He sat down.

She did not.

---

He watched her go round the desk the long way, which was six steps
where three would have done it, and he noted that and put it nowhere,
because he had spent three years learning that noting a thing about her
hands and then saying it out loud was the fastest available way to end
a conversation.

She opened the low cupboard on the left of the desk, the one with the
tea things on top of it and nothing that had ever been tea inside it,
and took out a bottle and a cup.

One cup.

"Right," she said. "Objections."

"Sorry?"

"You've made your statement. Now I'm going to tell you why it's the
stupidest thing anyone's said in this office in five years, and you're
going to sit there and hear all of it, and then you're going to go
home."

"Can I answer them?"

She poured. It was not a measure; it was a pour.

"You can answer them," she said. "It won't help."

---

"One. I'm the Hokage and you're a serving member of the Medical Corps
who I promoted eleven days ago."

"Yes."

"That's the whole objection. I'm not going to dress it up."

"I know what that objection is," he said. "It's not about you and me,
it's about the next one. If a Hokage can do this once then the post
carries it permanently, and everyone in every future room has to do the
arithmetic on whether they're being listened to or being courted, and
the arithmetic doesn't go away when the two people involved are honest,
because nobody in the room can see honest."

She had the cup halfway up. She put it down again.

"Go on."

"So it isn't a rule about conduct, it's a rule about what a signature
means, and the reason it's a good rule is that it protects a thing
neither of us owns."

"And?"

"And it's real, and I agree with it, and it doesn't apply."

"It bloody well does apply."

"It applies to a Hokage and a subordinate whose work she signs off,"
said Sazare. "She doesn't. She hasn't in fourteen months. Every
fabrication docket out of my desk goes through Yagi to the Corps board
and the Corps board is chaired by a man who voted against my post. Two
of the fifteen would take a complaint about me at eight in the morning
and be glad of it. There isn't a line on any paper in this village
where you're upstream of me."

"I *made* the post."

"You made the post and the council carried it eight to seven, and I
have read the minute. You spoke fourth. You didn't speak last, which
you would have done if you were driving it, and you let Ariake Fumi
close because she's the one nobody can accuse of liking me."

Tsunade looked at him for a long moment.

"You've been through the minutes."

"I go through all of the minutes. I've gone through all of the minutes
since the eighth month of last year, it isn't about this."

"No," she said. "No, it isn't, is it."

---

"Two," she said, and drank the first one down whole.

He watched her do it and did not say anything about it either.

"It lands on me. Not on you. That's not me being noble, it's just how
it works — you're eighteen, you're foreign, you're the crystal boy, and
there's a version of this where every single person in this village
decides you were used, and *that* version, Ruri, that version has you
walking out of a hospital with people who've worked with you for four
years unable to look at you."

"Yes."

"You say *yes* like it's a line item."

"It is a line item. It's the third worst outcome on the sheet and it's
about forty per cent likely and I'd survive it. The other two aren't
about me."

"Say them."

"Two of the fifteen use it to reopen the post and the fabrication
programme stops for eleven months while it's reviewed, and about
sixteen hundred articles don't get made, and Yagi's people go back to
buying steel that fails in the third month. That's one."

"And?"

"And it lands on *you*," he said. "Which is the same thing you just
said, and you said it as an objection on my behalf, and it isn't. It's
the objection. It's the only one of the first two that's got any weight
in it."

She poured again.

"Then you understand it."

"I understand it and I've costed it and the cost is on you and I can't
pay any of it, and that's the part I've had for eleven weeks and can't
put anywhere." He put his hands on his knees. "But the thing I'd say
about it is that it's a *concealment* problem, not a conduct problem.
It's expensive because it has to be hidden. It has to be hidden because
of one, and one goes away in—"

"Don't."

"— in four years, when you stand down, and—"

"*Don't* you dare do a timeline."

---

He shut his mouth.

That had come out of her at a different volume from the rest of it, and
it had come out fast, and for about a second and a half she was not
standing the way she had been standing.

Then she was again.

"You've answered two things," she said. "You've answered them well.
You've answered them the way you answer everything, which is by working
out what the actual constraint is and then telling me it's a good
constraint and then telling me it doesn't bind. And I'll give you both
of them, because I'm not going to sit here and pretend a boy who reads
the council minutes for fun hasn't thought about the council."

"Then—"

"Then there's a third one," said Tsunade, "and you're not going to be
able to do it, and I want you to try, because I think you need to sit
in this room and fail at something in front of me."

She turned round and put the cup on the desk.

"I'm fifty-three."

---

He had a thing ready.

That was the trouble; he had a thing ready, he had had a thing ready
since the eleventh week, he had built it and taken it apart and rebuilt
it and got it down to two clauses and he had it sitting there in his
mouth like an article on a felt.

And he could feel that it was the right answer to a question she had
not asked.

*She has not said a number as an objection. She has said a number.*

*There is a difference and I don't know what to do with it.*

So he did what he had done since he was seven, which was reach for the
better version, and there wasn't one, and he took the one he had.
