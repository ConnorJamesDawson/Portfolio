# Chapter 26, Scene 5 — The Offer

*Act 4, part B. Konoha General. Sazare Ruri, age sixteen.
POV: Sazare. ~2,255 words.*

---

They came on the third afternoon.

By then Sazare could sit for twenty minutes, stand for none, and bend the
repaired knee twelve degrees under Nurse Yagi's supervision. The village had
counted its dead sufficiently to begin using names. The ward had received
three patients from the arena, discharged five, and lost bed fourteen at two
in the morning.

The hospital counted differently from the village.

Village notices listed dead, missing and injured. The ward counted beds,
dressings, units of blood, hours of medic attention and people who could be
sent home without stairs. A dead shinobi disappeared from the first list once
buried. In the hospital he remained as the bed made available, the instruments
used, the relatives in the corridor and the surviving patient who received the
blood intended for him.

Ruri's name appeared in both systems. The newspaper called him a hospital
worker. The operations board called him an unaffiliated combatant under
amnesty restriction. The ward roster still said *RURI, S. — 2nd flr,
06.00–16.00*, because nobody had taken down the sheet from the morning of the
invasion.

On the second day, Sister Kaburagi returned from the arena with her left hand
bandaged and stood at the foot of his bed.

"You were here," she said.

"Yes."

"That was the instruction."

"Yes."

She looked at the newspaper folded beneath Handa's pillow, the evidence seal
around the washbasin and the splint holding Ruri's leg.

"I did not mean the street."

"No."

"I should have been clearer."

"It was clear."

She considered this, then touched two fingers to the bed rail and left to take
over a hospital that had operated without her for thirty hours.

It was the only conversation about heroism he trusted.

Ashida arrived first with his arm in a sling and the movement form tucked
under it.

"Box eleven," he said, sitting beside the bed. "Hospital."

"Yes."

"Box twelve." He unfolded the form. "Defended hospital approach during enemy
action. Departed assigned second-floor post without authorisation. Returned
without instruction. Subsequently admitted as casualty."

Sazare read it. "That's accurate."

"I know. It took six drafts."

"You spelled subsequently correctly."

"My wife did that one."

The Hyūga woman entered behind him. Her name was Hyūga Natsumi. She carried no
evidence case this time, only a report thick enough to require cord through
the upper corner. Inoichi followed, then a grey-haired jōnin Sazare recognised
from the amnesty hearing as Mitokado's adjutant. Nurse Emiko came last and
closed the ward screen around the bed.

Nobody brought a guard. Ashida remained, which might have meant the same
thing.

Before sitting, Natsumi inspected the basin seal and replaced one tag whose
ink had been disturbed by condensation. Kōji asked Yagi whether the interview
was medically permitted. Yagi said twenty minutes, no chakra demonstration and
no standing. He wrote all three conditions at the top of his notes. The care
with which everybody established authority made the meeting more serious than
if they had arrived with ANBU.

The ward continued outside the screen. A trolley wheel clicked once per
revolution. Bed five argued about discharge. At the women's end somebody
laughed and immediately apologised. The ordinary sounds did not stop for the
village's decision about what Ruri was.

The adjutant introduced himself as Sarutobi Kōji, no relation close enough to
be useful, and said, "This is not an interrogation."

"All right."

"It will include questions."

"Most conversations do."

Ashida looked at the blanket. Inoichi's mouth moved once.

Kōji laid three documents on the wheeled table. The first was Natsumi's
operational report. The second bore the hospital seal. The third was a printed
form with blank spaces at the bottom.

"Twenty-seven enemy dead are presently attributed wholly or substantially to
your actions," he said. "A further sixteen were incapacitated. The main
hospital remained functional. One hundred and fourteen casualties entered
through the approach before midnight."

Sazare looked at Emiko.

"One hundred and seventeen," she said. "Three came through the laundry yard
and the operations desk has decided a door is required for statistical entry."

She had come to correct the number. He understood why she was there.

Kōji continued. "Testimony from hospital staff and Konoha personnel
consistently describes your conduct as voluntary defence of civilians and
wounded shinobi at substantial personal risk."

"I was rostered here."

"You were prohibited from combat activity."

"Yes."

"You nevertheless stayed."

Sazare looked at the repaired window beyond the screen. "Yes."

Natsumi opened her report. Diagrams showed the hospital street from above,
each crystal activation marked in blue ink. Lines ran from the roof position
to sixty-three recovered fragments.

The map was wrong in seven places.

The first gate barrier had stood eighteen inches further east. The pharmacy
plate had been thrown by the paper bomb, not grown where marked. Two
activations attributed to the final field had occurred earlier under direct
contact. One body shown inside the admissions doors had fallen outside and
been moved during triage.

Sazare corrected them with the pencil attached to the report.

Natsumi watched each alteration. "You remember all of it?"

"No."

"You have just corrected seven positions."

"I remember the work. Not everything around it."

"You remember killing this individual?"

Her finger rested on the resonator user near the stationer's wall.

"I remember three fragments under his weight."

"Not his face?"

"No."

She moved her finger to the exclusion beside the steps. "And this?"

"A Konoha uniform with a torn shoulder. I didn't know whether he was alive."

"He was."

"Yagi told me."

Natsumi made a note. The report was not measuring remorse. It was measuring
control, memory and whether he could distinguish the people inside his own
technique. That was worse in a useful way.

"The final technique," she said. "Describe it."

"Recall in the other direction."

"That is not sufficient."

"It is what happened. My work retains a route for chakra returning to me. I
used the same route to send a simple growth instruction outward."

"To every fragment simultaneously?"

"Not every fragment. Every one I could find. Some had lost the ordered part.
Some were outside my range. I excluded those nearest the hospital steps after
the first activation."

"I observed one hundred and eight active pathways."

Sazare looked at the diagram again. "Then more than I could distinguish."

"Could you repeat it?"

"Not now."

"When recovered?"

"I don't know."

"Could another crystal user?"

Guren's name stood in the room without being said.

"I don't know," Sazare said.

"During the engagement," Natsumi said, "an enemy combatant compared your
structures to a female crystal user."

"Yes."

"Name?"

"Guren."

The first time he had said it in the room, the reactions were small. Kōji's
pencil stopped. Inoichi looked at Natsumi rather than Ruri. Ashida knew the
name already and did not move.

"You believe she was present?" Kōji asked.

"No. I would have recognised her work. They had prepared a countermeasure
based on hers."

"Meaning contact or intelligence exchange with Orochimaru's organisation."

"Meaning she was alive long enough for them to test it. Possibly still alive."

"And your technique resisted it."

"No. It broke repeatedly. The retained order survived in enough pieces that
breaking it made more positions. A close strike destroyed some seeds
entirely."

Kōji wrote this down. The distinction would reach somebody who preferred the
shorter version, and the shorter version would become *Sound cannot break it*.
Ruri could already see the sentence being made.

"Put that the countermeasure works," he said.

"You have just explained that it failed."

"It works on any seed it reaches. They used it badly because they expected one
continuous structure. The next people won't."

Natsumi looked at him for a long moment and added the sentence herself.

Kōji folded his hands. "The Council's original restriction was intended to
prevent an unverified foreign combatant from developing military capability
inside the village. Yesterday demonstrated that you already possessed
substantial military capability and that preventing supervised training did
not prevent its use. It prevented us from knowing what it was."

That was so reasonable that Sazare distrusted it immediately.

"What changes?"

"That is partly your decision."

The third document was turned towards him.

It offered provisional integration into Konoha's shinobi corps, subject to
medical clearance and an out-of-cycle evaluation. Combat training would become
permitted under supervision. His hospital post would remain. Movement inside
the village would no longer require daily reporting after a probationary
period. Fabrication quotas and audits remained. Deployment outside the walls
would require mission authority.

There were fourteen clauses.

He read them once for meaning and once for ownership.

Clause two placed him within the hospital command while on medical duty and
mission command while deployed. Clause four permitted supervised combat
instruction but did not define who selected the instructor. Clause six
converted the monthly substrate quota from an amnesty condition into assigned
production work at the same quantity. Clause eight required disclosure of
techniques reasonably capable of area denial. Clause nine prohibited
unsupervised replication of the reverse-recall field inside inhabited areas.

Clause eleven authorised the village to retain fragments produced during
official activity.

"That includes medical crystal removed from patients?" he asked.

Kōji read the line. "It could."

"Then it needs an exception. Material removed during treatment belongs to the
medical record and disposal procedure."

"You are negotiating?"

"You said it was an offer."

Inoichi looked down, but not before Ruri saw him smile.

Kōji drew a line beside clause eleven. "Subject to hospital disposal
authority. Anything else?"

"Who owns work developed independently?"

"Techniques developed by Konoha personnel are registered to the village."

"I developed this before becoming Konoha personnel."

"You have not accepted."

"Then the order matters."

For the first time, Kōji sat back. The meeting had arrived at the part none of
the prepared forms covered.

"Rank?" Sazare asked.

"Unassigned until evaluation. Genin for administrative purposes."

"Command authority?"

"None initially."

"Can I refuse deployment?"

"On medical grounds, through the hospital chain. Otherwise, no more than
another shinobi."

"Can I refuse fabrication intended for combat?"

Kōji did not answer immediately. "The present material agreement remains. Its
use is determined by the village."

There it was.

Inoichi watched him read the clause again. "It is an offer," he said. "Not an
amendment imposed on the amnesty. If you decline, the previous terms continue
while the Council reviews them."

"Reviews them how?"

"Honestly?"

"Yes."

"With considerably more fear than last time."

Natsumi removed something wrapped in dark cloth from inside her jacket and set
it on the table beside the documents.

The cloth opened around a metal plate engraved with the leaf spiral, fixed to
a new length of dark blue fabric. It had no scratches. Light from the window
travelled along the raised edge and stopped in the cut of the symbol.

"Personnel who performed beyond their rank during the invasion are being
considered for promotion," Kōji said. "Civilians who defended designated
positions are receiving commendations. You fit neither process cleanly. This
offer is the village's recognition that you acted as one of its shinobi when
you were under no obligation to do so."

"I was under an obligation to the hospital."

Emiko spoke for the first time. "The hospital didn't ask you to kill
twenty-seven people."

"It asked me to keep the entrance open."

"And you did."

The word *heroism* appeared twice in the attached commendation. Sazare read
around it. One hundred and seventeen admissions. Eleven immobile patients. No
confirmed friendly deaths caused by the final field. The numbers were useful.
The word was not.

"You don't need to answer today," Kōji said.

"When?"

"Before your evaluation can be scheduled. Not before you understand the
terms."

He and Natsumi left the report. Emiko returned to the desk. Ashida took his
movement form and followed because somebody still had to put it in a tray.

Inoichi remained behind the screen.

"It isn't freedom," Sazare said.

"No."

"It's a different ledger."

"Yes."

"You recommend it."

"I recommend that you decide what you want before you decide what it costs.
You usually do those in the other order."

Sazare looked at the headband.

"That seems irresponsible."

"It often is."

Inoichi left.

The afternoon moved across the ward. Handa slept. Somebody changed the water
jug. At four, Yagi came to bend Sazare's knee and found the documents spread
in clause order across the blanket.

She did not ask what he would do. She moved the metal plate onto the bedside
table so it would not interfere with the exercise.

"Twelve degrees yesterday," she said. "We want fifteen."

Sazare put both hands beneath his thigh and began to lift.

The headband remained where she had placed it, catching the light from the
window.

The cloth was the same dark blue as the straps on the evacuation chairs. The
metal plate was heavier than it looked; he had moved it once to read the
maker's mark on the reverse and found none. Mass-produced, standard issue,
replaceable. There would be hundreds in stores and dozens being recovered from
bodies across the village.

His clan had waited until graduation to hand over its techniques. Kiri had
never allowed him to graduate. The sealed scroll remained somewhere on a
stranger's shelf, and the only forehead protector he had ever worn had
belonged to a dead man and been used as a disguise.

This one had his name on the form beneath it.

He completed the fifteenth degree. The repaired joint moved through the centre
and stopped where Yagi's hand told it to. She lowered the leg to the mattress,
recorded the angle, and left the metal plate untouched.

Nobody asked him to put it on.
