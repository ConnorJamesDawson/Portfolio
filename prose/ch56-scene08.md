# Chapter 56, Scene 8 — A Name at the Gate

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, front steps. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~810 words.*

---

He went down at about half past six and there was a knot of people at
the front.

Not many. Six or seven — Ōtani off the door, two clerks who should have
gone home, a chūnin he did not know, and the woman who ran the tea
stall on the corner, who had come up the steps, which she had never
done in three years.

They were all looking down the hill toward the north gate.

"What is it?" said Sazare.

"Nothing," said Ōtani. "Somebody's come in."

"Who?"

Ōtani looked at him.

It was a particular kind of look and he had four hundred pages on it,
and it was the one people gave him when they had forgotten for a moment
that he was foreign, and had then remembered, and were adjusting the
sentence.

"Jiraiya," said Ōtani.

---

He had the folder entry immediately, because he had read the folder
about a hundred and forty times and could have recited the leadership
section in his sleep.

*Konohagakure: persons of note. Jiraiya, no clan name given. Of the
three Sannin. Intelligence, unattached — operates outside the standing
structure and has done since the second war. Teacher of the Fourth
Hokage. Summoning contract: toads. Whereabouts habitually unknown.*

*Kirigakure assessment: do not approach.*

He stood on the steps and turned the whole of it over and it did
nothing at all.

*That is a name and a set of facts and I have no weight on any of them.
He is one of three and I know the second one by his work and the third
one because she is upstairs, and this one is a line in a folder.*

*I have never seen a likeness. There isn't one in the folder. There is
a note in the margin of the copy at the annexe that says the office has
never held a current likeness of him, which I read at seventeen and
thought was a filing failure.*

*Whereabouts habitually unknown. Right.*

*So the question is not who he is. The question is why seven people
have come out onto a step.*

---

The tea-stall woman was crying.

She was not making anything of it. She had her hands folded in front of
her and she was looking down the hill and it was going down her face
and she was not doing anything about it, and he did not understand it
at all and stood there working on it for about eleven seconds and got
nowhere.

Then Shizune came down the stairs behind him.

He heard her stop.

He did not turn round. He had spent three days learning exactly how
much of a person you were entitled to see, off her, and he had the sound
of it and the sound was enough: a woman coming down at an ordinary pace
and putting a foot on the second-to-last step and not putting the other
one down.

"He's here," said Shizune.

"Yes."

"Who told you?"

"Ōtani."

"Right." She came down the last step. "Right."

He turned round.

---

Shizune's face was not any of the four hundred pages.

It was not relief and it was not dread and it was not the thing people
did when somebody came back off a border, and it was not what she had
looked like on the fourteenth in the outer office with a folded form in
her hand.

It had about three things in it at once and none of them were on top.

"Is this bad?" said Sazare.

"No."

"Is it good?"

"Ruri," said Shizune, "I have known that man since I was eleven years
old and I could not answer that question about any single day of it,
and I have had twenty years to try."

She went down the last steps and stood next to him and looked at the
hill.

"He'll be up here tomorrow," she said. "Or tonight. Or in four days,
and then it'll be at two in the morning and through a window."

"Should I not be here?"

"You should absolutely not be here," said Shizune. "And you are going
to be, because you turn up at the fourth hour, and there is nothing on
this earth that is going to stop you doing that, and I have thought
about it for four days and I have decided I am not going to try."

---

They stood on the steps for a bit.

Down the hill somebody laughed at something, a long way off, in the
particular way a village laughs when a thing has come back into it.

*Summoning contract: toads.*

He read it again off the inside of his own head, the way he read
everything, and it went past without catching on anything whatsoever,
and he put it away and thought about a bundle of flimsies tied as fifty
that had forty-nine in it.
