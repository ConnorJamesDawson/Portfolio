# Chapter 51, Scene 2 — Written To Somebody

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,744 words.*

---

He tried to specify it on the Sunday and it took him four hours and it
failed completely, which he had more or less expected and had done
anyway, because a failed specification was still a document.

*Statement. There is a thing I have been experiencing for some weeks
which is now interfering with the work — see the drainage seat, three
hours of a Saturday, unfinishable — and I have no name for it and no
column for it, and a thing without a name cannot be tested.*

*Method: state the candidate, then find the case that breaks it.*

*1. It is wanting to see her.*

*Broken. Test: would a letter do? Four pages, everything, sent up with
the surplus returns. I sat with that for nine minutes and the answer is
no, and I am not going to pretend the no is about efficiency.*

*2. It is wanting to be heard. An audience for the month's work.*

*Broken, and this is the one I was most confident of. I have had an
audience. Kanzaki had the whole of the material side on the thirtieth
and stood over two ledgers for an hour with her hands flat, which is
the largest reaction that woman has ever produced. Katsuyu was there
for four thousand of them and is the only creature alive who can see
what I do.*

*I have been heard more thoroughly this month than in the whole of the
last two years.*

*It did not touch it.*

*3. It is that she is the only one who can hold the whole of it.*

*Broken, and this is the one that surprised me. It was true in the
third month — I wrote four pages about halves and no whole and I meant
it. It is not true now. Katsuyu holds more of what I actually do than
anybody has ever held, including her, because Katsuyu can see the
operation and nobody else in the world can.*

*So the completeness is not it either.*

He sat back from the sheet at about the third hour.

*Then what is left.*

---

*Residue.*

*What she does that nobody else does, stated as flatly as I can manage:*

*I bring her an object and the conversation turns out to have been
about something else.*

*Reject table — becomes a sentence that would hurt people for a
century. Paper trap — becomes the difference between what interests me
and what I actually want. A hypothesis at seven in the morning on no
sleep — becomes the short list of families worth taking a child from.
A stiff shoulder — becomes chapter five arriving from a direction I had
not considered in seven months of trying.*

*Not one of those was the subject I walked in with.*

*Kanzaki takes the object and weighs it, which is her trade and is
extremely valuable and is the object. Yagi takes it and tells me what
it does to a patient. Ibiki takes it and tells me what it can be used
for. Katsuyu takes it and tells me what my hands did while I was making
it, which is the most useful thing anybody has ever done for me.*

*Every one of them is the size of the thing on the table.*

*She is the only person I have ever met who is bigger than the thing on
the table, and I wrote that down in the fifth month as an
observation about exhaustibility and filed it under her, and it is not
about her at all. It is a description of what I have been missing for
eleven years and did not know was a category.*

He read that back.

Then he wrote, underneath, the nearest he could get, and it took three
attempts and he kept the third because it was the only one that was not
dressed up:

*I want to bring somebody a rock and find out it was about something
else.*

*That is the whole of it. That is what I have got after four hours and
a ruled sheet, and it is absurd, and it is accurate, and there is no
version of it I could say out loud to a living person.*

---

He found the other thing on the Monday and he was not looking for it.

He had gone back through the ninth month for the reject figures —
Kanzaki wanted the two hundred and nine broken out by medium for the
series-break entry — and he had the sixth month's book open beside it
for a comparison, and at about the fourth page he stopped.

He read an entry from the sixth month.

*14/6. Yard aggregate, second issue, four kilos. Eleven articles,
three-deep, four seconds. Two rejects, both at the seat, both mine.
Ambient 24, dry. Bench three. Lens: Kanzaki's.*

Then he read one from the ninth.

*19/9. Five, tonight — and I sat on the floor for a while afterwards,
which I am recording because it happened and not because it is
interesting.*

*Aggregate again. Nine per cent, which is less than half of what it was
in the spring, and the whole of the difference is a fault I could not
feel and a slug could see from nine inches away in about four seconds.
Eleven articles. Two would have been rejects in the fourth month and
are not now.*

*(The pigeons have stopped. It is the tenth of the month tomorrow and
they stop when it turns; I have three years of this and I have never
written it down before, so there it is.)*

He put the two books side by side on the low table.

---

It took him about an hour to establish and it was not difficult, once
he had thought to look.

*Sixth month, and every month before it back to the eleventh month of
my thirteenth year: the entry contains the work. Figure, condition,
fault, disposition. It is complete and it is dull and it is written for
a man who will need to reconstruct a batch in forty years, and that man
is me.*

*Ninth month: the entry has a shape.*

*I am putting the interesting number first.*

*I am explaining things I do not need explained. "Which is less than
half of what it was in the spring" — I know what it was in the spring.
I have it on the page above. That clause is not for me.*

*I am leaving things out. There is no ambient on the nineteenth and no
bench number and no lens, and I have not omitted a condition from an
entry since I was fourteen, and the reason is that they are dull.*

*A private record has no dull. There is nothing to be dull for.*

*I am making asides. There is a bracket about pigeons.*

*I am, in four separate places this month, apologising in advance for
recording something.*

He sat with the two books.

*These are not the same document.*

*The ninth month is being written to somebody.*

---

He tried to date it and could not do it cleanly, and the not-being-able
was the worst part of the evening.

He went backwards. The ninth was unmistakable. The eighth was there but
thinner, and the seventh, and the sixth had the odd clause in it that
he could argue either way, and the fifth —

The fifth had it.

He went back further, four books, on the floor with the lamp down and
the tenth month getting cold at the window, and he found it in the
third.

*Fourth month of this year is already doing it. Third month is doing it
by the end and not at the beginning.*

*So: somewhere in the third month. Seven months ago.*

*I went up a hill at about eight in the evening with eleven pages and
she took the stack off her desk and put it on the floor, and we were
there until the fourth hour of the morning, and I came home and wrote
it up, and I have been writing to her ever since and I did not know
until about ten past nine this evening.*

*Seven months.*

*Two hundred and ten entries. Not one of them addressed to anybody, not
one of them shown to anybody, and every one of them shaped for a reader
who has never seen a line of it.*

---

He put the books away at about midnight and did the honest half sitting
on the floor with his back against the wall, in the place where he had
done the sphere every night for twenty weeks.

*So the specification failed and the ledger answered it instead, which
is what usually happens and I should have started there.*

*It is not about seeing her and it is not about being heard and it is
not about completeness.*

*I have a month of the best findings of my life. The take. The grip.
Twelve per cent. Three hundred kilos drawn and released and nobody
knows where. A number at the bottom of a page that has gone from two to
nine and no longer feels like holding.*

*I have told Kanzaki the parts that are hers. I have told Katsuyu the
parts she watched. I have told Yagi about the hand.*

*I have not told anybody the whole of any of it.*

*I assumed the reason was that there was nobody available.*

*There has been somebody available for thirty-nine days, fifteen
minutes' walk from this table.*

He looked at the wall for a while.

*I have not been unable to tell her.*

*I have been saving it.*

*I have been putting things aside, in order, in a book, in a hand
shaped for her, for thirty-nine days — the way a person keeps a thing
back to give to somebody — and I have been calling it a want of
opportunity, and there has been no want of opportunity, and I have
written the word "nothing" under the heading of what I am going to do
about it twice this year in two different books and meant two different
things by it and both of them were lies of a kind I have not got a name
for either.*

He did not write anything else that night.

At the bottom of the page, in the ordinary place, because it went there
every night whatever else did or did not happen:

*Exercise 1: eleven.*
