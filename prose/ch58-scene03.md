# Chapter 58, Scene 3 — No Seals

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1780 words.*

---

Jiraiya was in the office at the fourth hour on Wednesday, on the
windowsill, eating something out of a paper.

"He's been here since three," said Tsunade, without looking up. "He has
eaten a pear, most of a fish, and something I have decided not to
identify, and he has put none of it in the bin."

"Morning, kid."

"Good morning."

"Sit down, I've got a question and I've been saving it for two days
because I wanted to be sure it was the right one."

"Both of you," said Tsunade, "on the floor over there, out of my light,
and if either of you says one word about a schedule I will have you
removed by force."

---

"How do you do it with no seals?"

Sazare sat down on the floor by the wall.

"That's the question?"

"That's the question. Two days I've had it." Jiraiya swung his legs
round off the sill. "I've been through the folder again since Monday and
there is not one recorded instance of you making a seal. Not one. Yagi's
statements, the study, the Corps assessments — three separate people
have written *does not use seals* in three different documents and not
one of them has written *and this is impossible*."

"It isn't impossible."

"It's *unheard of*, kid. There is a nature transformation coming out of
your hands and there is nothing in front of it. That's not a style, it's
a—"

"It's not a jutsu," said Sazare.

---

Jiraiya stopped.

"Say that again."

"It isn't a jutsu. I don't do jutsu. I have never done a jutsu in my
life and I would not know how to start."

"You put nine inches of stem out of your palm."

"Yes."

"That's Mokuton."

"It's a Mokuton *article*," said Sazare. "It's a thing that came out.
That doesn't make the way I got it a technique, and I want to be careful
here, because everybody who has ever written about me has made this
mistake and it's on the front of the paper."

"Then explain it. Slowly. I'm fifty-four."

---

"A seal is a notation," said Sazare.

"Go on."

"That's the whole of it and everything else follows. A seal is writing.
It's a compression — somebody worked out a shape once, a long time ago,
and found a way to write the shape down so that it could be handed to
somebody who had not worked it out, and so that a man could hand it back
to *himself* reliably on a bad day in the rain.

"That's what it's for. It's not power. Nobody's ever thought it was
power. It's that a shape which took a genius eleven years is now four
positions and a boy of nine can have it.

"And it means you can carry forty of them. You know forty techniques.
You don't hold forty shapes in your head — nobody could — you hold forty
*notations*, and the notation calls the shape up when you run it."

"That's fair," said Jiraiya. "That's accurate, actually. I've never
heard anybody put it like that and I've taught it for thirty years."

"I can't read it."

---

He said it flatly.

"Not can't in the sense that nobody taught me. I was taught. Twice — at
the ward, in the second month of my first year, and again by a chūnin
instructor at sixteen who was extremely patient and gave it four
afternoons.

"There is a thing everybody else does when they look at a seal and I do
not do it. The positions go in and nothing comes back. It's the same
when I look at a fūinjutsu array and it's the same when I look at the
mark on—"

He stopped.

"Go on," said Jiraiya, in a slightly different voice.

"It's the same with all of it," said Sazare. "It is the one medium in
the world that is shut to me, and it is the medium every technique in
history is written in, and I worked that out at sixteen and I have not
had a productive thought about it since."

---

"So what do you do instead?"

"I hold the whole shape."

Jiraiya put the paper down.

"Every time," said Sazare. "From nothing. There is no compression and
there is nothing to call anything up with, so the only way I have ever
been able to do a thing is to have the entire structure in my head at
once, complete, while I'm doing it — every course, every way, every
junction, the whole article — and then put it out.

"That's not a discipline. It's not impressive. It's what's left when you
can't write anything down."

"How many can you do?"

"Three."

"...Three."

"Crystal, which is one operation and about nine hundred applications of
it. The wood, once, which took four months and nearly killed me. And the
gather, which I did not know was a thing until Monday."

He looked at his hands.

"You've got forty. I've got three. That is the actual trade and nobody
ever says it in that direction — everybody looks at a man with no seals
and thinks he has been *freed* from something. I haven't. I have got
three things and I will probably have four when I die, and every single
one of them cost me years, and a genin with a scroll can have more
techniques than me by Thursday."

---

"And there's no gap," said Jiraiya.

Sazare looked up.

"That's the bit, isn't it," said Jiraiya, and he had gone quiet. "That's
what nobody's written down in your folder because nobody's thought about
it for four consecutive seconds.

"A seal takes time. Not much. Half a second, a second, four if it's a
big one and you're careful — and that time is the *only* thing on this
earth between a shinobi deciding to do something and the world finding
out about it.

"It's not a safety feature. Nobody designed it. It's just an interval,
and every single one of us has spent our whole lives with one, and there
is not a person in this village who has not, at some point, got two
positions into something and stopped."

"Yes," said Sazare.

"You haven't got one."

"No."

"So when you decide—"

"It's already happened," said Sazare. "There's no interval at all. And
Hatake said it to me in the ninth month in about eleven words and it is
the reason the governor exists, and I did not argue with him and I am
not going to argue with you."

---

Jiraiya sat on the windowsill and looked at him for a long moment.

Over at the desk, Tsunade had stopped writing. She did not say anything
and she did not look up and the pen was not moving.

"Right," said Jiraiya. "Now the actual question, and I've been building
up to it for two days and I'd rather get it out badly than not at all."

"Go on."

"Can you teach me?"

---

"Teach you what?"

"Don't do that, kid."

"I'm not doing anything, I genuinely don't know which part you're
asking about."

"The gather," said Jiraiya.

He said it and then he sat with it, and there was about four seconds of
nothing at all in that room at ten past four in the morning.

"I can't gather and move," he said. "I told you on the roof. I'm too
slow and I'm not still enough and I never have been, and I have carried
two old toads on my shoulders for thirty years because they can do the
one thing I can't.

"I've made my peace with that as well. That one's harder than the other
one, and I've made it, and I'd have told you it was finished business
right up until Monday morning.

"And then a boy did it twice in a minute on a roof, one-handed, in a
fifth of a second, while he was upset about a slug, and told me it
wasn't interesting."

---

*Right.*

*Say the true thing first and the kind thing second, and if there is
only room for one, the true one.*

"I broke it," he said.

"What?"

"The exercise. I did it last night for the first time in six days and I
could not get past six, and it wasn't the six days.

"It's that I know what it is now. For eleven weeks it ran under
everything — you sit down and it comes and you count and there is nothing
in the middle. And on Monday you told me what the middle was called and
what happens if you take too much, and now there is a thing in the
middle, and the thing in the middle is me watching.

"And it needs me to go still, and I cannot go still while I am watching
myself go still. Nobody can. That's what still means."

Jiraiya put both hands over his face.

"Oh, kid," he said.

"It isn't a complaint."

"It is absolutely a complaint and you are entitled to it. I did that.
I did that to you on Monday on a roof at six in the morning and I did
it because I could not shut up."

"You gave me a word for what I am," said Sazare. "I have had four
hundred descriptions and not one category and you gave me a category in
about eleven seconds, and I would not give it back for the fifteen. I
want that said and then I'm not going to say it again because I don't
know how to say it twice."

There was a small noise from the direction of the desk.

Neither of them looked round.

---

"So the answer's no," said Jiraiya.

"The answer isn't no."

"Kid, you've just told me the only thing you could teach me is how to
stop paying attention, and I have spent thirty years learning to pay
attention, and you have just proved on your own floor that knowing about
it wrecks it."

"The answer isn't no," said Sazare. "The answer is that I can't teach
you, and those are different, and the difference is the whole of the
problem and I'd like about a day with it."

"Why can't you?"

"Because I've got nothing to hand you," said Sazare. "That's the whole
of it. I haven't got a notation, that's the entire premise of my life. I
learn by matching a finished article, and you can't match a crystal
article because you can't make one.

"And the thing I'd actually be handing you isn't a technique.

"It's the *absence* of one.

"And there is no way on this earth to demonstrate a thing that isn't
there."
