# Chapter 70, Scene 8 — Violating the Spec

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the north fields. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~880 words.*

---

He brought it to the field on the Monday, at the end of the
morning, in the format the field respected: a failure report.

"The programme is violating its own specification," he said.

Jiraiya, who had been pulling the practice swords out of the
ground, straightened up with the particular stillness of a man
who has been waiting twelve days for a delivery and intends to
enjoy signing for it.

"Go on."

"The specification was stated behind the bathhouse on the
twenty-seventh of the twelfth and restated here in week one:
the target is not me. The target is that she stops standing
at windows. Those were the terms — everything out here is in
service of one sentence and the sentence is not about him. I
am reporting that the sentence is failing. The training has
not made her safer from windows. It has built her a new one.
She has been waiting up for a man who falls asleep
mid-sentence, for eleven days, alone, in silence, because
every hour taking me from her is an hour she authorised and
believes in and will not touch — and the programme's own
architect taught me that a system meeting every clause and
failing its purpose is not compliant. It is *worse* than
non-compliant. It is wrong with a full set of signatures."

The field was quiet. Fukasaku, on the cloth, had closed his
eyes in the manner of an elder savouring a student arriving
somewhere on schedule.

"Twelve days," said Jiraiya.

"...Say that number again."

"Twelve days ago," said Jiraiya, spearing the second sword
back into the frost, "I watched that woman stand at the top
of the tower stair at six in the morning to get ninety
seconds of you, and I know what her face does, kid, I've
been reading it for forty years. I clocked it, I priced it,
and I left it in the ground on purpose, because the audit
was yours to run, not mine to hand you — you get *worse*
when you're told and better when you work it out; it's in
your file, I wrote it. So this—" he waved a hand at the
report, the drawings, the whole of it— "this was the
exercise. The real one. Sweeps are a hobby. THIS is the
trade: a man under full load, every hour spoken for by
something worthy, catching the account that never appears
on a docket before it fails. Eleven days from onset to
finding." He looked at Sazare for a long moment, and the
grin, when it arrived, had the other thing under it, the
thing he mostly kept for the notebook. "It took me until I
was fifty to run that audit inside a decade. In a year I
want you inside two days. Now show me the remediation,
because if it says 'train less' we're going to have words."

"It does not say train less. It says Thursday."

He laid it out: the mornings held at four but Thursday's
moved — no field, no yard, no board, no entries. A day with
nothing in it, weekly, structural, in the diary that does
not lie. The noons re-armoured — the through-and-out law
written down at last, one clause, no items at the tray, the
board's traffic rerouted to a standing Tuesday memo. And
the third document, the one he was not going to show anybody
before her: the file that would put her name inside the
spring.

Jiraiya read the Thursday order twice, and Fukasaku read it
once, and the old toad spoke first.

"Ma and I kept a day," he said, unexpectedly, to the frost
in general. "Four hundred years, boy. Every mountain keeps
a day or the mountain forgets what the training is *of*.
Approved, for whatever a toad's approval is worth on a
married man's diary."

"They're not—" said Jiraiya, and stopped, and looked at the
sky, and visibly decided the correction was above his pay
grade, and signed the Thursday clause as witness in the
margin with a flourish, under the words *the programme's
architect concurs: compliance restored.*

"One more thing," he said, as Sazare packed the case, and
the register he said it in was the bathhouse one, the one
with no jokes in the load path. "You caught it in eleven
days because you keep ledgers. Fine. Better than fine —
it's the best instrument on the continent and it found the
fault. But hear the old man's version once, and then we'll
never speak of it again: I knew a man who kept every
account in the village except that one. Finest shinobi of
his generation. The account failed in year nine, quietly,
no incident report, and he spent the rest of a short life
being magnificent at a window of his own." He shouldered
the swords. "There's no technique for it, kid. There's
just the audit, run early, run always. You've got the
habit now. Keep the habit longer than you keep me."

*Entry, Monday, field file and personal file, cross-
referenced for the first time:*

*Compliance restored, effective Thursday. The exercise was
real and I passed it late and passing late was the lesson.*

*Fukasaku-sama believes we are married. Jiraiya-sama
declined to correct him at ANY point. Neither did I.*

*Filed without further comment.*
