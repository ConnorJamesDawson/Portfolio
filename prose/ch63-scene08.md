# Chapter 63, Scene 8 — Her Hand

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Tsunade, age fifty-three.
POV: Tsunade. ~1210 words.*

---

He went out in under two minutes, which he had done every night for
twenty-three nights and which she had stopped being able to find
irritating at about the fourth.

She did not.

She lay on her back in the dark with his hand in hers on top of the
covers and worked, because there was nothing else to do at half past
twelve in the morning with a training ground nine hours away.

*You'll tell me when you tell me.*

*That is the last thing that boy said to me before he went to sleep and
he said it in about eleven words and then told me to go to bed.*

*And I have been round it four times in twenty minutes and it does not
come apart.*

*He knows there is something. He has got two candidates and he has told
me he has got two candidates and he has not asked which. He worked out my
reason for not telling him — worked it out, on his back, in the dark,
correctly, in better words than I had it in — and then he agreed with
it.*

*And then he told me he was not going to work on it.*

*He is going to walk down that hill in the morning with a thing he knows
I am carrying, and he is not going to touch it, and he will not touch it
on Saturday either, and he will still not have touched it in the fourth
month.*

*That is not patience. He has not got any patience — he has never waited
for anything in his life, he has got a slug telling him to be bad at
something for some weeks and it is costing him visible effort.*

*It is that he has decided the thing is mine.*

She turned her head on the pillow.

*Nobody has ever left a thing alone because it was mine.*

---

*Right. Do the work.*

*Because he asked me for something at ten past twelve and I gave him a
joke with a table in it, and it was true and it was the best I had and he
took it and he was right to take it.*

*And it is not the whole of it, and I have got about eight hours.*

She lay in the dark.

*He said: I am not asking you to think I'll win.*

*He said it deliberately, before the ask, so that I would not have to.*

*And I have spent three days in a state I could not get out of, which is
that I have money on him and I think he is going to lose, and I have been
carrying those two round as a contradiction and being quietly disgusted
with myself about it since Tuesday afternoon.*

*They are not a contradiction.*

She got that at about twenty to one and lay very still with it.

*They are answers to two different questions and I have been putting them
in the same column for three days like a bloody amateur.*

*Will he win on Friday? No. I have got the evidence and the evidence is
good and it says no.*

*Do I believe in him?*

*That is not about Friday. That has never been about Friday. That has not
been about anything since the eleventh of last month and possibly since a
gateway in the tenth, and I have known the answer to it for so long that
I stopped being able to see it as a separate question.*

*He asked for the second one.*

*He asked for the second one specifically and he ruled out the first one
in advance in his own sentence, and I did not hear it, and I gave him a
joke about the first one, and he took the joke and understood the second
one anyway because he has been reading me for three years.*

*He got what he asked for by accident.*

*And I am not going to be able to say the correct version to him now,
because it is half twelve and he is asleep and in the morning it will be
a training ground.*

She lay there.

*Fine. Then say it here.*

---

*I believe in that boy.*

*Not that he will win. He will not win, and I would put money on it if I
had not already put money on the other thing in front of Jiraiya like a
sentimental old woman.*

*I believe that a man who has been walked at his whole life and has never
once walked back is going to stand on that ground in the morning in front
of eleven people and find out something about himself in public, and that
he is going to get up off it, and that by Sunday night he will have four
pages and a table and a fault written down in his own hand with no
softening anywhere in it.*

*He has done that every single time. Every one. The hand, the licence,
the study, the fifteenth of the eighth month, the twelfth Friday, a smear
of grey crystal on the floor of my own long room.*

*There is not one instance in three years of that boy failing at
something and not coming back with the honest account of it.*

*That is what I believe in. That is the whole of it and it is not
nothing and it is considerably harder to find than winning.*

She looked at the ceiling.

*And there is a second half and I have got about seven hours so I may as
well have it.*

*I want him to win.*

---

*Which is not the same as thinking he will, and I have just spent an hour
establishing that they are different, so I am allowed both.*

*I want him to win at half past nine tomorrow morning on ground two in
front of Jiraiya and Sakura and a slug and about eleven other people who
have all heard that the Hokage of Konohagakure picked the wrong one.*

*I want it for no reason at all. I do not want it because it would be
useful and I do not want it because of the IOU, which I will pay without
blinking and which I have already decided how to pay.*

*I want it because he asked me for a thing at midnight and it was the
second thing he has ever asked me for in three years, and the first one
was a kiss.*

*Forty years of betting and I have never once wanted a card to come in
for a reason that was not money.*

She turned over.

His hand went with her, because he had not let go of it either, which she
had not noticed until it happened.

*Right.*

*Nine hours.*

*And I am going to stand on the edge of that ground with a face on and it
is going to be the wrong one whichever I pick, and there are two people
in this village who will read it, and one of them is on a bathhouse roof
and the other one has fifty-one sheets.*

*Let them.*

She shut her eyes and went to sleep at about ten past one, still holding
it.
