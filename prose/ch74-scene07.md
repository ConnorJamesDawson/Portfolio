# Chapter 74, Scene 7 — The Article, Not the Man

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Tenzō, age
twenty-seven. POV: Tenzō. ~2,770 words.*

---

Tenzō had been weighed a great many times and had never once been in
the room.

That was the first thing he thought, and he thought it fast, in the
first four seconds, while the boy was still saying *I need eleven
minutes and I need you to have an exit* — and it was not a
complaint, it was a *specification*, the kind of thing he wrote in
the file: dateless, factual, interpretation withheld.

*Item: in nine years in the shop I was assessed continuously by
eleven people whose names I know and an unknown number whose names
I do not, and I was never present for one minute of it. That is not
secrecy. It is the method. A subject who knows he is being assessed
adjusts, and an adjusted reading is a spoiled reading, and spoiled
readings waste materials.*

*Item: you found out afterwards. From what happened to you. You
were reassigned, or you were not; the ration changed, or it did not;
a boy you had trained beside stopped being in the corridor. The
assessment arrived as weather and you read the weather backwards to
learn what had been decided about you, and everybody in that
building could do it, and nobody ever said so out loud, because
saying so out loud was itself an adjustment.*

*Item, entered now, at nineteen minutes past two on the eighteenth
of the third month, and I want the time on it: a man crossed eight
feet of yard to tell me that I had been the subject of a decision
and that he was not prepared to let it stand.*

*Interpretation: withheld.*

*Interpretation, entered anyway, once, and struck out afterwards
if I have any sense: I have no protocol for this. I have never
needed one. Nobody has ever put me in the room.*

---

The boy gave it to him whole, and it took nine minutes rather than
eleven, and he did not soften one syllable of it.

He gave him the want first, which Tenzō noted as a professional
choice and a costly one — *he leads with what he wanted rather than
what he decided, because leading with the decision would be asking
for credit* — and the want was the fifth layer of a fighting style,
a root moving an inch under a committing foot, an article that
reached past forty feet, a yard that in eleven months somebody was
going to come for.

Then the ask, which was not *let me examine you* and never had
been. A commission. Ordinary made things, regularly, for about a
year, held fresh.

Then the three legs of the refusal, in order, in the flat grading
voice, with the pen down and both hands visible.

*One: it is the shop's shape. Output on order, indefinitely, for
somebody else's programme, from the one man alive who was
manufactured, and nobody ever asking whether the making was
anything to him.*

*Two: he cannot refuse me. He is attached to this yard by an order
with her signature on it. His remit is three lines. There is no
clause under which the man watched can decline a request from the
man he is watching, because nobody drafting it imagined the traffic
running that way.*

*Three: the rose was a gift, freely made, once, and I was about to
convert it into a supply.*

*Finding: no. Made at about twenty past two, on that bench, alone,
in four minutes.*

And then the boy stopped talking and stood in the middle of the
yard with his hands at his sides and waited, in the way of a man who
has finished a report and will not decorate it.

Tenzō put the manifest down on the crate.

He discovered, distantly, that his hands were not entirely steady,
which was a thing they did perhaps twice a year, and he watched them
do it with the same detachment he brought to everything and found,
this once, that the detachment was thinner than usual.

"Leg two is wrong," he said.

The boy's head came up.

"It's wrong," said Tenzō, "and it is wrong in a way that matters,
and I am going to correct it, and you are not going to enjoy the
correction. Sit down. You've been standing for nine minutes and
you're grey."

Sazare sat down on the crate opposite. Eight feet. Neither of them
remarked on it.

"You have identified the power relation," said Tenzō, "and you have
identified it in the form it takes on paper, which is the order and
the remit and the signature. That form is real and it is not the
one that would have got you what you wanted."

"Then say the one that would."

*Careful,* said nine years of the shop, from a long way off. *This
is disclosure. This is not in any remit. This goes nowhere, this
helps nothing, and there is no version of this that is compliant.*

*Noted,* he thought, *and overruled,* and heard himself say it.

"I would have said yes."

The kettle ticked.

"Not because of the order," said Tenzō. "Nobody in this yard has
ever given me an instruction and you would sooner cut your own hand
off than start, and I have known that since the second week. The
order is not the mechanism. I want you to write down what the
mechanism is, because you're going to meet it again in your life and
next time it might not be attached to somebody who likes you.

"I would have said yes because I want to be *kept*.

"That's it. That's the whole of it. Nine years in a place that
never once had to make me do anything, because it was the only
building in the world that had a use for me, and the arithmetic a
child does in that situation is very simple and it never comes out
of him afterwards: if I am useful they will keep me, and if I am
not useful I am a *disposal question*, and I watched the disposal
questions get answered." He kept his voice exactly level, because
level was the only delivery he had that survived this material. "So
you'd have asked me for wood on Tuesdays. And I'd have said yes
inside two seconds, and meant it, and been glad — genuinely glad,
that's the part nobody believes — and I'd have made you your
articles for a year, and it would have cost me something, and I
would not have been able to tell you what, and neither would you,
because it wouldn't have shown up for about two years and by then
we'd both have called it something else."

Sazare said nothing at all for a while.

"So the finding stands," he said at last, and his voice had gone
somewhere careful, "and the reasoning I used to reach it was
inadequate, and the true reason is worse."

"The true reason is worse. Yes."

"Then I would like to correct my own file in front of you, because
I have just been told something that no amount of reading would have
given me, and I am not going to pretend I worked it out." He had the
notebook out. Of course he had the notebook out. "Leg two, struck.
Replacement: *the man cannot refuse me and the reason is not the
order. The reason is that he was raised in a place where being
useful was the whole of the tenancy, and a request from somebody
whose yard he wants to stay in does not arrive at him as a request.
It arrives as an opportunity to be indispensable, and he will take
it, gladly, and pay for it out of an account neither of us can
audit.*"

He read it back once and shut the book.

"Thank you," said Sazare. "That is the most expensive thing anybody
has given me this year and I include Monday."

---

*Now,* thought Tenzō.

He had known it was coming since about the fourth minute. He had
watched it arrive from a long way off, the way you watch a front
come up a valley, and he had had time to be frightened of it and had
used the time on something else.

"Ask me anyway," he said.

Sazare went still.

"Tenzō-san—"

"No. Listen to the whole thing before you do the ledger face." He
folded his arms, which was as close as he came to bracing. "You've
built a very good audit and you've made a very good decision and you
have made it *about* me, in a room I was not in, and then you came
and told me, which is more than anybody has ever done and I am not
going to pretend it isn't.

"But it's still your decision. It's still a man in this yard
deciding what happens to Tenzō, correctly and kindly, on grounds he
worked out by himself, and then delivering the result.

"There is one thing that has never once happened in my life and it
is not being asked. I get asked all the time. Kakashi-senpai has
been building me doors for eighteen years and I have walked out of
about forty of them and he has never once made me feel it, and he is
the reason I am a person and not a file.

"The thing that has never happened," said Tenzō, "is being the one
who *decides*. Not choosing an exit somebody built. Making the call.

"So: ask me. Out loud, properly, the real ask, no exits, no
softening, and I will give you an answer that is *mine* — and then
we'll both know what it's worth, and you can put whichever one it is
in your book."

Nothing moved in the yard.

He watched the boy sit there with his hands on his knees and go
through it, and it was — Tenzō thought, and entered it later,
dateless — the most complete thing he had ever watched a person do.
Because he was watching a man decide to lose. There was no version
of the next minute in which Sazare Ruri got what he wanted; he had
already established that the yes would be worthless and the no would
cost him a layer of his own style, and he had to ask anyway, at full
volume, with nothing held back, or the whole nine minutes had been a
performance.

"Tenzō-san," said Sazare, and his voice was level and it was
costing him visibly and he let it cost. "I want you to make wood for
me. Ordinary articles, no larger than a hand, roughly weekly, for a
year, in this yard, so that I can hold them fresh and learn to make
it myself, and I want it because it will make me harder to kill and
for no other reason at all. That is the whole ask. There is nothing
underneath it. I am not going to tell you it would help the line or
the hospital or the road, because it would not; it is for me."

"Right," said Tenzō.

And then he took, deliberately, about twenty seconds, because he
had never in his life had twenty seconds that belonged to him, and
he intended to use every one of them.

"No," he said.

He watched that land, and he saw — this was the part he would keep —
that the boy did not look relieved. He looked as though something
had been taken off him with a knife, and he sat with it, and did not
argue, and did not offer a single one of the four excellent
consolations Tenzō could see him holding.

"Reasons," said Tenzō, "since you gave me yours.

"One is the one I just told you and you can have it as my answer as
well as your finding: I'd be doing it out of the wrong account and
I'd never find the bottom of it.

"Two is that you were right about the rose and it took me a while to
see it. I made that once. I have made one thing in my adult life
that nobody ordered, and it went to a hospital, and it was a rose
because there was no operational reason for it to be anything, and
if I start making you a hand-sized article every Tuesday then the
one free thing I ever did becomes the first delivery of a series. I
would rather have made one rose than fifty-two commissions.

"And three." He unfolded his arms. "You'd get it. That's the part
I want on your record, because you'll spend a year telling yourself
this cost you the layer and I want it costing you the right amount
and not more. A year of fresh articles and you'd have it. You'd be
making wood in a field by next spring and you would be enormously
harder to kill and I would have been part of that, and there is a
version of me that wants it very badly.

"And the version of me that wants it is nine years old and lives in
a corridor, and I am twenty-seven, and I have a yard, and I get to
overrule him.

"That's the answer. It's mine. It's the first one I ever made."

---

The boy stood up.

He did not say thank you, which Tenzō noted with approval, because
thank you would have been for the answer and the answer was not a
gift.

"Then it is closed," said Sazare, "by you, on the eighteenth, at
about half past two, and it does not reopen — not next year, not if
I change my mind, not if things get worse. If it ever reopens it
reopens because *you* raise it, and you will not, and I am not going
to spend a decade quietly hoping you will."

"Agreed."

"Then I have work, and so do you, and I am going to go and be
extremely bad company at a bench for about three hours."

He had got four feet — half the perimeter — when Tenzō said, "Wait."

*Don't,* said the shop.

*Noted,* he thought.

He worked the button off the third hole down on the left of the
jacket, which took a moment, because the thread was old and had
been put in by somebody who had never been taught to sew, and
then he crossed the four feet himself, which he had not done for
anything except work in ten weeks, and put it in the boy's hand.

Sazare looked at it.

"Tenzō-san, this is—"

"It's a button," said Tenzō. "It's badly made. It sits proud, the
holes are off square, and it's caught on things for eighteen years
because I never thinned the back and I never fixed it because
fixing it would have been a second go and I only ever wanted the
one."

"It is the only object on you that you chose."

"You worked that out in ninety seconds in the eighth month with a
cup of tea in your hand and you didn't say it, and I have thought
about that about once a week ever since." He put his hands in his
pockets. "You just refused the thing that was put in me. So you can
have the thing I made. It's the only part of that arrangement I've
got any standing to give away."

"You'll—" The boy stopped. He was looking at the gap in the row of
horn, five stamped, one absent, third down on the left, where
anybody in the world who knew how to look would see it inside a
second. "People will notice."

"Yes," said Tenzō. "That's the other half of it, and I'd rather you
didn't make me say the sentence out loud."

He went back to his eight feet and picked up the manifest.

Behind him he heard the boy stand there for a moment, and then he
heard the small sound of a coat being opened — not the shelf, he
thought, filing it; the coat; the place where the load-bearing
things live — and then footsteps going away toward the bench.

*Item,* the file received that night, in the bare room, with the
lamp low.

*Subject declined a thing he wanted. Reported the decision to its
object. Then asked anyway when invited, at full price, knowing the
answer.*

*Item: the button is gone off the jacket and I do not appear to
mind, which I have checked twice.*

*Item: the making part is not in danger. The making part has a yard
in it now.*

*Interpretation: withheld.*

*Interpretation: I was in the room.*
