# Chapter 53, Scene 2 — Nothing To Bring

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the yard and the tower stair. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,108 words.*

---

What did it in the end was the bench.

He went to the yard on the tenth at about seven, the way he had gone to
the yard at about seven for a hundred and twelve consecutive evenings,
and he had his coat off and the lamp lit and his hand on the drawer
before he understood that there was nothing in the drawer and nothing on
the felt and nothing to do.

He stood there for a while.

*Two hundred and fifty-one hours. Twenty-two weeks. Four to six months
costed in the fifth month on a sheet I still have, and one week over.*

*And it went out of that gate on the eighth in a cloth and there is
nothing on this bench.*

*I have not had an empty evening since the thirty-first of the fifth
month.*

He sat on the stool for about eleven minutes and did not light the
second lamp.

Then he put his coat back on and blew the first one out and went home,
and did not sleep particularly, and on the eleventh he worked a shift
and at twenty past four in the afternoon he walked up the hill.

---

He wanted it on the record that he had chosen the hour.

*Four twenty in the afternoon. Daylight. A working day, on my own feet,
having decided at about eleven this morning and having spent five hours
not changing my mind.*

*Every other time I have gone up this hill outside an appointment it has
been at night and it has been because something happened. The
fifteenth of the eighth month I do not count, because I did not choose
any part of that and I have four pages establishing that I did not.*

*This is the first time I have gone up here on purpose, in daylight,
with nothing in my hands.*

He had checked that four times and it was true. No folder. No article.
No protocol, no returns, no requisition, no chit, no notebook, and the
last of those had cost him about nine minutes at the low table and he
had put it in the drawer and shut the drawer and gone.

*I have made ninety visits to that building in three years and I have
counted them and every single one had a thing in it.*

*Even the four minutes on the seventeenth of the eighth month had a
thing in it — I went up to ask for a summon and I arrived with a
sentence I had rehearsed on the stair.*

*Today I have got a fault report with nothing in the second column.*

---

Shizune was on the outside stair with four folders.

She saw him at about twenty feet and she did not stop this time. She
came down another three steps and then stood on the fourth from the
bottom, which put her above him, and looked at him for about a second
and a half.

"You've nothing with you," she said.

"No."

"Nothing at all?"

"No."

Shizune moved the folders to the other arm.

"Twenty-one days," she said.

"Twenty-one."

"I have been at that desk for every one of them and I want you to know
that I am not going to say anything about any of it, and that I have
had four opportunities and have taken none of them, and that I have
been rather proud of myself and it has got extremely tiring."

"...I'm sorry."

"Don't be sorry at me, it makes it worse." She came down the last three
steps. "She's in. There's nothing on until six and there's nobody with
her, and I am about to walk down this hill with four folders and I am
going to walk *slowly*, and I want that noted as the entire content of
my assistance and not as a hint."

"Understood."

"And Sazare-kun." She was past him. "If you have gone all this way with
nothing in your hands, then whatever it is, do not put anything on that
desk when you get in there. Not a slip, not a figure, not a *sheet of
paper.*"

"Why?"

"Because I have been watching the two of you work at that desk for two
years," said Shizune, "and there has been an object on it every single
time, and I have thought about that a great deal more than is any of my
business."

---

He went up the outside stair.

*Second floor. The door on the left at the end. Ninety times.*

He got to the top and stopped, which he had never done, and stood on the
landing for perhaps twenty seconds with his hand not on anything.

*Right.*

*I have got no plan and I want that recorded as a decision and not as a
failure of preparation, because I had four hours this morning and I
could have built one.*

*Every plan I have ever taken through a door has been a document, and
the two occasions in the last year on which anything actually happened
were a gateway on the fourteenth and a yard at two in the morning, and I
had nothing constructed for either.*

*So: no opening. No order. No first sentence.*

*I am going to go in and say the true thing and it will come out badly.*

He knocked.

---

"Come."

He went in.

She was at the desk with the short stack at her right hand and the
green haori over the chair, and the eleventh month had come in properly
and the window was shut for the first time since the spring, and there
was a brazier going in the corner that had not been there in the eighth
month.

She looked up.

He watched her do the arithmetic — the hour, the day, the absence of
anything under his arm — and it took her about a second and a half,
which was the fastest he had ever seen her do anything.

She put the pen down.

"You've nothing with you," she said.

"No."

"Is somebody dying?"

"No."

"Is it the correspondence, the wood, the yard, the study, the surplus,
the notice, the seal office, or the hand?"

"No."

Tsunade sat back.

She looked at him for a moment, and then she reached over and moved the
short stack from the right-hand corner of the desk to the floor beside
her chair, which took about two seconds and which she did without any
particular ceremony, and which he had seen her do once before in his
life, in the third month, at about eight in the evening, with eleven
pages in his hand.

"Sit down," she said.
