# Chapter 68, Scene 1 — Allowed To Be No

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the north fields. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1350 words.*

---

The first morning began with paperwork, which he had not expected and
should have.

"Terms," said Jiraiya, in the frost, with his coat off and his breath
standing. "Same as the twentieth, because they worked. Everything out
here is consented to in advance and out loud. Fukasaku-sama witnesses.
You can stop anything by saying stop. I can stop anything by saying
stop. Nothing goes to the head that anybody has a choice about, and
nothing goes in the book—" he pointed at the coat where the notebook
lived, unerringly, first try— "until the walk home. I want your
attention out here, not your penmanship."

"Agreed."

"Sensible boy," said Fukasaku, from the folded cloth. It was the
first thing the toad had said since the gate. His voice was old gravel
and entirely comfortable, and he sat with his arms folded in the cold
like a stone that had opinions.

"Then the first exercise," said Jiraiya, and spread his arms out from
his sides, open, empty, the wooden sword left standing in the ground.
"Hit me."

Sazare looked at him.

"Hit me," said Jiraiya again, conversational. "Anywhere you like.
Hard as you like. I'm fifty-four and I've been hit by everything on
two legs and several things on four, and I am not going to block,
because blocking you at your current speed would be showing off.
There is no trick. There is no lesson hiding behind the lesson. The
whole of the exercise is this: I want you to decide to hurt me, and
then do it, and the answer—" he said it exactly as he had said it
behind the bathhouse— "is allowed to be no."

---

He stood in the frost with the wooden sword in his hand and ran the
derivation, because that was what he had instead of instincts.

*The frame is sound. Consent given, witness present, a medic
reachable, a man who has weathered four wars inviting a training
blow from a novice. Physical risk to him: effectively nil. He can
take anything I can deliver. The mathematics could not be safer if
it were printed.*

*So there is no reason arriving from the mathematics. Proceed to
the act itself.*

*Lift the sword. Choose a line — the shoulder, padded, meaty,
low-consequence. Load the step. And now the last component: the
decision. Not the motion — I have the motion, Sakai's eleven
seconds live in my legs whether I like it or not. The decision.
Choose, coldly, with nothing at stake and nobody in danger, to put
pain into a person.*

He stood there for a long time. The frost ticked in the grass.
Somewhere behind the hedge a crow had opinions about all of it.

The component did not arrive.

He went looking for it properly, the way he went looking for a
fault in a casting — worked inward along every approach he had.
Duty: absent; no one was in danger. Anger: absent; he had none in
stock for this man. The twentieth: he reached for whatever had put
a growing article through Naruto's shin, and found the truth he
already had in the minute — that had not been a decision. It had
been a side effect, a reflex wearing his hands, arrived and
regretted in the same second. There was no lever there to pull on
purpose. There was no lever anywhere.

"No," he said.

The word came out level and it cost more than anything level had
cost him in months.

"Say the whole finding," said Jiraiya, who had not moved.

"I cannot locate the decision." He kept the register, because the
register was how he kept anything. "The motion is available. The
frame is safe. And the component that chooses harm, cold — I have
gone in along six approaches and it is not that it refuses. It is
not *there*. I have never once used it, and I do not know if that
means it is untrained or absent, and I am aware that the difference
is the whole of my future in this trade."

The field was quiet.

"Right," said Jiraiya, and clapped his hands together once, briskly,
like a man who had just had a delivery arrive on schedule. "Day one,
first exercise, complete. Write that down on the walk home exactly
as you said it, because you said it perfectly."

"...Complete."

"Kid." Jiraiya picked up the standing sword and tossed it end over
end and caught it, easy, thinking. "On the twentieth of last month
you put a thing through a friend's leg without deciding to, and it
has been sitting in you since — don't argue, I read the incident
report and I've met you. The nightmare underneath is: what am I,
if that can happen without me choosing it. And the industry
standard answer, the one every academy on the continent runs on, is
to drill boys until the choosing stops being felt. Make it
procedural. Sand the decision down until it's a twitch." He speared
the sword back into the frozen ground. "I am not doing that to you.
Not to you specifically — a man whose whole craft is knowing
exactly what every act costs is not a man you anaesthetise; you'd
be sanding down the best instrument in the village to fix a
splinter. So we do it the long way. You are going to learn to find
the decision, hold it, look at the whole price the way you look at
every price — and then choose. Both directions. A yes that is
really yours. A no that is really yours. Today you found out the
no exists and works under pressure with a legend standing in front
of you being extremely charismatic about it. That is not the
failure, you enormous ledger. That is the baseline, and it is a
better baseline than nine-tenths of the shinobi I have served
with, most of whom never once in their lives got to *decide*
anything — the war always decided first, and they spent their
forties finding out what that costs."

Sazare stood in the frost and felt something he had no immediate
name for, and worked at it, and found it, and it was his shoulders,
coming down from somewhere they had apparently been living since
the gate.

*A baseline,* he thought. *He gave it to me as a baseline.*

*He has read the file. He knows baselines are my native language,
and he translated the worst morning of the year into it on the
spot, standing in a frozen field, without notes.*

*I have had four teachers. Sakai taught me to leave. Tōan taught me
to stay. Urushibara taught me to see. I have been wondering for a
fortnight what the fourth one teaches.*

*He teaches deciding. Of course he does. He has been advertising it
since the roof — I would rather you had the problem than the
answer. It was never about fighting.*

---

The rest of the morning was the body, and the body was arithmetic,
and the arithmetic was insulting, and he had expected that.

Footwork on frozen ground until the frozen ground stopped being a
fact he needed to think about. The two-feet band — *somebody inside
your arms wants your balance, not your blood; you have a knee and a
skull and eleven dirty tricks and we will get to all of them* — at
quarter speed, then half. Fukasaku watching the whole of it with
his arms folded, saying nothing, in the manner of an examiner from
a different discipline auditing a rival school.

At the end of it Jiraiya looked at the sky, called it noon minus a
quarter, and sent him off with one instruction:

"Eat whatever she puts in front of you, let her look at the
bruises, and don't uncap anything tonight. Tomorrow morning,
Fukasaku-sama talks, you listen, and *then* — " the grin arrived,
enormous, warming the field by a measurable increment — "then you
can show the class what's under that rule in your book. I've been
watching you not look at it for three hours. It's been like
training next to a man with a biscuit in his pocket."
