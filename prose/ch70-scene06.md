# Chapter 70, Scene 6 — Eight Feet

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Tenzō, age
twenty-seven. POV: Tenzō. ~1090 words.*

---

Tenzō had been watching the machine run hot for eleven days, from
eight feet, which was the correct distance for watching and the
wrong one for everything else the arrangement permitted him,
which was nothing.

The pattern file had grown a section. He kept it the way he kept
all of them, dateless facts in careful order, interpretation
withheld, because nine years in the shop had taught him that the
watcher who interprets soon watches nothing — but a file did not
need interpreting to have a shape, and this one's shape had been
legible for a week to anybody who had spent a childhood being
trained to read exhaustion on assets before the assets knew.

*Item: the subject now sleeps at the bench. Brief — four to
eleven minutes, seated, tool in hand, self-terminating. First
observed the third. Nightly since the eighth. He logs everything
and does not log these, which means he does not know.*

*Item: production quality unchanged. Production pace unchanged.
The work does not know either. The work is the last to know;
that was true in the shop as well.*

*Item: the second-floor lamp. Burning past midnight, eleven
nights of eleven. The subject walks beneath it each evening and
his head does not lift. In week one his head lifted every night.
He does not know that either.*

*Item: the Friday sessions with the slug have shortened. The
tea is made and half drunk. The market report — he used to
bring her prices from anywhere he had been; the slug waited for
them the way garrisons wait for post. None since the first of
the month.*

*Interpretation: withheld.*

*Notation, permitted because it is operational: assets in this
condition do not fail at the bench. The bench is over-learned;
the bench fails last. They fail at the account nobody put a
sentry on.*

He read that last line back, sitting in his bare room with the
lamp low, and looked at it for a while, and did not cross it
out, and did not send it anywhere, because there was nowhere it
went. The remit was three lines and none of them covered this.
The attachment was not able to prevent. It was able to refuse —
and there was nothing here to refuse. Every article the subject
grew was clean, considered, correct. A man could not lawfully
refuse a live working on the grounds that the operator had
stopped noticing lamplight.

But the shop had taught two trades, and only one of them was
watching.

The other one was the quiet arrangement of a perimeter, and a
perimeter, Tenzō had found across nine years of standing on
them, could be a wide and generous thing if the man holding it
chose to read his orders that way.

So the kettle in the yard was always at temperature now for the
afternoon's second hour, which was when the crash came, because
a man who drinks something warm at the right minute sits down
to do it, and a man who sits down lets the four-to-eleven
minutes happen in a chair instead of over a live article. This
required nothing but attention to the brazier, which stood
within the eight feet, and was therefore compliant.

The heavy crates of second-run blanks had begun, without
ceremony or comment, to be already at the bench end of the yard
when the subject arrived each afternoon, rather than at the
gate end where the runners left them. Nobody had moved them.
They were simply always there, the way weather is there.
Compliant.

And on the worst evening — the Tuesday, when the subject fell
asleep writing and woke at the wrong angle with the lamp
burned low and looked, for four seconds, like a man surfacing
with no idea which lodging of eleven he was in — there had
been, on the corner of the bench, a paper bag of roasted
chestnuts, still warm, and eight feet away a mild man reading
a supply manifest with total attention, and no conversation
about it whatsoever, then or since. Compliant.

*The button,* Tenzō had thought, walking home that night,
which was as close as the file ever came to interpretation.
*He asked me once why I kept it — hand-cut, wrong, two years
of catching on things — and I never answered, because the
answer was not for the arrangement. The answer is: the shop
took everything a person is and kept the parts that watch.
The button is the one part that makes. You keep the making
parts alive however small, or the watching eats you down to
the lens.*

*His making parts are not in danger. His whole day is making.
What is burning down in there is the OTHER small thing — the
part that gets kept alive for no operational reason, the
button-sized account, the one the second-floor lamp is also,
if I permitted myself interpretation, which I do not, waiting
up for.*

*Notation closed. Chestnuts, further, as indicated. It is not
much of a perimeter against this class of threat.*

*It is what eight feet allows, and eight feet was the correct
distance, and he chose it himself, on the first day, so that
he could not pretend I was furniture.*

*I am not furniture. Noted, insufficient data, kept.*

---

On the Friday — the day the yard's own ledgers would later
show the subject sat down at four with the slug and opened,
in his own words, an incident — Tenzō was at his eight feet
as always, and watched the audit happen the way you watch a
line finally go taut: the page, the stillness, the pen
stopping, the long minute in which the finest observational
instrument in the Land of Fire turned itself, at last, on
its own accounts.

He could not hear the words from the perimeter. He did not
need to. He had seen men discover deficits before — in the
shop the discovery had a choreography, and it ended badly,
in confiscation and worse — and he stood his post in the
frost-lit yard and watched this one end instead with a slug
saying something measured, and a boy writing something
fast, and the whole bearing of the machine changing in the
space of one page from *ground down* to *loaded* — the
specific posture, Tenzō thought, filing it under facts, of
a fabricator who has located the fault, priced the repair,
and already started drawing.

The chestnut bag went home empty that night.

The pattern file received one line, dateless, factual,
compliant:

*Item: the account has a sentry now. Watch reduced
accordingly. Kettle protocol remains in force.*
