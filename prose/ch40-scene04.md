# Chapter 40, Scene 4 — Three Questions

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,139 words.*

---

He found the gap doing an audit, which was the only way he ever found
anything.

It was the last night of the second month and he was closing the year's
ledgers the way he closed everything — reconciling the birthday page
against the people page, because chapter six had produced a list and
chapter three had produced a different list and he had never checked
them against each other.

Eleven names on the birthday page.

Handa, second of the third. Miyuki, fourth of the second. Kiyomi,
twenty-third of the eleventh. Tarou, sixth of the first. Ashida,
nineteenth of the first. Emiko, twelfth of the tenth. Kanzaki,
thirtieth of the sixth. Ōtaki Sue. O-Shige. Nemoto, and Nemoto's wife,
who had been dead for eleven years and whose date he had been given
unprompted on a Tuesday.

Yagi.

Eleven.

The people page had twelve.

---

He looked at that for some time and then went and found the third
ledger, the one with no unit at the head of the second column, and went
through it, and found the second gap in about four minutes and the
third in about nine.

*Gap 1. I do not know her birthday.*

*I asked eleven people over a fortnight. I asked Nemoto twice. I have
the date of a woman who died in the year I was seven because her
husband decided on a Tuesday that he wanted somebody to have it.*

*I did not ask her.*

*Gap 2. On the fourth Thursday of last year she asked me what I eat
when nobody is watching. I could not answer it and she made me drink
two for a pass.*

*I never asked her back.*

*That is a game with a stated rule — one question, one shot, both
directions — and I have played it four times since and I have asked her
about a callus, about the Council, about what she gambles on, about her
grandfather, and about whether being handed a name ever helped, and I
have never once asked her a question about an ordinary day.*

*Gap 3. There are two bells on that necklace that do not ring and have
never rung and nobody has ever explained. I found them in the eleventh
month. I had a pencil out.*

*She said: you may not take my necklace apart in a bar. I put the pencil
away.*

*I have sat opposite her approximately eighteen times since and I have
not raised it once.*

---

He sat back from the bench.

The obvious explanation was courtesy and he dismissed it in about four
seconds, because it did not survive contact with the evidence. He had
asked Yagi about a hole in her ear. He had asked Anko about the repair
under the arm of her dead mother's coat. He had asked Ochiai his name
and Nemoto about a lathe and Miyuki whether her husband had been bad at
being his friend, and he had asked every one of those knowing perfectly
well that the answer might cost the person something.

He was not being delicate. He had never been delicate in his life.

The second explanation took him about twenty minutes and it was the
right one and he did not like it at all.

*Asking is a procedure.*

*I did not have it. I got it out of a book on the bottom of the second
bay and it is chapter three and it is four pages long, and I have been
running it deliberately, as a drill, on a list, since the ninth month —
and every single question I have asked anybody in six months has been
the drill.*

*And a drill is a thing you run at a problem.*

*Yagi was a problem. Two years of a fact in a file I could not open.
Ochiai was a problem. Ashida was four hundred observations and no
wants. Every one of the eleven was a person I could not read, and the
procedure exists to open people I cannot read.*

*She has never been a problem.*

*She talks. She has talked to me for fourteen months without any
technique being applied to her whatsoever, and I have never had to
extract one single thing, and the drill therefore never ran, because
the drill runs on obstruction and there was none.*

He wrote the finding out and then wrote the cost underneath it, because
the cost was the part that mattered.

*Consequence: the person I know least about in this village is the
person I spend the most time with, and I did not notice for fourteen
months, and the reason I did not notice is that she has been doing all
of the asking.*

*Every one of the hard questions in that room has been hers.*

*What do you eat. What did you want to be before. Have you ever been in
love. Why don't you look at me. What did you want them to say.*

*I have answered every single one of those, including two I would not
have answered anybody else alive, and I have never asked her when she
was born.*

---

The last part he did not write for about an hour, and when he wrote it
he wrote it slowly.

*There is a version of this that is worse and I have been avoiding it
for the whole of the last hour by doing arithmetic.*

*The drill runs on obstruction.*

*She is not unobstructed. That is the error. She is extremely
obstructed and she has managed it so well for fourteen months that I —
who read a man's hip off the wear on a boot at eleven feet, who caught
a hospital's entire bed state from a stairwell with my back turned —
have registered her as a person with nothing in the way.*

*She talks constantly and she has told me almost nothing.*

*I know four facts about her past. A callus. A necklace she will not
have the mount taken off. A grandfather she has never used to win an
argument. And that four funerals ago somebody told her he would have
been proud and she wanted to put them through a wall.*

*Four facts. Fourteen months.*

*I have more than that on the auxiliary who has been on my floor for
three weeks.*

He put the pencil down.

*She is not open. She is* fluent.

*And fluency is what obstruction looks like when the person managing it
is very, very good at it, and I have spent my entire life doing exactly
the same thing in a different register and I did not recognise it
because mine is silence and hers is talk.*

He looked at that sentence for a long time.

Then he wrote, underneath it, in the flat hand:

*Do not do anything with this yet.*
