# Chapter 66, Scene 8 — The House Wins

*Act 5, part A. Tarugi, the room over the garden. Tsunade, age
fifty-three. POV: Tsunade. ~1330 words.*

---

She woke at the hour the tower had trained into her, the dead hour,
the one where the reports used to be, and lay still in a warm dark
that contained no reports of any kind, and took the inventory.

Snow on the window ledge, first of the year, an inch and building.
A boy asleep on his back beside her with her own hands, she observed,
already at their nightly burglary, both of them full of his shirt.
She considered releasing it, the way she considered it most nights,
and did not, the way she did not most nights, on the grounds that a
woman should be honest with somebody about wanting to hold on, and
her hands had volunteered for the posting before the rest of her had
finished the paperwork, and seniority counted for something.

The audit, then. She ran days the way he ran castings now. It was
catching. Everything about him was catching; she had a list.

*Item. The ferry, where he confessed to eleven seams and I confessed
to a shoulder, and we built the day's one law out of the two
confessions. Through and out. It held all day. Neither of us thought
it would hold past noon.*

*Item. The hat, which was the best three hundred and forty ryō the
programme never spent. Eleven years I've watched that boy price the
world and today I finally got to watch him do it for the pure joy of
the numbers, in a market lane, to an audience, wearing the result.
The stall man offered him a job. If the village ever falls through,
he has a future insulting silk.*

*Item. The slab.*

She let that one sit a moment, because it had been, by a distance,
the day's biggest finding, and she had nearly missed collecting it.

*I have watched him work for three years. Wards, theatres, the yard,
a training ground, my own hand. I have never once watched him work on
something that didn't matter — no patient under it, no programme over
it, no clock, no docket, no me needing him to succeed. Just an old
woman's luck and a lamp. And it turns out that with everything
stripped off, all of it, every reason — the work is the same. Exactly
the same. The same care at four minutes for a wall charm as for a
Hokage's hand.*

*Which means the care was never for the dockets.*

*I knew that. I'd written it down, even. There's a difference between
knowing a thing and holding the lamp for it.*

---

The pinwheel was in her bag, and her bag was by the wall, and she had
checked it was still there twice before the lamp went out, which was
one check for a thing and one check for a superstition, and she was
too old a gambler to pretend she didn't know which was which.

*Say it, then,* she thought, at the ceiling of the good room. *Say it
where nobody has to watch your face while you do.*

*I have lost at cards, dice, cups, wheels, lots, sticks, and one
memorable evening at go, for thirty-odd years, with a consistency
that beats most shinobi records. I have lost so reliably that the
losing became a joke, and I let it be a joke, because the joke was a
wall with a door in it that nobody ever opened, and behind the door
is the arithmetic: the only times the luck ever ran my way, the bill
went to somebody I loved. I won a bet the day before Nawaki drew his
route. I had a streak — an actual streak, four nights — the week
before Dan filed his transfer. The necklace came back to me twice.
I gave it away to a boy I believed in precisely because the last two
people who wore it paid my winnings for me, and if that is not a
confession of faith in the curse then there has never been one.*

*And today, on the first day in three years with nothing in it, on a
five-ryō cup game, for a paper pinwheel, in front of him — it landed
clean.*

*Four seconds. That's what I gave it. I counted them going past and I
knew he was counting them too, because he counts everything, and he
filed it and didn't ask, because he keeps a list of things that are
mine and not offered, and I know the list exists and it is the most
courteous document nobody has ever written.*

She turned her head and looked at the shape of him in the snow-light.

*Here is what I decided in second three, for the record I don't keep:
the curse can have the pinwheel. It was a five-ryō pot in a lake
town; even my luck can't leverage that. And if the universe is
running a book on me again — if the house wants to flag that I have,
against thirty years of published form, started winning — then the
house has finally noticed what I noticed in October, which is that
the stake changed. Fine. Noted. I am not going to stand at that
window.*

*I am not giving him up to a superstition. I buried the superstition
with better men than it. He mended a luck charm tonight in a
corridor, four minutes, free, and if luck is a thing that can be
mended then it can bloody well stay mended, and if it isn't, then a
pinwheel is a pinwheel is a pinwheel.*

*Besides.* Her thumb moved once over the knuckles of her own
trespassing hand where it gripped his shirt. *I've seen what happens
now when something tries to collect on him. It gets forty seconds,
and then the operator arrests it, and there is an incident report, in
my hand, in the file.*

---

The window was four steps away, and at some point between the
pinwheel and the snow she found she had sat up an inch toward it,
the old vector, the tower vector — get up, stand at the glass, watch
the village not need you while you fail to sleep — and she caught the
motion in the dark the way you catch a pickpocket, by the wrist, and
put it back down where it came from.

*No. That's the other habit, and Jiraiya's already named it, the
enormous gossiping wreck, and I am not giving him the satisfaction of
being right at this range. Deployment lists go up in spring. His name
goes on one eventually — has to; the whole point of Monday is making
that survivable, and I signed Monday myself, twice, once as a Hokage
and once as whatever the second signature was. Between now and spring
there is a window in the tower I will stand at plenty.*

*Not this one. This one's got snow on it and a garden under it and
nothing out there needs me till Friday.*

She lay back down. The boy slept the way he did everything, precisely
and without waste; somewhere in the eaves the snow made the sound
snow makes, which is the sound of a thing being taken care of.

*Monday the thirteenth, then. Four mornings a week, a field, and that
old fool is going to walk him out at dawn and start taking the creed
apart, bolt by bolt, and put something survivable where it stood, and
the boy is going to hate about a third of it and be flawless at all
of it and come home each noon with a new bruise and a new number.
And I get the noons. That's the arrangement I've made with the
spring, and the spring doesn't get a vote.*

*Paid in full,* she thought, drifting, her hands holding their
handhold, the seller confirming, one last time, in the small print
under sleep, that she had been overpaid — *received, one holiday.
One boy who reads walls. One hat.*

*The house wins. Let it. I'm keeping the pinwheel.*
