# Chapter 67, Scene 2 — What Things Cost

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~990 words.*

---

Katsuyu arrived at four on the Friday and settled onto her dressed
stone with the air of a creature attending the reading of a will in
her favour.

"You owe me a market," she said.

"I owe you a market."

He delivered it standing, from memory, because the terms of the day
had kept the notebook shut and so the whole of Tarugi's winter lane
existed nowhere in the world except behind his eyes, which made the
report, by his own reckoning, the single most perishable document he
had ever held custody of.

Dried gourd, forty a bundle, marked down from sixty and the sign
re-inked once, so the price had sat unmet for at least a fortnight.
Charcoal two-twenty firm, no give, the only unnegotiable in the lane,
because cold is not a negotiation. Pickled plums in fours and nines —
and he gave her the nines whole, the carrier's levy, the weight
threshold, the decade-old fine he had deduced in the jars' geometry,
and Katsuyu made the small sound she made when data exceeded its
container, which was the entire salary he drew from any of this.

"Winter pears at eleven the piece, twelve for a hundred and twenty,
which nobody in a town of eleven hundred buys, so the dozen price is
not a price, it is an advertisement of confidence. A felt hat, asked
nine hundred." He paused. "Sold at three hundred and forty."

"To whom?"

"To her. For me. I am wearing it."

"I had noted the hat," said Katsuyu, with the courtesy of a being who
had been looking at the hat for six minutes. "The market did that?"

"The market did that."

"Then I revise my valuation of markets upward," she said, "and I
will have the rest, please, including the parts you have decided are
not prices."

---

He gave her the rest — the cups game at five ryō, the paper prizes,
the ferry at three a crossing and free for the under-fives — and
then, because it was Katsuyu, and because fifteen Fridays had built
the one room in his week where a thing could be said before it was
load-bearing, he gave her the parts that were not prices.

"She laughed nine times on the eighth. I have the count. I did not
mean to have the count, but I have never once succeeded in not
counting, so I have it, and I have done arithmetic on it that I am
not proud of." He turned the kettle a quarter on its trivet, which
he had learned from watching Tenzō do it to a teacup, which was a
thing he had decided not to examine. "Nine in a day. Against a
baseline, since the eleventh of the eleventh, of — call it one and a
half a day, and most of those at the expense of some third party's
paperwork. The eighth was six times baseline. The eighth was the
best data I have ever collected, and every point of it was an
accident, and that is the finding. I have never made her laugh on
purpose. I have never done any of the small things on purpose. I
have been present, at increasingly close range, while she did them."

Katsuyu was quiet for a moment.

"You have decided to court her," she said. "Retroactively. Against
a completed conquest. You are aware that this is the wrong order?"

"Everything about me is in the wrong order. Jiraiya-sama has it in
writing."

"Mm," said Katsuyu, who did not disagree with things that were true.
"And your method?"

"Under development."

"Which means there is a literature and you are embarrassed by it."

The yard, which had heard the governor drafted and the chain designed
and a man decline a scalpel, absorbed this as well, because the yard
was where the true sentences happened and had no standards left.

"There is a literature," said Sazare, "of one volume, and its author
is a great man in a different field, and I am reading it the way I
read everything, which is for the load paths, and I am telling you
this because on the day it goes wrong I want one witness who knew the
engineering intent."

"Minuted," said Katsuyu, in Shizune's exact administrative flat,
which she had been practising for weeks and deployed now with the
timing of a creature who knew precisely what she was doing, and the
laugh got him before the guard did — out loud, unbudgeted, alone in
a yard with a slug at four in the afternoon.

"Six times baseline," said Katsuyu, watching him with all the
patience of her four hundred years. "You see. It is not a rare
event. It only wants somebody standing where it lands."

---

The capped question rode the whole session in the notebook, ruled
shut, and at the gate hour she looked at the line above the rule the
way she looked at everything, once, completely.

"Monday," she said. "Not before. You gave me the market instead of
the fuse and I am pleased with the trade. And Sazare—" she attained
her footnote register— "when you attempt your literature, whatever
it is: she has read everything, forgotten nothing, and waited three
years. You cannot surprise her with competence. Do not try. The
material she has no defence against is the attempt itself — a thing
tried sincerely, slightly wrong, by a man who has clearly practised.
I have watched her for fifteen Fridays too, you understand. You were
not always the only subject in the yard."

He stood at the gate for some time after she had gone, holding the
kettle, doing the thing he did with sentences that arrived heavier
than their word count.

*The attempt itself. Tried sincerely, slightly wrong, clearly
practised.*

*I can do slightly wrong,* he thought. *I have never once in my life
failed to do slightly wrong.*

*It is the first specification I have ever been handed that I meet
before starting.*
