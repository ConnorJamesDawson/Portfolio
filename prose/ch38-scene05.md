# Chapter 38, Scene 5 — Half a Second

*Act 4, part D. Konoha. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare.
~1,116 words.*

---

Chapter five defeated him for five weeks and the humiliating part was
the arithmetic.

*Duration of the act: approximately half a second.*

*Required equipment: none.*

*Required skill: none that I can identify.*

*Number of successful attempts in five weeks: nil.*

He wrote that out on the ninth attempt and looked at it and then wrote,
underneath it, *forty-one dressings today, two of them on faces*, and
then put the pencil down, because setting the two facts beside each
other did not produce an insight, it produced a noise.

---

He tried to do it to Ashida four times.

The first was at the gate on a Tuesday and he got as far as lifting his
hand about two inches and turned the movement into checking the strap
on his own bag, which Ashida did not notice and which Sazare thought
about for the rest of the evening.

The second was on the day Ashida told him Tarou had been up all night
with a chest and had come to work grey and had said *he's fine, he's
fine, they're always fine*, and Sazare had stood four feet away with
his arms at his sides and had said four accurate and useful things
about infant respiration and had watched Ashida be comforted by
approximately none of them.

The third and fourth were not really attempts. He counted them
anyway, because an honest ledger counts the ones you talked yourself
out of.

---

The obstacle, when he finally isolated it, was not what he expected.

He had assumed it was fear of the response — that the hand would land
and the person would flinch or freeze or ask what he was doing, and
that he would then be standing in a corridor with his hand on somebody
who did not want it there.

It was not that.

He established this by running the thing he was good at, which was
taking a problem apart, and what he found underneath was:

*I do not know what it means.*

*That is the whole of the obstacle and it is not an emotional
difficulty, it is a translation one.*

*When I put my hands on a patient it means: I am about to move you, or
I am counting something, or hold still. It is a sentence with one
meaning and no ambiguity and the patient understands it because there
are only about four things it can be.*

*A hand on a friend's shoulder is a word in a language I do not speak,
and it means a different thing depending on the hour and the room and
what has just been said and what the two of you were doing last week,
and everybody else appears to conjugate it without thinking.*

*I have watched Emiko do it. She did it to me in the eighth month over
a brass hook and it meant approximately nine things at once and I have
still not finished unpacking it.*

*I cannot use a word I cannot conjugate. That is not cowardice. That is
the same rule that stops me putting an unaudited piece of crystal in
somebody's arm.*

He read that back and knew it was true.

He also knew, within about four seconds, that it was the fourth
technically accurate reason he had constructed this year for not doing
something, and that the previous three had all turned out to be
scaffolding.

---

Sakura caught him at it on a Friday, which was worse than anybody else
catching him at it, because Sakura had known him for eleven days and
had already started saying things to him that nobody else would risk.

They were in the stairwell. She had told him something about a case on
the fourth floor that had not gone the way she wanted, and had got to
the end of it, and had stopped, and there had been about a second and a
half in which the correct thing was obvious even to him.

He did not do it.

Sakura picked her tray up.

"You were going to do something."

"...No."

"You were. Your hand did a thing." She was not accusing him; she
sounded interested, which was worse. "It came about four inches off the
rail and then it went back."

"I was going to put a hand on your shoulder."

"Why didn't you?"

"Because I don't know what it means," said Sazare, "and I have
established that I will not use a word I can't conjugate, and I wrote
four hundred words about it on Wednesday and it was all accurate and I
am aware that it was also scaffolding."

Sakura considered this with an entirely straight face.

"It means *that sounds hard and I'm still here*," she said.

"That's one meaning."

"That's the *only* meaning, it's not a complicated word." She shifted
the tray. "You're thinking about it like it's the crystal. It isn't.
Nobody's grading it. If you get it wrong the worst thing that happens is
about four seconds of it being slightly odd and then we both go back to
work."

"Four seconds is a considerable amount of time."

"Four seconds is *nothing*, you told me you can grow a wall in one."

Sazare stood in a stairwell and had, for the first time in eleven days
of knowing her, absolutely nothing to say.

"Right," said Sakura, and went up the stairs.

---

He did not manage it before the ninth month ended.

He wrote it up on the last night of it, at the bench, in the flat hand,
and the entry was four lines long and he did not soften any of it:

*Chapter five. Five weeks. Nil.*

*Attempts: nine, of which four were genuine and five were talked out of
in advance. Longest hesitation: approximately one and a half seconds,
in a stairwell, with somebody who had just told me something difficult
and who noticed my hand move and asked me about it afterwards.*

*Constructed reason: I cannot use a word I cannot conjugate.*

*Assessment of constructed reason: it is true and it is also the fourth
of its kind this year and the previous three were all scaffolding
around a thing I did not want to look at.*

*I do not yet know what this one is scaffolding around. I have not been
able to find it.*

*Note. Sakura says the word has one meaning and that nobody is grading
it. She is fourteen and she is almost certainly right, and I notice that
I have not been able to make myself believe her, and I notice further
that I have never once in my life been able to believe that a thing was
not being graded.*
