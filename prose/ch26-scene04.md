# Chapter 26, Scene 4 — Waking

*Act 4, part B. Konoha General. Sazare Ruri, age sixteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1,516 words.*

---

Sazare woke because his left leg did not hurt correctly.

For nine years pain had occupied the outside of the knee. It appeared on
stairs, in cold weather, when he turned without lifting the foot and for the
first ten minutes after waking. He knew its boundaries well enough to move
around it before opening his eyes.

The pain waiting for him now was larger but central. It belonged to cut
tissue, swelling and a rigid splint from ankle to thigh. The old diagonal line
was absent.

He looked for it twice before understanding that absence was information. The
splint prevented most movement, but when he tightened the thigh the kneecap no
longer pulled towards the outside. Muscles that had spent years correcting the
joint contracted against nothing and trembled.

Somebody had altered the arrangement while he was unconscious.

His first response was anger. It arrived before gratitude and was easier to
identify: a cold inventory of consent not given, hands inside an old injury,
decisions made because he was unavailable to object. Then he bent the toes
again and felt the lower leg move in a straight line for the first time he
could remember.

The anger remained. Something else stood beside it.

He opened his eyes.

The men's-end ceiling had nineteen hairline cracks. Handa was asleep in bed
eleven. Morning light came through the window, and the white star in its lower
pane projected a small broken circle onto the floor.

Sazare moved his toes.

"Don't," Nurse Yagi said.

She sat beside the washstand writing observations on a board balanced across
her knees. Her sleeves were clean. There was dried blood under one thumbnail.

"Those aren't connected to the knee," Sazare said.

"Today they are."

"What day?"

"The next one."

He looked towards the window. Smoke remained above the eastern roofs in a low
grey sheet. The enormous shape was gone.

"The hospital?"

"Still here."

"The patients?"

"The eleven are still eleven. Handa complained throughout."

From bed eleven, without opening his eyes, Handa said, "The breakfast was
late."

"By how much?" Sazare asked.

"Four hours."

"That is late."

Yagi put the board down. "Before you begin inventorying the building: the
Third Hokage died yesterday. The invasion has been repelled. Casualty numbers
are incomplete. Several districts are still under emergency control. You are
under medical restriction and security observation, both of which mean you
remain in that bed."

Sazare took the information in the order given. The Third had signed the
ruling that allowed him to put medical chakra into another person's body. He
had monitored a file personally when doing so cost time and produced nothing
visible. He had spoken to Sazare twice.

"Who signs the forms?" Sazare asked.

Yagi's expression changed and then was put away. "At present, people with
delegated authority."

"All right."

"No, it isn't."

"No."

He put his right hand over his eyes. Chakra moved badly when he looked for it,
present in shallow pools rather than a continuous supply. Every pathway to the
hand felt scraped. The left was worse. He could find no connection to the
street from the bed.

"How many?" he asked.

Yagi knew which number. "Twenty-two confirmed dead in the approach attributed
primarily to crystal trauma. Five more probably shared with other defenders.
Fourteen wounded. Those numbers will change."

Twenty-two. He had counted twenty-four bodies and two moving.

"Names?" he asked.

"Enemy casualties are still being identified."

"The Konoha dead."

Yagi gave him the defender from the road first: Seki Tomonari, chūnin,
thirty-one, one daughter. The body beneath the exclusion had belonged to Ueda
Masao and had been alive when the field began. He remained alive now with a
spinal injury. The unarmed figure at the gate was a visiting merchant who had
picked up a fallen forehead protector to get through the road; Sazare had
counted him as enemy and the official count had not.

The correction reduced one number and made the arithmetic worse.

Yagi read until Sazare asked her to stop. There were too many names without
positions he could connect to them. He needed the road map and did not have
it. Without positions, they became deaths elsewhere in the village, and he had
no right to organise those for his own use.

"Konoha?"

"Not among those twenty-two. The Hyūga report says you excluded the hospital
side. One Konoha shinobi received a foot wound from crystal and is expected to
recover."

Expected. A hospital word meaning the ledger remained open.

"Ashida?"

"Concussion, twelve stitches and a broken collarbone. He has already attempted
to file your movement report from bed."

"Daichi?"

"Alive. Burns, blood loss, two fingers. Mika is uninjured apart from the
scarf. Ren lost his lower leg."

Sazare lowered his hand. "My knee."

"Reconstructed."

"The new damage?"

"All of it."

He looked at the splint, then at her.

"You could do that?"

Yagi stared at him.

"You work in a hospital."

"For four months."

"Long enough to have shown somebody the joint."

"It functioned."

"It failed on a roof."

"A building hit it first."

The treatment room returned to him in pieces: Yagi's hands, the ceiling moving
sideways, his own answer about chakra. Then nothing until the ward.

"Who repaired it?"

"Fujita, with me assisting."

"How long?"

"Forty-three minutes of operating time. More with interruptions."

"Material?"

"Three wire anchors removed, the old tendon graft retained, ligament repaired
and part of the patellar track resurfaced with medical chakra. No crystal,
before you ask."

"I wasn't going to."

"You were looking at your hands."

He had been considering whether a thin crystal guide could keep the new
alignment fixed during rehabilitation. He stopped considering it where she
could see.

"May I read the operative notes?"

"When you can remain conscious while sitting."

"Those conditions are unrelated."

"They have become related administratively."

"Buildings—" She stopped, closed her eyes briefly and began again. "Six weeks
before combat load. You will need rehabilitation because half the muscles in
that leg have spent nine years doing somebody else's work."

"I don't do combat."

The ward became quiet in the immediate area around the sentence.

News had reached the beds before he woke. Bed five looked over and then away.
The stonecutter at bed one had a folded newspaper beneath his good hand, its
front page showing the hospital gate behind a title too large to read upside
down. Somebody had drawn a circle around a figure on the roof.

"They've called you the Blue Wall," Handa said.

Sazare closed his eyes.

"It wasn't a wall."

"I told them that."

"Who?"

"Everybody. One reporter, two clerks, a woman looking for her son and the boy
from the kitchen yard."

"Why were they allowed in the ward?"

Yagi said, "They weren't."

"The reporter was very quick," Handa said.

"There will not be another one."

Sazare opened his eyes. "How did they get my name?"

Neither answered immediately.

The hospital had admitted one hundred and seventeen people through a road full
of his work. His name was on the roster, the audit forms, Ashida's reports and
every observation sheet he had signed for four months. Secrecy had not
survived because nobody betrayed it. It had simply become smaller than the
number of witnesses.

Handa opened his eyes.

Yagi picked up the observation board. "There are four people downstairs who
would like to discuss that."

"Now?"

"When you can sit without fainting."

"I can sit."

"You have been horizontal for twenty-nine hours."

"That doesn't prevent sitting."

"No. Blood pressure prevents sitting."

Yagi lowered the bed rail and showed him how to move the repaired leg: one
hand beneath the knee, one beneath the heel, lift as a single unit, no
rotation. He performed the sequence once on the mattress. The leg felt
borrowed. Its weight arrived in different muscles, and the kneecap tracked
through the centre instead of sliding towards the outside.

"Again," she said.

He did it again.

At the third repetition, the inner thigh cramped hard enough to pull his
breath short. Yagi waited without helping until it released.

"That muscle has been unemployed for nine years," she said. "It will object to
the new post."

"Can it be reassigned?"

"No."

"Then it can object while working."

Handa made a sound from bed eleven that might have been a laugh and became a
cough.

Yagi raised the head of the bed ten degrees. The room remained level. Ten
more. The ceiling cracks moved towards the edge of his vision. At thirty
degrees his hearing thinned and the small broken circle of light from the
window doubled.

"Still sitting?" she asked.

"Technically reclining."

"Good. Technical reclining is all you're cleared for."

She left him there for four minutes by the ward clock before lowering him
again. He spent the interval counting breaths and refusing to look at the
newspaper beneath the stonecutter's hand.

Sazare put both hands on the mattress and pushed himself upright.

He fainted before his elbows were straight.

The second time he woke, the head of the bed had been raised mechanically and
Yagi had written *not yet* on the plaster above it in blue pencil.
