# Chapter 39, Scene 4 — Six Tables

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, off the west end of the market row. Sazare
Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,476 words.*

---

He read the room before he read the people, which was the whole of what
happened to him that evening and everything after it was consequence.

He came in at eight minutes past the seventh hour on the tenth,
expecting Yagi and a corner table, with his coat still on and the cold
of the market row still in it, and the door swung to behind him and he
stopped four feet inside it.

*Six tables. All six occupied.*

*Nobody is facing the door.*

That was the first thing and it arrived before any of the faces did.
Every single table had been turned. Not moved — *turned*, so that the
long axis ran across the room rather than toward the entrance, and the
consequence of that was that of the eleven people in that room, not one
of them was sitting in a position from which they could look at
somebody coming in without deliberately turning their head.

*And nobody is standing up.*

*And nobody has stopped talking.*

Emiko was mid-sentence at the second table with her hands going. Handa
was in a chair with arms at the near end with his back three-quarters
to the door, arguing with Nemoto about something, with a stick hooked
over the chair back. O-Shige had the third table and was pouring. Anko
had her boots on the fourth and was being told about it by somebody.
Ōtaki Sue had the slate on the table in front of her and was writing on
it with enormous concentration. Kanzaki was at the fifth with her
glasses up on her forehead, reading the wine list as though it had been
badly compiled, which it had.

Sakura was at the sixth with Yagi.

Nobody turned round.

---

He stood four feet inside a doorway with his coat on and did the thing
he did, which was to take the room apart, and it took him about four
seconds and it went:

*This was arranged.*

*Not the people. The people are easy — eleven people who like me is a
fact I have had considerable trouble with for a year but it is not
difficult to arrange, you ask them and they come.*

*The tables were turned. Somebody moved six tables in a room that is
not theirs, and turned every one of them ninety degrees, and did it
before I arrived, and told eleven people not to stand up and not to
stop talking and not to look at the door.*

*Which means somebody knows.*

*Somebody in this village knows that I cannot walk into a room where
people are facing one thing.*

*It is not in my file. I have never said it out loud to anybody. It is
not in the Yamanaka report, because he went in looking for a handler
and came out talking about a splint. Ibiki does not have it. Kōji does
not have it. It is not written down in any building in the Land of
Fire.*

*It is in a crowd in the fog with a scaffold at the front of it and I
have not described that afternoon to one living person.*

*So nobody knows. Somebody* worked it out.

He was still standing in the doorway.

Yagi said, without getting up and without raising her voice, from the
sixth table, in exactly the tone she used to tell him a dressing was
due:

"Shut the door, you're letting the cold in."

---

He shut the door.

He got his coat off and did not remember doing it. Somebody took it —
Sakura, he established afterwards — and he sat down at the sixth table
because it was the nearest one and because Yagi kicked the chair out
about two inches with her foot without breaking off what she was saying
to Sakura, which was a story about a surgeon.

Nobody made a speech.

That was the second thing, and it went through him about a minute later
than the tables had, and it was worse.

There was no moment. Nobody stood up and got the room's attention.
Nobody said his name. There was no point in that evening at which
eleven faces turned toward him, and this was so plainly deliberate, and
so plainly *engineered* by somebody who had thought about it for
longer than four seconds, that he had to put his hands flat on the
table for a moment.

What there was instead was Handa shouting down the room at him about
dovetails.

"Oi. Ruri. Tell him."

"Tell him what?"

"That a through dovetail is not a lesser joint, it is an *honest*
joint, and that a man who puts a lapped dovetail on a tool chest is
hiding something."

"That's an aesthetic position and not a structural one."

"Say it again."

"A lapped dovetail is stronger in racking by about—"

"*Say it again*," said Handa, delighted, to Nemoto, "he's agreeing with
me and he doesn't know it."

---

Tsunade arrived at the eleventh hour and did not come in as the Hokage.

She came in in the plain coat, and the room did not stop — because the
room had been told not to, and because half the room had known her for
thirty years and the other half had been at the second table for three
hours by then — and she took the empty chair at the fifth table next to
Kanzaki, who did not look up from the wine list and said, "It's badly
compiled," and Tsunade said, "It's always been badly compiled," and
that was the whole of the arrival.

Sazare watched it from the sixth table.

*She was late on purpose.*

*She came in at the eleventh hour, into a room that had been running
for four hours, so that her arrival would not be an event either.*

*She has arranged an entire evening around not producing a single
moment in which anybody has to look at me, and she has arranged her own
entrance not to be one, and she has not spoken to me yet.*

He got up and went over.

She was already talking to Kanzaki about a supply figure and she did
not stop.

"Sit down," she said, without turning her head.

He sat down.

They talked about the supply figure for about eleven minutes, the three
of them, and it was a genuinely interesting problem and Sazare had two
useful things to say about it and said them both, and at no point did
anybody mention a birthday.

---

He got it out at about the ninth minute of the second hour, when
Kanzaki had gone to argue with the woman behind the counter about the
wine list.

"You turned the tables."

Tsunade turned her cup a quarter.

"The tables were like that when I got here."

"They were not. This room has six tables and they run along the long
axis, they have run along the long axis every single time I have been
in it in fourteen months, and tonight every one of them is across."

"Perhaps she rearranged."

"She has not rearranged that room in eleven years. There is a mark on
the floor by the third table where the leg has been standing since
before I came to this country." He kept his voice flat, because it was
the only way to get any of it out. "Somebody turned six tables ninety
degrees and told eleven people not to stand up."

Tsunade did not say anything.

"How did you know," said Sazare.

"Know what?"

"That I can't—" He stopped. He made himself say the actual words, in a
room, out loud, to a person, for the first time in six years. "That I
can't walk into a room where everybody is facing the same way. That it
has to be at an angle. That if eleven people had turned round when that
door opened I would have got about four feet and then I would have
found a reason to be somewhere else and I would have believed the
reason."

"You've never told anybody that."

"No."

"No," Tsunade agreed. "You haven't."

She drank.

"It's not in your file," she said. "I'd have found it. I read that file
four times a year and there is nothing in it about crowds, and Inoichi
wrote nine hundred words about the middle of you and not one about a
scaffold." She set the cup down. "You told me. Third Thursday of last
year. You were talking about a market at Kanezaki and you said —
entirely in passing, as a professional observation, in the middle of a
sentence about sightlines — *markets are fine, it's audiences.*"

Sazare sat with that.

"That's four words."

"That's four words," said Tsunade, "and I have been doing this for
thirty-five years, and four words is a great deal more than most people
give me."
