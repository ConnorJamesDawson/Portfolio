# Chapter 72, Scene 1 — Four Bowls

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, Naruto's apartment. Uzumaki Naruto, age
sixteen. POV: Naruto. ~910 words.*

---

Naruto cleaned the apartment three times, which was two and a
half more times than it had ever been cleaned for any reason,
and the third time was when he found out that cleaning was one
of those jobs that gets bigger while you do it, like weeding, or
apologising.

It wasn't that the place was dirty. It was that he'd never
looked at it with somebody else's eyes in it before, and
somebody else's eyes turned out to change what everything was.
The frog wallet on the shelf: fine. The plant, Ukki, doing
great, actually thriving, best thing in the room: fine. The
poster: fine, classic, staying. The stack of instant cups he
kept for emergencies even though he could cook now — proper
cook, Iruka-sensei had taught him the basics years ago and the
hospital cookbook had taught him the rest during the seven
weeks of the frame, when reading recipes he couldn't stand up
to make had been a specific new kind of torture: those he
shoved in a cupboard, and then took out again, because Sazare
would open the cupboard. He would definitely open the cupboard.
The guy read *rooms*. Sakura-chan said he'd read the whole
hospital off a corridor once.

Let him read the cups, then. The cups were true.

The actual problem was the table.

He stood in front of it for a while with his hands on his hips,
doing the arithmetic he'd been not-doing since Tuesday, when
he'd stood in the quartermaster's gate — cleared, stamped,
*cleared*, seven weeks of frame and crutches and the itch
inside the bone all signed off in one green stamp — and heard
himself say, before he'd planned anything: *tonight. My place.
You're coming. I owe you a meal and I can stand at a pot now,
so it's happening, don't bring the notebook, definitely bring
the notebook.*

The arithmetic was: one table. One chair.

There'd never needed to be two. That was just maths. A chair is
for a person, he was one person, and the window sill worked
fine on the rare occasion Iruka-sensei came up, and Sakura-chan
had been inside exactly twice ever and stood up both times with
her arms folded, and — that was the whole list, actually. Two
people in six years. Why get a chair if no one comes over? He'd
said that out loud once, to Sazare as it happened, back in
December, moving day, and Sazare had gone quiet and written
something down, and Naruto had figured it was about crystal.

Sazare was always writing something down. Weird guy. Great
guy. The best kind of weird, the kind where the weirdness
turned out to be a whole extra person hiding inside the quiet
one, and you only got to see him if you barged in without
knocking, which — Naruto had worked this out on the crutches,
somewhere around week four — nobody had ever done for the guy
in his whole life. Everybody knocked. Everybody had *always*
knocked. You could tell, because of how his face did the tiny
surprised thing every single time you didn't.

So: no knocking. And a meal, because a meal was the thing
Naruto had, the thing that was his to give. People fed you or
they didn't — that was the whole world when he was small, the
entire sorting of everybody into their two kinds, and
Ichiraku's counter had been the one warm place in it, and a
bowl put down in front of you without being asked was still,
at sixteen, cleared, stamped, the most anybody could say to
him. He knew other people said it other ways. He'd decided,
around week five, watching the signatures pile up on his leg
frame, that he didn't care. The bowl way was the true way.

He put the one chair on the guest side of the table.

He'd take the sill. The sill was his spot anyway; you could
see the monument, and Ukki lived there, and the draught wasn't
that bad if you kept your jacket on. Guest gets the chair.
That was just manners. That was probably in a book somewhere,
in whichever chapter they put table stuff.

The broth had been going since two. Not ramen — he'd fought
himself about that for a whole day and won, because ramen was
Ichiraku's and you don't serve another man's best thing in
your own house — but the hotpot from page sixty-one of the
hospital book, the one he'd read nine times from the bed, the
one with the eggs in, doubled on everything because Sazare
was a skinny guy who trained with Ero-sennin four mornings a
week and somebody had to do something about it.

At six exactly — *exactly*; you could set a watch by the guy;
he probably WAS a watch somewhere underneath — the knock came.

Two knocks. Wood on wood. A knock and a lean.

Naruto grinned at the door for a second, at the whole of it —
the pot going, the two settings, the chair on the right side,
the room with somebody else's eyes about to be in it and
nothing in it he minded being seen, which was maybe a first —
and went to let him in.

"It's open," he called, which was true for one person in the
village, and had been since a yard in January, and if the
frosty quiet guy ever figured out he'd caught the habit off
him, Naruto was going to charge royalties.
