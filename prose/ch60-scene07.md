# Chapter 60, Scene 7 — The Removal

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, the long room. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1860 words.*

---

They tried it the way he had designed it and it failed in about forty
minutes, and he had it written down as a failure before the hour was
out, and it was the best forty minutes of the day.

*Attempt one. As specified.*

*Me to Katsuyu: I lay a course on the table with her on the felt at nine
inches, and she watches, and she reports what my hands and the rest of
me are doing.*

*She did it. Four minutes, eleven observations, and two of them were new
and I have written both down.*

*Katsuyu to Fukasaku: she describes it.*

*And that is where it went.*

---

It did not go the way any of them had expected, which was that the two
mountains would not be able to understand one another.

They understood one another perfectly.

Katsuyu described what she had watched for four minutes and Fukasaku
followed every word of it and asked three questions and all three were
good questions and one of them was better than anything Sazare had
thought to ask in three months.

And at the end of it Fukasaku sat back and said, "Aye. And?"

"And that is what he does," said Katsuyu.

"I know that's what he does. I can *see* what he does, I've been
watching him do it since he sat down." Fukasaku tapped the table.
"That's not the problem, lass. I've got no difficulty with your
description and I don't think I'd have any difficulty passing it on.

"The trouble is that when I pass it on to that great lump over there,
he'll understand it perfectly and be no better off, and we'll all have
spent a morning."

"Why?" said Sazare.

"Because you've all been describing the *outcome*," said Shima.

---

She had not moved off her seat all morning.

"Say it," said Fukasaku.

"He goes still and something comes in. Lovely. That's what happens.
That's not how it happens and it isn't a thing anybody can do on
purpose."

She looked at Sazare.

"You said this morning you'd got nothing to hand him."

"Yes."

"You said what you'd be handing him is the absence of a technique, and
you can't demonstrate a thing that isn't there."

"Yes."

"And that's the bit that's wrong, and you've been wrong about it for a
fortnight, and I said you'd get it off the work."

She put a foot on the table, which Shizune wrote down and then crossed
out.

"An absence isn't what's moving. Nobody's handing anybody an absence.

"What you'd be handing him is a *removal*, and a removal is a positive
operation with a before and an after, and any fool can watch one."

---

Sazare sat very still.

*Say it back before you get pleased about it.*

*I have been running it as: he needs the thing I have got. And I have
not got a thing — I have got a hole where everybody else has a notation
— so there is nothing to move and the whole exercise is impossible.*

*That is wrong at the first step.*

*He does not need the hole. He needs whatever is in his that is not in
mine.*

*The transfer is not from me to him. There is no transfer.*

*There is a comparison, and then there is a subtraction, and the thing
being subtracted is his.*

He got up.

"It's his," he said.

"Go on, lad," said Fukasaku.

"He's got a procedure."

Jiraiya looked up.

"You've got a procedure," said Sazare. "You have to have one. You told
me on the roof — thirty years, and you sit, and you're still, and you
wait, and it's too slow, and you carry two of them on your shoulders
because you can't do it and move.

"That's a method. It's got steps in it. You built it yourself over
thirty-one years and nobody wrote it down and it has never once been
called a technique, and it is doing exactly the job a seal does: it is a
fixed form that gets you reliably to a shape.

"And it is *in the way*."

---

"That's not—" Jiraiya stopped. "That's the training. Fukasaku taught me
that."

"Aye," said Fukasaku, "I did."

"So it's—"

"It's how you teach a thing to somebody who cannot do it," said
Fukasaku, "and it is what everybody on that mountain has been taught for
four hundred years and it works, and it has produced you, and you are
one of four.

"And I have never once had a pupil who did not need it, so I have never
found out what it costs.

"There's your control again. Second one this morning. I do not care for
the rate."

---

"So run it the other way," said Sazare.

He was on his feet now and he was talking too fast and he knew it and did
not stop.

"Nothing goes from me to him. That was never available and we have just
spent forty minutes proving it.

"What we do is this.

"I gather. Katsuyu watches me and reports — she's done it seventy-two
times, it's the only thing she has ever done to me, and she is the only
creature alive with three months of data on what I do.

"He gathers. Shima watches him and reports — she has got thirty-one
years of data and I have got nine days.

"And then the two of them put the reports next to each other and find
the *difference*. Not what I do. What he does that I don't.

"And whatever that is, it is his, and it is in the way, and it is
invisible to him for the same reason my take was invisible to me for
eleven years, which is that there has never been anybody in the room.

"Then we take it out."

He stopped.

"And that is not teaching him a technique," he said. "It's finding a
fault. It's the only thing Katsuyu has ever done to me — she has never
taught me one single thing about crystal, not in nineteen evenings. She
has only ever told me what I was doing wrong.

"She does not teach the technique. She teaches the person out of the way
of it.

"And that is transmissible, because a fault can be described, and a
description can go along a chain, and there is a before and an after and
anybody in this room can see both."

---

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Then Fukasaku laughed — one short noise, like a board going.

"Sit down, lad," he said. "You've had it."

"Is it right?"

"It's right, and you got it in about a minute and a half off a foot on a
table, and I have been on that mountain for four hundred years and I did
not have it when I came through that door."

"Shima had it."

"Shima had it in the corridor," said Shima, "and I have had it for
thirty-one years and I have never once had a reason to say it, because
until this morning there has been nothing to compare him to."

---

They ran it after that and it took the rest of the morning and about
half the afternoon and it was, by a distance, the most tedious thing
Sazare had ever done.

He gathered eleven times.

Katsuyu reported eleven times, and by the fourth she had stopped saying
anything he had not already heard, which he minded slightly and did not
mention.

Jiraiya gathered nine times, on the floor of a long room in his own
village with his own summons watching him and a woman he had known since
he was twelve holding a pen at the end of a table, and Sazare had never
seen anybody do anything so badly in front of people on purpose.

*He is letting four people watch him be the worst at the thing he is best
at.*

*I have got nothing at all for that and I am not going to try and get
one today.*

And then Shima and Katsuyu put the two reports next to each other.

It took them an hour and eleven minutes and neither of them hurried, and
twice Fukasaku told them both to slow down, and once Katsuyu said *I do
not think that is the same word we are using* and they went back four
steps and did it again.

---

The difference was one thing and it took an hour and eleven minutes to
find and it was, when they found it, ludicrous.

"He counts," said Shima.

"...I don't count," said Jiraiya.

"You count. Not out loud. Not numbers, either, before you argue with
me — you've got a run in your head and it's always the same length and
you get to the end of it and *that's* when you let it come. Every single
time. Nine out of nine this afternoon and I would put it at every time
in thirty-one years."

"That's the settling," said Fukasaku. "I taught him that. You take the
breath and you go down the count and at the bottom of it you're still,
and that's when—"

He stopped.

"That's when you *decide* it's time," he said.

"Yes," said Katsuyu.

"And he doesn't."

"He does not decide. That is the whole of the difference and it took me
an hour to see it because it is not a thing that is there, it is a thing
that is not.

"He goes still and it comes when it comes. He is not waiting for a
moment. He is not choosing one. There is no point in the operation at
which Ruri does anything at all, and I have watched him seventy-two
times and I have never once seen a decision in it, and I did not know
that was remarkable until this afternoon."

---

"Because I couldn't build a count," said Sazare.

Everybody looked at him.

"That's the whole reason, isn't it. It's not virtue and it's not talent.
A count is a notation. It's four steps and a fixed form and it gets you
reliably to a place — it's a seal made out of breathing.

"I have never been able to hold a notation in my life. So when she
taught me, in the eighth month, she could not give me one, because she
hasn't got one either, and there was nothing to put in the gap.

"So there's a gap.

"And the gap is the thing he wants and it is not a technique and it is
not an absence. It's a step that everybody else has got and I never
learned to install."

He sat down.

"It's still in the way," he said. "It's in the way at the exact point
where he needs it not to be, which is when he's moving, because you
cannot run a fixed-length count while somebody is trying to kill you.

"That's why he's slow. It isn't age and it isn't stillness. He is
running four steps he does not need any more and has not needed for
about twenty years."

Fukasaku sat back and put his hands on his stick.

"Right," he said. "Well. Take it out of him, then."
