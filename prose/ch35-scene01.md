# Chapter 35, Scene 1 — Nothing Stopping You

*Act 4, part C. Konoha, the administration building. Sazare Ruri, age
seventeen. POV: Sazare. ~1,316 words.*

---

He went back to Kudō's office eleven days after the delegation left,
which was nine days longer than it should have taken him and about
three weeks shorter than he had expected.

She had moved desks. The office was the same office and she was against
the window now instead of the partition, which meant either a promotion
or a reorganisation, and the state of the desk — four trays where there
had been two — said reorganisation.

"Ruri." She was pleased to see him, which he had begun over two years
to recognise as a thing some people simply did. "You've come about the
query."

"Yes."

"It's still open. I've kept it open. I put a note on it in the winter
saying *hold at claimant's request, do not close*, because there's a
clerk in this section who closes things on a six-month rule and I did
not want you coming back in a year to find it gone."

Sazare stood in front of the desk with his hands at his sides.

"Thank you," he said. "That wasn't your job and you did it anyway."

Kudō looked briefly startled, and then extremely pleased, and then
covered both by opening a drawer she did not need to open.

---

She had the file out in about forty seconds, which told him she knew
exactly where it was.

"Right. Where we left it." She spread it. "Ward eleven. Workshop
premises with attached dwelling, trade: fine metal and mineral
fabrication. Provisional valuation — which is still rubbish, by the
way, I had a look at their methodology in the spring and it's a floor
area calculation with no allowance for fixtures, which for a workshop
is absurd — and appealable. Claimant field blank. Status *Cl. ext.*"

"And the reason I didn't file."

"You said if you put your name on the form the only thing that changed
was that they'd know where you were." She said it back verbatim,
without looking at the note, and Sazare registered that she had
remembered it for eleven months. "Which was correct, and which I
thought at the time was the single most reasonable thing anybody had
said to me across this desk."

"It's not correct any more."

"No." She turned the file round. "It stopped being correct on the
fourteenth."

He looked at the sheet she had put on top, which was a normalisation
office circular, and which he had not seen and had not needed to see,
because he had stood against a wall while every line of it was agreed
out loud.

*Establishment personnel of Water origin: standing foreign orders
withdrawn. Locations and assignments now matters of treaty record and
are exchanged as routine.*

"They know where I am," said Sazare.

"They know where you are, what you do, which floor you're on and what
you're contracted to produce for the next four years, and all of it is
in a signed document with a Mizukage's seal on it, and the whole point
of it is that it's *not secret.*" Kudō tapped the circular. "So the
thing that was stopping you has been taken away from you. It hasn't
been resolved. It's just gone, and it went without asking you, and I
imagine that's a strange thing to be told."

"Yes."

"I'd been sitting on this for a fortnight wondering how to put it and
that was worse than I'd planned." She winced. "Sorry."

"No. That's exactly it." He put both hands on the back of the chair he
had not sat down in. "It's the second time in a month. The charge went
the same way. It didn't get *disproved*, it got vacated, because it had
never been properly constituted, and I stood there waiting to feel
something about it and there was nothing to feel because there'd never
been anything there."

Kudō thought about that.

"This one's different, though," she said.

"How?"

"Because that one was somebody else's paperwork being corrected." She
turned the file the rest of the way round so it faced him squarely.
"This one's yours to fill in."

---

He sat down.

"Talk me through it," he said. "Properly. Assume I know nothing about
foreign probate, because I know nothing about foreign probate."

"Right, and it's not probate, that's the first thing." Kudō got a
second sheet out and started drawing on the back of it, which he
appreciated more than he could have explained. "Probate's a will and an
estate and an executor. This is a reparations claim against a state
schedule, which is a completely different animal and has about a
tenth of the paperwork and about nine times the waiting."

"How long."

"Realistically? Two years to first determination. Could be three. The
fourth tranche went out in the spring and they're still processing
claims off the *second*."

"And the valuation."

"Is rubbish and is appealable, and I'd appeal it, and the appeal adds
about a year." She kept drawing. "So call it three years, and at the
end of it you get a number that is probably still forty per cent short
of what that building was worth, and there is a real chance you get
nothing at all because a section in Kirigakure decides something about
the register that I can't predict from here."

"That's honest."

"It's the only useful thing I've got. I'm not going to sell you a claim
form." She turned the drawing round. "Here's the whole process. Six
boxes. Five of them are addresses and dates and the schedule reference,
and I can fill all five of those in myself off the file in about four
minutes, and I will, because that's my job and there's no reason for
you to hand-write a survey grid reference."

"And the sixth."

Kudō put her pen down.

"The sixth is the claimant declaration," she said. "Name, relationship
to the registered holder, and a statement that the claim is made in
good faith. It's four lines and it has to be in your hand, not mine.
That's a Kiri registry requirement and I can't get round it and I've
already checked."

"Why does it have to be mine?"

"Because it's the one part of the document that's an assertion rather
than a record." She said it simply, as an administrative fact, without
the faintest idea what she was handing him. "Everything else on that
form is something that can be looked up. That bit's you saying a thing
is true. They want it in the handwriting of the person saying it."

---

Sazare Ruri looked at a box on a form.

"That's it," he said.

"That's it. Four lines. And I'd want it done in the office, in front of
me, because I have to witness it, and then it goes in the Thursday bag
and it's gone."

"When can I do it?"

"Now, if you like. I've got the form here and I've got twenty minutes
and you're already sitting down."

He was already sitting down.

He had come in through that door eleven minutes earlier having decided
the thing on the walk over, and there was a pen in a stand nine inches
from his right hand, and Kudō was already filling in the five boxes
that could be looked up, and the whole of it was going to take four
minutes.

"Not today," said Sazare.

Kudō's pen stopped.

"That's fine," she said. "Genuinely, it's fine, it's been open eleven
months and it can be open eleven more. Do you want me to hold it?"

"Yes."

"Same note?"

"Same note."

He got up, and thanked her, and went back to the hospital, and did not
work out until nine days later that he had walked into that office
having entirely decided to do it and had stood up without doing it, and
that he had not, at any point in the intervening nine days, asked
himself why.
