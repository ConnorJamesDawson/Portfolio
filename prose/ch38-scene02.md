# Chapter 38, Scene 2 — What She Remembers

*Act 4, part D. Konoha General, the stairwell. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,400 words.*

---

He got it wrong immediately, which he had expected, and then it went in
a direction he had not modelled at all.

He caught her on the half-landing at the eleventh hour with the tray
going up and said, "Haruno-san."

She stopped.

"Yes?"

And the four seconds arrived exactly as the book had said they would,
and what he produced at the end of them was:

"Your sandals have been resoled."

Sakura looked at him.

Then she looked down at her own feet, and back up, and said, with
extremely careful politeness, "...They have, yes."

"Not by you."

"No, there's a man on the Nakadōri who—" She stopped. "Sorry, is there
something wrong with them?"

"No."

"Right."

"They're good work. The stitching's better than the original." He heard
himself and understood, with total clarity and no ability whatsoever to
correct course, that he was four exchanges into a conversation about
footwear with a person he had wanted to speak to for fifteen months.
"That wasn't what I came to say."

"I did wonder."

"I'm Sazare Ruri. I'm the ward lead on the second floor and the
medical crystal technician and I have been in this building for about
two years and I have never spoken to you and I have worked out
recently that this is a fault of mine rather than an accident, and I
have been trying for four days to think of an opening remark and what I
have just done instead is your shoes."

Sakura put the tray down on the sill of the stairwell window.

"Okay," she said. "Start again, then."

---

"You don't have to—"

"No, I want to, because you've just done the thing where somebody
apologises for the whole of a conversation while it's still happening,
and I do that, and I've been told it's exhausting." She folded her
arms. "Also I know who you are. Everybody in this hospital knows who
you are. You're the one who did the street."

"Yes."

"And you mended the trolley on the fourth floor that maintenance has
had a chit open on since before I started, and you did it in about six
minutes at half past five in the morning and left the chit in the tray
so they'd still get paid for it."

Sazare had not been aware that anybody knew about the chit.

"That's — how do you know about the chit?"

"Because I asked who'd done it," said Sakura, "about a year ago, and
nobody could tell me, and I asked Shizune-san, and *she* knew."

"Why did you ask?"

"Because it was a good repair and nobody had put their name on it."

They stood in a stairwell.

"Right," said Sazare. "That's — right. Yes. I had a different opening
and it was worse than the shoes."

"What was it?"

"*Do you remember me.*"

Sakura's face did something complicated.

"Oh," she said. "Oh, you *idiot*."

---

"You were on the road," she said. "Eleven days. You had a bad knee and
a pack with a picture frame in it that you wouldn't let anybody carry,
and Sasuke-kun slowed down for you twice without saying anything, and
Naruto talked at you for four hours a day from about the fifth
morning, and you answered every single question he asked properly."

"That's—"

"And you asked me about the tree." She had gone slightly pink and did
not appear to have noticed. "On the ninth day. Coming down the long
hill, in the afternoon, with the sun in front of us. And I told you it
was tree-walking and it was the second control exercise and I got about
four sentences into how it worked and Kakashi-sensei said my name and I
stopped."

Sazare found that he had put a hand on the stair rail.

"You remember that."

"I have thought about it approximately once a week for two years," said
Sakura Haruno.

---

He did not say anything.

"You want to know why," she said. "I can see you doing the thing with
your face where you're deciding whether it's rude to ask."

"...Yes."

"Because it was the first time in my life anybody wanted something I
knew." She said it fast, the way people say a thing they have had
assembled for a long time. "I was twelve. I was on a team with the last
Uchiha and the loudest boy in the Land of Fire and a jōnin who read a
book instead of teaching us, and I had the best control on that team
and I did not know that, and the only thing I was actually good at was
*remembering things*, and remembering things is what you get told off
for being good at, because it means you're a swot and not a shinobi."

"Sakura—"

"And then there was a person on a road who was four years older than me
and could do something with stone that I had never seen anybody do, and
who had been in the Land of Waves being more use than the three of us
put together, and he asked me a *question*." She had stopped folding
her arms at some point. "And I answered it. And he said *that's
plenty*, and he meant it, and he wasn't being kind, he'd got what he
wanted and it was enough and he said so."

"It was enough. It was—" He stopped, and made himself say the actual
thing rather than the smaller one. "I had been working alone on a bench
for eight months on a thing I could not name. You gave me a name for
it. And then you told me it was the *second* one, which meant there was
a first, and that there were enough of them to be numbered, and that
somewhere there was a room with somebody at the front of it saying the
words out loud to children."

Sakura had gone extremely still.

"I walked eleven days on that," said Sazare. "It's in my notebook. It's
still in my notebook. It's the reason I came through the gate instead
of going round it."

---

Neither of them said anything for a moment. A trolley went past on the
floor below with the bad bearing in it, which he had fixed three times
and which had come back three times.

"Two years," said Sakura.

"Yes."

"You've been on the second floor for two years and I've been on the
fourth and the fever ward for fifteen months and neither of us has said
a word."

"No."

"Why didn't *you*?"

"Because I thought you wouldn't remember," said Sazare, "and I have
worked out in the last four days that I was not protecting myself from
finding out that you didn't. I was protecting the version where you
did."

Sakura absorbed that.

"That's the saddest thing anybody's said to me this year," she said,
"and I work in a hospital."

"Why didn't you?"

And Sakura Haruno, apprentice to the Fifth Hokage, who had walked past
him on those stairs about forty times, said:

"Because you never looked up."

---

He thought about that for the rest of the day and most of the
following one.

*She is correct and it is worse than she knows.*

*I did look up. I looked up every single time. I have her sandals, both
pouches, the flap wear on the lower one, the clip, the nails, the scar
on the back of the left hand and the fact that she carries a tray on
her forearms because she dropped one.*

*I have fifteen months of observation on a person I have never
addressed, and from four feet away, on a staircase, what that looked
like to her was a man who did not raise his head.*

*Because I do it without moving. That is the whole of my technique and
it always has been. I established at nine years old that a person who
is visibly looking at things gets asked what he is looking at, and I
have spent eleven years perfecting a way of taking an inventory of a
room without anybody in the room knowing I have done it.*

*It works. It has never once failed.*

*And the cost of it, which I have never priced until this afternoon, is
that for eleven years every person I have ever catalogued has
experienced me as somebody who did not look at them.*
