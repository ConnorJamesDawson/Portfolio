# Chapter 60, Scene 4 — Before

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, the long room. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1350 words.*

---

They used the long room on the second floor, which was the one with the
scrubbed table and the sink and the locked cupboard, because it was the
only room in the building with nothing in it that mattered and because
it had a door that shut properly.

Shizune had cleared it herself at seven.

She had taken out the two chairs and the rack and the four boxes of
whatever had accumulated in there since the sixth month, and she had
scrubbed the table, which nobody had asked her to do, and she had put a
second lamp on the sill and lit it although it was daylight.

Then she had sat down at the end of the table with the minute book and
a new nib and had not said one word to anybody for forty minutes.

---

"You all right?" said Sazare.

"No."

"Can I—"

"You can stop asking," said Shizune. "I have taken the minute of four
rooms in this building that I would rather did not exist and I have never
once been frightened before I went in, and I am frightened this morning,
and I would like to be allowed to be frightened without being *managed*
about it."

"All right."

"Thank you."

She squared the book.

"Ruri."

"Yes."

"The door at the end of the corridor was open at twenty past five this
morning."

He did not say anything.

"I am not raising it," said Shizune. "I am telling you that I know, so
that neither of us spends today wondering whether the other one is
wondering, because we have got two mountains coming in at ten and I need
my whole attention and so do you."

"Understood."

"Good." She dipped the nib. "It's the fourth thing I have decided not to
know since the eleventh and I have stopped counting, and I want you to
be aware that I have stopped counting on purpose."

*I have not stopped counting.*

*It is four.*

*And she has just told me, in about nine words, that she is doing this
deliberately and that she will go on doing it and that she does not want
credit for any of it.*

He did not say thank you.

He worked out, standing at the end of a scrubbed table at twenty past
eight in the morning, that saying it would make her acknowledge the
whole of it, and that she had built the sentence specifically so that he
could not.

*Right.*

*I will find another way.*

*Not today.*

---

Tsunade read the four conditions out at nine.

She did it standing at the head of the table with the scroll in one hand
and she did it in the voice she used in a briefing, which was flat and
unhurried and left no room anywhere.

"One. Authority over the room.

"This is my building and it is my tower and it is my authority, and I
will say that out loud when they arrive and I will say it once. Not as
Hokage — I am not putting a village's rank on a room with two mountains
in it, that would be an insult and it would also be a lie, because
whatever is about to happen in here is nothing to do with Konohagakure.

"It's mine because it's my house.

"And there is a second half and it goes in the minute now.

"If Ruri says stop, everything stops. Immediately, at any point, from
anybody, for any reason or none. He does not have to say why and nobody
asks."

Shizune wrote it down.

"Two. Ruri states plainly at the start what is being transferred, in his
own words, out loud, in front of everybody.

"Three. Shizune holds the pen. Everything said is minuted. Nothing goes
out of this room except the minute and the minute stays in this
building.

"Four is his and he answers it himself and I have not seen his answer and
I am not going to before he gives it."

She put the scroll down.

"Any objections, now, in this room, while it is still just us."

Nobody had any.

---

Jiraiya was not right.

Sazare had it by about twenty past nine and he had it off nothing at all
— off the way the man was standing, which was still, and off the fact
that he had come in at eight and had not eaten anything.

"You've been to that mountain for thirty years," he said.

"Thirty-one."

"So what's the matter?"

Jiraiya looked at him for a second and then, to his considerable
surprise, answered it.

"I've never had them here."

"...Sorry?"

"I go *there*, kid. That's the whole shape of it and it has been the
shape of it since I was twelve — I go up the mountain, I do as I'm told,
they feed me, I come back. Thirty-one years and about four hundred
visits and I have summoned them here perhaps eleven times and every
single one of those was a fight and none of them lasted an hour.

"Fukasaku has never been in a room I *live* in."

He rubbed the back of his neck.

"And there's a thing that happens when somebody who has only ever seen
you in one place sees you in the other one, and I have watched it happen
to other people and I have laughed at it, and I have not once in
thirty-one years thought about it happening to me.

"He's going to see the village. He's going to see the tower and the
table and the woman who runs it and a boy of eighteen with a lump of
crystal on his hip, and he is going to see *me* in the middle of it.

"And I've got no idea what he thinks I am. Not really. Not off the
mountain."

Sazare stood there and did not have anything.

"That's not what I expected," he said eventually.

"No."

"I expected the fourth condition."

"The fourth condition is going to be the best hour of my year," said
Jiraiya. "The fourth condition is going to be *magnificent* and I have
been looking forward to it since Wednesday and I intend to enjoy the
whole of it.

"This is just an old man being stupid in a corridor and I'd be obliged
if you left it here."

"Left."

---

At about twenty to ten he went and stood at the window.

*Right. Say the plain thing to yourself before you have to say it to
four other creatures.*

*What is being transferred.*

*Not crystal. Say it properly. Crystal is the test article and it is the
test article because it is the only thing I can definitely do and
because nobody can fake one.*

*What is being transferred is the absence of a notation.*

*A man is going to be taught to make a shape without a seal in front of
it, and the reason he cannot be taught it by me is that I have got
nothing to hand him, and the reason it might work along a chain is that
two of the three links have already done it and nobody noticed.*

*And when I say it out loud in about twenty minutes I am going to have
to say it without decorating it, and I am going to have to say it to a
toad who has been asking about obligation since Wednesday.*

He looked down at the courtyard.

*And then he is going to ask me question four.*

*And I have got one answer and it is eleven words long and Jiraiya has
told me to say it exactly, and I have not been able to improve it in
three days, and I am fairly sure that is because it cannot be improved
and not because I have run out.*

Behind him, at the end of the table, Shizune said, "Ten o'clock."

Jiraiya reached for the scroll on his belt.

And Katsuyu said, from the corner of the sill where she had been since
half past eight without saying anything at all:

"You will not need that. They are at the gate."
