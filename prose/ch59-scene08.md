# Chapter 59, Scene 8 — Damage Control

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~2260 words.*

---

He told her at the fourth hour on the Friday and he did not arrange any
of it.

"I did something on Thursday night and I'm going to give you the whole
of it in order, and I'd rather you didn't stop me in the middle, because
if you stop me I'll rebuild it and the rebuilt one will be softer."

Tsunade put the pen down.

"Go on."

So he gave her the whole of it in order.

The exercise, and the gather not terminating, and the four seconds where
he understood it was still coming. The correct action, which was to
close, which he had had in front of him, which was one movement he had
made ten million times. And that he had not made it.

Thirty to forty seconds, estimated, unstated, because he had lost the
count in the twenties and had not noticed.

The yard being in the room.

The scar aching at about thirty.

The close, which was ordinary, and which had worked, and the palm
afterwards which was unchanged in every respect including sensation to
the pulp of all five.

The four findings, in order, including the third one, which was that
there is no gauge and you find the edge by going over it.

And then the fourth one.

"And I'm pleased about it," said Sazare. "I want that in the account
because it's the actual finding and everything else is arithmetic.

"There was no fear in that yard. None. Not at any point. The stone
should have been the loudest thing in there and it did not arrive, and
I have had it in the front of my head continuously since the sixteenth
and it wrecked the exercise for a fortnight, and on Thursday night it
simply was not present.

"And that's the same thing that's made the articles clean. It isn't two
effects. It's one. I haven't got room for the watching at the moment,
and the watching is the fault in my hands and it is also the only brake
I have ever had, and I did not know the second half of that sentence
until eleven minutes after I stopped."

He finished.

"That's all of it," he said. "There isn't any more and I haven't left
anything out."

---

Tsunade did not say anything.

She sat behind the desk with both hands flat on it and looked at him for
a long time, and he watched her face do absolutely nothing at all, which
was the thing it did perhaps twice a season and which he had never in
three years been able to read.

Then she got up.

She came round the desk without hurrying and stood in front of him, and
he looked up at her.

She hit him.

---

It was an open hand and it was not a gesture.

It took him across the left side of the face and it turned his head
about thirty degrees and he heard it before he felt it, and the chair
went back an inch on the boards.

He sat there.

*Right.*

*Open palm, left cheek, mid-force for her, which is to say she has
controlled it to about a fortieth of what is available and has done that
inside the movement, which takes more skill than the strike.*

*No cut. Lip's intact. There'll be a mark and it'll be up in about nine
minutes and gone by noon.*

*Sting is —*

He stopped taking the reading, because it had stopped being the thing in
the room.

His face had gone.

That was the first thing he registered and he registered it entirely
from the inside, because there was no mirror in the Hokage's office and
there never had been: that the thing which had been on his face
continuously since twenty past four on Saturday morning, which had been
remarked on by five people and could not be turned off and which he had
written two pages about, was not there.

It lasted about four seconds.

Then it came back.

---

"Kami," said Tsunade.

She was standing over him with her hand still open.

"It came back," she said.

"Yes."

"I hit you and it *came back*."

"Four seconds," said Sazare. "I counted it. I'm sorry, I can't do
anything about it — I've been trying since Sunday and there's nothing to
try with, I haven't got the machinery for taking a thing off my face
because I have never once needed it—"

"Ruri."

"—and I want to say that I know what that was, because you'll think I
don't. That wasn't temper. Your hand was flat and you took about a
fortieth of what you've got and you did the controlling inside the
movement, which is harder than the hit, and you did it in about a
quarter of a second while you were frightened.

"So that was fear. That's all that was. And it's a completely reasonable
—"

"*Ruri.*"

"Sorry."

---

She was breathing like somebody who had come up a hill.

"You held it open for forty seconds."

"Thirty to forty. I can't state it."

"On your own."

"Yes."

"In a yard."

"Yes."

"Four days after two separate parties told you in writing—"

"That was about the fifth thing," said Sazare, "and this wasn't the
fifth thing, and I know exactly how thin that distinction is and I made
it at the time and I made it in about a second and a half and it was
not a good-faith distinction. I've written that down as well."

Tsunade closed her eyes.

"You've written it down."

"It's in the entry. Third paragraph."

"You have written down, in your own book, in your own hand, that you
made a bad-faith distinction in order to let yourself do the thing that
turns people into *statues*."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because it's true," said Sazare, "and because if I don't write the bad
ones down then the book is just a record of a man being right, and I
have got four hundred pages on people whose books are that."

---

She stood there for a moment.

Then she put a hand over her own eyes and said something under her
breath that he did not catch, and when she took the hand away her face
had changed in a way he had no reading for at all.

He looked up at her.

"I didn't think you could look better than you did on Saturday," he
said.

Tsunade stopped dead.

"You said—"

"You've just hit me across the face and you're standing over me
frightened and you're doing about four things at once and none of them
are being managed," said Sazare. "That's the first time I have ever seen
your face not working. Not once in three years. Not in the yard, not on
the hill, not on the eleventh.

"And I'd like it recorded, because I'm going to want it later and I
haven't got a notebook on me and I'm not going to be able to write it
down for about an hour."

There was a silence of about two seconds.

"Kami's sake, Ruri," said Tsunade, "you sappy little bastard."

"Yes."

"That is the — *no*, don't agree with it—"

"I'm not agreeing," he said. "I'm confirming."

---

She went back round the desk and sat down and put her head in her hands
for a moment.

He sat in the chair with his face going up on the left side and waited,
because there was clearly more and because it was going to be the part
that mattered.

"Right," said Tsunade, from behind her hands. "Here's what's going to
happen and you're not going to take it apart.

"You're sleeping here."

He did not follow.

"Sorry?"

"Upstairs. Second floor, the room at the end past the treatment room —
it's got a bed in it, it has had a bed in it since the Third, it is where
I sleep four nights in five and you worked that out off a shirt on the
fifteenth of the eighth month and did not have the sense to keep it to
yourself."

"...I said that to Shizune."

"Shizune tells me everything," said Tsunade, "and I will be having a
separate conversation with you about that at some later date. Not this
week."

---

"Give me the reasoning," said Sazare.

"You're going to get the reasoning whether you want it or not, and
you're going to get all four, and the fourth one is the true one, so
listen to the first three properly and don't stop at them.

"One. You have been broadcasting for six days.

"Don't argue, I have had it from three separate directions. Yagi's had
three people ask her. Kanzaki said *well* at you. Sakura has got a
column for everything and there is a thing on your face she has never
been able to score and she told you to your face that she couldn't. That
is four people in a building where everybody has three years of a man
with nothing on him at all.

"And you cannot turn it off, and I am not asking you to, because there
is nothing in you to do it with and you would only make it worse by
trying.

"So the cover has stopped being *nothing false is ever said*, because
nothing false has been said and it has not mattered for six days.

"Two. If you are in this building, then a great deal of what you do is
explicable. A man who is in the tower at strange hours is a man on a
posting. A man who walks up that hill at four in the morning from a room
over a persimmon tree, every day, for a fortnight, with *that* on his
face, is a story with an end to it that anybody can guess.

"Three. And this one's paperwork, and it is not a lie.

"You're a sage. That word came out of Jiraiya's mouth on a roof on
the sixteenth and there are four people alive and one of them is under
my roof. There is a training arrangement, it is real, it started in the
eighth month, and it has produced a documented petrification injury and
a documented ceiling event and there is a review from Shikkotsu in
eleven sheets with my name on the covering note.

"Supervised senjutsu training with medical oversight, on-site, for a
Corps sergeant with a prior near-fatal incident in the same discipline.

"There is not one word of that which is false, and I can put it through
the Corps board on Monday, and it will go through in about eleven
minutes because the alternative is signing off on you doing it in a yard
on your own, which — as of Thursday night — is a thing I can *evidence*."

---

He sat with that for a while.

*She is right on all three and the third one is better than right, it is
elegant, and she has built it out of the worst thing I have ever told
her.*

*I gave her the yard at ten past four this morning and she has turned it
into a docket by twenty past.*

"And the fourth one," he said.

Tsunade looked at him across the desk.

"The fourth one is that you sat in a yard on your own on Thursday night
and held the thing open for forty seconds and you were *pleased*," she
said, "and I have got nothing to put against that. Nothing. There is no
argument, there's no order I could give you that you wouldn't obey and
then find a distinction inside in a second and a half, and there is no
version of the next month where I am not thinking about it every time
you are out of this building after dark.

"So I want you where I can see you.

"That's it. That's the fourth one and it isn't reasoning, it's just
true, and I would like you to notice that I have said it out loud rather
than hiding it inside the other three, because *you would have found
it*."

---

He looked at the desk.

"There's a problem with it," he said.

"Say it."

"It's the most dangerous thing you could possibly do."

"I know."

"The cover holds because nothing false is said. That's fine. But putting
me in this building at night, under a document with your name on the
covering note, is not a cover — it's a *concentration*. Everything that
was spread across a hill and a yard and a hospital is now in one place
with a door on it, and one observation from anybody at any time gets the
whole of it at once instead of a piece.

"You've traded a large number of small exposures for one very large
one."

"Yes," said Tsunade.

"You knew that before I said it."

"I have known it since about half past four this morning, which is when
I decided, which was before you told me about the yard and while you
were still on the second finding."

She picked the pen back up.

"So that's the arrangement," she said. "It's on paper Monday. Bring what
you need tonight."

He sat there.

"Hime—"

"Ruri," said Tsunade, without looking up, "I am the Hokage of
Konohagakure and I have made a bad decision on purpose with full
knowledge at four o'clock in the morning, and I am at peace with it, and
if you take it apart in front of me I am going to hit you again."
