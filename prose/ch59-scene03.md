# Chapter 59, Scene 3 — Lighthouse

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower and the hospital. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1690 words.*

---

Shizune got it in about four seconds.

She came up at the fifth hour with the delegation folder and stopped in
the doorway of the outer office, and he was at the second desk with the
lamp on and about ninety acknowledgements in front of him, and he looked
up and said good morning.

That was all of it. He looked up and said good morning.

She stood in the doorway for slightly too long.

Then she came in and put the folder down and sat at her own desk and did
not say one single word about it, all day, which he had expected and
which he had not expected to find difficult.

At about eleven she said, "Ruri."

"Yes."

"Nothing."

"You said my name."

"I know I did," said Shizune. "I'm counting. I told you I'd keep asking
and I'm going to keep asking, and this is not the morning, and I would
like it on the record that I have decided that four separate times since
half past five."

---

He had a problem and he had it identified by about the ninth hour of the
first day.

*I cannot stop doing it.*

*And I want to be precise about what "it" is, because I have got no face
— that is established, everybody in this village has told me so, Sakura
put it in a briefing in front of two witnesses eight days ago.*

*So it is not that I am smiling. I do not have the machinery to smile at
somebody deliberately. I have tried it twice, at sixteen, in front of a
window, and both attempts are written down and the word I used was
alarming.*

*It is that there has been nothing there for three years and now there
is something, and the something is not being managed because there is
nothing in me to manage it with.*

*Everybody in this building has three years of baseline on me.*

*Three years of a man with nothing on his face at all. That is the
comparison set. That is the whole of the comparison set, and it is very
large and very consistent and it is held by about four hundred people.*

*A light in a lit room is a light. A light in three years of fog is a
lighthouse.*

He wrote that down in the margin of an acknowledgement and then crossed
it out because it was decorative, and then found that he had written it
again two pages later without noticing.

---

Kanzaki got it on the Monday.

He went up to the yard at six to collect the reject stock and she was
still there, which she should not have been, and she was at the far
bench with her back to him doing something with the tare weights that
she had been pretending was work for about eleven years.

"Evening," he said.

She turned round.

She looked at him for about two seconds with her glasses down her nose,
and then she turned back to the tare weights.

"Well," said Kanzaki.

"There's nothing—"

"I haven't said anything."

"You said *well*."

"I'm seventy-two," said Kanzaki, "and I have said *well* about four
hundred thousand times and it has never once meant a single thing, and
if you are going to start reading my monosyllables you will be here till
Thursday."

She put a weight down.

"There's a lamp on that stool, Ruri."

"I know."

"I have no idea how it got there."

---

Emiko got it on the Tuesday, in the corridor on the second floor, and
she did not say anything at all, which was worse.

She had asked him whether he had eaten every working day for three years
and it was not in his book once because it was not work.

She asked him on Tuesday and he said yes, and she said good, and then
she stood there.

"What?" said Sazare.

"Nothing," said Emiko. "You just look — "

She stopped, and did something with her mouth, and did not finish it,
and went off down the corridor with the trolley.

He stood in the corridor for a moment.

*She did not finish the sentence.*

*She has finished eleven hundred sentences to me in four years, including
several that I would rather she had not, and she has never once stopped
in the middle of one.*

*Which means the end of it was a thing she decided not to say to me, in
a corridor, on a Tuesday.*

*And there are two candidates and one of them is that she thought it
would embarrass me, and the other is that she worked out what it was
about.*

---

Yagi got it on the Wednesday and Yagi did not have any manners about it
whatsoever.

He was in the fabrication room signing out four hundred grams of reject
against a docket that would not exist until the first of the twelfth
month, which was a small irregularity that everybody had agreed not to
look at, and she came in for a splint and stopped.

"Four days," she said.

"Sorry?"

"On the fifteenth, on the second floor of that tower, I asked you how
long and you said four days, and I sat down on my own bag."

"Yagi—"

"That's twelve days now," said Yagi. "And I said to you at the time that
at some point in the next month you'd want to talk to somebody about it
and it would not be Shizune and you knew where the ward was."

She picked the splint up off the rack.

"You have not come to the ward."

"No."

"You are not going to."

"No."

"Right," said Yagi. She checked the splint against the light, which she
did with every single one, which he had never once told her was
unnecessary because it was not unnecessary. "Then I'll say the one thing
and then I'll leave it, and it isn't advice.

"Whatever's happened to you, it is on you like a lamp, and there is not
one person on my floor who has not remarked on it, and three of them
have asked me and I have told all three to mind their business.

"You have spent four years being the most illegible man in this
building. Congratulations. You are now the most legible."

---

Sakura got it on the Wednesday evening and Sakura was the one he had
been dreading.

She caught him on the south stair, which was hers, and she did not do
any of the things the others had done.

She stopped four steps up and looked at him for a long moment.

"Huh," said Sakura.

"Good evening."

"No, hang on." She came down two steps. "Hang on.

"I have got fifty-one sheets on you. Fifty-one. I have got a column for
eye contact and a column for volume and a column for whether you finish
sentences, and I have got about nine hundred separate marks in three
years, and there is a thing I have never once been able to score
because it has never once happened."

"Sakura—"

"You're *pleased*," she said.

He did not answer.

"Not pleased about a thing. I've seen that — you were pleased about the
reject table and you were pleased about the rose, and both of those were
about an object and both of them lasted about eleven minutes.

"You're just *pleased*. All the time. Since about Saturday."

"That's — "

"Don't tell me you're not going to tell me," said Sakura.

"I'm not going to tell you."

"I *know* you're not going to tell me. That's the second time in a
fortnight and you did it in an outer office in front of Shizune and I've
thought about it every day since." She came down the last two steps.
"Which is fine. It's yours. I'm not asking.

"But I'm going to say one thing as the person who has spent fifty-one
sessions on a stair trying to teach you to be legible to other people,
and I want you to actually hear it and not take it apart.

"You've got no volume control on this."

"No," said Sazare.

"You know that?"

"I worked it out at about the ninth hour on Saturday. I have got no
machinery for putting a thing on my face and I have got none for taking
one off, and I have never needed either, because there has never been
anything on it."

"So everybody can see."

"Everybody can see."

Sakura looked at him for a moment.

"Then I hope it's a good one," she said, and went on up the stair, and
did not look back, and that was the entire conversation.

---

He walked home over the hill on the Wednesday night and did the
arithmetic and did not enjoy any of it.

*Five days. Five people, and I am not counting the ones I have not
noticed, and I have stopped being able to trust the count of the ones I
have not noticed.*

*Shizune, who knows and said nothing and is counting.*

*Kanzaki, who has not asked and will not and knows exactly what it is,
because she is seventy-two and has been in this village for fifty years
and has watched about four hundred of them.*

*Emiko, who did not finish a sentence.*

*Yagi, who has had three people ask her and has told all three to mind
their business and who told me to my face that I am now the most legible
man in that building.*

*Sakura, who has fifty-one sheets and nine hundred marks and a column
for everything, and who did not ask, and who said* I hope it's a good
one.

*The cover is that nothing false is ever said.*

*Nothing false has been said. Not one word, by anybody, at any point.*

*And it does not matter in the slightest, because I have been standing
in the middle of that building for five days broadcasting on every
frequency there is, and I cannot turn it off, and I do not want to.*

*That last part is the actual problem and I am going to have to write it
down.*
