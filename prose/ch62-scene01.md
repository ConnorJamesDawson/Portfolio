# Chapter 62, Scene 1 — Eleven Minutes

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1790 words.*

---

He had not slept in the room since the twenty-seventh of the eleventh
month and he had been back to it nine times, which was the whole of the
problem and which he had been aware of for a fortnight.

Nine times in seventeen days. Each time for a specific thing — the
second notebook, the good nib, the winter coat, the tin with the
graded grain in it, the tin with the ordinary grain in it because he had
taken the wrong one, twice — and each time he had stood in the middle of
it for between four and eleven minutes and then gone away again.

*I am running it as a store.*

*I have got a room I do not sleep in that I go back to for things, and I
have been doing it for seventeen days, and I know exactly what that is
because I have got about four hundred pages on it.*

*That is what she does with a house.*

He came up on the Saturday morning at eight and did the inventory
properly, because if he was going to do it he was going to do it in the
only way he had.

It took eleven minutes.

---

*Inventory, 14/12, room over the persimmon tree, taken 08:00–08:11.*

*Books. Fourteen complete, in the box under the sill. Four in use — two
on the low table, one on the shelf, one in my coat. Total eighteen.*

*Weight: I have not weighed the box and I have carried it and I would
put it at fourteen kilos, and the four in use at about three, so
seventeen.*

*Clothes. What I am wearing. One other set of the same. The winter coat,
which is on me. Three shirts, two of which are Corps and are not mine.
Boots on my feet, no second pair.*

*Bedding. A futon that came with the room and is the landlord's. Two
blankets, one of which is O-Shige's and has been for two years and which
she has never once asked about and which I am not going to be able to
give back without an argument.*

*Tools. The tool roll. Eleven items, all mine, all bought.*

*Tins. Two.*

*The low table. Not mine. It was in the room.*

*The lamp. Mine. Bought in the eleventh month of my sixteenth year for
about a hundred and ninety and it is a bad lamp and I have never
replaced it.*

*One cup. One bowl — Kurihara Nao's, returned twice, given back both
times, and after the third time I stopped trying.*

*The felt roll and what is in it, which is on my hip and is not going in
a box.*

He stopped and looked at what he had written.

*That's it.*

*That is the whole of it and I have been in this room since the ninth
month of my sixteenth year, which is two years and three months, and I
was in a room over the laundry for eight months before that and in the
ward dormitory before that.*

*Eleven years since Kiri.*

He put the pencil down on the low table that was not his.

---

*Do the second column.*

*What have I bought.*

He did that one from memory and it took considerably longer than eleven
minutes and he did it sitting on the floor with his back against the
wall.

*The tool roll, over about four years, item by item.*

*The lamp.*

*Boots, three pairs, of which two have gone.*

*Books — blank ones, eighteen of them, at about ninety ryō each, which
is the single largest line in the whole of my life and comes to about
sixteen hundred.*

*Nibs. Pencils. Chalk.*

*Two hundred grams a week of fish at eight per cent over the going rate
for eleven weeks, which I entered under rent.*

He stopped.

*Nothing else.*

*Say it properly and do not let it go past. In four years in this
village, on a wage, I have bought tools, a lamp, boots, notebooks and
the things I write in them with.*

*There is not one object in this room that is here because I wanted it
in a room.*

*Nao's bowl is here because she gave it to me. The blanket is here
because O-Shige decided in the second month of the year I was
seventeen. The table was here when I came. The futon is the landlord's.*

*Everything I own does a job.*

*And I have got seventeen kilos of it and it is all paper.*

---

He got up and stood in the middle of the room for a bit, which was the
thing he had done nine times in seventeen days.

*Right. The actual problem, which is not the inventory.*

*The books.*

He went and put his hand flat on the box under the sill.

*Fourteen. The yard log and the bench log and the ledger proper and the
four smaller ones, plus the three years and a bit since the gate.*

*Everything is in there. That is not a figure of speech and I would
defend it in front of anybody: everything I have thought since I was
fifteen is in that box in order, indexed, dated, with the bad ones in
it.*

*The take is in there. The grip. The fifteenth of the eighth month at
twenty-two twenty with* risk: none identified *underlined, and I have
never crossed the underlining out.*

*Eleven seconds in a gateway in the tenth month.*

*And the fifteenth of the eighth month at five past eleven at night,
four clauses in a bracket in the ordinary notation, with a line ruled
through it the next morning and a note underneath saying that striking a
thing out does not remove it.*

He took his hand off the box.

*The finding is not in there. That is the one thing that is not in
there and it is not in there because a woman made me lose an argument
about it in an office on the second of the twelfth month, and I said
*agreed* in about eleven seconds and then told her that I was getting
used to it.*

*She is in there.*

*Not the second of the twelfth. Her. Three years of her, in my hand,
dated, indexed, with the bracket notation on about four hundred entries
and a column at the bottom of every page.*

*And I built the index myself and it is a good index.*

*Anybody who got that box would not have to read fourteen books. They
would have to read about nine pages of index and then go straight to the
four entries that mattered, and it would take them under an hour, and
they would have the whole of it — the dates, the eleven seconds, the
bracket, the ruled line, the four visits a week, the fourth hour.*

*I have written the most efficient possible summary of the most
dangerous fact in this village and put it at the front of the box for
the convenience of whoever opens it.*

He stood there.

*And the door does not lock.*

*It has never locked. There is a hasp and no staple and I have had the
staple in the tool roll since the third month and I have not put it on,
because there has never been anything in here worth taking and because I
would have had to ask the landlord.*

---

He went down at about half past eight and stood on the landing for a
minute.

The tap was dripping. It had been dripping since the fourth month and he
had fixed it twice and the washer was the wrong size and the right size
was not made any more.

Below him, at the far end of the second-floor passage, somebody's kettle
went on.

*She's up.*

*She is always up. She has been up at half past eight every day for
seventeen days and I have been getting a thing out of a room nine times
and I have not been down that stair once.*

*Nine times.*

*I have come up that stair, taken a nib, and gone away again, and her
door has been four feet from the bottom of it every single time, and I
have got a completely defensible reason for every one of the nine and
they are all true and not one of them is why.*

He looked at the door at the bottom of the stair.

*I have been putting it off since the twenty-seventh of the eleventh
month.*

*And I have not written it down anywhere, and I have been writing
everything down since I was nine, and the entry for the twenty-eighth
says* moved to tower, effective. Room to be cleared *and there has been
no line under it for seventeen days.*

*That is the second thing this month that I have not put in a book, and
the first one was a woman in a chair at twenty past four in the
morning.*

*Those are not the same class of thing at all.*

*Except that both of them are things I did not want to have handled.*

---

He went back up and got the box out from under the sill and put it by
the door where the handcart could take it, and then sat on the floor
with his back to the wall and waited for two genin who were coming at
nine because Sakura Haruno had said, on a south stair on the Thursday,
in a voice that permitted nothing, that a man does not move fourteen
books and a futon on his own like an animal.

*She has organised it.*

*I did not ask her. I mentioned it. I mentioned it in about nine words
as a fact about my week, in the middle of something else, and she had it
arranged inside about a minute and told me the time rather than asking
it.*

*Which is the fourth time somebody has done a thing for me this month
without being asked, and I have decided not to count that one, because
counting it would put O-Shige's blanket and Nao's bowl and a plum from a
man on a stool in the same column as a backdated request and a folded
form.*

*They are not in the same column.*

*I have got two columns and they have never once balanced and nobody on
this street has ever tried to make them.*

The persimmon outside the window had four leaves on it and the rest were
on the roof of the bathhouse.

At nine o'clock somebody hammered on the street door hard enough to be
heard on the second floor, and a voice that had never in its life
considered the hour or the neighbours shouted his name up the stairwell.
