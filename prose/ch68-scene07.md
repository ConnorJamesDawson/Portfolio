# Chapter 68, Scene 7 — Three Keepers

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1010 words.*

---

The Friday session convened with a larger register than any Friday
in its history: one slug on a dressed stone, one elder toad on a
folded cloth placed — after a negotiation conducted entirely in
courtesies — at the stone's exact altitude on a stacked pair of
kiln bricks, and one Sannin on the reject bin, which he had chosen
himself, inspected, pronounced *thematically correct*, and could
not now be dislodged from.

"The yard has never had visitors," said Katsuyu, "in fifteen
Fridays. I am given to understand by the manner of the arrival
that I am about to learn why."

He told her.

He told it in order, because she was owed the order: the month
sheet and the unchanged mass; the fourth reading; the question,
written and capped on her own instruction, eleven days under the
rule; Fukasaku's first answer — the oil, the medium, the garden
of statues, *Shikkotsu has no spring*; and then the uncapping,
and the sentence that now had three keepers, and the three terms
the eldest keeper had set on it in a frozen field.

Katsuyu was silent for a long time. The brazier ticked. Fukasaku
watched her with the particular patience of one mountain
regarding another.

"Four hundred years," she said at last, "we have answered the
same way. A slug cannot teach what a slug delivers whole — we
are a flood or we are absent, and so the road is shut, and we
tell them the road is shut, and the telling is the kindest thing
on the shelf. I have said it myself. I said it to *you*, in
December, in this yard: the only creature who could teach you
has never taught anybody anything." She moved, a slow settling,
the nearest her body came to squaring shoulders. "I should like
the record to note that I was accurate and wrong, which is the
combination this yard exists to detect. The teacher was never
going to be a creature. Slugs do not make springs. *Fabricators*
make springs. We have spent four centuries waiting for a
teacher, and what the road was waiting for was a wellwright."

"Years away," said Fukasaku, to nobody, in the tone of a man
laying a course of bricks. "With my veto on every shortcut."

"With his veto," Katsuyu agreed, "and mine beneath it, and I
will say the reason out loud so it is in the boy's book in
everybody's presence: Sazare. On the twentieth of December you
fell into the spring and lived. The garden at Myōboku is full
of the ones who did not, and your own right hand has been to
the door of that garden and back. The work is not secret
because it is a treasure. It is secret because every step of
it, wrongly hurried, is the square." She let that stand in the
cold air, plainly and without cruelty, the way she said
everything. "You have three keepers and a governor and a woman
in a tower. That is more supervision than any project in the
Land of Fire. It is approximately sufficient."

"Approximately," said Jiraiya, from the reject bin, "is the old
girl being generous. Kid — say the standard back. The one Pa
set."

"Not a weapon," said Sazare. "A syllabus. Not for me — I am the
toll somebody already paid. For whoever comes after, on a road
that has been shut since the world was young."

"That's the one," said Jiraiya. "That sentence is now the
project. Everything that serves it, we build. Anything that
only serves *you*, the toads and I will be impolite about. Ma's
oil made sages for four hundred years because the mountain
never once let it become a shortcut, and the mountain had the
advantage of not being eighteen and in a hurry." He stood,
stretched until something in his back reported, and grinned
the enormous grin. "Right. Week one's closed. Bruise count?"

"Eleven. Four load-bearing."

"Footwork?"

"Improved from insulting to merely embarrassing. Your words, at
Thursday."

"Decision located?"

"No," said Sazare. "Baseline holds. You said it would hold for
months and to stop auditing it weekly."

"And are you auditing it weekly?"

"I have moved the audit to fortnightly as a compromise with
myself, and I am declaring the compromise in front of
witnesses, which is the only enforcement mechanism that has
ever worked on me."

"Fortnightly," said Jiraiya, to Fukasaku, with the air of a
man displaying a prize vegetable. "Took me eleven years to
learn fortnightly. Week one, gentlemen. Same time Monday."

---

He walked up through the early dark afterwards, past the
lit second-floor window, doing the week's honesty at the pace
of the lane.

*Week one, closed. The field has a shape now: mornings for the
body and the decision, the answers arriving at Fukasaku's pace,
the spring under three keepers and a standard I can say in one
breath. The creed stands unrevised and surveyed, waiting for
the morning that earns it. The count stands at twenty-two, four
of them this week, one of them at a squid stall, and the
best of them through a bathtub.*

*The noons are the fixed point the whole week turns on. She
inspects bruises I do not have and I permit the inspection,
and neither of us has ever once mentioned that this is the
arrangement, because the diary says lunch, and the diary does
not lie, it merely underdescribes.*

*And the evenings are a syllabus nobody wrote, arriving one
increment at a time, from both directions now.*

*Monday she stopped sending me to the landing. Wednesday my
mouth asked her chest a structural question and survived, and
then my mother's hands came back from the water after eleven
years and dressed the Hokage's hair by lamplight, and the room
where the shop lives stood open an inch all night and nothing
was lost.*

*I have been in this village three years. I have been alive in
it since the eleventh of the eleventh.*

*Same time Monday. All of it. Every version of Monday there
is.*
