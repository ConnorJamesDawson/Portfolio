# Chapter 63, Scene 1 — The Slugs Have a View

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1990 words.*

---

He asked on the Monday evening, in the yard, at about eight, and he asked
badly because he had been building the sentence since Naruto had gone off
down the hill with his hand wrapped in a bit of cloth.

"There's a thing on Friday and I'd like you to come, and I want to be
clear at the front that Friday is your day and I am asking you to spend
it on something that is not this yard, and that is a different ask from
the ordinary one and you can say no to it separately."

"I know about Friday," said Katsuyu.

"...How?"

"I would like to sit down before I answer that."

She was already on the felt. She had been on the felt for eleven minutes.
He had learned in about the fourth week that this was a thing she said
when she was about to enjoy herself and had no way of showing it.

"Go on," he said.

---

"There is a wager," said Katsuyu.

"A what?"

"A wager. A bet. On the twentieth, on the ground, between yourself and
the Uzumaki boy." She settled slightly. "It was proposed by Myōboku on
the fourteenth and it has been accepted, and I want to say at the front
that Shikkotsu has never done this before in four hundred years and that
there was a great deal of discussion."

Sazare sat down on the yard stone.

"The toads are betting on a training match in Konoha."

"The toads are betting on a training match in Konoha."

"Between two humans."

"Between two humans, one of whom is on their contract and one of whom is
not on anybody's."

"How do they even know about it?"

"Fukasaku left this village on the second of the twelfth month," said
Katsuyu, "with a great deal in his head and nothing on paper, and he has
been at home for a fortnight with nobody to tell, and he is four hundred
years old and has just had the most interesting five days of his life.

"He wrote on the fourteenth. It was four lines and three of them were
about the wager."

---

"What are they betting *with*?"

Katsuyu did not answer for a moment.

"That was the discussion," she said.

"Go on."

"There is no currency. Myōboku has no use for anything we have and we
have no use for anything they have, and there is no arrangement of any
kind between the mountains and never has been, which is the entire
difficulty and which they got to about a line and a half in.

"So Shima proposed the stake."

"Which is?"

"The loser answers three questions," said Katsuyu, "put by the winner, in
person, truthfully and at length, at a time of the winner's choosing."

He looked at her.

"That's not a stake," he said. "That's the most valuable thing either of
them has got."

"Yes."

"Four hundred years of no contact and the first thing they have ever
arranged with each other is—"

"Yes," said Katsuyu.

"Whose idea was the questions?"

"Shima's."

"And the toads think they are getting three questions off Shikkotsu."

"The toads think they are getting three questions off Shikkotsu," Katsuyu
agreed, "and Shikkotsu thinks it is getting three off Myōboku, and I
have been listening to my elders for four days and I do not believe one
of them cares in the slightest who wins."

---

*Right.*

*Two mountains that have not spoken in four hundred years have invented a
reason to have to talk to each other afterwards, and both of them have
made sure that they lose nothing either way.*

*And they have hung the whole of it on a training match between a
sixteen-year-old and a man who has never been in a fight.*

*They are not betting. They are opening a channel and pretending it is a
bet so that nobody has to say* we would like to talk to you.

*Fukasaku sat in a room on the second of the twelfth month and said he
did not know what any of it was under, and had it minuted, in those
words, so that somebody in ninety years would know he did not know.*

*And then he went home and thought about it for twelve days and wrote
four lines.*

"Katsuyu."

"Yes."

"Which way are yours?"

"On you," said Katsuyu, "unanimously, and I want you to understand that
this is not a compliment. Not one of them has ever seen a human fight and
not one of them has any idea what the Uzumaki boy is, and they have all
gone the same way for the same reason, which is that you are the one they
have heard about."

"That's the worst possible basis for a bet."

"It is," said Katsuyu. "I said so. I was outvoted by a considerable
margin and one of them told me I was being *modern*."

---

He got the lamp lit and the felt straight and did about eleven minutes of
ordinary work before he came back to it, which he did because he had not
been able to put it down.

"So you'll come."

"I would have come on Friday regardless," said Katsuyu. "It is Friday.
And you have asked me and I have said yes and that is the fifteenth
time, and I would like it recorded that I would have said yes if there
had been no wager and no ground and no boy, and that you would not have
been able to tell the difference, and that this has been true since the
sixth Friday."

He wrote that down.

He did not decide to. He got the book out of his coat and wrote it on the
first clear page, sitting on a yard stone in the cold, and she waited
while he did it because she had waited about seventy times.

"There is a second thing," she said, when he had finished.

"Go on."

"I have been asked to convey something and I have decided how I am going
to do it, and my decision is that I am going to say it exactly as it was
given to me and let you have the whole of it."

"That sounds bad."

"It is not bad. It is only strange." Katsuyu paused. "It was said to me
by the third elder — the one who talks about moss — and she said it in
the ordinary way, as advice, as a thing everybody knows.

"She said: *the day you want to ask is a better day. Waiting is a way of
asking.*"

---

He did not move.

*Say it back.*

*The day you want to ask is a better day. Waiting is a way of asking.*

*That is not anything. That is a sentence with the shape of wisdom and no
content in it whatsoever, and it does not survive four seconds of being
looked at — waiting is not a way of asking, waiting is the absence of
asking, that is the whole distinction and it is the only interesting part
of the idea.*

*Which means somebody has broken it.*

*And to break a sentence you have to have had one.*

He put the pencil down very slowly.

*Chapter two.*

*Book one, chapter two, section four, and I wrote it in the ninth month
of last year at that low table with a lamp on, and I wrote it three times
because I could not get it short enough, and the version that went in
was:*

*Ask on the day you want to ask. Waiting for a better day is a way of not
asking.*

He sat on the yard stone.

"Katsuyu."

"Yes."

"Say it again."

She said it again, in exactly the same words, in the mild voice she used
for the fourth floor is holding.

"That's mine," he said.

---

She did not say anything for about four seconds.

"You are certain."

"It's chapter two. It's section four and it took me three attempts and I
have got the two rejected versions in the same book about nine lines
apart, and I could take you to the page in about a minute if the box were
not in a dye-works behind a hospital.

"And it is *wrong*. It has come apart. The whole of the thing was the
difference between not asking and waiting, and somebody has taken the
*not* out and it has turned into the opposite of itself and it now says
that putting a thing off is a form of doing it."

"Yes," said Katsuyu. "I can hear it now that you have said it. I could
not hear it before."

"How many people has it been through?"

"I do not know. I gave the book to the fourth elder in the ninth month
and she took it to Shikkotsu and it went into the room where things are
kept, and to the best of my knowledge nobody has opened it since."

"So it isn't the book."

"It is not the book," said Katsuyu. "The book is on a shelf and has not
been consulted. **I** read it aloud, over four evenings, to eleven of
them, because that is what we do, and after that it was theirs."

---

*Fourteen months.*

*Fourteen months and it has been through eleven of them and out again and
back to her by way of a third elder who thinks it is a thing everybody
knows, and the original is on a shelf four hundred miles away in perfect
condition and nobody has touched it.*

*I wrote a manual about how to be a person and it has become a proverb
that means the opposite of what it said, and the only correct copy in the
world is being kept safe by people who will never read it.*

He put his hands on his knees.

*And I gave it to them.*

*I gave it to them on purpose and I was pleased about it and I wrote down
that evening that I had given away the only thing I had ever made that
was not about an object.*

*A thing that reproduces cannot be recalled.*

*I wrote that in the eleventh month about a completely different problem
and I meant crystal, and I have got it underlined once in a book in a
dye-works, and I have just found out that I ran the experiment fourteen
months before I had the theory and did not notice it happening.*

"Are you going to correct it?" said Katsuyu.

"...No."

"May I ask why not."

"Because there are eleven of them and they have got it and it is theirs
now," said Sazare, "and if I write to the third elder and tell her that a
thing she has been saying for a year is a broken version of a sentence
written by a human she has never met, then I have not fixed a proverb, I
have taken one off somebody.

"And because I would have to be right about which version was better, and
I am fairly sure I am, and *fairly sure* is not enough to do that to a
four-hundred-year-old.

"And because—"

He stopped.

"Go on," said Katsuyu.

"Because I have got a Friday coming up," said Sazare, "and I asked you on
a Monday for it instead of waiting for a better day, and it did not occur
to me until this second that I have been doing what my own book said
without once having read it back.

"So it works. The original works. I have got about four hundred pages of
evidence that it works and every single page of it is me.

"And the broken one is what eleven creatures on a mountain are going to
be saying in two hundred years, and there is nothing I can do about
either of those facts, and I would like a minute."

She gave him a minute.
