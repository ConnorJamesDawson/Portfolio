# Chapter 31, Scene 4 — Reproducible

*Act 4, part C. Konoha, the walled range. Sazare Ruri, age sixteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1,458 words.*

---

They did the crystal test inside a walled range at the north end of the
grounds, on ground that had been dug over and levelled and then sown
with sixty pounds of river aggregate specifically so that it would
answer him, which Sazare found more unsettling than anything else that
happened that day.

Somebody had prepared a field for him. Somebody had sat at a desk and
worked out what he would need in order to demonstrate the thing that
had killed twenty-two people, and had requisitioned it, and it had come
on a cart.

Hyūga Natsumi was already there when he arrived, which he had expected,
and had brought a folding chair, which he had not.

She was perhaps twenty-eight, and Sazare looked at her properly for the
first time — in the street he had seen a Byakugan and a voice giving
instructions, and afterwards a report and an evidence seal, and had
never once assembled the person. She was tall and stood with her feet
turned very slightly out and her weight forward on the balls, which he
now recognised, four days after a chestnut stall, as a fact about
somebody who expects to move without warning and has stood that way so
long she can no longer stand any other. Her hair was cut off square at
the shoulder — recently, and by somebody competent — and her hands were
long and completely unmarked, no calluses anywhere, which given what
she did for a living meant she had never once needed to hold anything
tightly.

Her jacket was immaculate and about two years old. There was a
re-stitch at the left belt loop where a pouch had been torn away.

*Rung two: that came off in the street.*

*Rung three: she's kept the jacket.*

"You're staring at my loop," Natsumi said, without activating anything.

"Sorry."

"Don't apologise, it's in your file now. Two people have written down
that you do that." She unfolded the chair. "Sit for the briefing.
Ubukata's ceiling is a third and I have his figure and I intend to
enforce it, and I'd rather tell you that at the start than have an
argument in the middle."

---

The brief was short and the important part of it was not the part
Sazare had prepared for.

"Konoha does not need to know whether you can do it again," Natsumi
said. "We know you can do it again. I watched a hundred and eight
pathways leave a boy on a roof and I have thought about very little
else for four months." She set out nine seeded fragments on the
aggregate in a rough arc at ranges from six feet to about thirty. "We
need to know whether you can *stop*."

"The Hyūga in the street told me to stop and it took eleven seconds."

"The Hyūga in the street was me, and it took nineteen." She straightened
up. "Which I put in the report as *incomplete withdrawal* and which
somebody upstairs has read as *dangerous* and somebody else has read as
*acceptable in an emergency*, and both of them are guessing, because
there is no test in this village for the thing you do."

"So you've written one."

"I've written one this morning, on my own, badly, and I want you to
know that it's mine and not the village's, because if it's a bad test
you should say so and I would rather hear it from you than find out
from a corpse." She held out a sheet of paper with a diagram on it in
her own hand, and Sazare read it, and it was a good test.

Nine fragments. Activate all nine. Then, on a called signal, shut down
a specific numbered subset while holding the rest — and then, on a
second signal, shut down everything.

"The subset is the point," he said.

"The subset is the whole point. Anybody can stop. Stopping is easy;
stopping is falling over. I want to know whether you can stop *some*."

---

He did it four times and the fourth was the one that mattered.

The first run, all nine came up in about a second and a half, and the
subset — four, six and seven — went down in under two seconds each,
and everything else went down on the second signal in about four.

"That's better than the street by a factor of five," Natsumi said.

"It's nine fragments on flat ground in a walled range with nobody
throwing anything at me."

"I'm aware. Write that down, Fuchida." The clerk wrote it. "Again."

The second run he did with his eyes shut, which she had not asked for
and did not object to. The third he did with Natsumi calling the subset
while the growth was still rising, which cost him nearly six seconds on
the shutdown and produced a shear on fragment two that neither of them
had intended.

The fourth run, she put a person in the field.

She did it without warning — walked into the arc herself between the
signal and the growth, in the two-second window, and stood at eleven
feet with three live fragments inside a stride of her feet.

Sazare stopped everything within about a foot and a half of her in
under a second and did not stop anything else, and then discovered that
his hands were shaking and that he had been holding his breath for
long enough to see it at the edges.

Natsumi did not move for four seconds. Then she stepped out of the arc,
and said, to the clerk, in exactly the voice she had used all morning:

"Exclusion held. One point five feet, under one second, unwarned. Note
that I entered without notice and that this was not on the sheet."

"You could have told me," Sazare said.

"Yes."

"Ubukata's ceiling—"

"Was not the risk I was taking." She turned. Her face had gone, he
noticed, the particular colour that faces go about a minute after
somebody has decided to do something and then done it. "You have
maintained an exclusion around a living person once, in a street, under
load, while dying of chakra exhaustion, and it is the single most
important fact in your entire file and there is no version of me
signing off on it because you told me it happened."

"So you walked into it."

"I walked into it." She folded the chair. "I'd have done it whoever you
were. Don't make it about you."

---

The honest part came at the end, and he gave it unprompted, because
Kanzaki had trained him for eleven months to give exactly this part
unprompted.

"You'll want to record that none of this tells you what you want to
know."

Natsumi looked up from the sheet. "Go on."

"Nine fragments is nothing. In the street there were sixty-three that
were recoverable and an unknown number that weren't, and the shutdown
problem isn't linear, because the reason nineteen seconds happened is
that some of the routes had caught in structures that had found fresh
mineral and were feeding themselves." He said it flatly, the way he
had learned to say everything of this kind. "Nine, I can stop. Thirty,
I don't know. A street, I have one data point and it was bad, and the
right answer is that nobody in this village — including me — knows
whether I can turn that off, and the only way to find out is a test
that I would refuse to perform inside a populated area and you should
refuse to authorise."

There was a pause.

"Fuchida," Natsumi said. "Take that down verbatim, and when you write
it up I want it in the summary and not the appendix."

"Verbatim?"

"Including *I don't know*. Twice." She capped her own pen. "Ruri. One
question, off the sheet."

"Yes?"

"On the roof. When you cut the exclusion around Ueda — the man with the
torn shoulder, the one you thought might be dead." She was watching him
with an ordinary pair of eyes, which he found more difficult than the
alternative. "You lost every fragment beyond him for a second when you
did that. I saw it happen. Did you know you were going to?"

"No."

"Would you have done it if you had?"

Sazare thought about it properly, because she had asked it properly.

"Yes," he said. "But I'd have hesitated, and the hesitation would have
cost about a second, and I don't know what a second was worth at that
point in the street."

Natsumi wrote that down too, and did not tell him whether it was the
right answer, and he understood on the walk back that she had not
known either.
