# Chapter 61, Scene 8 — The Name Nobody Says

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~2280 words.*

---

He took it up that evening and put the copy on the desk and said, "Fifth
sheet is the ask and the fourth sheet is the thing you need to read
first," and then went and stood at the window and did not watch either
of them read it.

Jiraiya got to the fourth sheet about a minute before she did, because
he read faster and did not care about being seen to.

He said one word. It was not a long word.

Then Tsunade got there.

He heard the paper stop.

---

She did not say anything for some time.

"Ruri."

"I'm all right."

"Come here."

"I'd rather stand at the window for a bit, if that's all right, and I'd
rather nobody was kind about it for about four minutes, because I have
been managing it since two o'clock and it is holding and I do not want
it disturbed."

"Right," said Tsunade.

She let him have the four minutes.

*It is a fact about the world and it does not care.*

*It has been true since before I was born. There has been a woman of
about thirty in the Land of Sound for the whole of my life and she was
there in the ninth year when a man in a shed told me I was the last one,
and she was there when I got to that gate at fifteen, and she was there
last week while I was scraping a floor.*

*Nothing has changed. Not one thing in the world has changed today.*

*The only thing that has changed is that I have found out, and the whole
of what I am feeling is about the finding out, and if I am not extremely
careful about that distinction I am going to spend a fortnight being
angry at a letter.*

*And I am not going to be able to be careful about it, because I am
eighteen and it is the largest fact about me and it has just been
corrected by an enemy in a subordinate clause.*

---

"Right," he said. "Go on."

"How much of it do you want?" said Tsunade.

"All of it. In whatever order you've got it."

"He's telling the truth."

"Yes."

"You're sure?"

"He hasn't got a reason to lie about it and he has got about four
reasons not to," said Sazare. "A lie there is a lie I could find out
about, eventually, at his cost, and he does not waste a line and he does
not spend credibility on something decorative.

"And he flagged the motive himself. *I am aware it is not free of
motive.* Nobody puts that in unless the rest is straight."

"Then it's true and you've got a live one four hundred miles away."

"Yes."

"Which changes about eleven things and one of them is Kudō's claim,"
said Tsunade, "and I am not going to make you look at any of that
tonight."

---

Jiraiya put the fifth sheet down.

"He doesn't want crystal," he said.

"No."

"He's said so in the first line, which is the single most expensive
place he could have put it."

"That's why I believe it," said Sazare.

"Aye. Me too, and I don't want to." Jiraiya sat back. "So say the rest.
You've had six hours on it and you've been standing at that window with
your shoulders up since you came in."

"He's got a Shōton user in the building."

"He has."

"So why is he writing to me?"

---

He came away from the window.

"That's the whole letter. Everything else is packaging and the packaging
is very good and I nearly missed it twice.

"He has got the best crystal user in the world under his hand. He has
seen a great deal of her work. It is combat work and it is very fine.

"And he wrote one line about her that he did not need to write.

"*I do not believe she has ever asked why it does not last.*

"That is not gossip. He does not put a line in a letter for
decoration — I have got four exchanges off him and there is not one
spare sentence in any of them.

"He was not telling me she exists. He was telling me what she *can't
do*.

"She uses it. She has used it for thirty years. She is better at using
it than I will ever be and I would put money on that and I have never
seen her work. And she cannot tell him one single thing about why it
holds or why it goes, because she has never once needed to ask, because
it has always simply worked for her.

"He has looked at that woman and he has looked at four Corps papers by
an eighteen-year-old in a hospital in a hostile country, and he has
decided that the eighteen-year-old is the one who has got the thing he
wants.

"It's not a compliment. It is a man establishing which of the two of us
he needs, and he has told me the answer in order to make the ask land,
and it worked, and I have been sitting on a step in the cold since two
o'clock and it is still working."

---

"There's a fourth thing," he said, "and it's the one I actually came up
here about."

"Go on."

"Second sheet."

He put a finger on it.

"He answered a question about a splint cohort and about two hundred
words of the answer are not about the splint. He has gone from the
interval to the reason for the interval to *why is the interval the same
across three unrelated materials*, and then he has stopped.

"That's page eight."

Jiraiya swore.

"That's the six-day problem," said Sazare. "That's the whole of it. It
took me four years and a rose and a hand and I have never written one
word of it to him, and he has got there off published papers and one
question about a cohort, and he is about three months behind me and
closing.

"And the letter was written on the thirtieth, so it is not about the
second, and the arithmetic is clean and I have done it four times.

"It does not matter that it's clean.

"He was three months behind me on the thirtieth of the eleventh month.
He does not have to know anything about what happened in this building
to get there on his own. He is going to get there.

"And when he gets to *the close is the mechanism*, he is one step from
where I was on the twenty-first, and about two from where we all were on
the second."

---

Nobody said anything for a while.

"Tenzō," said Jiraiya.

Tsunade did not move.

"Say it," she said.

"You know what I'm going to say."

"I know what you're going to say and I want it said in front of the boy
and in front of me, out loud, because I have been not saying it for nine
days and it has not helped."

Jiraiya put both hands on the table.

"We have got one instance," he said. "One. Nought point one one grams,
five witnesses, a minute book — and the man who made it is a sage.

"So the first thing anybody with a brain says when they finally read
that page is: *senjutsu did it.* Not that a bloodline is a technique.
That a sage did an unusual thing with natural energy, which is what sages
are for, and that it tells you nothing about clans at all.

"And I would say it. If somebody handed me that minute cold I would say
it inside about four seconds and I would be *right* to say it, because
one instance with a confound in it is not a finding, and the boy has
written four hundred pages about exactly that."

"Yes," said Sazare.

"There's one case in the world that closes it."

"Yes."

"Say the name, then," said Jiraiya. "One of us has got to and I have
been in this village nine days."

"Tenzō," said Sazare.

---

He said it and the room did not change and that was somehow worse.

"Mokuton," he said, "in a man who has no line and never had one. Not
learned. *Put there* — by a procedure, deliberately, by a person we can
all name, on sixty children, of whom he is the only one who lived.

"He is walking around this village.

"He is the only human being alive who is a demonstration that the
property can be moved into somebody it did not belong to, and he did not
consent to any of it, and he was about six."

He stopped.

"And there is no confound in him," he said. "That's the whole point.
He's not a sage. He is not remarkable in any way that would give a
sceptic a second explanation. He is a man with wood in him that was put
there by somebody else and he is the cleanest piece of evidence that has
ever existed about anything.

"And I am not going near him."

---

"You'll have to," said Jiraiya.

"Not this year."

"Kid—"

"Give me the price," said Sazare, "and I'll tell you why not this year,
and then I'd like to stop talking about it, because I have got a letter
to answer and I have been putting it off since two o'clock.

"One. He is Root, or he was, and the difference between those two is a
question I am not qualified to have an opinion about, and the man who
would have to be gone through has a line that runs to the Five Kage
Summit and has been quiet since the ninth month, and the quiet is the
news.

"Two. Anything I ask for goes across that man's desk. Not officially.
There is no official. It goes across it anyway and both of you know it.

"And three, and it is the only one of the three that matters.

"I would be asking a man to *be evidence*.

"Not to help. Not to collaborate. To stand in a room and let people
establish a fact about the world using the worst thing that ever
happened to him, and to do it in front of witnesses, with a weight on
it, and a minute.

"Sixty children. He is the one that lived, and everybody in this village
has agreed for twenty years not to look at him about it, and I have got
about nine hundred pages on what it is like to be the last one, and I
found out this afternoon that I am not even that."

He put his hands on the back of the chair.

"So no. Not this year, and not by letter, and not through anybody.

"When it happens, it is going to be me in a room with him, on my own,
with him having been told exactly what I want and exactly what it costs
and having had a month to think about it. And if he says no then it is a
no and the finding stays at one instance with a confound in it and I
live with that."

Tsunade said, "That is the correct answer."

"It's the correct answer and it has got a clock on it now," said
Sazare, "and the clock started on the thirtieth of last month, and I
cannot see the face of it."

---

Jiraiya went at about nine and Shizune had gone at seven and the office
was quiet.

"Right," said Tsunade. "The ask."

"Yes."

"Are you going to answer it?"

Sazare stood in the middle of the room and did not answer for some time.

"There's a thing about the ask," he said, "and I got it at about four
o'clock and I have not said it to anybody yet because I wanted to be
sure.

"He has asked me for the grammar. How it is structured in my head. Four
paragraphs and he will be grateful, and if I cannot say then that is an
answer and the fee is paid.

"And I can't say. That is completely true. I have never been able to
say. I have been unable to say it since I was sixteen and I was unable
to say it to Jiraiya on the eighteenth and I stood in a long room on the
twenty-eighth and told two mountains that what I had to hand anybody was
the place where a technique is not.

"So *I cannot say* is a true sentence and it has been true my whole
life."

"But."

"But we found out on the second."

He looked at the desk.

"There is no grammar. That's the finding. There is nothing in my head to
describe, and the reason nobody can be taught it from me is that there
is nothing to move — and what you do instead is find what the other
person has got that I haven't and take it *out*.

"That is an answer to his question. It is the complete answer. It is
four paragraphs and I could write it tonight and it would be the best
thing I have ever written to him and it would be entirely true.

"And it is the second of the twelfth month, and it is the one thing in
the world that does not leave this building.

"So the honest answer and the forbidden answer are the same answer," he
said. "And *I cannot say* has been true for eleven years and it stopped
being true nine days ago.

"And I am going to write it anyway."
