# Chapter 57, Scene 8 — Stone

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower roof. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1730 words.*

---

"There are three roads," said Jiraiya. "Three places a human being can
learn it, and there have only ever been three, and I have been to one of
them and Orochimaru went to another and you have had the third one come
to *you*, in a yard, on a Friday, for free, which has never happened in
the recorded history of any of it."

"Mount Myōboku."

"Toads. Yes. That's mine, and I'll tell you what it teaches in one word
and it's not a flattering one. It teaches you *yourself*. You go up there
and you sit still and you find out exactly what you are and how much of
it there is, and everything you get out of it comes out of that."

"And the snakes?"

"Ryūchi. Taking." Jiraiya said it without any particular weight.
"Everything that place teaches is about getting a thing out of something
else and into you and keeping it. I'm not going to pretend that's a
neutral description and I'm not going to pretend I'm impartial about the
man who went there."

"And Shikkotsu."

"Shikkotsu is the world," said Jiraiya. "That's the whole difference. The
toads teach a man to know what he is. The slugs don't care what you are.
They teach you to hold what's out there, and to give it out again, and
their entire art is *capacity* — how much can you carry, how long, how
many ways can you divide it before it stops being useful.

"That's why they heal. It isn't a medical tradition, it's a consequence."

---

*That is the first sentence anybody has ever said to me that explains
Katsuyu.*

*Nineteen evenings. Not one of them about power. Every single one about
holding — how much, how long, how many at once, and the exercise which is
a sphere and a count and nothing else, and I have gone from two to
fifteen and never once asked what the number was for.*

*Capacity. Not strength.*

*It has been the answer to the whole of it for four months and I have
been standing inside it.*

"The gather's the same," said Jiraiya. "That's the useful bit for you.
Whatever road you come up, the gather is the gather — you go still, it
comes in. Toads, snakes, slugs, doesn't matter.

"What's different is what you're taught to do with it once it's in, and
what happens to you if you get it wrong."

He looked at Sazare.

"Which brings me to the thing I actually want to ask you, and I've been
working up to it for about ten minutes."

---

"Has it ever gone wrong?"

Sazare did not answer immediately.

"Define wrong."

"No," said Jiraiya. "No, kid. I'm not letting you have that one. You know
what I'm asking. Has it ever gone wrong."

"Yes."

"When?"

"Fifteenth of the eighth month," said Sazare. "Twenty-two twenty. It's in
the book and I can give you the whole of it."

"Give me the whole of it."

---

"I was matching a Mokuton article. A rose, nine days old, saturated — one
of Tenzō's, off a windowsill, and the protocol was to hold the reference
and try to make my own alongside it, and the prediction was nothing and
the risk was *none identified*, underlined.

"It worked. I got nine inches of stem out of my own palm at half past
ten.

"And about four minutes after that my left hand started going to stone
from the fingertips."

Jiraiya went absolutely still.

"Painless," said Sazare. "That's the thing everybody gets wrong about it
— it doesn't hurt at any point, there's no warning at all, and the only
reason I noticed was that I put my hand on a bench and the sound was
wrong.

"It moved up. It was decelerating and it was not certainly self-limiting.
It reached the mid-metacarpal in eighteen minutes and it was staggered —
further up the index and middle, and it never touched the thumb.

"I went up the hill. She came down the stair at a run and worked on it
for six hours and arrested it at the carpus, and I have got an
eighty-five per cent hand and a patch on the palm that isn't skin and
never will be.

"And she didn't know what it was. She said *I know what it's called* and
she said it in a particular way and I have thought about that voice for
three months.

"I filed it as a crystal accident. Contact with a saturated article.
Over-draw through an open route."

---

Jiraiya put his hands over his face.

He held them there for about four seconds and then took them off, and he
did not look amused about anything at all.

"That was not a crystal accident," he said.

"I—"

"Kid. *Listen to me.*"

He got off the parapet and crouched down in front of him again.

"There is one way a sage dies. One. It is not in combat and it is not
exhaustion and it's not anything you'd put in a report.

"You take in more than you can hold, and you can't spend it, and it
doesn't go away.

"And you turn to stone.

"From whatever's nearest the source, outward, painlessly, and you can
watch it happen to your own arm, and it does not stop because you want
it to. That's the ceiling on the whole art and it's the reason there are
four of us. It is not that senjutsu is difficult. It is that most of the
people who tried it are *statues*, and some of them are still standing
where they were when it took them, and I have seen two of them, and one
of them was a man I liked."

He tapped the roof.

"Fifteenth of the eighth month. Nine days of natural energy in a
saturated article, and you held it, and you drew off it while you
matched it, and your route was open. That's not an over-draw through a
crystal pathway.

"That's the ceiling. You hit the ceiling, at seventeen, on a bench, on
your own, four months before anybody told you the word for what you
are.

"And you should be a statue."

---

Sazare sat on a roof.

*Right.*

*Do not rush this.*

*He is right. I know he is right, immediately, in the way I knew the
diamond was a seal, and I do not have the step — I have the recognition
and I will find the step later or not at all.*

*It explains the thumb.*

*Fifteenth of the eighth, staggered, further on the index and middle,
thumb spared, and I have had that as an unexplained detail in a margin
for three months. The thumb was not on the article. The other four were
and the thumb was not, and the line moved in proportion to* contact*,
which is not how a chakra pathway propagates and is exactly how an
intake does.*

*I wrote that down. I wrote it down and I put a bracket round it and I
did not have a hypothesis and it has been sitting there since the
sixteenth of the eighth month, and the correct hypothesis was one I
could not form because I did not have the word.*

*And the reason it stopped at the carpus is that a woman worked on it for
six hours.*

*She did not know what it was and she stopped it anyway.*

"Why am I not?" he said.

"Not what?"

"A statue."

---

"Two reasons," said Jiraiya, "and one of them's her, and I'll come to
that, and the other one is going to annoy you.

"You close.

"You've done it since you were a child, haven't you. Nobody taught you,
you just do it — you finish the operation and you shut, straight away,
same movement, and you have never in your life left a route open on
purpose."

"...Yes."

"That's why you're alive. That reflex is the only thing between you and
a plinth and you built it at about seven years old for reasons that had
nothing to do with any of this."

"It's also," said Sazare slowly, "the reason everything I make is dead by
Friday."

Jiraiya blinked.

"Say that again."

"The close is why my work degrades. It's the whole of the six-day
problem. I lay the structure and then I shut the route, and what I've
built is a sealed jar with a finite amount in it, and it runs out, and
there is nothing I can do about it because the move that keeps me alive
is the same move.

"It's on page eight."

"I read page eight."

"You read it as an engineering problem."

"I read it as an engineering problem," Jiraiya agreed, "because that is
how it is written, and because the man who wrote it did not know he was
describing his own throat."

---

He sat back down on the parapet and was quiet for a bit.

"I'll tell you the price of the rest of this," he said, "because you're
going to get it off me for nothing and I'd rather you knew what it
costs.

"I can't do it clean."

Sazare looked up.

"Thirty years," said Jiraiya. "Mount Myōboku, since I was a young man,
and I am one of four people alive and I cannot gather it on my own and
hold it and fight at the same time. It's too slow and I'm not still
enough and I never have been.

"So I carry two old toads on my shoulders and they gather for me and
feed it in, and it works, and it is the finest thing I have got, and it
is *assisted*.

"And when I take it I get the eyes and I get the — you'll see it, and
you'll be polite about it, and I'd rather you weren't.

"There has never been a clean version of me. There isn't going to be.
I'm fifty-four."

He looked out at the village.

"So when I tell you what that road costs, kid, understand that I'm not
being modest and I'm not fishing. I'm the best there is at it and I'm
the worst example of it, and both of those are true at once, and I am
telling an eighteen-year-old on a roof at six in the morning because
that is what this information costs and somebody should have paid it for
me."
