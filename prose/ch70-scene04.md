# Chapter 70, Scene 4 — An Embarrassment to the Profession

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Tsunade, age
fifty-three. POV: Tsunade. ~1070 words.*

---

She diagnosed herself on the Wednesday, in the register nobody
read, because the symptoms had reached the threshold where a
medic either charts them or surrenders the licence.

*Presentation. Female, fifty-three, previously robust.*

*Onset: insidious, approximately three weeks, coinciding with —
be honest, doctor, honesty is the whole of this register — with
the patient's own successful re-engineering of one boy's entire
waking life.*

*Symptoms, in escalating order of professional shame:*

*The patient has begun timing the hair ritual. Not enjoying it —
she has always enjoyed it — TIMING it, watching the fifty passes
approach their end the way she once watched the last of a
winning streak, because the passes are now the whole of the
day's talk and the talk is now four sentences and the four
sentences are worth more to her than the entire border digest.*

*The patient reads his diary block in the mornings. She wrote
half the entries in it herself, or authorised them, or chairs
them, and she reads them anyway, the way you re-read a letter
you already know, looking for a gap that isn't there. There is
no gap. She has checked. She built it not to have gaps.*

*The patient has developed a documented hostility toward a
brassiere line named in her own honour. It gets his hands all
afternoon. She is jealous of a supply chain. The line is a
dedication to her and she wants to set its kilns on fire. The
patient recognises that this is not the behaviour of the fifth
Hokage of the Land of Fire. The patient no longer cares.*

*The patient is keeping a collection. Fourteen statements to
date, issued by the subject from the borderlands of sleep,
uncertified, unwitnessed, ranging from the anatomical to the —
last night's ran "they should print your" and terminated. Print
my WHAT. On what document. The patient has spent professional
hours on the question. The patient will be taking the question
to her grave.*

*Diagnosis: pining. Textbook, florid, first presentation in
this patient since age approximately fourteen, an interval of
thirty-nine years, during which she has buried the two men she
permitted herself and arranged her entire subsequent life so as
never to be exposed to the pathogen again.*

*And the exposure route, when it finally got her, was a
seventeen-year-old with a reject table. Eighteen. He was
eighteen by then. The register will note the patient checked.*

*Prognosis: chronic. Treatment: available, contraindicated.
See below. Christ, it's embarrassing.*

---

The treatment section was the part she kept coming back to,
because the treatment section was the trap, and she had built
the trap herself, and it was a *good* trap, which was the
insulting part.

She could fix it in one sentence. That was the plain fact she
lived with now, nightly, at the window she was not standing at.
Shizune ran the diary. The board answered to her. The training
order carried her signature — both of her signatures, the
office's and the other one. One word — *lighten it* — and the
machine that ate his evenings would gear down by Friday, and he
would never even know a hand had moved, because the hand moved
everything; that was what the hand was *for*.

And she had sat with the treatment on the desk in front of her,
twice, and both times the same three clauses had closed over
her like a governor.

*Clause one: every hour of that schedule is real. The mornings
keep him alive — the mornings are the difference between the
boy Itachi looks through and the man Itachi has to plan for,
and spring is coming, and the lists go up whether I pine or
not. The line pays a debt to half my roster. The spring opens
a road four hundred years shut. There is no fat in it. I would
be cutting muscle, his, to feed a want, mine.*

*Clause two: he chose all of it. Chose it, built it, defends
it line by line, is HAPPY — happier in the work than I have
ever seen a human being, tired the way a man is tired when
every hour lands somewhere that matters. The thing I fell for
is the engine. Throttling the engine to keep the warmth nearer
the seat is how you end up holding a machine that hums and
does nothing, and I have read that file. I have WRITTEN that
file, about medics' husbands, in this exact register.*

*Clause three, the one with the teeth: if I use the office for
this — if the Hokage reaches into a man's docket to procure
her own evenings — then everything I signed becomes suspect
backwards and forwards. The training order, the board, the
attachment, the cover: every document with both our names near
it is currently, rigorously, defensibly REAL, and that realness
is the whole architecture. He taught me the principle himself,
with a paper in a coat pocket: the corruption is never the
document. It is the gap between what you take and what would
have been given. There is no gap in our files. I am not
opening one to get my boyfriend back.*

*So: treatment contraindicated. Patient advised to endure
symptomatically.*

*Patient's response to advice, recorded verbatim: the patient
is fifty-three years old, can bench-press the gate, has
governed a military power through a war winter, and is lying
in the dark composing a symptoms list because a boy fell
asleep in the middle of calling her beautiful.*

*An embarrassment to the profession of grown women. Chart
closed. Reopened. One addendum.*

*Addendum: the warmth is not gone. The register will carry
that in full, because the register is where I keep what is
true when the rest of me is being a teenager about it: nothing
is WRONG. He has not drifted one degree. The hands still find
me in his sleep; the ritual has not missed a night; the
fourteen uncertified statements are, if anything, escalating
in ambition. The warmth is not gone. It is unstaffed. Every
department that used to run it has been seconded to the war
effort, and the war effort was my idea, and I approved every
secondment personally.*

*Which is why this is pining and not grievance. A grievance
has an address. This just has a window.*

*And I am NOT standing at it. That, at least, is still true.
Ask the chair. The chair and I have become very close.*
