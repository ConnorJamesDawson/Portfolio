# Chapter 71, Scene 9 — Two Pages

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the second floor. Tsunade, age
fifty-three. POV: Tsunade. ~1040 words.*

---

The court reconvened upstairs, in dry clothes, with tea, because
some evidence could not be examined in a bathtub, and the
evidence in question was two pages long and currently being
surrendered by the defendant with the particular reluctance of
a man handing over not contraband but *drafts*.

"The notes are in the back," said Sazare. "The corner folds are
structural, not sentimental. Except one."

She took the small orange book into custody, sat cross-legged
on the bed with it under the good lamp, and read, while its
owner sat at the desk in a state of visible administrative
distress, watching his marginalia meet its subject.

The two pages were the fourteen faults, numbered, in the
smallest of his hands, and reading them was — she had expected
comedy, and there was comedy; what she had not expected, and
should have, was that reading a dirty book's margins would
turn out to be the closest thing to reading his mind she had
ever been permitted.

*Fault three: the injury on p.61 is written as pleasurable.
It is a cervical strain. The author has confused a gasp's
causes. A medic would stop the scene.* — and she remembered,
vividly, across twenty years, throwing this exact book at a
wall for this exact page.

*Fault seven: the plumbing (p.88, the "endless" hot spring)
implies a nine-hundred-ryō repair recurring monthly. The
innkeeper's economics do not survive the romance.*

*Fault nine: five performances in seven hours (p.101) at the
male lead's stated age of forty-one is not romance. It is a
medical presentation warranting bloodwork.* — she laughed out
loud at that one, the full bark, and watched him flinch and
then watch her, hungrily, cataloguing the entry, forty-nine
or fifty by now, she had lost her own count of his counting.

And then the three that were not faults, at the bottom of the
second page, in a hand smaller still:

*Note: ch. 2 — the four apologies and four forgivenesses are
a procedure. It works. Verified.*

*Note: ch. 11 — a person who means a thing says it once.
Saying it twice is written here as meaning it more. It is
not. It is a repair.*

*Note: ch. 14 — the woman who cannot ask is the best-written
person in the book and nobody in the book notices her,
including the author. Somebody should write her the whole
story.*

She sat with the last one for some time.

*He read a dirty book,* she wrote later, in the register, *the
way he reads everything — and it made him kinder. Fourteen
faults and three mercies, and the mercies are the finding: he
walked into the most cynical genre alive and came out carrying
the one character nobody else could see. The filth was never
in the book's reach. You cannot corrupt a man who audits.*

The verdict she delivered from the bed, formally, with the
book closed in her lap.

"Judgement. On the charge of reading filth: guilty, with the
court's compliments on the annotations. On the charge of
running a courtship out of its chapters: guilty, and the
court has reviewed the outcomes—" she touched the shelf
beside her, the pinwheel, the brush, without looking,
because her hand knew where everything was— "and rules the
method sound and the results exceptional. Sentence:
suspended, on two conditions. One: the collection stands —
the book lives on that shelf now, with the rest of the
archive. Evidence goes where evidence goes; you taught me
that. And two—" and here the court permitted itself, at
last, the smile it had been suppressing through the entire
proceeding, the slow one, the one with weather in it—
"chapter twelve. The scene you could never finish reading.
One day — not tonight, fabricator; on a date the court will
choose, without notice, when your composure has had time
to grow back and can be properly harvested — you are going
to read it to me. Aloud. In the bath. To the end of the
page. THAT is the sentence, and there is no appeal, and
the court advises you to train for it, because the court
intends to heckle."

She watched the sentence land — watched him run the full
projection, the bath, the book, the voice, her, and arrive
at the only true answer, which was the deep end, again,
always, forever:

"Accepted," said Sazare, faintly, pink to the hairline.
"The court is merciless."

"The court is *inspired*. Now." She set the book on the
shelf herself — next to the brush, spine out, orange
against the lacquer, the most disreputable exhibit in the
museum of them, and it looked, she thought, exactly right
there, which she would deny under seal — and turned back
with the last item of the docket, the one her voice went
quiet for. "Final question, and the trial's over. The
book didn't walk into your coat. Chapter and verse,
Sazare: where did you get it?"

And she watched the last calculation he would make that
night move behind his eyes — the swift honest arithmetic
of a man who did not lie and had one friend to protect
and knew, absolutely knew, what the answer cost — and
watched him choose the record, because he always chose
the record.

"Jiraiya-sama. The twenty-seventh of the twelfth. Behind
the east bathhouse." A breath. "He said — his words —
that I would need it, because he had heard I was a bad
kisser."

The lamp burned. The tower was very quiet.

"Did he," said Tsunade, in a voice like the ground
before the ground comes alive, and reached for her coat.

"Hime, it is eleven at night—"

"Oh, not *tonight*," said the Hokage of the Land of
Fire, hanging the coat back up with terrifying calm and
climbing into bed like a front moving in from the coast.
"Tonight I am extremely busy being fond of you. Tomorrow
is Friday, fabricator. He'll be in a field at six sharp,
with witnesses, on ground I closed once before." She
pulled him down into the dark by one wrist, arranging
him where the arrangement went, her hands finding the
shirt, the last word arriving against his shoulder with
a satisfaction so profound it was nearly purring.

"Attendance is mandatory. Bring your notebook. The
count is about to get an entry you'll want in ink."
