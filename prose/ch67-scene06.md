# Chapter 67, Scene 6 — Row One

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the evening market by the east wall. Sazare
Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~920 words.*

---

He executed the first deliberate entry on the Sunday evening, and he
executed it with the two disciplines the study had cost him a
nosebleed to learn: no theatre, and no protocol.

"There is a stall by the east wall," he said, at the top of the
tower stairs, in his coat, holding hers. "It sells grilled squid on
skewers to people who eat it standing up in the cold for no reason
anybody has ever documented. I have walked past it for three years
and priced it forty times and never once bought anything. I would
like to go and eat squid standing up in the cold for no reason. With
you. That is the entire plan. There is no second part."

Tsunade looked at him for a moment with her arms in the sleeves.

"No second part," she said.

"None. I checked the plan four times for a second part. It is
structurally free of purpose. That is the point of it, apparently.
I am told the small rows do not generate paperwork."

"Told by whom?"

"By an inventory I conducted against myself on Thursday night," he
said, which was true, and complete, and wore its truth the way his
sentences always wore it, and if one small orange consultant had
gone unminuted in the account, the account survived audit anyway,
because omission, as established across three years and one
intelligence file, was the single discipline in which he had never
required instruction.

"Squid," said the Hokage of the Land of Fire, and took her coat.

---

The stall was where it had always been, hissing under its awning in
the blue cold, run by a broad woman with forearms like a
boat-builder and a scowl she wore the way other people wore aprons,
and the evening trade stood around its brazier in the universal
posture — shoulders in, skewer up, faces lit from below like
conspirators.

Nobody recognised her. That was the east wall in January: dock
workers off the timber yards, two chūnin too cold to gossip, a
grandmother supervising a boy through his first whole squid. The
hood did some of it and the sheer unlikelihood did the rest —
context is a henge, she had said once on a ferry, and the context
of a Hokage is never, ever a squid stall.

He paid — *he* paid, eleven ryō the skewer, the first food he had
ever bought her in his life, entry one in a column three years
empty, and he handed the skewer across with both hands the way the
Corps handed over instruments, and if she noticed the ceremony
underneath the gesture she gave no sign beyond taking it with both
of hers.

The squid was good. It would have been enough for it to be warm,
but it was actually good — charred at the edges, sweet glaze,
smoke — and they stood in the crowd of strangers with their
shoulders in and their skewers up, two conspirators lit from below,
eating standing up in the cold for no reason, and the no-reason
held. He checked it twice, from inside, the way he had checked the
day at Tarugi: no docket. No audit at the end of it. Nothing this
evening was for anything.

"You've stopped surveying the awning," she said, around squid.

"The awning is somebody else's problem."

"Three years," said Tsunade, to the brazier, wonderingly, "and I've
finally got him saying it about *awnings*."

And the boy at the next station over — the grandmother's boy, six
or seven, three-quarters through the whole squid that was plainly
the largest undertaking of his year — chose that moment to lose the
final piece off his skewer to gravity, watched it land in the
brazier coals with the whole of a small face's available tragedy,
and pronounced, with feeling, to the market at large:

"*Bollocks.*"

The grandmother's swat arrived on schedule. The stall woman's scowl
broke first, which by the crowd's reaction was a documented
never-event, and Tsunade — Tsunade put her skewer hand on Sazare's
shoulder and *went*, the real one, the past-all-denominations one,
the sound from under the bathwater now unfiltered and at full
amplitude in the open air of a market where nobody knew she was
anybody, and he stood very still under her hand and received it the
way the frames had received the morning sun.

*Eighteen,* he logged, at the bottom of a count that had started on
a Thursday. *In the wild. On purpose is arguable — I did not
produce the boy — but I produced the evening, and the evening
produced the boy, and I am ruling it admissible. The methodology
section can argue with me when it gets home.*

"His grandmother," said Tsunade, wiping her eye with the back of
her skewer hand, "is going to hear that word at every meal for a
year, and it is going to be my fault for laughing, and I regret
*nothing*, I want that minuted—"

"Minuted," said Sazare.

They walked back the long way, which was the first time in his life
he had ever taken a long way on purpose, and it was only at the
tower door, with the cold in their coats and the glaze still on
their fingers, that she stopped, and looked at him under the lamp,
and said, in the register she signed things in:

"Row one."

*She read the column headings off a bath conversation a day later
and has been carrying the format since.*

*Of course she has.*

"Row one," said Sazare, and held the door.
