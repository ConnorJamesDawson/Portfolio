# Chapter 49, Scene 4 — Things She Assumed Were Obvious

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,812 words.*

---

He wrote her up on the Thursday evening, at the bench, in the ordinary
way, because everything got written up.

*Subject: Katsuyu. Three days. Observations on the observer.*

*Interest appears genuine and appears to be increasing rather than
decreasing, which is the opposite of what I predicted. My working
hypothesis is novelty. She has been summoned four hundred times in four
hundred years into rooms where somebody was dying, and she has now
spent three days in a hospital corridor and a quartermaster's yard with
nobody dying in either, and it is simply new.*

*Corollary: this will wear off, probably inside a fortnight, and I
should get what I need before it does.*

He was aware, writing it, that Katsuyu was on the corner of the bench
about nine inches from the page and could presumably read it, and he
decided that this did not matter, on the grounds that he wrote nothing
about anybody that he would not say to them.

He had been eighteen for nine months and had held that principle since
he was about fifteen and had never once had it tested.

"That is not right," said Katsuyu.

---

"Which part?"

"The reason." A slow settling of the mantle. "I have been trying to
work out whether to say so, because you have written it in your own
book and I do not think a person's book is a thing one interrupts, and
then I decided that you would want the correction more than you would
want the privacy, and that if I was wrong about that you would tell
me."

"You're not wrong about that."

"Good." She shifted forward. "It is not novelty. Novelty is what I felt
on the first morning and it lasted about two hours and it was mostly
the drains."

Sazare put the pencil down.

"Then what."

"You are asking me a question and expecting an answer that I have,"
said Katsuyu, "and I have found that I like it very much, and I did not
know that about myself until Tuesday, and I have been sitting on
various things for three days trying to establish whether it is a
respectable thing to like."

"That's—"

"That is one half. The other half is not about you at all." She said it
in exactly the same mild register. "The other half is that you are in
the middle of something with Tsunade-sama, and I have never been in the
middle of anything with Tsunade-sama, and I have found that I want to
see how it comes out."

---

He did not write that down, which was itself unusual, and he noticed
that he had not and did not do anything about it.

"There isn't a middle of something," he said.

"Ah," said Katsuyu.

"Don't do that."

"I have been told that twice this week and I still do not entirely
understand what I am doing." A pause. "May I say a thing which I think
is obvious and which I am beginning to suspect is not, because that has
happened four times since Tuesday and I have decided to stop assuming?"

"Go on."

"Tsunade-sama has never summoned me for a person who was not dying."

The yard was very quiet.

"In four hundred and eleven years I have been called by nine people and
she is the fourth of them, and she has called me perhaps four hundred
times in forty-one years, and every single one of those was a field
station or a roof or a ward or a road, and on every single one of those
occasions somebody was going to be dead by the morning unless
something was done." Katsuyu did not move. "On the fifteenth of this
month she called me at twenty past eleven at night for a young man with
a hand injury and a pulse of eighty-eight who was in no danger of dying
at all, and who was, on any reasonable assessment, going to be
perfectly all right in the morning with one fewer hand.

"I noticed at the time. I said nothing, because it was not my business
and because she was working, and I have been thinking about it for four
days."

Sazare sat on a stool in a yard in the eighth month.

"That's not evidence of anything," he said. "She's the head of the
medical corps. It was an unknown mechanism. I was the only case—"

"That is all true," said Katsuyu, "and I would like to put the second
thing next to it."

"...All right."

"She divided me."

---

He looked at his own shoulder, where a piece of the Slug Queen had been
sitting for three days getting sap on his collar.

"She has never done that," said Katsuyu. "Not for anybody. She has
divided me across a village and across a battlefield and across a ward,
which is a different act — that is me being in nine places for her. She
has never once given a part of me to another person for that person's
use, and she did it on Tuesday morning, in about ninety seconds,
between a delegation and a border argument, and she did it by putting
her coat on and leaving the room so that it would be my decision and
not hers."

"That's—"

"You are going to say that it is a research instrument and that the
contract is untouched."

"It *is* a research instrument and the contract is untouched."

"Yes," said Katsuyu. "And she has never done it before."

---

He sat with it for a considerable time.

The yard went orange and then went grey. Kanzaki came out at some point,
looked at the two of them, said nothing at all, and went back in, which
was — he thought afterwards — probably the most tactful thing that
woman had done in thirty-one years.

*I wrote four pages in the fifth month.*

*I headed the last section WHAT I AM GOING TO DO ABOUT IT and I wrote
"nothing" under it and I gave three reasons in descending order of how
much I liked saying them, and I said the third one was the true one,
and it was.*

*Every one of those three reasons was constructed on a premise I did
not write down because I did not know it was a premise.*

*The premise was that it was mine.*

*That it was a thing happening in me, about her, at a distance, and
therefore a thing I could decide about alone, the way I decide about
everything — and that deciding to do nothing was a complete act,
because there was only one party to it.*

He got the notebook and did not open it.

*A woman has come down a staircase at a run in her shirt at eleven at
night, and summoned a creature she has never summoned for a living
person, and asked it a question she has not asked in forty-one years,
and given away a part of it, and covered up an incident she should have
filed, and stated the cost of that out loud in front of me on purpose
so that I would carry the accurate version.*

*And I have four pages in a book concluding "nothing" on the grounds
that I would rather have the conversation than find out.*

*I did not decide to do nothing. I decided not to look.*

*Those are different and I have been very comfortable indeed for three
months on the difference.*

---

"I have upset you," said Katsuyu.

"No."

"You have not written anything down for eleven minutes."

"That's not upset. That's the other one." He put the notebook away
without opening it. "Katsuyu — can I ask you something you might not
want to answer, and can you say no, and can you say no in a way that
doesn't cost you anything?"

"I do not know whether I can do the third part."

"Then don't answer it," said Sazare. "I withdraw it. That's a real
withdrawal and not a manoeuvre and I'd like you to check me on that in
about four days when I've had time to be dishonest about it."

There was a pause.

"You are the first person who has ever asked me whether I was free,"
said Katsuyu, "and now you have offered me a no twice in four days, and
I think I would like to say that out loud once rather than have it be a
thing I noticed."

---

He got there at about the ninth hour and he got there sideways, which
was how he got everywhere.

He could not do anything about any of it. That was established. He was
not going to say a word to the Hokage about a slug's four days of
arithmetic, and he was not going to say a word to Sakura about eight
months, and there was no move available anywhere that was not a man
making himself feel better at somebody else's expense.

What he could do was work.

*So. The open article.*

*I need a test piece and I have been putting off specifying it for two
days because every version I get is either useless or dangerous.*

*Constraints, properly:*

*It must have no function whatsoever. An open article is a route in
both directions and I do not have a one-way and until I do, nothing I
make can be near a patient, near a ward, or near a person who is not
me.*

*It must be small, so that if it draws it draws a small amount.*

*And it must be a shape I already hold completely — because I am going
to be doing something I have never done with the close, in front of a
witness, and I will have nothing left over for the geometry. Every
scrap of attention I have will be on the one thing I have never once
been conscious of.*

He stopped there.

*I know four shapes at that level. A bezel. A retaining figure. A
splint plate.*

*And one other.*

He sat quite still.

*I copied it so exactly that his crooked third thorn is on mine. Four
thorns, two leaf pairs, the brown mark at the margin of the second
leaf. I do not know what a rose is and I could not build one from the
idea of a rose, and I hold that specific object more completely than I
have ever held anything, because I made it once with my eyes shut in
four minutes and it cost me a hand.*

*It has no function. It is small. And it is the only complicated shape
in the world that is already finished in my hands.*

He wrote it down.

*Test article: a rose. In crystal. Open.*

"Katsuyu," he said. "Are you free on Saturday?"

"...Yes," said Katsuyu, after a moment. "Yes. I am."
