# Chapter 65, Scene 4 — Nothing Wrong With You

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, behind the east bathhouse. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2160 words.*

---

"Right," said Jiraiya. "Now the hour."

He got a roll of paper out of his coat and unrolled about four feet of it
on the flags of the drying yard and put a stone on each end, and there
were eleven things drawn on it in a hand that was considerably better
than anything else about him.

"Look at those."

"I can't read them."

"I know you can't read them. Look at them anyway."

---

They did it for an hour and it was the strangest hour of his month.

*One. Simple containment. He has told me what it is because I asked.*

*Two. Same, with a release condition.*

*Three through six, which are a progression of something and I do not
know what.*

*Seven, which is nine inches across and has about four hundred strokes in
it.*

*Eight through eleven, which he covered with his hand when I got to
them and said we would come back to.*

"Now," said Jiraiya. "Number one. What do you see?"

"Nothing."

"Kid—"

"That is the accurate answer and it is the answer I have given four
people in eleven years and every one of them has heard it as modesty,"
said Sazare. "I do not see nothing in the sense of not looking hard
enough. I look at it and the positions go in and *nothing comes back*.

"There is a thing that happens in everybody else when they look at that
and it does not happen in me. It has never happened. I was taught twice —
once at the ward at fifteen and once by a chūnin instructor at sixteen
who was extremely patient and gave it four afternoons and was, at the end
of them, more upset about it than I was."

"That's what you can't do," said Jiraiya. "I did not ask you what you
can't do.

"Describe it."

---

"...Describe it how?"

"The way you'd describe a piece of stone."

Sazare looked at the paper.

*Right.*

"It was laid in one operation," he said. "There is no join anywhere and
no pause — the stroke width is consistent all the way round and it does
not thicken at the corners, which is what happens when somebody stops and
starts.

"The boundary is deliberate. It is a closed figure and it was closed on
purpose and the closing stroke was put down last, and you can see that
because the closing stroke crosses two others and neither of them crosses
it.

"The density is not even. There is more of it toward the middle than at
the edge, and the gradient is smooth and it looks intentional and not
like somebody pressing harder because they were getting tired.

"So: something with a boundary, laid in one go, with a deliberate closing
act, and it holds more at the centre than at the rim."

Jiraiya did not say anything.

"Is that useless?" said Sazare.

"Say number two."

---

He did number two.

"Same construction. Same hand, obviously, same width, same closing act.

"There is a second figure inside it that is not part of it and was laid
afterwards — the ink sits on top of two strokes and it is *drier*, which
means there was a gap, and the gap was long enough for the first one to
take.

"And the second figure is not closed. There is a gap in it of about a
tenth of an inch on the lower left and it is the only unclosed thing on
this sheet and it is not a mistake, because a man who lays four hundred
strokes without a join does not leave a tenth of an inch open by
accident.

"So there is a shut thing with an open thing inside it, and the open
thing was put in afterwards, and the open thing is a *door*."

"Number seven," said Jiraiya.

---

Number seven took him eleven minutes and he did not enjoy any of it.

"This one has been laid in four passes and I can tell you the order.

"The outer ring first, closed, one operation. Then the four quarters,
anticlockwise, starting from the lower right, and I can tell you it was
anticlockwise because of the way the strokes overlap where they meet.

"Then about three hundred small ones inside the quarters and those were
done over more than one sitting — the ink changes at two places and one
of them is halfway through a run, so somebody stopped in the middle of a
row and came back.

"And then one stroke, last, in the middle, and it is the only one on the
whole figure that touches nothing.

"The density is wrong."

Jiraiya's head came up about an inch.

"Say that again."

"The density is wrong," said Sazare. "The gradient goes centre-outward
in the three inner quarters and it goes edge-inward in the fourth, and I
have looked at it four times because I assumed I had it backwards.

"The lower left quarter is loaded the other way round from the other
three.

"I do not know what any of it does. I have got no idea what this is for
and I could not begin to guess. But if it were a piece of stone I would
tell you that whoever made it changed his mind about that quarter and did
not go back and redo the other three, and that the fourth junction is
where it will fail."

---

The drying yard was quiet.

Jiraiya sat on the wall with his hands on his knees for about eleven
seconds.

"That's an eight-point barrier array," he said.

"All right."

"It is the standard construction. It has been the standard construction
for about a hundred and forty years, it is in every fūinjutsu text in
four countries, and about nine hundred people alive can lay one.

"And there is a fault in it at the fourth junction."

"...Is there."

"It was found in the second war," said Jiraiya. "By a man in the sealing
corps at Kumo, by accident, after about ninety years of everybody using
it. It cost him four fingers and it is the reason the modern one has got
a compensating stroke on the fourth quarter and mine has not got one
because I drew that from memory this morning out of the old text.

"You had it in eleven minutes.

"Off the ink."

---

*Right.*

*Do not get excited. Sit with it and take it apart before it becomes a
thing I want to be true.*

"I did not read it," said Sazare.

"No."

"I want to be extremely clear about that because it is the whole of the
distinction. I have got no idea what that figure is for. You told me it
is a barrier and I have taken it on trust and if you had told me it was a
storage seal I would have taken that on trust as well.

"I described an *object*. I said where the strokes sat and what order
they went down in and where the material was thick, and I would have said
exactly the same eleven sentences about a splint or a piece of yard
aggregate or a course laid by a man in Kiri in the ninth year.

"And it happens that in this case the sentences also tell you something,
because a seal is a made thing and a made thing has got its history in
it."

"Yes," said Jiraiya.

"So it is not a way in. It is not that I can read them after all if I
squint. It does not get me one single technique, ever, and it never will."

"No."

"Then what is it?"

---

Jiraiya rolled the paper up.

He did it slowly and he did not look at it, and Sazare had watched enough
people put a thing away deliberately to know that it was being done in
order to have something to do with his hands.

"There's a thing about seals," he said, "and I have been at it for
forty-one years and I am one of about four people alive who is properly
good at it, and I am going to tell you the whole of it in about eleven
words and then I want you to be quiet for a minute.

"Making one and breaking one are not the same trade."

He put the roll in his coat.

"Everybody assumes they are. They are taught together and they are in the
same books and the same men do both, and it is one of the great
convenient lies of the discipline.

"Making one is *composition*. You learn the notation and you learn what
the parts do and you put them together and you get a thing that works. It
is grammar. It is the most beautiful grammar anybody has ever built and I
have spent my life on it and I would do it again.

"Breaking one is not grammar. Breaking one is looking at an object and
working out where the man who made it got tired.

"And every single person who has ever been good at breaking has been good
at it *despite* being a maker, because a maker looks at a seal and reads
it, and reading it tells you what it is supposed to do, and what it is
supposed to do is exactly the thing you have got to stop believing."

---

Sazare sat on the wall.

*Say it.*

"I cannot read them," he said.

"You cannot read them."

"So I cannot be told what it is supposed to do."

"You cannot be told what it is supposed to do," said Jiraiya, "and you
have got about eleven years of describing objects to a standard I have
never seen in a human being, and I have just watched you find a
hundred-and-forty-year-old fault in eleven minutes by looking at the
*ink*.

"And I am not going to say the next sentence."

"You've said it."

"I have not said it and I am not going to, and I want that on the record
because in about four years somebody is going to ask me when I knew."

---

He got off the wall.

"Here is what I am going to say instead, and it is the only advice I have
got and it is worth having.

"Do not go near it.

"Not this year. Not next year. Do not practise it, do not write about it,
do not tell anybody what you did on this yard this afternoon, and if
somebody puts a seal in front of you and asks you what you see, tell them
you cannot read it, which is *true*.

"Because a man who can break seals is a thing that four countries want
and nobody trusts, and you have already got a price on your head and a
correspondence with a man in the Land of Sound and a woman who goes to a
window.

"And because you have already done it once and it has already cost
somebody something."

Sazare went very still.

"The twelfth of the eleventh month," said Jiraiya.

---

*Right.*

*Of course he has got that. I told him on a roof on the sixteenth and I
gave him the whole of the derivation and he confirmed it and told me I had
it word for word.*

*A store. A boundary and a closing stroke and the density sat toward the
centre rather than the edge. A way in and no way out. Open a long time
and not drawn on, so one demand, fixed, enormous, and she knows what it
is because you cannot size a store without knowing the load.*

*Four minutes. In a kitchen, off a face at four inches, at eighteen years
old, having never read a seal in my life.*

*I did not think of that as reading a seal.*

*I have never once thought of it as reading a seal. I have had it in my
head for five weeks as a thing I worked out about* her*, and it was not.
It was a barrier array and about four hundred strokes and a density
gradient, and I did the same eleven sentences I did on that sheet this
afternoon, and the object happened to be on somebody's forehead.*

"I did it to her," he said.

"You did it to her," said Jiraiya, "and you got it right, and there are
four people alive who know what that thing is and you made yourself the
fourth by looking at it for four minutes while she was asleep.

"So that is the reason. Not the countries and not the price.

"You have already proved that when you look at a made thing you find out
what it is, whether or not anybody meant you to, and there is a woman in
that tower with one on her head that she has never told you about.

"Leave it alone, kid."
