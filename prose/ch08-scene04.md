# Chapter 8, Scene 4 — Taken

*Act 1, beat 24. Sazare Ruri, age thirteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,900 words.*

---

Somebody picked him up.

He was on his knees on the cobbles in the space the crowd had made around him,
which was about nine feet across and getting bigger, and he was looking at the
platform, and somebody came in from behind and took a double fistful of the back
of his coat and lifted.

"Up. *Up.*"

He did not go up. His legs were not part of the arrangement any more.

"Right," said the voice, and shifted, and got him over a shoulder, and Sazare's
ruined arm swung and hit something and the world went white and stayed white for
some time.

---

He came back in an alley.

He was against a wall, sitting, and a woman had his right arm in both hands and
was doing something to it above the elbow with a strap and her whole weight, and
there were two others at the mouth of the alley with their backs to him.

"—not the *artery*, look at it, it's not the—"

"It's not going to be the artery, is it, it came out the *back*—"

"Then what's all this—"

"That's his hand. That's just his hand."

The woman looked at Sazare's face and saw that his eyes had opened.

"Can you hear me?"

He could hear her.

"What's your name?"

He did not say anything.

"He's shocked. Leave him."

"He's not shocked, he's *looking* at me. What's your name?"

---

He would think about that afterwards more than almost anything else, because he
could have said it. His mouth worked and his throat worked and he was entirely
conscious and he understood the question perfectly.

He did not say it because of a thing his mother had said on a step in the seventh
month of the year he turned eleven, with her arm round him, in a voice he could
still hear.

*If anybody ever asks you what you can do, you tell them you make earrings.*

Which was not the same question. He knew it was not the same question. He was
thirteen years old and had been awake for two days and had just watched four
people die on a platform eleven yards away, and what was in his head, with total
clarity, in his mother's voice, was: *if anybody ever asks you.*

So he did not answer, and he went on not answering, and after a while it stopped
being a decision and became a condition.

---

They moved him three times that night and he was awake for two of them.

At some point somebody put something between his teeth and somebody else did
something to his forearm that he had no words for at all, and there was a long
period afterwards of lying on boards in the dark listening to men argue in the
next room in low voices about what had happened in the square.

He heard most of it. He was not meant to.

He heard *a hundred and forty seconds*. He heard *six*, twice, and then *eight,
Tomi's not coming back*. He heard somebody say *it was a kid* four times in
different tones. He heard a man he did not know say, "Well, whoever he is, he can
do that," and another man say something short that Sazare did not catch, and the
first man say, "I'm only saying what he did."

And he heard, once, quite clearly, in a voice that was tired all the way through:

"He's Kohaku's."

Then the room went quiet for a while, and after that they talked about something
else.

---

On the third day a man came and sat down on the floor beside him with his back
against the wall.

Sazare knew him. It took a moment because everything took a moment; the man was
greyer and had lost about a stone and had a burn up one side of his neck that was
new.

"You," said Ryōsuke.

Sazare looked at him.

"Four months, I said." Ryōsuke put his head back against the wall. "That was
seventeen months ago. I did wonder."

He did not say anything else for a bit.

"They told me you won't give a name," he said. "That's all right. I know it."

Sazare shut his eyes.

"I'm going to tell you three things," said Ryōsuke, "and then I'm going to leave
you alone, and you can do what you like with them.

"The first one is that we were six minutes off. Not you. Us. We had a signal
coming at twenty past and eight men in an alley and we'd have had them off that
platform in four seconds and out through the ice-house yard, and it was a good
plan, and it might have worked. Might. I've never got it above about one in three
and I've been over it every night since.

"The second is that you took a hundred and forty seconds off it, and because of
that six of mine are dead, and Tomi is dead, and your family are dead, and every
one of those things happened because a thirteen-year-old put a spike through a man
in front of four hundred people.

"And the third is that if I'd been stood where you were stood, at thirteen, with
my mother third in the line, I would have done exactly the same thing, and so would
every man in this building, and any one of them who tells you different is lying to
be kind."

He got up.

"That's all three," he said. "They're all true at once. Nobody's going to help you
put them in an order."

---

He was in that room for eleven days.

On the fourth day a woman came and did something further to his arm and told him,
without being asked, that he would keep it and that he would not get all of it
back, and that if he wanted the fingers he would have to work at them every day
for a year, and she showed him how, and left him a rubber ball.

He worked at them every day for a year.

On the ninth day somebody left food, and he ate it, and then ate everything they
brought him after that, which he was ashamed of and could not stop.

On the eleventh day Ryōsuke came back with a coat that was too big and said they
were moving, and that it was four hours on foot and there was a boat at the other
end, and that he could come or not.

Sazare stood up.

---

There were fourteen of them in the boat and it went out at the second hour of the
morning into the fog, and nobody talked.

He sat in the bow with the too-big coat round him and his right hand in his lap
turned palm-up because that was the only way it was comfortable, and he watched
the shape of the Land of Water go from a coastline to a line to nothing at all,
and the fog took it, the way the fog took everything.

The woman beside him was about thirty and had a bag between her feet and had not
slept.

Somewhere past the second hour she said, without looking at him and without any
particular interest, the way you say a thing to fill a boat:

"What do they call you, then?"

Sazare looked at the water.

"I make earrings," he said.
