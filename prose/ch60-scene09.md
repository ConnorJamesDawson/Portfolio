# Chapter 60, Scene 9 — What Fukasaku Met

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, the long room. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1750 words.*

---

He asked at about half past one, when Jiraiya had stopped and the room
had gone quiet and Shizune had ruled a line under the minute.

"You said to ask you when you were in the room."

Fukasaku was on the corner of the table with his stick across his knees.

"I did," he said.

"You said you'd decide then."

"I did."

He looked at the boards for a while, at the place where the smear had
been before they scraped it up.

"Aye, go on," he said. "Ask it."

"You've met a slug before. A long time ago. Not on either mountain."

"I have."

"Will you tell me?"

"I'll tell the room," said Fukasaku, "because there's four of you in it
and one of them is holding a pen and I have thought about this for
eleven hours and I would rather it was written down badly tonight than
lost properly."

Shizune picked the nib back up.

---

"It was a very long time ago and I'm not going to give you a number,
because I'd be guessing and I'd guess low and then you'd all build on
it.

"Before this village. Before any of them. There were people and there
were roads and there were no walls anywhere that I remember.

"I was young. I was younger than that lump on the floor and I had no
business being off the mountain and I was off it anyway, in the way that
young things are, and I was in a bad country in the wet season.

"And there was a slug in a yard."

He tapped the stick.

"That's the whole of the first bit. It was not remarkable. It was a slug
about the size of a dog on a piece of wall in the rain, and I had never
seen one and I had been told there were none south of the water, and I
went and sat on the same wall because I was young and I wanted to see
what would happen."

"What happened?" said Sazare.

"Nothing happened. That's the honest account. We sat on a wall in the
rain for about half a day and it did not ask me one question and I did
not ask it one, and I have thought about that half-day for centuries.

"There was a human in that yard."

---

Nobody moved.

"He was old," said Fukasaku. "Sixty, seventy. He had no clan name — I
know that because I asked him, at the end, and he thought it was a
funny question and he laughed at me.

"And he was making something.

"I sat on a wall in the rain for half a day and watched a man with no
line and no name make a thing I have never seen before or since, and I
have been on that mountain for four hundred years and I have seen
everything anybody in your world has got.

"It's not on any of your lists. It is not one of the ones you have got a
word for. I could not tell you what element it was because I do not
think it was an element and I have not got the language.

"And he did it with no seals."

---

Sazare put both hands flat on the table.

*Do not go fast.*

*Do not go fast, do not decide what this is, write it down and take it
apart tomorrow.*

"You're certain about the seals," he said.

"I am certain about the seals because I did not know what a seal *was*,
lad. I was young and off the mountain and I had never met a shinobi.

"I found out about seals about ninety years later and the first thing I
thought when I saw one was: *the old man in the yard did not do that.*
And it has been sitting in me ever since."

"And the slug?"

"The slug had taught him," said Fukasaku.

"Did it say so?"

"It did not say a word to me the whole half-day. He said so. He told me
at the end, in about nine words, and he thought that was funny as well."

---

"What happened to him?" said Tsunade.

"He died," said Fukasaku. "Not then. I do not know when. I went back to
the mountain in the dry season and I did not go south again for about
forty years, and by then there was no yard and no wall and a different
kind of country, and I asked in three villages and nobody had heard of
him.

"And the thing he made is gone."

He put the stick down on the table.

"That is the part I have not said out loud in some centuries.

"Whatever that was — and it was a *whole thing*, it had a shape and it
had a use and he had spent a life on it — it stopped existing when he
did. There was nobody standing next to him. He had no line and no clan
and no pupil that I ever saw, and there was no writing, because there
was nothing to write it in and he could not have read it if there had
been.

"It went out like a lamp."

---

The room did not say anything for a long moment.

*Right.*

*Say the first one and get it out of the way, because it is the small
one and it is going to be in the way of the big one.*

"It's testimony," said Sazare.

"Aye," said Fukasaku.

"It's a memory, of a half-day, from four hundred and some years ago,
held by a creature that is not a human, about a man with no name in a
country that isn't there any more.

"Ariake Fumi would take that apart in about nine minutes and every one
of the nine would be technically correct, and she would be right to, and
I would have to sit there and agree with her.

"It is worth exactly what a slug's word is worth, which is nothing, and
I wrote that on page fourteen myself."

"That's right," said Fukasaku, "and I'd not have told you if you'd said
anything else."

"So it confirms the paper and it does not help it."

"Aye."

"There is still exactly one piece of evidence in the world," said
Sazare, "and it weighs nought point one one grams and it is in a fold of
paper in my coat."

---

He got up and went to the window.

*Now the big one.*

*And I am going to have to say it out loud, because Shizune has got a
pen and because it is the only thing that has happened tonight that I
will not be able to reconstruct tomorrow if I let it go.*

"It went out like a lamp," he said.

"Aye."

"Say it back to me. A man had a technique. Nobody had written it down.
There was no notation and no clan and no pupil, and when he died it did
not go anywhere, it *stopped*."

"That is what I am telling you."

"Then that is what a kekkei genkai is."

He turned round.

"That's the whole of the paper. That's nineteen pages and four months.
There is no substance in the blood. There is nothing being handed down —
what gets handed down is instruction, and a family, and four generations
of people standing round a child who is four instead of fourteen, and it
produces the same result so reliably that everybody for eight hundred
years has called it inheritance.

"And I have been writing it as though the finding were *good news*.

"I have got it in the paper as a liberation. It can be taught, therefore
it is not owned, therefore nobody is special and nobody is filth and my
mother and my sister died for a property that does not exist.

"That is all true and I have not got it wrong.

"And I have missed the other half completely."

---

"If it is not in the blood," said Sazare, "then there is nothing keeping
it.

"Nothing. A bloodline that was real would be *safe* — you could kill
every practitioner in a country and the property would come back in the
grandchildren, because it would be in them, and that is what everybody
thinks is happening and it is why the Mizukage's men were told to be
thorough.

"But it isn't in them. There is nothing to come back.

"So every single one of these is one death away from gone. Not one
generation. One *death*. One old man in a yard in the wet season with
nobody standing next to him.

"And there is no register anywhere of what has already gone out, because
a thing that goes out like that does not leave a hole that anybody can
see. Nobody misses a technique they never heard of."

He looked at the table.

"Kiri killed about four thousand people in eighteen months," he said.

Nobody said anything.

"I have spent four years thinking about that as four thousand people,
and I have got the burial register with three hundred and eleven
*unknown*s in it and an old woman in a quartermaster's yard filling them
in in her own evenings.

"It is four thousand people.

"And I do not know how many of them were the last one, and neither does
anybody, and there is no way to find out, because whatever they could do
went out with them and left nothing behind to count.

"They did not just kill my mother and Kohaku for a property they did not
have.

"They put out an unknown number of lamps and there is no list."

---

He stood at the window with his back to the room for a while.

Behind him, at the end of the table, Shizune had stopped writing.

*And that is the other half of the paper and it is not going in the
paper.*

*Because it does not help and it cannot be evidenced and it would be the
single most useful paragraph in the world to a man like Danzō, and
because I would be handing four countries a reason to be quick.*

*It goes in a book. My book. Tonight. And it does not leave the book.*

"Lad," said Fukasaku, after a while.

"Yes."

"That was worth the four hundred miles."

"It's half past one in the morning," said Tsunade, "and every single one
of you is going to bed."
