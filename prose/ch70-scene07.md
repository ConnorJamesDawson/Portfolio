# Chapter 70, Scene 7 — The Flat Count

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~970 words.*

---

The audit found it on the Friday, because Friday was when the
week's ledgers got balanced, and he ran the personal ledger last,
at the end of the yard day, with Katsuyu on her stone and the
kettle doing its work.

He almost did not run it at all. That was the detail that
frightened him afterwards, in the honest entry: he had been
tired enough to consider skipping the one audit that had never
once been skipped, and had run it on discipline alone, the way
you check a governor clause you already know is fine.

The count was flat.

He sat looking at the page for a while. The laugh ledger, kept
since a Thursday in January, current total forty-one — and the
last entry was dated the second of the month. Eleven days. The
best data series of his life, the one whose collection had
reorganised his entire understanding of what he was for, had
flatlined for eleven days, and he had not noticed, and *not
noticing* was not a thing that happened to him. Not noticing
was other people's failure mode. He was the man who noticed;
it was the whole of the kit; it was, Jiraiya said, the one
thing on the continent he did at a Sannin's grade.

"Katsuyu-sama," he said. "I need a witness. I am opening an
incident."

"Witnessed," said Katsuyu, and settled.

He worked it the way he worked every incident, out loud, on
paper, causes eliminated in order.

*Finding one: the count is flat because the events stopped.
Verify: cross-reference the coda log — the evening talk has run
four sentences or fewer for nine consecutive nights. The study
file: no scene attempted in sixteen days; the last entry is a
fitting note dated before the full set delivered. The noons:
items present in four of the last five. The through-and-out
law: not invoked once this month. The row ledger: no entry
since the brush.*

*Finding two: the events stopped because the operator was
absent. Not absent from the building. Absent from the post.
Present at every appointment and asleep inside my own conduct
at all of them — a watchman making his rounds unconscious,
touching every door, checking nothing.*

*Finding three — and this is the one the incident is FOR:
between the second and today she has been at that post alone.
The teasing attempted three times, logged by me as weather.
The coda held open every night by her, four sentences offered
into a fog, until the fog fell asleep mid-compliment. She has
been running the entire warmth of this arrangement single-
handed for eleven days, which is the precise condition I
identified in January — the unilateral filling of the small
rows — and swore in writing not to be worth, and I have been
worth it for eleven straight days with a full ledger and no
entry, because—*

The pen stopped.

"Say it," said Katsuyu, who had been watching the page fill
with her ancient, unhurried attention. "You are one line from
it and the line is the incident."

"Because I have been embezzled," said Sazare, slowly, "by my
own projects. Every hour is spent on something true — the
field, the line, the road — and every one of those hours was
budgeted out of an account I never listed, because it never
appears on any docket, because it is unwritten law and
unwritten law has no clerk. The account is *us*. The evenings,
the noons, the coda, the teasing, the hundred small
appointments that are not appointments. I have been paying
for three excellent things out of the one fund I promised to
keep filled, and the fund has been running a deficit for
eleven days, and the auditor was asleep, and the other
account holder—" he looked at the flat count, and the
register very nearly did not hold, and he let the wobble
stand in his voice because Katsuyu was a witness and
witnesses get the whole of it— "the other account holder has
been covering my withdrawals in silence, at cost, without
one word of complaint, because she decided I was worth the
shortfall. Again. And I have made her do it *again.*"

The kettle ticked. The lamp burned in its circle.

"Now the question I came to witness," said Katsuyu. "Fifteen
Fridays you have brought me your work. Three Fridays you have
brought me your schedule. So: Sazare. What does she do in the
evenings now?"

He looked at her.

"She holds the brush," he said. "Fifty passes. And then I am
asleep, and the talk we built the whole ritual to carry has
nowhere to go, and she—" and the outside view arrived at
last, eleven days late, delivered by a slug in a frost-lit
yard: the second-floor lamp burning past midnight in his
peripheral vision every night that week, logged and never
read; the tray sent back over a tea blend; the collection he
did not know she was keeping but could suddenly, with total
certainty, deduce; the whole silent architecture of a woman
pining inside her own tower and defending, with three
correct clauses, her decision never to say so.

"She waits up," he said. "She has been waiting up. Katsuyu-
sama, she has been *waiting up*, and I have the second-floor
lamp in eleven days of peripheral logs and never once ran
the file."

"Now you have run it," said Katsuyu. "Findings noted,
incident open. And since I watched you build the remedy for
her side of exactly this in January, in this yard, out of a
bath and a squid stall, I will save us both the hour: the
remedy is not guilt, which is a closed article and spends
itself holding its own shape. The remedy is engineering.
You have until Monday. Bring me the drawings."
