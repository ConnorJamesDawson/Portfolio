# Chapter 60, Scene 6 — Question Four

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, the long room. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1680 words.*

---

"Nothing," said Sazare.

"Say it properly, lad."

"I have given Shikkotsu nothing. There is no contract and no term and no
obligation and I have rendered nothing at any point, and the whole of
that is true and none of it is a form of words."

Fukasaku sat with his hands on the table.

"Then you'd better give me the account," he said, "because I have come
four hundred miles on foot to hear it and I would like it in order."

---

"Eighth month," said Sazare. "Fifteenth. I put my hand on a saturated
Mokuton article and it started going to stone, and the Hokage stopped it
at the carpus after six hours and neither of us knew what it was.

"Nineteenth. I asked Hime whether her summon would come and look at what
my hands were doing, and she said she would ask, and Katsuyu came, and
she found eleven years of faults in one evening that I could not see and
would never have seen.

"After that she came on Fridays.

"Eleven Fridays. I asked eleven times and she said yes eleven times and
I have never once assumed the twelfth, except that I did, on the
thirteenth of this month, by forgetting, and I went up the hill on the
twentieth and told her so and she said she had not minded.

"In the tenth month I asked her how the first contract came about,
because I wanted to know how it works, and she told me. And then I
thought about signing one.

"And I decided not to."

"Why?"

*Say it exactly and do not improve it.*

"Because if she is contracted, she comes because she is contracted," he
said. "And then I would never be able to tell the difference again."

---

Nobody moved.

Fukasaku sat entirely still for what he later put at nine seconds, which
was a long time in a room with six people in it.

"Say that once more," he said.

"And then I would never be able to tell the difference again."

"Eleven words."

"Yes."

"You've counted them."

"I've had them since the tenth month," said Sazare. "I've tried to
improve it four times and I can't, and I've concluded that's because
it's finished and not because I've run out."

---

"Right," said Fukasaku.

He put both hands flat on the table and looked at them for a while.

"I'll tell you what I came here to say," he said, "and then I'll tell you
what's actually happened to me in the last minute, and the second one is
going to be considerably less dignified.

"Everything Myōboku has ever given a human has been given under
contract. Every one, since there were records, and there is no exception
and there has never been an argument about it. You sign, you owe, we
give.

"And it is not about payment. I want that clear, because every human who
has ever heard it assumes we are merchants and we are not. Nothing we
have ever taken off a human was worth what we gave.

"It is that the obligation *keeps it honest at both ends*.

"A thing you owe for is a thing you cannot afterwards pretend was a
gift. And a thing you are owed for is a thing you cannot afterwards
pretend you gave freely. It cuts both ways and it cuts hardest on us,
and in four hundred years it has stopped exactly the thing you would
expect it to stop, which is an old creature getting fond of a young
human and then wanting something for it.

"That is the argument. That is the whole architecture and I have
believed it since before your village existed."

He looked up.

"And the boy has just said the same sentence from the other side."

---

Jiraiya said, "What?"

"Think, lad." Fukasaku did not turn round. "He has refused a contract for
the exact reason we require one.

"We say: sign, so that neither of us can ever pretend about what this is.

"He says: do not sign, so that neither of us can ever pretend about what
this is.

"It is the same fear. It is precisely the same fear, put the same way
round — *I will not be able to tell the difference* — and it has produced
opposite answers, and I have been chewing on it for about forty seconds
and I cannot find the place where one of them is wrong."

---

"May I say something," said Katsuyu.

It was the first time she had spoken since Fukasaku sat down, and the
whole room turned to the sill.

"Please," said Fukasaku.

"You have asked what the boy has given Shikkotsu," said Katsuyu, "and he
has answered *nothing*, and he is correct in every particular, and I want
to say that his answer is the honest one before I say that it is not the
whole of it.

"He has given me a thing you have no name for, and neither have I, and I
have been trying to find one since the tenth month.

"He asked me.

"That is the whole of it. In four hundred years nobody has asked me for
anything. I have been called and I have come and I have done what was
needed and I have been thanked properly, by good people, and by her,"
— and Sazare did not look at Tsunade — "and none of that is asking.

"I did not know until a Tuesday morning in the tenth month that there
was any part of me that wanted to be asked. I found it out at four
hundred years old in a cold yard, and I told him so, and he has not
forgotten it since and I have watched him not forget it.

"And on the twentieth of this month he stood in that yard and told me
what I am, because he could not find a word for it and used my own
method on me instead.

"I did not know what to do with it. I still do not."

She was quiet for a moment.

"So he has given Shikkotsu nothing," said Katsuyu. "And he has given me
something that your architecture cannot price, and I do not think that
is a flaw in your architecture. I think it is that nobody has ever tried
to put it on the sheet."

---

Fukasaku did not answer for some time.

"That's the undignified part, then," he said eventually.

"Say it," said Jiraiya.

"I have been running an argument for four hundred years and I have never
once had it tested, because nobody has ever done it the other way," said
Fukasaku. "Not once. There has been no control. I have got four hundred
years of a system that works and no instance whatsoever of the system
not being there, and it never occurred to me that that was a hole in it,
and I am the oldest thing on that mountain.

"And then a boy of eighteen with no clan asks a slug on a Friday and
gets eleven yeses and owes her nothing, and it has not gone wrong."

"It's been three months," said Shima.

"It's been three months," Fukasaku agreed. "That is exactly right and it
is the only thing standing between me and having to go home and think
about my whole life, and I want the room to know that I am holding onto
it with both hands."

---

Jiraiya had not said anything for a while.

Sazare looked at him and found that he was sitting very still with his
elbows on the table, and that whatever was happening on his face was not
for anybody.

"Thirty-one years," he said.

"Aye," said Fukasaku.

"I know what I owe you to the day. I've always known. I've been proud of
knowing — I've said it out loud to people, I said it to him on a roof a
fortnight ago, I said *he knows to the day what he owes us* like it was
a thing to be pleased about."

"It is a thing to be pleased about."

"Is it?" said Jiraiya. "Because I've just sat here and listened to that
boy explain why he wouldn't sign, and every single word of it was about
whether the other party wanted to be there, and I have never once in
thirty-one years asked myself whether *you* wanted to.

"You were contracted. So the question didn't exist."

Fukasaku put his stick against the table leg.

"I wanted to," he said.

"You'd have had to come anyway."

"I would have had to come anyway," said Fukasaku, "and that is the whole
of the boy's point, and I have just spent four hundred years being on
the wrong side of it without noticing, and I am not going to resolve it
this morning in a human tower with a slug watching me."

---

He turned back to Sazare.

"Right, lad. My answer, and then we do the work.

"I asked the question because I would not walk into a building without
knowing whether you had taken something without paying for it. That was
the whole of it and I'd ask it again.

"You have not. What you have done is worse than that and better than
that and I have got no framework for it, and I have decided — just now,
sitting here — that I am not going to get one by refusing to be in the
room.

"So I'll do the work.

"And I want two things minuted."

Shizune's nib stopped.

"One. Nothing that happens in this room today is under a Myōboku
contract, because there isn't one, and I am not going to pretend
otherwise on a piece of paper.

"Two. I do not know what it is under instead.

"Write both. Write the second one exactly as I said it, because in
about ninety years somebody is going to read this minute and I would
rather they knew that I did not know."

Shizune wrote it down.

"Now," said Fukasaku. "Somebody put a piece of stone on this table."
