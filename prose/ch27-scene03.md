# Chapter 27, Scene 3 — What He Did Not Say

*Act 4, part B. Konoha General. Sazare Ruri, age sixteen.
POV: Sazare. ~2,026 words.*

---

Ibiki conducted the intelligence review in Sister Kaburagi's office because
the formal interview rooms were being used to identify bodies.

The office had one chair for visitors. Ashida took it by right of broken
collarbone. Sazare remained in the evacuation chair. Inoichi stood by the
filing cabinet, and Ibiki sat behind Kaburagi's desk with a stack of reports
that belonged nowhere near her patient rosters.

"This is not a prosecution interview," Ibiki said.

"Kōji said that."

"Kōji wants you to join. I want to know whether you withheld actionable
intelligence before an attack that killed Konoha personnel."

"That's clearer."

"Good. Start with the market."

Sazare described the three Sound protectors at the dumpling stall: metal
dimensions, stamped note, horizontal cut, identical die characteristics, lack
of wear. He described the Takahashi lodging house, hospital presentations,
absent carts and shortened egg delivery.

Ibiki stopped him there. "Eggs."

"Four trays short."

"How does that indicate invasion?"

"It doesn't."

"What did it indicate to you?"

"That demand reported by lodging houses had not become demand at suppliers.
Either the visitors were provisioned separately, the occupancy figures were
wrong, or somebody had created the appearance of civilian festival traffic
without its consumption."

"And the linen cart?"

"Separate observation."

"Which meant?"

"Nothing by itself."

Ibiki placed both hands flat on the desk. "At what point did nothing become
something?"

"When the road emptied and the first impact reached the hospital."

"After the attack began."

"Yes."

"Before that, did you believe Sound was preparing hostile action?"

"I believed the delegation was not what it claimed to be."

"Did you report that?"

"No."

Ashida looked at his completed movement form. He had been told one part at
breakfast, not the market sighting weeks before.

"Why?" Ibiki asked.

Sazare gave the reason he had built at the stall. A probationary foreigner
reporting foreign shinobi during a multinational examination did not become
trustworthy. He became a person who informed on people wearing the wrong
forehead protector. The protectors might have been new because the village was
new. Three from one die proved procurement, not purpose. Konoha already knew
Oto was present because Konoha had invited it.

"Those are reasons the information might not be actionable," Ibiki said. "They
are not reasons to withhold it."

"Reporting it would also be information about me."

"Yes."

"That I examine foreign shinobi without instruction. That I compare their
equipment, follow their lodging use and draw conclusions from supply."

"All true."

"And prohibited by no clause because nobody thought to prohibit looking."

"Also true."

"I thought the report would change my assessment more reliably than it changed
yours."

Ashida set the movement form down.

"You could have told me," he said.

Ibiki looked at him. "This is my interview."

"I was his liaison."

"Then ask."

Ashida turned towards Sazare with the careful stiffness of somebody protecting
a collarbone and something less visible.

"You told me about the lodging house on the morning. Why not before?"

"I didn't know what it meant before."

"You didn't know on the morning either."

"No."

"I wrote *nothing again* in box twelve for three months. You saw me write it."

"Yes."

"Was there something every time?"

"No."

"How would I know?"

Sazare had no answer that did not repeat the act being examined.

Ashida picked up the form again. "You thought I was a pipe to upstairs."

"You were."

"I was also sitting at the gate every morning."

"Yes."

"You could have given it to me. If it was nothing, I could write nothing.
That's the job."

He did not raise his voice. That made the rebuke fit more closely around the
thing Inoichi had said: nobody in the middle, because Sazare treated
intermediaries as routes rather than people capable of holding uncertainty.

"Yes," Sazare said.

Ashida waited for an apology. Sazare apologised for things that were not his
fault and never for things that were. The habit reached his mouth and stopped.

"I should have told you," he said instead.

Ashida nodded once. It was not forgiveness because the injury between them did
not require the word. It was receipt.

Ibiki leaned back. "It would have."

Ashida looked up.

"Would you have acted?" Sazare asked.

"On three new headbands and nine hospital presentations? No. I might have sent
somebody to count the lodging house. I might have put the note in a tray. It
might have arrived under six hundred reports about pickpockets and gambling."

"Then—"

"Do not improve your decision with my uncertainty. You did not know what I
would do because you chose not to find out."

Sazare closed his mouth.

Inoichi spoke from beside the cabinet. "Why did you tell Ashida about the
hospital numbers on the morning?"

"He said it was quiet."

"That isn't why."

"It is what happened."

"You gave him enough to notice and not enough to act."

Ashida's expression changed. The rice cake, the Takahashi house, the question
about why Sazare was telling him: all of it reached the office four days late.

"You wanted the information outside you," Inoichi said, "without becoming
responsible for putting it there."

"Possibly."

"No. Consistent was the word I used before. This is consistent too. You keep
deciding alone what uncertainty other people are permitted to receive."

The office contained Kaburagi's duty roster, a cupboard of patient files and
one chipped cup filled with red pencils. Ordinary records of decisions
transferred from one person to another so nobody had to remember the whole
hospital alone.

"Would reporting it have stopped the invasion?" Sazare asked.

"Almost certainly not," Ibiki said.

"Would it have changed the hospital assignment?"

"Unknown."

"Then the outcome—"

"Is not the measure of whether you should have told us. You made a defensible
decision. It was also wrong. Both conditions survive."

Sazare looked at the reports.

"What happens?"

"The omission enters your assessment. The market observations enter
intelligence retroactively. Future integration terms will require reporting
suspected hostile preparation whether or not you consider the evidence
sufficient. Somebody else gets to decide sufficiency."

"Another mind-walk?" Sazare asked.

Inoichi's attention sharpened. "Why?"

"To verify whether I knew more before the attack."

Ibiki said, "Would you consent?"

The square returned before Sazare answered: his mother's hands, Kohaku on the
step, Ine in cold water, somebody standing behind and to the left being
careful. Twenty-two minutes had removed eleven days from him and produced the
word *consistent*.

"Yes," he said.

Inoichi shook his head. "No."

Ibiki looked at him. "Your reason?"

"The first walk established that his reported memory structure was consistent.
A second, targeted after a traumatic event, could tell us what he now
remembers believing. It cannot reliably separate contemporaneous assessment
from hindsight. The harm is certain and the evidentiary gain poor."

"He consented."

"Consent does not improve the technique."

Sazare looked at Inoichi. "You went in when I asked before."

"Yes."

"What's different?"

"Before, nobody would act without it and you had no other evidence to offer.
Now we have reports, witnesses, supply records and your omission admitted
against interest. We do not repeat an invasive procedure because it makes the
file feel cleaner."

The refusal unsettled him more than another walk would have. He had offered
access to the worst place in himself as payment for being believed. Inoichi
had declined because the institution already possessed enough.

Ibiki marked *no further Yamanaka examination recommended* on the review
sheet.

"That recommendation can be overridden," he said.

"By whom?"

"At present, the acting Council authority. Under the integration terms,
through medical and command review."

Another reason the signature mattered.

"And if the report changes my assessment?"

"It will."

"Even if I'm wrong?"

"Especially then. Institutions cannot learn the shape of your wrong
conclusions if you only show them the right ones."

Ibiki pulled a blank intelligence slip from the stack and placed it in front
of Sazare.

It contained four boxes: observation, source, assessment, urgency.

"Write the market sighting as you would have written it then. No hindsight."

Sazare wrote the three protectors, identical manufacture, new condition,
observed on market row. Source: direct observation. Assessment: probably
central issue from a newly formed village; unusual uniformity; no hostile
conduct. Urgency: none.

Ibiki read it.

"This would have entered the foreign-delegation file," he said. "A clerk might
have matched it to equipment recovered after the Forest of Death. Or not. That
is the honest size of the missed opportunity."

"Small."

"Yes."

"Twenty-seven people died in the hospital street."

"Also yes. Do not force the two numbers to explain each other."

He stamped the slip *RETROSPECTIVE* and placed it in the file. The report now
existed, uselessly late and in the form it should originally have taken.

It was an answer built to appeal to him. That did not make it false.

Ibiki opened a second file.

"Guren."

The name was followed by a physical description Sazare had supplied four
months earlier. Violet hair. Red eyes. Crystal release without visible lattice
or preparatory seed. Last seen leaving a road north of Kanezaki.

"We recovered two relevant documents from the attackers," Ibiki said. "One is
a countermeasure briefing. It describes resonant disruption trials against
crystal structures produced by a woman identified as Guren. The second is a
movement order signed with a field cipher associated with Orochimaru's
network. Her name appears in the distribution list."

"Date?"

"Eight days before the invasion."

Eight days was not history, rumour or a body nobody had found.

"Alive," Sazare said.

The word changed his breathing. Inoichi noticed; Ibiki probably did. Ashida
looked down at the form as if allowing privacy inside a room designed to
remove it.

Sazare had spent four months using Guren as the answer to a conditional
question. If Konoha refused him, he would find her. If nobody else could teach
him, she could. If the road closed, there was another road. She had remained
hypothetical enough to be useful.

Eight days made her real again.

Real meant located somewhere, eating, sleeping, making choices, teaching Sound
how her crystal broke or being used for the experiments without consent. Real
meant he could want to find her and fail.

He put both hands flat on his knees. The repaired one did not move beneath the
splint.

"As of eight days before the attack, almost certainly."

"Where?"

"Not stated. The order originated outside the Land of Fire."

"Can I see it?"

"No."

"The section with her name."

"No."

"Why tell me?"

Ibiki glanced at Inoichi. "Because withholding actionable information would be
inconsistent with the recommendation we have just made to you."

There was dry humour in it, buried deep enough that Ashida missed it.

Ibiki produced a copied diagram instead. It showed the cursed mark from the
wounded Sound shinobi's neck, three curved shapes inside a circle, with
medical annotations tracing channels beneath the skin.

"You examined this during treatment."

"Yes."

"What is it?"

"Writing."

"It is a seal."

"The inscription is a seal. The body is the writing medium."

"Difference?"

"A seal specialist asks what the marks mean. I want to know what can accept
them without rejecting the instruction."

Inoichi said, "Crystal."

"Possibly."

Ibiki turned the copied diagram back towards himself. "The surviving prisoner
claims the mark was applied by Orochimaru. Our specialists cannot safely
remove it and disagree about whether damaging the visible inscription will
trigger it."

"I can't read it."

"We know."

"Then why ask me?"

"Because you looked at it once while exhausted and identified a problem three
seal specialists reached six hours later."

"That doesn't make me able to solve it."

"No. It makes you somebody they want in the room."

The village's interest had already widened beyond the street.

Ibiki closed both files. "One more question. On the roof, you had a viable
northern route. Natsumi's report says you began moving towards it and
returned. Why?"

There were answers involving eleven patients, a repaired window and a week
with a shape. None seemed admissible in the office.

"The hospital couldn't be evacuated in time," Sazare said.

"That's why staying was useful. It isn't why you stayed."

Sazare looked at Inoichi. Inoichi waited without helping.

"I wanted it to remain," Sazare said.

Ibiki made one note.

He did not ask what *it* meant.
