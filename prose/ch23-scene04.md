# Chapter 23, Scene 4 — Sixty Ryō

*Act 4, part A. Konoha General, and Ichiraku. Sazare Ruri, age sixteen.
POV: Sazare. ~2,550 words.*

---

The boy came into the ward at the ninth hour on a Tuesday in the second
month with his left hand wrapped in what appeared to be somebody's
neckerchief and a great deal to say about it.

Sazare was at the far end doing the corner bed, which was the spinal
injury, and had his back to the door, and knew who it was before the third
syllable.

"—and it wasn't even the *jutsu*, that's the thing, the jutsu was fine,
the jutsu was *perfect*, it was the landing, and Sakura-chan said—"

"Sit," said Nurse Yagi.

"I'm just *saying*—"

"Sit *down*, Uzumaki, you're dripping."

Sazare finished what he was doing, because you finish what you are doing,
and turned round.

He was two inches taller than he had been on a causeway in the eleventh
month and still about a foot of noise wider than he was. Same orange, or a
different orange with the same intentions. Blue eyes, three thin lines on
each cheek, blond hair going every direction, the protector on his
forehead pushed slightly askew, and a hand wrapped in a stranger's cloth
that had gone through in two places.

He saw Sazare and his whole face did something Sazare had no defence
against whatever.

"*You're here!*"

"I'm here."

"They let you *stay*?"

"Conditionally."

"That's—" Naruto Uzumaki stood up off the chair he had just been made to
sit on, entirely without noticing, and Nurse Yagi put a hand flat on his
shoulder and put him back on it. "That's *brilliant*. That's — I asked
Kakashi-sensei about four times and he did the thing where he says *mm*
and then talks about something else, and I thought they'd—"

"Hand," said Nurse Yagi.

---

It was a laceration across the base of two fingers and the heel of the
palm, about two inches, filthy, and it had been wrapped by somebody in a
hurry with something that had been in a pocket.

"How?"

"Fence."

"How."

"There was a *fence*," said Naruto, with dignity, "and I was going over it,
and there was a bit of wire on it that I didn't—"

"Right," said Nurse Yagi. "Ruri. Clean it and close it."

Sazare looked up.

"On a patient?"

"On a patient. It's two inches, it's clean-edged under the muck, he's
twelve and he heals like a *lizard*, and if you make a mess of it he'll be
fine in a fortnight anyway." She was already moving down the ward. "And
he'll hold still, because he'll want to see."

"I *definitely* want to see," said Naruto.

---

It took eleven minutes and about nine of those were cleaning.

Naruto held still for all of it, which surprised Sazare and which he
found out later was not a general property of the boy but a specific
respect for anybody doing a thing properly in front of him; and he talked
for the entire eleven minutes and asked, by Sazare's count, twenty-six
questions, of which about nineteen were about what he was doing and why,
and the answers to which he took in and kept.

Then Sazare put his palm over it and held the feed level for four minutes
and closed it.

Badly. It left a pink ridge that a competent medic would have taken out in
about six seconds, and Nurse Yagi looked at it in passing on the way back
up the ward and said, "Mm — you drifted at the end, you always drift at
the end," and kept walking.

Naruto turned his hand over twice in the light with his mouth open.

"That's *amazing*."

"That's very bad, actually. There's a woman on this floor who'd have done
it in six seconds and she's not even—"

"Yeah, but *you* did it," said Naruto, "and you couldn't do it in
November."

And Sazare Ruri, sixteen years old, stood in the middle of a hospital ward
with a bowl of dirty water in his hands, and had to turn round and go and
put the bowl somewhere.

---

He read the ward while he was doing it, because he read everything, and it
took him about four seconds and he wished it had taken longer.

There were nine people on that floor. He had them all.

**Bed two**, the leg ulcer, eighty-one, had looked up when the boy came in
and had looked at him for about a second and a half and then had looked at
the ceiling and had not looked again.

**The genin with the foot**, thirteen, in bed five, had gone very slightly
pink and had watched the whole hand business sideways with enormous
interest and had said nothing at all, because saying something would have
required a position.

**The jōnin with the shoulder** had said, "Oi," when Naruto came past, in
a perfectly friendly way, and Naruto had said "Oi!" back, and that had been
entirely genuine on both sides.

**And the ward auxiliary**, a woman of about fifty who came up twice a day
with the linen, had come in with the trolley at the far door, and had seen
who was in the chair, and had gone out again and come back four minutes
later, and had done the far end first, and had gone out without doing the
near end at all.

She had not said anything. Nobody had said anything. There was nothing
whatever on that ward that anybody could have written on a form.

And Naruto Uzumaki had sat in the middle of it with his hand out, talking,
and had not registered one particle of it, in the way of somebody who has
been standing in a draught since birth and does not know that rooms can be
warm.

*No*, Sazare corrected himself, immediately, because he did not permit
himself the comfortable version of anything. *He registers all of it. He
has registered every one of those four things and about nine more I
haven't got, since he was about four years old, and he has decided — at
some point, deliberately, at a cost I cannot begin to price — to go on
talking anyway.*

*That's not obliviousness.*

*That is the single most expensive thing I have ever watched anybody do.*

He put the bowl down and did not say one word about any of it, then or
ever.

---

"Right, come on," said Naruto, at the door, when Nurse Yagi had signed him
off. "Ramen. My treat, 'cause of the hand."

"I can't."

"Why *not*?"

"Because I have to tell my liaison before I go anywhere new," said Sazare,
"and he'll have gone home at the fourth hour, so it'd be tomorrow."

Naruto stopped in the doorway of a hospital ward.

"Your *what*?"

"My minder. I'm a probationary resident. I've got conditions." He said it
flatly, because it was flat. "Movement's reported. There's a form and it's
got twelve boxes and box eleven is where I've been."

"That's—" The boy's whole face changed and it changed *fast*, which
Sazare had not expected and which he had about half a second to observe.
"That's not — you fixed everybody's *hands*, you came all the way here and
told Kakashi-sensei the truth about a thing that made you look bad, and
they've got you on a — for how *long*?"

"A year at least. Probably longer."

"That's *rubbish*!"

"It's reasonable, actually," said Sazare. "If I were them I'd have written
worse ones. I know exactly what I look like on paper and I've read the
paper."

"I don't care what you look like on *paper*—"

And then Naruto Uzumaki stopped.

Sazare watched him do it, and it was the single most surprising thing that
happened to him in Konoha that year: the boy stood in a doorway with his
mouth open and his fists up somewhere round his chest, plainly with about
four more sentences loaded and ready — and *put them down*.

Not because anybody had told him to. Sazare could see the whole of it
happening on his face, at twelve years old, in about a second and a half:
the fury; then something else arriving behind it; then a decision.

"Yeah, all right," said Naruto. "Tomorrow, then."

And he grinned, and said, "There's a place by the west road and it's the
*best* one, and the old man does a thing with the pork you wouldn't
believe," and went off down the corridor at a half-run shouting something
back over his shoulder about how Sazare had to try the miso.

Sazare stood in the ward doorway.

*He's had that done to him,* he thought. *That's what that was. Somebody
has been furious on his behalf in front of him, at some point, and he
found out that it made him have to manage them, and he has worked out —
at twelve — that when a person tells you a bad thing about their own life
in a flat voice, the kindest available response is to be angry for exactly
four seconds and then take them to lunch.*

*Nobody taught him that. There is nobody who would have taught him that.*

---

Ichiraku was a stall on the west road with six stools under a striped
curtain, and it held eight people at an absolute squeeze, and there was a
man of about fifty behind the counter and his daughter beside him and a
great deal of steam.

They were half a bowl in before Sazare understood that the entire
establishment loved that boy.

It was not indulgence. He watched it for twenty minutes to be sure,
because he did not trust himself on this subject any more and had said so
to himself in writing. The old man argued with Naruto about the amount of
chilli. The daughter told him off about his elbows. When Naruto said
something outrageous about a mission the old man said "*Did* you," in the
voice of a person who has been listening to this boy talk rubbish across
that counter for eight years and has decided to enjoy it.

And when the second bowl went down in front of him nobody said anything at
all about who was paying.

*Sixty ryō*, said the board, in chalk, above the pass. *Naruto Special
— 60.*

Sazare Ruri sat on a stool under a striped curtain and did the arithmetic
without wanting to, because he could not not.

Sixty ryō was a bowl of noodles. Four hundred thousand ryō was a chandler
on a shelving beach at the top of the ebb. Which is to say that a woman who
moved thirty-one people across nine hundred yards of tideway for whatever
they had in their pockets had cost, at the going rate, six thousand six
hundred and sixty-six bowls of noodles.

He put the number in the tray, loose, not beside anything, and picked up
his chopsticks.

---

"What was she like?" said Naruto.

"Sorry?"

"Your sister." He said it with his mouth full and no preamble whatsoever
and absolutely no idea that he had done anything. "You said on the road
you had one. What was she like?"

The steam went up off the counter. Somebody outside went past with a
handcart.

Two people in the Land of Fire had asked Sazare Ruri about his family. One
of them was sixty-eight years old and had a hat and a pipe and had wanted
to know what his mother *made*, because forty years of reading forms had
taught him to be suspicious of the parts nobody thinks are relevant.

The other one was twelve and had asked because he wanted to know.

"She was fifteen when I was eight," said Sazare. "She was the cleverest
person I've ever met and she'd have been offended to hear me say it, and
she came in at three in the morning about four nights a week for four
years and ate standing up. She taught me a code as a game and I thought it
was a game for the entire time. She lied to our mother's face at a kitchen
table for thirteen months and she was *good* at it, and I watched her do
it about a hundred times and never once saw it.

"She used to steal chisels. She stole me one for my birthday when I was
eight and pretended she'd bought it and I knew and she knew I knew.

"And she got about — " he stopped, and started again, and got it out
level, "— I don't know the number. Nobody will ever know the number. She
got some quantity of frightened strangers out of a district that doesn't
exist any more, and it killed everybody in our house, and I have been
trying for four years to work out whether she was wrong to and I can't do
it, and the last time somebody asked me I said no."

Naruto put his chopsticks down.

"She sounds brilliant," he said.

And that was all, and he did not say anything about how it must have been,
or how sorry he was, or how Sazare must miss her; and he picked his
chopsticks back up and started on the egg.

Sazare sat with his hands round a bowl and found that his eyes had gone
hot and that this was going to keep happening in this country and that
there was nothing whatever he could do about it.

---

At the end, over the last of it, Naruto said — not as a confidence, not
leading up to anything, in exactly the tone he had used about the pork:

"I'm going to be Hokage, by the way."

Sazare looked up.

He had, by then, been in Konoha for four months. He had heard that
sentence secondhand about eleven times, in the market and on the north
road and once in the ward, always in the same faintly worn way that people
say a thing everybody has heard.

The old man behind the counter did not look up from the pass. The daughter
went on wiping. Nobody laughed. Nobody agreed. It went into that stall and
was absorbed by the walls the way a sentence is when eight years of people
have decided how they are going to handle it.

And Sazare Ruri, who had been reading people professionally since he was
eight years old, and who had assessed this exact boy from behind a root
plate on the evidence, correctly, in every particular, and had written
*he'll be dead inside two years* —

— sat on a stool and turned it over and found, honestly, at the bottom of
it, that he had no idea.

None. Not a leaning, not a probability, nothing he would have put in a
ledger.

"All right," he said.

"You don't believe me."

"I don't *know*," said Sazare. "That's different, and it's rarer, and
you'd want to hang on to it, because there's about four hundred people in
this village who think they know and every single one of them has less
evidence than I have."

Naruto Uzumaki thought about that for a moment with a piece of pork
halfway to his mouth.

"That's the weirdest nice thing anybody's ever said to me," he said.

"Yes," said Sazare. "I'm working on it."
