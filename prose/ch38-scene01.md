# Chapter 38, Scene 1 — Forty Times

*Act 4, part D. Konoha General, the second floor. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,352 words.*

---

He had passed her about forty times and had never said one word to her,
and he could tell you what her sandals were.

That was the whole of it and he arrived at it standing at the top of
the second-floor stairs on a Tuesday in the twelfth month with an
observation board under his arm, watching a girl of fourteen come up
the flight below him with a covered tray, and he did not move out of
the way in time and she went past him with the tray held level and said
"Sorry" without looking up, and was gone through the doors into the
women's end.

*Rung one.*

Standard-issue medic sandals, third year of wear, resoled once by
somebody competent — the stitching was not hers, it was too even. Two
pouches, both on the right, the upper one for instruments and the lower
one worn at the flap in a narrow band from a hand going to the same
place several hundred times a day. Hair off her face and pinned with a
plain clip. Nails cut back to nothing, which every medic in that
building did within about a month of starting and which she had plainly
done for longer than that.

There was a scar on the back of her left hand, about an inch, that had
been closed by somebody very good and recently enough that it was still
pink.

*Rung two: she carries the tray with her forearms rather than her
wrists, which nobody does unless they have dropped one.*

*Rung three: she dropped one, once, and it mattered enough that she
changed how she carries them.*

He stood at the top of the stairs for some time.

He had, over about fifteen months, established the following without
ever once deciding to establish it: that she came in on Tuesdays and
Fridays and sometimes on the Sunday; that she went to the fever ward
first and the women's end second and never came to the men's end at
all; that she had started coming about two months after the Fifth was
inaugurated; that the auxiliaries called her Haruno-san and the two
senior nurses called her Sakura and that she did not appear to mind
either; and that Fujita, who did not defer to anybody, moved a
scheduled procedure once to accommodate her.

He had never spoken to her.

He had never spoken to her, and until about ninety seconds ago he had
not registered that as a thing that required an explanation, and now
that he had registered it he found that he had an explanation ready and
that it was four pages long and had been sitting in a notebook since
the tenth month.

*Chapter three. Of Inquiry, and the asking after a person's affairs.*

*Cannot do.*

---

He did the round and did not get any of it wrong and could not
afterwards have told anybody a single thing about it.

At the end of it he went into the sluice, which was empty, and stood
with his hands on the edge of the sink for about four minutes, and did
the arithmetic properly, because he had learned by then that the only
thing worse than the number was not having it.

*I have been in the same building as Haruno Sakura for approximately
fifteen months.*

*I met her before that. On a road, in the eleventh month of my
sixteenth year, for eleven days.*

*She is the person who told me what tree-walking was.*

He put both hands flat on the porcelain.

*She gave me the word. Off the top of her head, in about nine seconds,
on a hill, because I asked her a question and she answered it and
Kakashi stopped her before she could give me the method, and I said*
that's plenty*, and it was, and it was the first name anybody had ever
given me for anything I could do.*

*I have thought about that afternoon about four hundred times. It is in
my notebook. It is the reason I know there is a first control exercise
and a second one and enough of them to be numbered.*

*And she has walked past me on those stairs forty times and I have never
opened my mouth.*

---

The reasons assembled themselves without being asked, the way reasons
did, and he made himself write all of them down that evening and then
made himself grade them.

*1. She is the Hokage's apprentice and I am a technician on a
probationary classification and there is a considerable difference in
standing.*

He looked at that one for a while and then wrote *false* beside it,
because it was, comprehensively — he had told the Fifth Hokage her
degradation interval was too coarse, he had argued four clauses of a
treaty through Kōji, he had refused Kanzaki a test in her own yard.
Standing had never once stopped him saying anything to anybody.

*2. There has been no occasion. We work different ends of the floor.*

*False.* Forty occasions. He had counted them.

*3. She may not remember me.*

He stopped on that one.

He wrote, underneath it, slowly:

*This is the true one and it is not a reason, it is a fear, and I have
been carrying it for fifteen months without once looking at it.*

*If I speak to her and she does not remember, then the afternoon on the
hill was an afternoon on a hill. It was eleven days of walking beside
three people who were kind to me in the ordinary way that people are
kind to somebody they are escorting, and the sentence that reorganised
my entire understanding of what I was allowed to know was a
twelve-year-old girl answering a question in passing and forgetting it
by supper.*

*Which is almost certainly what it was.*

*And at the moment I do not know that. At the moment there is a version
in which it mattered to somebody other than me, and I have been
protecting that version for fifteen months by not testing it, in
exactly the way I protected the reparations entry by not filing it.*

He put the pencil down.

*I have now done this twice with a form and once with a person.*

---

The other thing he wrote that night, at the bottom of the page, was the
part that took him longest and which he nearly did not write at all.

*There is a fourth reason and it is worse than the third.*

*She was there.*

*She was on that bridge. She was twelve years old and she was on the
bridge at Nami and she saw the whole of it — the mirrors, and the boy in
the mask, and what happened afterwards on the beach and on the
headland — and she was standing about ninety feet from me for two weeks
while I made every single decision I made that fortnight.*

*She is the only person in this village who was there.*

*Not the invasion. That was mine and there are four hundred witnesses to
it and it is written up in three offices. The other one. The
fortnight I have never told anybody about in full, including Inoichi,
who did not go looking, and including Tsunade, who has not asked.*

*If I speak to her she may bring it up, and she will not know she is
bringing it up, because to her it will be* do you remember Zabuza's
apprentice, *and to me it is a boy of fifteen who wanted one person to
need him and who is under four feet of turf on a headland because he
got it.*

He read that back.

Then he wrote, in the flat hand:

*None of the above is a reason not to say good morning to somebody on a
staircase.*

*Chapter three, page eleven: "The inquiry need not be large. It is
frequently better that it is not. The reader will find that the
difficulty is never in the question but in the four seconds preceding
it."*

*Tuesday. She comes in on Tuesdays.*
