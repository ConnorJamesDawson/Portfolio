# Chapter 49, Scene 3 — Nine Out of Ten

*Act 4, part D. Konoha General, the fourth-floor landing. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,655 words.*

---

He went to find Kaburagi on the Wednesday morning because she had told
him to ask her tomorrow and because he had been unable to work out the
answer on his own, which had irritated him for most of the night.

*Start with the ones who are not frightened of it.*

He got as far as the fourth-floor landing.

Sakura was coming up with a tray and the day's second-floor bloods and
her hair pinned back the way she wore it on a long shift, and she was
fifteen now — had been since the sixth month, and had said so twice
and had not made anything of it — and she came up four steps and
stopped and looked at his left shoulder.

Katsuyu said, "Good morning."

"Morning, Katsuyu-sama."

And then Sakura laughed, and it was a perfectly ordinary laugh, and she
put the tray on the rail and got her satchel open and took out a folded
sheet of paper.

"Right," she said. "New criterion."

---

It was the marking scheme.

He knew it at nine feet. Folded three times and carried in a satchel
for fourteen months, annotated down one margin in a hand that had got
smaller towards the bottom of the page, four criteria and ten marks and
the failure modes written out because he would want the failure modes
more than the definitions.

She had added to it perhaps twenty times since the fifth month. It had
a second sheet now. He had watched her write *found a door, went
through it, stopped when it was finished* in a box on a staircase in
the fifth month and had thought about it for a fortnight.

"Criterion five," said Sakura, writing. "*Did you get the thing without
asking for it?*"

"Sakura—"

"Three out of three." She turned the sheet round so he could see it,
still smiling. "That's you on nine. Best score anybody's ever had off
this and I've been running it fourteen months."

There was about a second and a half.

It was not a long time. He measured it afterwards against other things
he had measured and it came out at somewhere between one and two
seconds, and it was the interval in which two people standing on a
landing both heard the same sentence.

Then Sakura said, "That was a joke," and the tone had gone.

---

He did not say anything.

That was the correct move and he knew it was the correct move for about
four seconds before he did it, and doing it was the single hardest
thing he had done in that building, because there were nine sentences
available and every one of them was true and helpful and would have
made it worse.

*It isn't a contract.*

*I didn't ask for it, I asked her whether she was free.*

*It's a divided piece and the main body is with Tsunade and the
arrangement is untouched.*

*It is a research instrument and it will be gone by the ninth month.*

All true. Every one of them an explanation, delivered downward, to a
person who had just told him it was a joke.

So he said nothing, and stood on a landing, and watched her fold the
sheet back up along the three creases it already had.

"It was a joke," said Sakura again.

"I know."

"Don't do that."

"Do what?"

"The thing where you agree with me in a *voice*," she said. "You've got
a voice you use when you're being careful with somebody and I have
heard you use it on patients and I would rather you just—"

She stopped.

She put the folded sheet in the satchel and picked the tray back up and
her hands were completely steady, and Sazare stood four feet away and
read her the way he had read a fishmonger's thirty years off the backs
of his fingers.

---

*She is not angry.*

That was the first thing and it was the thing that made the rest of it
land, because he had constructed anger on the way up the stairs the
moment he had seen her face, and there was none in it anywhere.

*Her hands are steady because she has decided they are going to be.
There is nothing in the jaw and nothing in the shoulders. She has
looked at the tray twice in ninety seconds, which is a thing people do
when they want a reason to be somewhere else, and she has not once
looked at my shoulder since the first second, which is a thing people
do when they have already looked enough.*

*She is embarrassed.*

*She made a joke and it came out as the true thing and she has been
standing here for ninety seconds being embarrassed about having said
it, and she is going to go and think about it for a week, and the week
is going to be worse than the ninety seconds.*

He did the thing he was bad at.

"Have you been given one?" he said.

Sakura stopped with the tray at the top of the stairs.

"...What?"

"A piece. Or the contract, or whatever the right word is — I don't know
the right word and I've had it on my shoulder for a day and a half,
which is part of the problem." He kept his voice ordinary, which took
some doing. "You've been her apprentice for two years and three months.
Have you been given one?"

There was a pause.

"No," said Sakura.

"Have you asked?"

"You don't *ask*." She turned round. "You don't ask for that. It's not
a thing you put in for. She'll do it when — she said *when you're
ready*, in the third month of last year, and that's the whole of the
conversation and it's the only time it's ever been mentioned and I have
thought about it approximately every day since."

"And are you?"

"Am I what?"

"Ready," said Sazare. "By your own assessment. Not hers."

Sakura looked at him for a long moment.

"I've been ready for about eight months," she said.

---

He did not fix it.

That was the other correct move and it was harder than the first one,
and he managed it by holding onto a single sentence he had written in a
notebook in the fifth month after a conversation on a staircase and had
underlined: *she is better at this than I will ever be, and the thing
she needs from me is not assistance.*

"Right," he said.

"That's it? Right?"

"I've got four things I could say and I've been through all of them
while you were talking and every one of them is me making this smaller
so I feel better." He put his hands in his pockets. "You've been ready
for eight months and nobody's said anything, and I asked a question at
seven in the morning on Tuesday because I wanted a witness for a bench
test, and I've got one on my shoulder inside four minutes, and I have
not earned any part of that and there is no version of it where I did."

"That's not—"

"It's the whole of it," said Sazare. "It's arithmetic. You've done two
years and three months of the hardest work in this building and I asked
a slug whether she was free, and if I stand here and explain to you
that it's a divided piece and a research instrument then I am
explaining arithmetic to somebody who can do arithmetic."

Sakura stood at the top of the stairs with a tray.

"You're not going to say anything to her," she said. "About the eight
months."

"No."

"Because I will—"

"I'm not going to say anything to her," said Sazare, "and I want to be
clear about why, because if I get it wrong you'll spend a fortnight
wondering. If I go to the Hokage and tell her that her apprentice
thinks she's ready, then the thing you get, you get because I said so,
and it will be *mine* for the rest of your life."

"...Yes."

"I've had that done to me four times and I know what it costs and it
costs exactly what you think it costs."

Sakura nodded, once.

"Six out of ten," she said.

"For what?"

"For the last ninety seconds." She got the tray properly onto her hip.
"You didn't stop when it was finished. Criterion four. It was finished
at *right* and you kept going for four sentences."

"They were good sentences."

"They were *excellent* sentences and criterion four doesn't care."

She went up.

---

He found Sister Kaburagi in the linen room at about half past ten and
did not need to ask her anything.

"You've met Haruno," she said.

"On the landing."

"Mm." Kaburagi went on counting sheets. "That's the one I meant. I'd
have said it yesterday and you'd have gone and found her and made a
speech at her and it would have been considerably worse, so I told you
to come back today, and you have come back today having already had it
happen to you properly, which is the only way anybody learns anything
in this building."

"She made a joke."

"Of course she made a joke. She's fifteen and she has been in this
hospital since she was thirteen and she is extremely good at being
pleasant about things." Kaburagi put a sheet on the pile. "You want to
watch for the jokes, Sazare. The ones who come and tell you straight
have already decided to forgive you. It's the ones who make it funny
you've got a fortnight of work with."

He stood in the doorway.

"There's nothing I can do about it," he said.

"No," said Kaburagi. "There isn't, and that's the correct answer, and I
notice it's taken you a day and a half to reach it, which is about a
week faster than last year."
