# Chapter 26, Scene 1 — The Street

*Act 4, part B. Konoha Crush. Sazare Ruri, age sixteen. POV: Sazare.
~2,590 words.*

---

Sazare pulled.

The plate around his forearm softened under his right palm. Chakra returned
through the surface course, warm where the air was cold, thinned by the
mineral it had persuaded and the distance it had travelled. He followed the
returning line down through the seed and let it continue past him in his mind,
towards the fragments below.

The first was at the foot of the roof. The second lay in the western gutter.
Then three together beneath the broken gate, one against the admissions step,
six around the overturned tables, two inside the stall and others without
positions he could name. Some answered clearly. Some were little more than the
memory of pressure. All of them pointed the same way when he touched them:
home, towards the chakra that had made them.

He had practised this at a table with a bowl beneath his hands.

At contact, recall was cheap. Across a room it cost nearly twice what came
back. At twenty yards a finished tile slumped at its edges and retained the
centre because his reach failed before the structure did. Beyond that, he
could feel his own work without moving enough chakra to alter it. Kanzaki had
called the distinction useless until measured and then spent two Thursdays
measuring it.

The furthest clear answer now came from the hospital gate, twenty-nine yards
downslope. The pieces at the bend were further. Smoke, walls and bodies
changed nothing about the sensation; distance changed everything. The nearest
fragment felt like a tool held in his other hand. The furthest was a word
heard through two closed doors.

He counted the answers he could separate.

One. Two. Five at the gate. Four on the road above it. The plate beneath the
admissions window. Three inside the forecourt. A cluster where the first
resonant strike had thrown the gutter rib. Seven around the stall, though two
might have been the same broken face touching in the road. Beyond those, a
haze of his own work without edges.

Thirty-one distinct routes.

The attackers below numbered twenty-seven. They were not aligned with the
routes. Some stood on bare paving. Some had two or three fragments within
reach. One at the hospital door had kicked every visible piece away from his
feet and stood in the clean space he had made.

Sazare's first conclusion was that the field could not work.

His second was that he had no alternative requiring less than two functioning
legs.

He stopped pulling.

For an instant the street became silent inside the work. The hospital doors
bent inward. A Sound shinobi put one foot on the admissions desk behind them.
Another raised a resonator towards the second-floor windows. Twenty-seven
people occupied the approach between the bend and the entrance, not counting
three bodies that might still be moving.

Sazare pushed.

Nothing happened.

The nearest fragment took chakra, warmed and remained a fragment. The plate on
his arm thickened beneath his hand. Every familiar habit had sent the
instruction to what he touched rather than to the route beyond it.

Below, the resonator discharged. The men's-end glass whitened.

Sazare changed the instruction.

Not *grow*. Too much shape. Too many decisions. He used the first thing he had
ever taught crystal to do when he was eight years old and had not known the
word for chakra nature: continue the line already present.

The plate answered. At its buried seed, the course reversed direction. Chakra
went away from his palm, down the remembered path and out.

The fragment at the foot of the roof moved.

A point no longer than his smallest finger grew from its broken edge. It came
fast because it had no weight to carry and no second course to lay. The Sound
shinobi standing over it looked down.

Sazare pushed harder.

The street changed colour.

It changed in an order his eyes could not follow but his hands could. The
contact plate fired first. The fragment at the eave followed. Then the
hospital gate answered in three separate pulses, each delayed by the distance
between a heartbeat and the next. The bend did not answer at all until he
forced more chakra into the outward route. When it did, the effort struck the
inside of his elbow hard enough to bend the arm despite the plate.

There was no sound of crystal growing at that scale. There were many small
sounds: paving ticking, cloth tearing, sandal leather parting, one high glass
note from a spike that grew into another piece and split itself. Beneath them
came the human response.

The defenders inside the hospital did not know what was happening. A Konoha
spear withdrew through the door gap. Somebody dragged the admissions desk
backwards, away from the blue points rising beyond it. The safe line Sazare
had imagined from the roof did not exist. There were wounded people in the
forecourt, defenders behind the doors and one Konoha body in the road whose
hand still moved.

He had to build the safe line while the field was active.

Blue-grey lines appeared wherever the fighting had left material: narrow,
abrupt, without the broad faces of walls. One went through a sandal and foot
and stopped against the bone above. One entered the back of a knee. A flat
plate under the admissions window produced four points along the same fracture
and took two men through the thighs. The remains of the gutter rib grew
sideways into the people using the wall for cover. A piece caught in a trouser
cuff extended upwards.

Men shouted. The first sound was surprise rather than pain.

The field was too much to feel separately. Twenty pathways opened through him
and became thirty. Each took a different amount. Some fragments exhausted the
order in a thumb's breadth. Others found mineral beneath them and continued.
His simple instruction met their broken faces and obeyed the face: up from
one, sideways from another, back along a course from a third. He could not aim
them. He could only choose which answers to feed.

The hospital side.

He closed everything within six feet of the steps. The exclusion cost more
than the growth. Every route had to be found and refused. One fragment beside
the door answered late and put a point through a Sound sandal before he shut
it. Another lay under a Konoha body whose chakra still moved. Sazare cut the
line and lost all the fragments beyond it for a second.

The body belonged to the defender who had bought the seventh second for the
stretcher. Sazare recognised the torn shoulder of the uniform. Alive or dead,
the man occupied the route between the gate fragments and Sazare's reach.
Cutting around him was not a physical action, but his mind insisted on
treating it like one. He imagined a groove beside the man's ribs, sent chakra
down it, and felt the path split.

The crystal beyond answered again.

The fragment beneath the body remained still.

That was selection. Crude, expensive, one person at a time. He could do it.

He found Ashida under the roof section because three fragments lay around him
and none beneath him. Daichi was harder: crystal touched his burned sleeve.
Sazare stopped that route at the cloth and lost two usable plates on the other
side. Mika and Ren were outside his sensation entirely. He did not know
whether that meant safe or dead.

Every exclusion reduced the field. Every uncertainty became a place he refused
to activate. The result was not the largest attack he could make. It was the
largest remainder after he had removed the people he could not accept killing.

The Sound formation broke towards the walls.

That drove them into the pieces left by the first barriers.

The crystal at the gate grew from both sides. It did not form a wall. It made
the space in which the wall had failed briefly full of crossing lines. Four
people entered it together. One came out, leaving part of a sleeve and enough
blood to mark each step.

Somebody below understood.

"Get off the crystal!"

There was no part of the road without it.

The order created panic rather than movement. One man jumped onto an
overturned table whose underside contained a seeded plate. Another climbed the
pharmacy shutter and put both hands beside a fragment embedded by the
explosion. Two tried to use a fallen comrade as a clear surface. The body lay
across three pieces too small to see through the blood.

Their preparation against Guren made the mistake worse. They understood large
faces, visible pillars and continuous crystal that could carry a destructive
vibration. They had been taught to break structure and advance through what
remained. The road around the hospital contained almost nothing except what
remained.

One resonator user planted his feet and swept both arms. Air pressure struck
every standing face between the bend and gate. The faces failed together. For
an instant the field went flat.

Then forty new edges answered instead of twelve old ones.

The man had enough time to look at the apparatus on his arms as if it had
betrayed him.

They went high. Six shinobi leapt for rooflines at once. Sazare felt the
fragments lose their loads as feet left the ground. He had nothing placed
above them. For one second the centre of the street was clear.

Then the thin plates thrown there by earlier resonant strikes answered from
walls, shutters and eaves. A fragment embedded in the dumpling-stall sign grew
through the hand that caught it. Three pieces on the pharmacy roof produced
points along the landing edge. One shinobi changed direction in the air and
collided with another. Daichi, alive under the remains of the stall awning,
raised his blade and killed the one who fell nearest.

The technique had no centre except Sazare, and he was beginning to come apart.

His right eye lost colour first. The road became grey on that side, blue
crystal distinguishable only because he could feel it. His hearing lagged:
impacts reached him after he saw bodies move. The roof beneath his right knee
seemed to tilt despite the chimney remaining vertical.

He adjusted his hand on the forearm plate and left skin behind on a rough
fracture. Blood crossed the crystal. The plate tried to grow from the iron in
it and produced a red-rimmed tooth beneath his palm. He stopped that route
too.

The exclusions accumulated until maintaining them required more attention than
the attack. Hospital steps. Konoha body. Ashida. Daichi. A wounded civilian
behind the stall counter whom he had not seen until a fragment there took
weight. His own hand. Each became a held absence inside the outward pressure.

He understood then why Guren used large forms. Large forms were simple. A wall
did not need to know whom it was allowed to be a wall for.

Heat crossed his chest from left to right. His vision narrowed until the
hospital window and the nearest chimney remained sharp and everything else
shook. Chakra did not leave smoothly through pathways used one at a time. It
found every weak place in them. His fingers locked on the plate. Blood came
from his nose and ran over his upper lip.

He kept pushing.

The attackers who had reached bare roof were outside the field. The ones in
the road were not. A Sound shinobi used a resonator at his own feet. The
crystal around him broke into smaller pieces. For half a second he believed
that had helped.

Every smaller piece retained part of the line.

The next growth was finer. Dozens of needles entered through boots, calves and
the hand he had put down to rise. He stopped moving.

"Guren's doesn't—" somebody shouted.

The rest became a wet sound as a plate against the stationer's wall extended
through his back.

Sazare found the speaker by the clearer response of the crystal under load. He
did not know the man's face. He knew the pressure of his weight on three
fragments and the resonator attached to his arm. The name had come from that
part of the street twice.

He could have cut those lines and left him alive.

He did not.

The three fragments continued towards one another.

The man ceased to be a source of information.

He folded around the growth rather than falling away from it. The resonator
struck the paving and discharged once without hands operating it. Nearby
fragments powdered. Sazare felt their ordered centres vanish in the pulse.

The loss travelled back to him: not pain, exactly, but the abrupt absence of
something he had been holding. He knew in that moment that the connection
could be severed, that Sound's countermeasure did work if it reached the seed
closely enough, and that the difference between his crystal and Guren's was
not immunity.

It was redundancy.

The street contained more seeds than the resonators could find before the
seeds killed their owners.

The decision registered and produced nothing that could be used before the
next one.

At the bend, new movement entered the road. Sazare reached for it and found no
crystal beneath the first rank. Konoha flak jackets emerged through the smoke.
A Hyūga led them, pale eyes fixed not on the hospital but on the lines between
Sazare and the ground.

Her head turned sharply towards the roof.

"Stop!" she shouted.

Sazare tried.

The outward pathways did not close together. Some had caught in the fragments;
some continued feeding structures that had found stable mineral. Ordinary
recall was a hand drawing straw back through a narrow opening. This was the
straw split into a hundred wet fibres with the far ends tangled round the
street.

He pulled.

Crystal nearest the Konoha advance slumped. The points withdrew badly, leaving
ordinary mineral and blood. A spike under a falling Sound shinobi softened at
the base and changed the direction of the fall. A long face against the
pharmacy collapsed outward, forcing the Konoha line to halt.

Pulling one structure was slow.

Pulling all of them was slower.

The Hyūga reached the bend. She struck two approaching Oto shinobi without
looking away from Sazare. Blue veins stood at her temples. She could see every
failure he was trying to hide from himself.

"Hospital side clear," she called. "East gutter live. Roof edges live. Do not
touch blue material."

The Konoha shinobi divided around the field with the confidence of people who
had finally received an instruction they understood. They took the bare
roofline, entered through the laundry yard and hit the surviving attackers
from three sides.

Sazare released the plate.

The reversed connection failed at once. Some crystal remained standing. Some
fell. A hundred fragments became only material again, though he could still
feel the nearest ones faintly when he looked.

Below, nobody occupied the centre of the street on their feet.

Sazare counted movement because he did not want the other number.

Nine Konoha shinobi advancing. Five Sound withdrawing over the eastern roofs.
Three wounded attackers moving in the road. Daichi beneath the awning. Ashida
still under the roof section, one arm out. Mika nowhere visible. Ren already
carried uphill.

Twenty-four bodies in positions that had contained moving enemies when he
pushed.

Two of them moved afterwards.

The Hyūga looked up at him through tile, smoke and the crystal plate as if
none of those things existed.

"Stay there," she said.

The instruction was sensible.

Sazare's damaged knee slid out from behind the batten.

He went down the roof.
