# Chapter 39, Scene 5 — Half a Second, Overshot

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, off the west end of the market row. Sazare
Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,437 words.*

---

He did not decide to do it.

He established this afterwards, at considerable length, over about four
months, and it remained the single least satisfactory finding in eleven
hundred entries: there was no moment of decision. There was no
four-second gap. There was no check that ran and was overruled, and no
check that ran and failed, and no check.

What there was, was this.

Tsunade said *four words is a great deal more than most people give
me*, and picked her cup up, and Sazare looked past her at the room.

Six tables, turned ninety degrees.

Handa in a chair with arms, at the near end, with his stick hooked over
the back, in a room three streets further than he had walked in a year.
Ōtaki Sue with a slate on the table, at the eleventh hour, who went to
bed at nine. Nemoto, who had not been past the end of the block since
the winter. Anko with her boots on a chair. O-Shige pouring for people
who had not ordered anything. Kanzaki arguing about a wine list.
Sakura, fourteen, in a drinking house on a Friday, holding his coat
over the back of her chair because there had been nowhere to put it.

Yagi, who had told him she was doing something on the ninth that she
could not move and would not lie about.

Eleven people, in a room somebody had turned round, on a date three
months after the fact, because a boy had told a woman in a bar that his
mother had stopped writing the dates down in the year he was eleven.

Sazare Ruri stood up.

---

The next thing he was aware of, and he was extremely clear about this
afterwards because it was the first thing in the sequence he could
actually date, was that his face was against the shoulder of a
grass-green haori, and that his arms were round somebody, and that they
had been for a measurable interval.

The interval was the part he could never subsequently reconstruct.

He had put his arms round the Fifth Hokage of Konohagakure.

He worked it out from the geometry, the way he worked out everything —
the position of his own feet relative to the chair he had been in, the
angle, the fact that his right hand was flat between her shoulder
blades and his left was not, the cold spot on his cheek where the silk
was, the smell of it, which was sake and lamp oil and something under
both that he did not have a name for. He assembled the whole of it in
about a second and a half from the evidence, in exactly the way he had
assembled a street from sixty-three chalk circles, and arrived at the
conclusion.

*I am hugging her.*

*I did not decide to do this.*

And then, immediately after, in the same second, with no gap at all:

*She has not moved.*

---

She had not.

He catalogued it. He could not not; it was the only thing he had ever
been able to do and it did not switch off for this any more than it
switched off for anything else, and so what he came away with — the
whole of what he came away with, and he wrote it down four days later
and read it about four hundred times over the following two years
without ever once being able to interpret it — was a list.

*Interval before any response: approximately one and a half seconds.
Possibly two. During which she did not move at all, including her
hands, which stayed where they were on the table.*

*Her breathing stopped for the first second of that and started again
in the second.*

*She did not put her cup down first. It was in her right hand when I
took hold of her and it was on the table afterwards and I do not know
when that happened, which means she did it during, which means she
made a decision.*

*Her left hand arrived first, at the back of my shoulder, flat. Her
right came about half a second after and higher, at the back of my
head, and stayed.*

*She did not speak.*

*She has said something in reply to every single thing I have said to
her for fourteen months, including four things that did not require a
reply. She did not speak.*

*At some point she took a breath that was audible at four inches and I
do not know what that was.*

*Duration, total, from the geometry and from Handa's account four days
later: between six and nine seconds.*

*Six seconds is a wall. I have grown a wall that lasted six seconds and
I know exactly what six seconds is and this was longer than that.*

*I do not know what any of the above means.*

*I have written it down because I do not know what it means and because
I have a rule about the ones I cannot classify, and I am aware that
this is the longest uninterpretable entry in this book.*

---

She let go first, which he was grateful for at the time and thought
about differently later.

She let go, and put one hand flat on the table, and looked at the wine
list that Kanzaki had left there, and said, in a voice that was almost
entirely ordinary:

"Sit down before you fall down."

He sat down.

"I didn't—"

"I know."

"I did not decide to do that."

"I *know*," said Tsunade. "I watched you not decide to do it. You went
up like somebody had lifted you." She turned the wine list over,
apparently to read the back of it, which was blank. "Don't apologise."

"I wasn't going to apologise. I don't know whether one's owed. I can't
work out what category it—"

"Ruri." She put the wine list down. "There is no category. Nothing has
happened that requires anybody to do anything. You have had eleven
people turn up to a thing you did not know was happening and you are
eighteen years old and you had a reaction, and I am not going to sit
here and let you take it apart in front of me and file it as a
transgression."

"That's what I do."

"I'm aware of what you do. I've read three years of it in your own
handwriting." She picked her cup back up, and he noticed — because he
noticed everything — that it took her two attempts to get hold of the
handle. "Have a drink."

---

He did not notice the room until about four minutes later.

That was the part he was most ashamed of afterwards, and it was also
the part he could least have prevented: for four minutes he sat at the
fifth table with his hands round a cup and ran the inventory, over and
over, trying to get the interval to resolve, and did not once look up.

When he did look up, the room was exactly as it had been.

Six tables, turned. Handa arguing. Ōtaki Sue writing. O-Shige pouring.
Nobody standing, nobody facing the door, nobody looking at the fifth
table.

Nobody looking at the fifth table.

He put his cup down very carefully.

*Eleven people are in this room and every single one of them has
arranged their face.*

*Anko is looking at a wall. Anko does not look at walls. Anko has
looked at every door in every room she has ever been in since she was
eleven years old and she is looking at a wall four feet from a woman
she does not trust.*

*O-Shige is pouring for a glass that is full.*

*Sakura has gone the colour of a plum and is talking to Yagi with great
animation about nothing whatsoever and Yagi is not listening to her,
because Yagi is looking at the table, and Nurse Yagi has never in two
years looked at a table.*

*They all saw it.*

*They all saw it and every one of them has decided, independently, in
about six seconds, to be somewhere else with their eyes.*

*Which is kind.*

*And it is also eleven people simultaneously performing not having seen
a thing, which is the single loudest thing a room can do, and I have
been reading rooms for eleven years and there is no version of this in
which I do not know exactly what has just happened.*

Handa, from the near end, without turning round, in the voice of a man
resuming an argument:

"Right. Ruri. Lapped dovetails."
