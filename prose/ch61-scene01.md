# Chapter 61, Scene 1 — Nobody Else Can Read It

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, the long room. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2360 words.*

---

Shizune went home at about a quarter to two.

She took the minute book with her as far as the outer office and then
stopped in the doorway and came back and put it on the table in front of
Tsunade instead, and said, "Not mine," and went, and did not explain
which of about four things she meant by it.

Jiraiya took the toads down at ten to.

Fukasaku had been on the corner of the table with his stick across his
knees since half past one and had not said anything else, and when
Jiraiya put a hand down he got onto it without any fuss at all, which
Sazare noted and put away, because a creature who had walked four
hundred miles rather than be summoned had just allowed himself to be
carried down a staircase and there was something in that he did not have
the frame for yet.

"Tomorrow," said Fukasaku, from the doorway.

"Tomorrow," said Tsunade.

And then the door went, and the third board on the landing went, and
after that there was nothing in the building at all.

---

He stood in the middle of the long room.

It was twenty to two in the morning on the second of the twelfth month.
There was a scrubbed table with nothing on it but a minute book and a
lamp and a small fold of paper. There was a mark on the boards where
they had scraped, which was not really a mark, which was a thing you
could only see if you knew where to look and had been kneeling on it
four hours earlier.

The second lamp on the sill had gone out at some point and nobody had
relit it.

*I ought to feel something.*

*That is not self-pity and I am not going to let it become self-pity. It
is an observation and I have had it four times in the last hour and it
keeps coming back, so it goes in.*

*It is done. It has been done since twenty past midnight. And I have
spent the four hours since scraping a floor and walking to an annexe and
standing at a window saying the worst thing I have ever worked out, and
at no point in any of it has there been —*

*I don't know what the word is.*

*A moment. Whatever people have.*

*I have never had one and I would not recognise one and I do not know
whether that is because there has not been one or because I am not
built to.*

---

"Ruri," said Tsunade.

She was still at the head of the table.

"Come here a minute."

He went over.

"Sit down."

He sat down.

She turned the minute book round so that it was facing him and opened
it at the last page, and put one finger about halfway down.

*0.11 g. Weighed on the seed balance, quartermaster's annexe, 00:41,
2/12. Present: S. Ruri, Fukasaku of Myōboku. Recorded by S. Kato at
01:04 on the report of S. Ruri.*

"Read that," she said.

"I've read it."

"Read it again, and then I'm going to say a thing, and you're going to
sit there and let me say all of it and not take a single piece of it
apart until I have finished."

"All right."

---

"You made history tonight," said Tsunade.

He opened his mouth.

"*Not a word.*"

He shut it.

"I've been sitting at this table for two hours working out how to say
this so that you can't get out of it, and I have not managed it, so I am
going to say it plainly and you are going to have to just take it.

"Every kekkei genkai on this continent has been a fact about *blood* for
eight hundred years. Not an opinion — a fact. It is in the treaties. It
is in the marriage law of two countries. It is the reason four thousand
people are in the ground in the Land of Water and it is the reason
Konohagakure has a clan structure at all, and there is not one person
alive who has ever seriously doubted it, and the ones who did could not
prove it.

"At twenty past midnight tonight a man with no clan and no line made
Shōton on the floor of this room.

"It was terrible. It weighed nought point one one grams. It came apart
in four seconds and it had no note in it and you have written all of
that down in your own hand and I have read it twice.

"And it *happened*.

"That's it. That's the whole thing. Nobody has ever done it, nobody has
ever seen it done, and there are five witnesses and a weight and a
minute, and you built the method in a kitchen argument at five in the
morning off a question nobody had thought to ask."

---

"And here's the second half," she said, "and it's the reason I have made
you sit down for it.

"Nobody is going to read it."

He did not say anything.

"Not this year and not in ten. That book is going in a place I have not
decided on yet and there will be an argument about it tomorrow and you
are going to lose that argument.

"You cannot publish. You have never been able to publish and you agreed
with the reasoning on the first of the eighth month and you have not
stopped agreeing with it, and tonight has made it worse, because up
until midnight you had an argument and now you have got an *object*.

"So there is no paper. There's no announcement. Nobody in this village
is going to know, and nobody at the Corps board, and nobody on that
council — and in forty years, when somebody finally does read that page,
you will be dead or old and it will be a footnote with a date on it and
somebody else's name will be on the work that comes after it."

Tsunade put her hand flat on the open book.

"You have made the largest finding of the century and it goes in a
drawer.

"And I am the only person in the world who can tell you what you did
tonight, because I am the only one who is allowed to have read it.

"So I am going to."

She looked at him.

"You did well today, Ruri," she said. "That is not enough and there
isn't any more and it is what I have got.

"You did *extremely* well."

---

He sat at the scrubbed table and could not do anything at all with any
of it.

*Right.*

*Take it apart later.*

He did not take it apart later either. He tried, twice, in the next
fortnight, at the low table with a pencil, and got about four lines in
both times and stopped, because the entry was worse than the thing, and
that was twice now in eleven days and he had begun to suspect it was
going to be a category.

What he had, sitting there at two in the morning, was smaller and had no
shape and was not writable.

*Nobody has ever told me I did well.*

*That is not true. Say it accurately. Yagi has told me I did well about
four hundred times and it is the ordinary noise a superior makes at the
end of a thing and it has never once meant anything to either of us.
Kanzaki said* that'll do *once in the ninth month about a splint and I
wrote it down because it was the only one of about nine hundred that was
not automatic.*

*Nobody has ever told me I did well about a* finding*.*

*There has never been anybody who could. That is the whole of it and it
is not anybody's fault. You cannot be told you did well by a person who
cannot see what you did, and for eleven years there has not been one,
and I built the entire apparatus of my life around not needing it and
the apparatus worked.*

*And she has just read the only page in the world that shows it, and
told me, at two in the morning, in an empty building, with nothing on
the table.*

---

He got up.

He did not decide to. He was aware, afterwards, going back over it, that
there had been no interval of any kind — that it had been the same class
of thing as the ward in the ninth month and the hill in the seventh and
the office on the eleventh, which was that he had found himself already
doing it.

He went round the corner of the table and put his arms round her.

She was still sitting down, which made it a bad hug in the mechanical
sense, and he got it wrong in a new way this time — too far round, with
her chair in the way — and she said "*hn*" and got an arm out and it
sorted itself.

"Fifth time," she said, into his shoulder.

"You've been counting?"

"Of course I've been bloody counting. I've been counting since the ward
and there is not one of them you have thought about first."

"No."

"Don't start now," said Tsunade.

---

He turned his head and kissed her.

He had no procedure for it.

That was the whole of it and he knew it at the time and he wrote it down
four days later in the flattest possible language, because there was
nothing else to write: that he had never done it, that he had no
reference at all, that the nine pages in a borrowed book at sixteen did
not go anywhere near it and that a girl on a staircase at fifteen had
said something he had not understood, and that at two in the morning on
the second of the twelfth month he turned his head and did it anyway
with nothing whatsoever to go on.

It was bad.

He was aware of it being bad *while it was happening*, which he had not
known was possible and which he would have said in advance was the worst
imaginable circumstance: the angle was wrong and he came in too fast and
his hand was in a place that did not help and he corrected twice, badly,
mid-way, which was the thing he did on a bench when an article was going
wrong and which was catastrophically the wrong instinct.

He got about four seconds of it.

And it did not matter in the slightest.

*It is not any better.*

That was the finding and he had it about a second and a half in and it
went straight through him without touching anything.

*She has done it three times and she is extremely good at it and I have
just done it appallingly and it is not any better and it is not any
worse and it is exactly the same thing.*

*None of it is in the execution.*

*Eleven years of being a man who thinks the execution is the whole of
the object, and it is not in the execution at all.*

---

She let him get to the end of it and then put a hand flat on his chest
and pushed him back about four inches and looked at him.

"That," said Tsunade, "was terrible."

"Yes."

"That was genuinely one of the worst—"

"I know."

"You *corrected*. Twice. Mid-way."

"I know."

"Like a man adjusting a *splint*."

"That's exactly what it was," said Sazare. "It's a bench instinct and
it's wrong and I'll have it out by about the fourth time."

Tsunade put her forehead on his shoulder and made a noise that was not
laughing and was quite close to it.

"Kami's *sake*," she said.

---

They stood there for a bit.

The lamp did what the lamp did. Somewhere three floors down a door went,
which would be Ōtani coming on, because it was two o'clock and Ōtani came
on at two, and he had come on at two on a Thursday for eleven months and
would say good morning in the same voice in four hours.

"Hime," said Sazare.

"Mm."

"Shall we go to bed?"

He heard it land.

He heard it land and then, about a second later, he heard the *other*
thing that was in it, arriving from somewhere behind him, entirely
without permission.

*Oh.*

*That was — I meant it is two o'clock in the morning and we have been
awake for twenty-two hours and there is a bed on this floor.*

*I did mean that. I want that established. That is what I said and it is
what was in my head and I have not got a second meaning in my head, I
have never had a second meaning in my head about anything, I do not
construct them and I cannot detect them and Sakura has a column for it.*

*And it is in the sentence anyway.*

*It got in on its own.*

He stood in the middle of the long room at two in the morning with his
arms round the Hokage of Konohagakure and understood, with complete
clarity and no comfort whatsoever, that he had just said something with
two things in it for the first time in his life and that he could not
account for either of them.

Tsunade did not say anything for about two seconds.

"Yes," she said. "Come on."

---

They went down the corridor and she put the lamp out.

He lay on his back in the dark and was fairly sure he was not going to
sleep, and had a great deal to be doing with, and got about four minutes
into the first of it.

Then she found his hand and took hold of it.

That was all. There was nothing else and neither of them said anything
and she did not turn over, and her hand was in his on top of the covers
between them in the dark on the second floor of the tower.

He went out in under a minute.
