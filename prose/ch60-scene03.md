# Chapter 60, Scene 3 — Hime

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's office. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~2350 words.*

---

"Can I show you something," he said. "It'll take about eleven minutes
and it's the reason I brought it downstairs."

Tsunade looked at the felt roll under his arm.

"Is this going to make my morning worse?"

"Yes."

"Put it on the desk."

He put it on the desk and unrolled it.

---

"Thursday afternoon," he said. "The article went at half past nine on
Friday morning, six days to the hour, and I wrote up the result and the
result is that the close is the mechanism and order isn't.

"I want to say the next bit carefully because it is not a result. It's an
inference, and it is the only inference available, and I have not tested
it and there is no way to test it that does not involve doing it."

"Say it."

"If the degradation is the close, then a route that is never closed does
not degrade."

Tsunade put the pen down.

"That's the whole of it," said Sazare. "That's the entire argument and it
is two lines long and I have had it since the Friday morning and I have
been walking round it for a week.

"Everything I have ever made is a sealed jar. I lay the structure, I
close, and what is in there is what it has got, and it runs out on the
sixth day. Nine hundred articles. Every one.

"I have never in my life left one open on purpose."

"You've left one open by accident," said Tsunade.

"Yes."

"On the fifteenth of the eighth month."

"Yes," said Sazare. "And I want that in the account before I go any
further, because you were going to say it and it is the correct thing to
say."

---

He put his hand on the hilt.

"May I?"

"...Go on."

He drew it.

It came out of the hilt over about a second and a half, the way it had
always come, and it was twenty-two inches and it was not a blade in the
sense that a smith would have recognised — it was one continuous thing
with the hilt, laid rather than fitted, and it came up out of the graded
core the way a course came up out of a grain.

And it was clear.

He heard her draw breath and did not say anything about it.

*The light goes the whole way in. I have had that since the twenty-first
on articles the size of my thumb and this is twenty-two inches of it and
I have not got used to it either.*

*Four voices, seated, clean.*

He held it up and did not close.

---

"That's the difference," he said. "That's the whole demonstration and
that's all there is to see.

"Ordinarily that comes out, does its four seconds or its six, and I
close, and then it's dead in a week whether I use it again or not. The
hilt survives because the hilt was never open — the hilt is two hundred
and three hours of graded conductor and it is the thing I actually made,
which I have had in writing from Shikkotsu since Tuesday in language I
did not enjoy.

"This one I drew at four on Thursday afternoon.

"It is now Saturday morning. Forty-two hours."

"And?"

"And nothing," said Sazare. "That's the finding. There is no
degradation. Not a fraction. The note is where it was on Thursday, the
light goes as far as it went on Thursday, and I have measured both twice
a day.

"Forty-two hours is not six days and I am not claiming it is. But every
article I have ever made was already measurably down by hour thirty and I
have got nine hundred of those and it is the single most consistent
figure in fourteen books.

"This one has not moved."

---

Tsunade sat back.

"What's it costing you?"

*There it is.*

*Four seconds. She has had the entire thing in four seconds and gone
straight to the only question that matters, and I have been on it since
Thursday afternoon and I have not got a clean answer.*

"That's the right question," he said, "and I've got a partial.

"It isn't drawing off me. I want to establish that first because it's the
one everybody will assume. It's drawing off the world — same as
everything else, same as the rose. Nature energy in, structure holds,
that's the whole of the mechanism and it does not need me at all once
it's laid.

"The problem is not what it's drawing.

"It's whether I'm in the path."

He turned the thing over.

"The rose was in my palm. Bare hand, direct contact with an open drawing
structure, and it filled through me because there was nothing in between
— and eighteen minutes later the line was at the mid-metacarpal.

"That is what an open article does to somebody who is standing in the
circuit."

"And you're not."

"I don't think I am," said Sazare, "and here's why, and it's the part
that has been sitting in my chest for two days.

"The hilt."

---

"Nineteen inches of graded conductor. Two hundred and three hours.
Every single one of those hours was spent on one problem, which was
controlling where the flow goes and at what rate — I have got eleven
pages of it and I thought the whole time I was building a *tap*.

"I was not building a tap.

"I have been building an isolator for seven months and I did not know
it. The reason I am not a statue this morning is that there are nineteen
inches of correctly graded material between my hand and an open route,
and it was made by a man who did not know what he was making it for.

"Which is the third answer off that leaf, word for word. *Stop treating
the blade as the article. The hilt is the thing you made.*

"They meant it about craftsmanship. It is not about craftsmanship. It is
the only reason this is survivable and none of us worked that out until
Thursday afternoon, including them."

Tsunade was quiet for a moment.

"You said partial."

"I said partial."

"Give me the rest."

"I don't know that nineteen inches is enough," said Sazare. "I know it
is enough for forty-two hours. I do not know that it is enough for six
months, and there is no way of establishing that except six months, and
if it turns out not to be enough the way I will find out is my hand.

"And there is no gauge. I wrote that on Thursday night in a yard and I
have not found a way round it since."

---

"There's a second cost," he said, "and it's smaller and it's certain.

"It sings."

"I can hear it."

"You can hear it at four feet in a quiet room," said Sazare. "I have
tested it. It is below what most people notice — you get it under the
lamp and under a corridor, and I have walked past nine people with it
under a coat and not one of them turned round.

"Handa would hear it. He has lost the top of his range and he still has
three voices, and he has been listening to stone for fifty years, and if
that man walks past me in the annexe he will stop.

"And there will be others and I do not know who they are.

"I cannot damp it. I have tried four things and all four either killed
the article or made it louder, and I am not going to keep trying,
because every one of those attempts was me putting my hands on an open
route and I have had that conversation with myself and lost it twice
already this week."

---

"Third," he said, "and then I'll stop, and this one's yours more than
mine.

"The hold figure has stopped being an exercise number."

Tsunade looked up.

"Fifteen minutes," said Sazare. "That's what I can carry at a full
gather, and I have been putting it in a column at the bottom of a page
for eleven weeks as though it were a score.

"It is not a score. It is a working constraint and it always was and I
did not know what it was for until Thursday.

"An open article on my person is a live route running continuously,
every hour, while I am doing everything else. Eating. Sleeping. Sitting
in a room with two mountains in it.

"And what I hold, and how much of it, and for how long — that is now
about a thing on my hip and not about a sphere on a felt.

"I do not know what that number needs to be. I know it needs to be
bigger than fifteen and I know that the exercise stopped working a
fortnight ago because a man told me what it was called."

---

Tsunade got up and came round the desk and looked at the thing on the
felt without touching it.

"Say the last one," she said.

"Which last one?"

"The one you've been not saying for eleven minutes, Ruri. The reason
you want to *carry* it. You've given me three costs and no benefit and
you would not have brought it down here to tell me about hazards."

*Right.*

"Two reasons," he said. "The first one is that it works. It's the first
thing I have ever made that will still be there next month, and I have
been making things that die on Friday since I was nine, and I want to
find out what it's like to own one.

"The second one is worse and you're going to like it less."

"Say it."

"I'm a lighthouse," said Sazare. "You said so on Friday morning and you
gave me four reasons and the fourth one was the true one, and I have not
been able to do a single thing about my face in eight days and I am not
going to be able to.

"So I can't take the light off.

"But I can put another one next to it."

---

Tsunade closed her eyes.

"You want to give them something else to look at."

"A man with no face who has never carried anything in three years turns
up on the first of the twelfth month with a new post and a new object on
his hip," said Sazare. "That is what they will talk about. It is
visible, it is interesting, it is *explicable*, and it arrives in the
same week as Fabrication Research.

"They will talk about the blade for a month and they will not be
talking about my face, and by the time they have finished with the blade
the face will have stopped being new.

"And nothing false gets said, because it is a real object and it is my
work and the post is real."

"That is the most cynical thing you have ever said to me."

"Yes," said Sazare. "I worked it out in about four minutes on Thursday
night and then I sat on a floor for a bit, because I have never in my
life designed a thing to be looked at."

---

Tsunade stood over the desk for a while.

"Carry it," she said.

"...That's it?"

"That is not *it*, that is the first of about nine sentences." She
picked up the pen. "Carry it. You'll do it whether I say so or not and
you'd tell me you were going to and then be honest about it for the rest
of my life, and I would rather have it on a docket.

"Measure it twice a day and write both down and give me the sheet on the
first of every month. If the note moves, you close it and you come and
find me in whatever room I am in. If the hand does anything at all —
anything, Ruri, cold, ache, a fingertip going odd in the bath — you
close it and you find me.

"And you do not draw a second one while that one is out. Not for a
demonstration, not for the toads, not for anybody."

"Agreed."

"And it doesn't have a name."

He looked up.

"What?"

"You've had that thing on a bench for seven months and there's a blank
panel on the guard and you have not written on it," said Tsunade,
"because you couldn't think of anything, and I have watched you not
think of anything since about the ninth month."

"I hadn't got that far."

"You never got that far because you never asked what it was *for*," she
said, "which is question six, and I have read the leaf, and you are not
going to solve that this morning either."

---

He looked down at twenty-two inches of clear crystal on a felt on the
Hokage's desk at ten to eight in the morning.

"Hime," he said.

There was a silence of a particular quality.

"*No*," said Tsunade.

"It's got a lot going for it."

"It has got nothing going for it."

"I say it about four times a week in front of other people and it is the
single largest hole in the whole arrangement," said Sazare, "and I have
been unable to do anything about it since the fortieth chapter of my own
life. And if there is an object of that name on my hip then every single
person who hears me say it has got something innocent to reach for."

"You have thought about this."

"I thought about it on the way down the stairs."

"On the way down the *stairs*."

"It's about eleven steps," said Sazare, "and it is a very good idea and
I would like it minuted."

Tsunade put both hands flat on her own desk.

"It is not going to be called Hime," she said, "and I want that
recorded, and I want the date on it, because I have been in this village
for fifty-three years and I know precisely what happens to a joke like
that once Jiraiya is in the building."
