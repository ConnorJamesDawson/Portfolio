# Chapter 57, Scene 1 — Four O'Clock

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Tsunade, age fifty-three.
POV: Tsunade. ~1570 words.*

---

She had been in since twenty to four because she had not been able to
sleep, and she was not going to dress that up as diligence.

*Third night.*

*Third night in five, and the other two I was unconscious, and one of
those was not sleep either, it was nine measures and a boy carrying me
over a roof.*

She had the compensation files in front of her and she had read the
same one twice.

*Right. He'll be here at four.*

She put the file down and looked at the door.

*And what are you going to do about that, exactly.*

---

He had come at four on the Friday and she had asked him why and he had
said no.

She had thought about that for four days. She had thought about it in
a Fire Temple meeting and in the bath and standing in a corridor with a
folder she was not reading, and the conclusion she kept arriving at was
one she did not much like.

*He said no.*

*That boy has never refused anybody anything in his life. He tells the
truth like other people breathe out — you ask him what he had for
breakfast and you get the breakfast and the reason and how long it
took, and I have watched him hand people information that cost him
enormously because they asked for it in a room.*

*And he sat in that chair at ten past four in the morning and said no,
and I said good, and I meant it, and I have been chewing on why I meant
it since.*

She knew why.

*Because if he'd answered it, he'd have had to say a sentence, and
whatever the sentence was I'd have had to do something about it.*

*And he knew that. That's the bit. He didn't refuse me because he was
embarrassed. He refused me because answering would have put it in the
room and he wasn't going to make me carry it at four in the fucking
morning.*

*Eighteen years old.*

---

She got up and went to the window.

The village at that hour was a black shape with four lights in it and
she had stood at that window at that hour perhaps eleven hundred times
and it had never once been restful.

*Say the rest of it. You've got twenty minutes.*

*I have not agreed to anything.*

*That's true, technically, in the way things are true when you're
writing a report you don't want read. I have not said yes. I have not
said one single word to that boy about what any of this is, and every
time it has come near being said I have put a hand out and moved it.*

*And I kissed him in front of Shizune on Thursday morning.*

She put her hand flat on the frame.

*Deliberately in front of Shizune. Not despite. Because.*

*Because if it happens in front of a witness, it's a thing that
happened in a kitchen with three people in it, and it can be a joke on
Monday, and I made it a joke on the way out of the door with the worst
sentence I could find at short notice, and Shizune knew exactly what I
was doing and left anyway.*

*And I have known Shizune for twenty years and she has never once let
me get away with anything, and on Thursday morning she got up and put
her cup in the sink and went out and shut the door.*

*That was her standing down.*

*And I have not thanked her for it and I am not going to, because
thanking her would mean saying what she stood down about.*

---

*Right. The other one. Get it over with.*

She did not turn round from the window.

*I remember my hands.*

*I was drunk. I was extremely drunk and I have been drunk for forty
years and I know precisely what I remember and what I do not, and I do
not remember the roofs and I do not remember the window and I have got
nothing at all between the office and about half past six on Thursday
morning.*

*I remember my hands.*

*I remember not letting go. I remember him trying to put me down and I
remember deciding — deciding, at some level that was still working —
that he was not going anywhere, and I remember shutting my hands and
that is the last thing in the entire night.*

*And he has never mentioned it.*

*He woke up in it. He woke up in a bed he had no business being in with
both my fists in his shirt and he lay there and did the arithmetic on
my breathing and looked at my forehead for four minutes and got called
a pervert for something else entirely, and he has not said one word.*

*He will not. He'd have to have a reading for it, and he hasn't got
one, and he doesn't say things he can't support.*

*So it sits there.*

*And it's the largest thing either of us has got and neither of us is
ever going to touch it, and I find — standing at this window at four in
the morning — that I do not want it touched.*

---

*Now the one you've been avoiding for four days.*

She made herself do it properly, out loud, in her own head, in four
sentences, the way she made her students do it.

*He looks at me.*

*Not the way men look. I have had forty years of men looking and I have
got a list and I gave him the list on Wednesday night at volume and he
sat there and did not defend a single item on it.*

*He looks at me the way he looks at a joint under a lamp. He has been
doing it for three years. He knows I come in at four. He knows what I
do with my hands before I read a number. He knows that I read the ones
with a family name on them first and alone and he knows I have never
told anybody that, and he told me so, in my own office, out of memory,
with no notebook, standing up.*

*Nobody has ever seen me.*

She stood at the window and had that plainly and did not soften it.

*Dan loved me. Dan loved me properly and I have never once said
otherwise and I never will. Dan looked at me and saw somebody worth
following and he was right and he died of it.*

*He did not see this.*

*Nobody has, for fifty-three years, and I built the whole of the
outside so that nobody would, and it worked, it worked completely, and
then a foreign eighteen-year-old with no manners and no filter walked
up a hill with eleven pages and started taking readings.*

*And the worst of it — the actual worst of it, the thing I could not
say to Shizune and cannot say to anybody —*

*— is that it's a relief.*

---

She heard him on the stair at three minutes to.

She knew the tread. That was the thing she had noticed on the Saturday
and had been irritated with herself about ever since: that she had
started knowing the tread, the way she knew Shizune's, the way she had
known four people's in her whole life.

*Right. Nothing happened. It's Wednesday.*

*Thursday.*

*It's whatever day it is and you are the Hokage and there is a stack of
compensation files and a delegation at nine.*

She went back to the desk and sat down and pulled the files over and
had the pen in her hand before he got to the landing.

He knocked.

"It's open," said Tsunade.

---

He came in and sat down without being told, which was new, and which
she noted and did not remark on.

He looked terrible.

*Sleep: not enough, and not the kind that helps. He's been up since
about two and he'll have been lying there since before that. Colour's
fine. He's eaten, or Shizune's made him.*

*And there's something else and I haven't got it.*

"There's a docket on the corner," she said. "Fourth page. Tell me
what's wrong with it."

"Nothing's wrong with it."

"Read it first."

"I read it on Sunday," said Sazare. "It came up under the temple file
at three o'clock and I read it then. There's nothing wrong with it,
because you struck clause four and reissued with the schedule attached,
which was the right thing to do, and it's come back approved.

"And there's a covering minute with my name on it."

Tsunade did not look up.

"Is there."

"Yes."

"Well," she said. "Somebody must have put it there."

---

That was as much as she was prepared to do about it and she got about
nine seconds of quiet for her trouble, and then the window at the end
of the room opened from the outside.

She did not move.

She had exactly one thought and she had it with complete clarity, and
it was not about security.

*Oh, you absolute bastard, not this morning.*

"Hime," said Jiraiya, from the sill, "your guard's asleep, your latch
is shot, and you look like death."

"It's four in the morning."

"It is." He got a leg over the frame. "So what the hell are *you* doing
awake?"
