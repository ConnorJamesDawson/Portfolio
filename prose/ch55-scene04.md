# Chapter 55, Scene 4 — What It's Called

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's house. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1600 words.*

---

Shizune did not move.

"You saw it," she said.

"I was four inches away from it for six hours."

"Most people are four inches away from it and see a diamond."

"I know," said Sazare. "I've been one of them for three years. I've
looked at it about four hundred times and I have never once *looked* at
it, and I want that on the record because it's the single worst piece of
observation of my entire life and I'd like to know how it happened."

"How did it happen?"

"Because it's on the coinage," he said. "It's on the coinage of two
countries and it's on the front of the tower and it's on every likeness
in the folder, and when a thing is on everything you stop seeing it,
and I have written four hundred pages on exactly that failure and put
it in the front of the book and I did it anyway to a woman I—"

He stopped.

Shizune waited.

"To a woman I look at," he said.

---

"It's called the Byakugō," said Shizune.

"Say it again."

She said it again.

"Thank you." He did not write it down. He had nothing to write it down
with and would not have written it down in that kitchen if he had.
"What does it do?"

"No."

"Shizune—"

"No. That's the name. That's what you asked for and that's what you've
got, and I'll tell you exactly why you're not getting the rest." She put
her cup down. "It isn't mine.

"There are three people alive who know what that is for. She's one.
I'm one, because I was there when she finished it and I have held the
other end of a great many things since. And Jiraiya's one, and he found
out by being told, in the ordinary way, by her.

"That's the whole list, Ruri. Three. It is not a secret in the sense of
a document — nobody's ever classified it, there's no notice, you
couldn't breach it if you tried. It's a secret in the sense that she
has told two people in thirty years and both times she chose the
person and the day."

"So I ask her."

"So you ask her," Shizune agreed. "And I want you to understand what
you'd be doing, because you'll do it well and you'll do it politely and
you will not have the first idea what it costs.

"It is the single most expensive question available to you. Not the
most dangerous. She wouldn't be angry. She'd tell you.

"It's the most expensive."

---

He sat and worked it.

He had about four minutes' worth and Shizune let him have them, which
was itself a piece of information, because a person who wanted the
subject closed does not sit in silence while you open it.

*What have I got.*

*A store. A way in and no way out. Open for a long time — decades, at a
guess, and I would defend decades, because of the density distribution
and because there is nothing on this earth that fills at any useful
rate.*

*Volume: large. I cannot put a number on it and I would not try, but it
is not a pocket. It is not a day's work in there.*

*And she is fifty-three and she has been doing it since before she
stopped, which Shizune said last night without saying it and which is
the only date anybody has ever given me about her.*

*So: thirty years of putting something in.*

He turned the cup round.

*Now. What is a store for.*

*A store is for a demand that is larger than the supply. That is the
entire content of the concept. Nobody builds a battery for a thing they
can do off the line. You build a store when the thing you are going to
have to do one day cannot be done out of what you have got on the day.*

*So there is a demand.*

*It is a fixed demand and it is enormous and she knows what it is,
because you cannot size a store without knowing the load, and she has
been sizing it for thirty years.*

*And it is one demand and not many, because a store that gets drawn on
regularly does not sit at that density.*

*She has been filling it, and not spending it, for thirty years.*

*For one thing. Once.*

He put the cup down very carefully.

*It is not for a patient.*

*You do not spend thirty years of your own capacity on a patient. There
is no patient on earth worth thirty years and she is a doctor and she
knows that better than I do, and she would not do it, and if she would
do it she would have done it eleven hundred times by now for eleven
hundred people and she has not spent a drop.*

*It is for her.*

*It is a woman who decided at about twenty-three, in a field station,
with something on a table she could not stop, that there would be one
day when the thing in front of her was too big, and that on that day
she would be able to pay for it.*

*And she has been putting it aside ever since. In her own body. Where
she cannot get it out and nobody can take it off her.*

*She has spent thirty years getting ready to spend herself.*

---

"You've got it," said Shizune.

"I've got some of it."

"You've got enough."

"Yes."

"Are you going to ask her?"

"No," said Sazare.

Shizune let out a breath that she had evidently been holding for
somewhat longer than the conversation.

"Why not?"

"Because I'd be asking her to say it out loud, and it's a thing she has
arranged her entire life around not having to say out loud, and the
only reason I'd want it said is so that I could have it." He looked at
the shutter that nobody had opened. "That's not a question. That's a
requisition with a question on the front of it."

"Ruri—"

"And because I know what it is now, near enough, and knowing it hasn't
helped and won't." He heard how flat that came out and did not soften
it. "That's twice this morning."

"Twice?"

"It's not important."

---

They sat there for a bit.

Somewhere out at the front of the house a cart went past on the lane
and a man said something to a horse, and the ordinary eighth-hour noise
of a village came up the hill, and neither of them said anything.

"Shizune," he said.

"Mm."

"Somebody has to tell Ibiki."

She went completely still.

"There is a concealed relationship between the Hokage and a serving
member of the Medical Corps," said Sazare, "and the serving member is
running a live foreign correspondence with an agent of Otogakure under
a written arrangement, and the third exchange is due in the eleventh
month, and the fee has already been agreed.

"That is not a rumour and it is not a moral question and it is not
about whether anybody has done anything wrong. It is an unreported
vulnerability with a name and a date on it, and it has been unreported
since about eleven o'clock last night, and it gets worse every day
nobody says it.

"I know exactly how this looks from a desk in T&I, because I have spent
two years learning what things look like from that desk, and the whole
of my standing with that man is built on one thing, which is that when
something happens to me I go and tell him about it before he finds
out."

"You'd be handing him the two of you."

"Yes."

"You'd be handing him *her*."

"I'd be handing him a fact that already exists," said Sazare, "and the
only variable left is who tells him and when."

---

"Not yet," said Shizune.

He looked at her.

"I'm not saying never. I'm saying not this morning, not this week, and
not by you on your own out of a sense of tidiness at eight o'clock in
the morning after the night you've just had." She was leaning forward
now. "You would walk into that building today and put it on his desk
because it's *correct*, and it is correct, and you would not have
spoken to her about it first.

"She's the Hokage, Ruri. It's her post and her risk and her decision
and you would be making it for her, this morning, alone, because you'd
worked out that it was right.

"That's the same thing you did last night with the table."

He sat back.

*That is a good argument.*

*It is a good argument and it is not the reason she is making it, and I
can tell, and I am not going to say so.*

"All right," he said.

"All right?"

"Not yet."

And that was all it took, and it took about two seconds, and he sat in
a cold kitchen and heard himself agree to hold a thing back, and knew
precisely what he had just done.

*I have never lied to anybody.*

*I am not going to lie about this either. Nobody is going to ask me and
I am not going to say a false word about it in my life.*

*I am simply not going to report it.*

*And I have just found out that those are not the same thing, and that
I can do the second one, and that it took me two seconds.*
