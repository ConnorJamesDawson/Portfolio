# Chapter 57, Scene 7 — A Word for It

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower roof. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1410 words.*

---

"Katsuyu," said Sazare.

Jiraiya did not move for about four seconds.

"Say that again."

"Katsuyu. The slug. She's Hime's — she's on the Hokage's contract, and
she came the first time in the eighth month because I asked, and she has
come on eleven Fridays since, and there's no contract, it's an
arrangement of the second."

"There's no *contract*."

"No."

"You are not on a contract."

"No," said Sazare. "I asked her how the first one came about and she
told me, and I decided not to sign one, and the reason is that if I sign
one then she comes because she is contracted to and I would never be
able to tell the difference again."

Jiraiya put both hands on his knees and stared at the roof for a while.

"You daft bastard," he said, with what appeared to be genuine affection.
"You beautiful daft bastard."

---

"How long?"

"Eleven Fridays. Nineteen evenings altogether, some of them not
Fridays."

"And before that?"

"Nothing. Nobody."

"Kid," said Jiraiya, "I want you to be very precise with me now, and I'm
not being clever. Before the eighth month of this year, who taught you?"

"Nobody taught me anything."

"Somebody taught you to—"

"No," said Sazare. "That's not modesty and it isn't a complaint. There
was no one. I got out of Kiri at seven and a half and I did it on my own
in a series of rooms until I was fifteen and then I did it on my own in
a hospital.

"Kanzaki taught me to weigh a thing. Yagi taught me what a splint does to
a patient. Ibiki taught me what a thing can be used for. None of them
have ever seen the operation and none of them could tell me one word
about it if they had.

"Katsuyu is the first person in my life who has watched my hands and told
me what they were doing. Eleven years of faults in one evening, in the
eighth month, and every single one was there because there was no adult
in the room, ever, at any point."

---

"When did you last see her?" said Jiraiya.

It was a completely ordinary question.

It was the next thing you would say. It had no weight on it at all and
he asked it in the same voice he had asked about the tin, and Sazare
stood on a roof in the dark and found that he could not get to it for
about two seconds.

"The Friday before last," he said.

"That's—" Jiraiya did the arithmetic. "That's eleven days."

"Twelve."

"You said eleven Fridays running."

"I did."

"So what happened to the twelfth?"

"I forgot," said Sazare.

---

He said it flatly and he did not decorate it.

"I want to be exact, because it would be very easy to make that sound
better than it was. I did not decide against it. I was not busy — I was
at a desk doing correspondence, and I could have gone up the hill at any
point between the fourth hour and the ninth, and I did not think about
it once.

"Not once. The entire day went past.

"She was four hundred miles away in a forest on a Friday and nobody came
and no reason was given, and she will not have minded and she will not
have written it down, and that's worse.

"I have said to myself in my own room, in those words, about four times,
that I have never once assumed the twelfth. I was proud of it. It's the
only thing about my own conduct I have ever been proud of.

"And then I assumed the twelfth by not noticing it existed."

Jiraiya was quiet.

"Do you know what did it?" he said.

"Yes."

"Say it."

"No," said Sazare.

"...All right," said Jiraiya. "All right, kid. Two for two on that
answer this morning."

---

He got up off the parapet.

"Right," he said. "Sit down. I'm going to tell you something and you're
going to want to be sitting down for it, and then I'm going to be
unpleasant to you for about ten minutes and you're going to let me."

Sazare sat down on the roof.

"What you just did with that stone," said Jiraiya. "Twice. Four seconds
each, one hand, on a roof, in the middle of a conversation, while you
were upset about a slug.

"Has anybody ever told you what that is?"

"It's a course. Three deep, fine damping coarse. It's the smallest thing
I know how to—"

"No," said Jiraiya. "Not the crystal. I don't care about the crystal.
The half-second before it."

Sazare stopped.

"There isn't a half-second before it."

"There is. I watched it twice and it's the same both times and it is
about a fifth of a second and you have no idea you're doing it." Jiraiya
crouched down in front of him. "You go still.

"Not still like a man concentrating. I know what that looks like, I've
been teaching children for thirty years. You go still the way *water*
goes still. Everything stops — and something comes in, and then you
spend it, and then you're back, and the whole thing is over before most
people have finished blinking.

"That's a gather, kid.

"That's a sage gathering natural energy, and you did it twice in a
minute without thinking about it, and then you told me it wasn't
interesting."

---

Sazare sat on a cold roof and did not say anything for a while.

*Say it back and see if it holds.*

*I know about natural energy. I have known about natural energy since
the eighth month, because a slug told me. I have got eleven pages on it
and nineteen more that depend on it, and the whole of the middle of that
paper is that a kekkei genkai is a technique for giving structure to
something that is already in the world.*

*I know what I am doing. I have known for four months. I could give a
lecture on it.*

*I have never heard a word for the person who does it.*

"There's a word," he said.

"There's a word."

"She never gave me one."

"No," said Jiraiya. "She wouldn't. They don't have words for it — that's
not a criticism of slugs, kid, it's that they don't have *words* for
anything, they've got four hundred years of showing you. Nobody at
Shikkotsu has ever needed to name it because everybody there has always
been able to see it."

"What is it?"

"Senjutsu," said Jiraiya. "The people who do it are sages. That's the
whole of it. It's not a rank and there's no examination and nobody hands
it out. It's a description."

---

*Sage.*

He turned it over.

*I have had four hundred descriptions applied to me and I have written
down every one. The crystal boy. The Kiri asset. The fabricator. A
Shōton user, in eleven documents, all of them wrong in the same way. Case
four in a study. An article in somebody else's file.*

*Every single one of them is about what I do.*

*Nobody has ever put me in a category.*

*And this is not a category of what I do. There is no crystal in it and
there is no wood in it and there is no hospital in it. It is a word
about what I am, and a man I met ninety minutes ago has just handed it
to me on a roof because he watched my face for four seconds.*

He found, with some irritation, that he could not see very well again,
and that it was the second time in a week, and that there was no data in
it whatsoever.

"How many are there?" he said.

"Sages?"

"Yes."

"Alive?" Jiraiya sat back down on the parapet. "Me. You. Orochimaru, at
the snakes, and he'd say he was and I'd say he isn't and we'd both be
being political about it. There'll be one or two out of Shikkotsu that
nobody's met.

"And there was a fourth one but he's dead, and there's a fifth one who's
sixteen and eating his own weight in noodles about a mile from here and
does not know yet."

"That's four."

"That's four," said Jiraiya. "In the world. And one of them found out
what he was at twenty past five this morning off a man sitting on a
gutter."
