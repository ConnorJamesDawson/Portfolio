# Chapter 67, Scene 5 — Filed Under Sweet

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the second floor. Tsunade, age
fifty-three. POV: Tsunade. ~1060 words.*

---

She wrote the evening up the way she wrote everything now, in the
register nobody would ever read, lying on her back in the dark with
the boy asleep beside her smelling of cedar and good decisions.

*Entry. He tried to do something nice for me.*

That was the finding, whole. She turned it over a few times to check
it for load, the way he had taught her to do with sentences without
ever once knowing he was teaching, and it held. Everything else about
the evening — the theatre-grade towel placement, the water verified
to a Tarugi decimal, the patches of copper, the four-count she'd
watched him lean on like a man on a rail in a gale, the *nosebleed*
— all of it was subsidiary detail under one heading, and the heading
was: he looked at eight weeks of being loved unilaterally, decided
the ledger was uneven, and mounted an operation about it. Planned,
provisioned, and executed into the teeth of his own nervous system.

*For me. At catastrophic personal cost. The idiot.*

She lay in the dark and grinned at the ceiling like a girl.

---

The funny of it was going to last her years. She knew good comedy;
she had drunk with the best-travelled liars of a generation; and she
was prepared to state, for the record she didn't keep, that she had
never in fifty-three years seen anything to touch the moment the
finest observational instrument in the Land of Fire — a man who
could read a barrier array's history off its ink, who had found a
hundred-and-forty-year-old fault in eleven minutes, who tracked her
moods off the pressure of her signature — looked at six feet of
approaching Senju and simply, comprehensively, blue-screened.

*If you are going to do something that sexy, please warn me.*

Level. Dead level. The camber register, the one he used for grading
stone, deployed with a nosebleed starting, and she had wanted
simultaneously to frame the sentence and to eat him alive, and had
compromised on medical intervention followed by unlicensed use of
the mouth.

*I crawled,* she noted, for honesty's sake, because her ledger kept
her sins too. *I regret nothing. He proposed finding a limit; I
located it for him; that is called assisting a study, and I have
signed off worse methodology this month. And the nosebleed was a
medical event, and I treated it promptly, with the appropriate
technique.*

*Fifty years of the Mystical Palm. Wars, plagues, the poison desk at
Suna. Tonight's caseload: one (1) nosebleed, romantic, self-
inflicted, patient stable.*

*Shizune can never know. Shizune will know by Tuesday.*

---

Underneath the funny, where she did her actual accounting, the entry
ran longer.

*Item. Two months in this bed. Two months, and the man can still
barely look at me — has to ration it, budgets three seconds at a
time and pays for the third with his sinuses. And that, right there,
is the whole testimony, if anybody's taking any.*

*I have been looked at my entire life. I know the taxonomy better
than any woman alive. I got the assessing look at twelve, the
acquiring one at sixteen; I've had my figure appraised by lords,
generals, medics who should have known better and shinobi who knew
exactly — and every man who ever wanted the packaging solved the
looking inside a week. Looking is cheap when it's the point. It goes
smooth. It goes practised. Comfortable men look comfortably.*

*He sleeps beside the whole of it, every night, two months, and one
towel's worth of Tuesday-grade matter-of-fact still knocks his
vocabulary down to eleven words. Because he is not after the
packaging. He never once was — I watched him fail to notice it for
two years while he was busy learning my signature pressure, my
Friday face, the way I stand when the news is from the border. He
fell for the operating system and is now gamely, catastrophically,
attempting to install the rest of me, and his hardware keeps
catching fire, and he keeps rebooting it, and coming back, and
trying again.*

*That's the one, ladies. That's the rare one. You can have the
smooth lookers, the lot of them.*

---

*Item,* she wrote, last, in the space where her register kept the
things that had actually landed.

*He said: you decided I was worth the shortfall, and I do not intend
to be worth the shortfall. Sat in four feet of water with his nose
freshly cauterised and read me my own private ledger back — the one
I never told him existed, the unilateral entries, eight weeks of me
quietly making the small weather because the big weather kept
coming on its own — and he'd found it, itemised it, and arrived
with a repayment plan.*

*Nobody has ever audited me for the purpose of owing me more.*

*And then the arse thing.* She had to stop and breathe for a moment;
the aftershocks were apparently still live. *Three seconds. He gave
it three whole seconds — I could feel the count, the absolute
scholar — and then assessed his own systems, concluded structural
failure was imminent, and conducted a fighting withdrawal to the
bottom of the tub. A shinobi of the Hidden Leaf. Tactical submersion.
And I laughed like — gods, like before. Like properly before. I
heard myself doing it and didn't recognise the sound off the first
note, it's been that long, and he came up out of the water looking
like a half-drowned owl and watched me do it as if I were the
Sage's own last miracle, and started* counting*.*

*He thinks I didn't see the counting. I see everything he does with
that face. He was filing my laugh, dating it, the lunatic, and if
either of us ever asks me under oath why him, of every man in five
nations — him — the honest answer is on tonight's page:*

*because when he does something nice for me it nearly kills him,
and he has already scheduled doing it again.*

She turned over. The hands went to the shirt in the dark, both of
them, full grip, seniority exercised, and the last line of the
entry filed itself from the very edge of sleep, in the space
between true sentences, where the best ones lived:

*Gift received. Ledger uneven in my favour, whatever the boy
thinks. Let him keep trying to fix that forever.*
