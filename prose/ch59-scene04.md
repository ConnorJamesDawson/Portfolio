# Chapter 59, Scene 4 — Radiant

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard and the room over the
persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2380 words.*

---

He went up to the yard on the Saturday evening because there was nothing
else to do with himself and because he had four hundred grams of reject
and no bench to put it on until the first of the twelfth month.

He was not intending to make anything.

He set out to run a grading check, which was two hours of extremely dull
work with a lamp and a felt and a nib, and he got about eleven minutes
into it and then laid a course on a piece of yard aggregate because his
hands were doing it before he had finished thinking about the check.

It came up in three and a half seconds.

He looked at it.

---

*That's wrong.*

*That is not the four seconds. Four seconds is the four seconds. I have
laid that course eleven thousand times since I was nine and it is four,
and it has been four in a ward and four on a bank and four at
thirty-nine degrees with a fever and it was four on a roof on Monday in
front of a man who was watching my face.*

*It has never been three and a half.*

He put it down and did it again.

Three and a half.

*And that is not the whole of it.*

He picked the lamp up off the stool and held it behind the course and
looked through.

---

*The light is going in.*

*It is going most of the way in and coming back and it is coming back
without the scatter, and there is scatter in everything I have ever made
because there is always disorder at the junctions and disorder is what
scatter is.*

*I have got about nine hundred articles in eleven years and I have held
a lamp behind most of them, and the best of them go about two-thirds and
come back cloudy at the edge.*

*This is going the whole way.*

He sat down on the yard stone with the thing between his fingers and the
lamp behind it and did not move for some minutes.

Then he put his palm flat on it and listened.

*Four voices.*

*Clean.*

*Not four voices with two of them beating and one of them wandering,
which is what a first course sounds like and what every first course I
have ever laid has sounded like. Four voices, seated, and the top one is
about a tone above where I have ever heard it and it is not sharp, it is
just — up.*

*It sounds like a finished article.*

*It is a course on a bit of yard rubble laid in three and a half seconds
by a man who was not paying attention.*

---

He worked until about two in the morning and made eleven more and every
single one of them was better than anything in his drawer.

It was not close. That was the part he kept coming back to and kept
having to write down again in a firmer hand: it was not a marginal
improvement and it was not a good evening. The worst of the eleven was
better than the best article he had produced in the ninth month, and he
had spent four hours on that one and had been pleased about it for a
week.

He walked home at half past two with them in a tin and did not sleep.

---

It took him until Monday to get it, and when he got it he sat on the
floor of his own room for about a quarter of an hour without writing
anything down.

*It is not the crystal.*

*I have been at the crystal for two days looking for a materials answer
because that is where I look, and there isn't one. Same stock, same
grading, same tin, same hands, same lamp, same yard. I have controlled
all of it and there is nothing in the materials and there was never
going to be.*

*It is me.*

*And the mechanism is not new and it is not mine and it is in chapter
one of a book I read when I was fifteen.*

---

*Chakra shapes differently according to the state of the person shaping
it.*

*That is not a controversial statement. That is not a finding. It is on
about page nine of every basic text there is, it is why they teach
breathing before they teach anything else, and every shinobi alive knows
that you produce differently in a panic than you do sitting down, and
differently again on a field with your heart going.*

*Everybody knows this.*

*And it does not wreck them, and the reason it does not wreck them is
the reason I could not see any of it for eleven years.*

*A seal is a fixed form.*

*It does not care what mood you are in. You run the notation and the
notation does the shaping — most of it, I would put it at nine parts in
ten and I would defend nine — and your state modulates whatever is left
over.*

*So for everybody else, a bad day is a ten per cent problem on a shape
that is held rigid by something outside them.*

He put the pencil down.

*I do the whole hundred by hand.*

*Every time. From nothing. There is no fixed form anywhere in the
operation because I have never had access to one, and the only thing
holding the structure is me holding it.*

*Which means my state does not modulate the last ten per cent of my
output.*

*It is the entire output.*

---

He got up and got the box down.

It took him until four in the morning and he did not enjoy any of it.

*Fourteen books. Three years and a bit of yard log, plus the four
smaller ones.*

*Question: is there a correlation between the quality of my work and the
state I was in.*

*Method: I cannot search for it, because I never recorded the state. I
have to read.*

He read.

It was there.

It was there in the way that a fault is there once you know where the
fault is, which is to say obviously, in ink, in his own hand, on about
four hundred pages, with a materials explanation written underneath
almost every one of them.

*Third month of last year. Nine articles rejected in one week and I
attributed it to a bad delivery of aggregate and wrote two pages about
the supplier. That was the week of the seventh, and the seventh was the
day of the memorial, and I did not put the memorial in the book because
the memorial is not work.*

*Ninth month. Four days of everything coming out at about eighty per
cent and I put it down to the damp. It was the four days after the
gateway and the eleven seconds.*

*The whole of the sixth month of my first year, which I have got written
up as* poor stock throughout*, and which was the six weeks after I found
out what had happened to the burial register.*

He sat on the floor with the books round him.

*I have got eleven years of a direct readout of my own emotional state,
taken daily, in my own hand, and I have filed the entire thing under
materials.*

---

*And then the other one, and I have been coming round the edge of it for
about half an hour and I am going to write it now.*

*The reject table.*

He put both hands flat on the floor.

*Four hundred rows. Every material I ever tried and every one that
failed and by how much. It is the best thing I have ever produced. It
went to the Corps board and it went through and the study went with it,
and a man came in through a window at four in the morning and told me it
was the funniest thing he had read in eleven years, and it is the entire
reason anybody in this village takes anything I say seriously.*

*It is four hundred rows of failures.*

*And I do not know how many of those rows are the material and how many
of them are me on a Tuesday.*

*I want to be careful. I am not saying the table is wrong. Most of it
will hold — the gross figures will hold, yard aggregate at twenty-two is
not twenty-two because I was in a mood, it is twenty-two because it is
yard aggregate.*

*But there will be rows in it that are wrong by ten or fifteen per cent
in one direction, and I do not know which rows, and there is no way to
find out except to run the whole thing again.*

*Four hundred rows. Eleven months.*

*And I cannot even start until I am in a state I can trust, and I am not
going to be in one of those for some time.*

---

*Right. The last part, and it is the one I like least.*

*I cannot measure it.*

*I have got the mechanism and the mechanism is sound and I would defend
it in front of anybody. I have not got a magnitude, and a mechanism
without a magnitude is not a finding, it is an opinion with a diagram.*

*To get the magnitude I would have to measure output against state
across a range, and to do that I would have to be able to establish my
own state accurately, and I cannot, because I am in it.*

*I am inside my own sample.*

*That is the whole of it and there is no way round it that I can see at
four in the morning. Everything I produce for the duration of this is
data, and every reading I take of myself while producing it is taken by
the thing being measured.*

He wrote it out properly and then read it back and then wrote the last
line, and it was the first entry in fourteen books that had a
hypothesis in it and no figure underneath.

*Theory recorded. No magnitude available. Not testable from this
position.*

*I have never written that before and I do not like the look of it.*

---

*And the word is wrong.*

*I have been through four books this evening looking for a term and the
only one anybody uses is* lovesick*, which is not a term, it is a joke
about somebody. It is what people say about a person who is being
tiresome at a table.*

*This is not that.*

*What I have got is a sustained elevated state that has been running
continuously for five days without a trough, that has altered my
physical output by a margin larger than four years of practice, that is
not producible on demand, that I have no control over the onset or
duration of, and that is visible from nine feet to anybody who has known
me a fortnight.*

*It should be studied. Somebody should have studied it. There is not a
line about it in any medical text I have got and there are nine pages
in a novel about the etiquette of the approach.*

He wrote out a protocol for reaching it, because that was what he did.

It took eleven minutes and it was four steps long, and he read it back
at about half past four in the morning and understood, entirely, that
step one was *have somebody take hold of your coat at twenty past four
in the morning*, and that not one part of it was under his control, and
that a protocol you cannot execute is not a protocol.

He drew a line through the whole of it.

Underneath, he wrote: *Not reproducible. Not a method. Do not build
anything on this.*

Then he sat and looked at the tin with eleven of the best articles of
his life in it for a while.

---

*Ex. 1, across the week: 6, 4, nothing, nothing, 7, 6.*

*Twenty-fifth: nine.*

*I do not know why and I have not been able to reconstruct it and I have
decided not to chase it, which took about four minutes and which I would
like recorded because I have never once in my life decided not to chase
something.*

---

The first course went at half past nine on the Friday morning.

He had it on the low table under a cloth with the date on the outside
because he had made it at twenty past nine on the Saturday and he was
not going to leave a thing like that unmeasured, and he took the cloth
off on his way out and held the lamp behind it.

The light stopped about a third of the way in.

He put his palm on it and got two voices and a wobble, and by that
evening it would be four and by Sunday it would be chalk.

*Six days.*

*Same as everything else. To the hour, as far as I can measure it,
which is not very far but is far enough.*

He stood in his own room with the best article he had ever made going
opaque in his hand.

*So the order is not the mechanism.*

*Better order at the start does not buy one minute of duration. The
degradation is not the disorder catching up with a good article faster
or slower — it is completely indifferent to how good the article was.*

*Which means it is the close. It was always the close. I have said so on
page eight and I have believed it for four months and I have never
actually had a controlled pair to prove it with, and now I have got
one.*

*Best article of my life. Six days.*

*Worst article of my life, ninth month, four hours, cloudy at every
junction. Six days.*

He put the cloth back over it and wrote it up standing at the table with
his coat on.

*Result: the close is the mechanism. Order is not.*

*This is the most useful thing I have found out this year and it has cost
me the best thing I have ever made, and I would do it again this
afternoon.*
