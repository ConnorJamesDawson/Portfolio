# Chapter 33, Scene 6 — Two Records

*Act 4, part C. Konoha, the administration building. Sazare Ruri, age
seventeen. POV: Sazare. ~1,610 words.*

---

They came to him separately, from two different offices, for two
different reasons, on the same Thursday afternoon.

That was the part he could never afterwards make anybody understand.
Everybody he ever told it to assumed there had been a moment of
revelation, somebody laying two documents side by side and saying
*look*. There was not. There was a clerk in personnel with a
reparations schedule and a form to be signed, and forty minutes later
a different clerk in the same building with the annual foreign-threat
review, which was a standing requirement for any shinobi on the
establishment with a foreign-origin file and which he had signed
without reading in each of the two previous years.

Two forms. Two clerks. Neither of whom had any reason on earth to have
read the other's.

---

The first one was called Kudō and was perhaps twenty-six and had the
particular brightness of somebody who had been given a piece of work
they considered good news and had been looking forward to delivering
it.

"Kirigakure reparations schedule," she said. "It came through the
normalisation channel in the spring. This is the fourth tranche and
it's the first one with a Konoha establishment name on it, so it's
come to me, and I've had it for two weeks because nobody could work out
whose desk it was."

"Reparations for what?"

"Property, in the main. Confiscated or destroyed under purge-era
orders. There's a separate schedule for persons and that one isn't
open yet and I'm told it may not be for years." She turned the sheet
round. "Your family's workshop and dwelling are on it. Ward eleven."

Sazare looked at the entry.

It was four lines. The address was a survey reference. The description
of the property was *workshop premises with attached dwelling, trade:
fine metal and mineral fabrication.* There was a valuation, which was
low by about forty per cent, and a note that the valuation was
provisional and appealable.

And there was a claimant field, and it was empty, and beside it, in
the status column, in a printed abbreviation that Kudō had to explain
to him because it was internal to the Kiri registry, was the entry:

*Cl. ext.*

"Clan extinct," said Kudō. "It's on about a third of the schedule.
Where there's no surviving claimant the property reverts and the
compensation goes into the general reconstruction fund instead."

"Then why has it come to me?"

"Because somebody in our normalisation office cross-checked the
schedule against our own establishment roll and flagged that we've got
a Ruri." She was pleased about this; it was, professionally, a good
catch. "So it's a query, not a payment. You'd have to make a claim,
they'd have to review it, and honestly it'd take two years and the
valuation's rubbish and I'd want you to know both of those before you
decide anything."

"They have me as extinct."

"They have the *clan* as extinct. It's a register entry, it's not — "
Kudō stopped, and reconsidered the sentence, and Sazare watched her
do it. "It's a register entry made by somebody working off a district
roll that ends where the district ended, and nobody's updated it,
because updating it requires a survivor to come forward and no survivor
has."

"For four years."

"For four years," she agreed. "It's not sinister. It's just what
happens to a list when the only person who could correct it is somewhere
else."

---

The second clerk was a man of about fifty called Serizawa who had done
the annual foreign-threat review for eleven years and had the manner of
somebody performing a task he had long ago stopped believing in.

"Sit down, this is nine minutes." He had the folder open before Sazare
had got the chair straight. "Standing requirement, annual, for anybody
on the establishment with a foreign-origin file. You review your own
entry as held by the originating state, you confirm or correct our
copy of it, you sign. Third time for you."

"I signed the last two without reading them."

"Most people do. Most people's are three lines and haven't changed
since the Second War." He turned the extract round. "Yours has
changed."

Sazare read it.

It was a photographic copy — grainy, off a page that had been
photographed at a slight angle, so the print ran uphill toward the
gutter. He recognised the layout instantly, from a room over a stable
in Tsuzura, at fifteen, with a broker's fat sweat-swollen book open on
a counter and a tab for the Land of Water.

*RURI, SAZARE.* His clan. His technique, named. A face that was four
years out of date and had been taken from a district register when he
was twelve.

And a price, and the word *alive* boxed at the bottom of it.

And, underneath, appended, in newer type set in a different face
because it had been added at a later printing:

*STATUS AMENDED. Subject confirmed serving, Konohagakure
establishment, medical corps auxiliary. Retrieval standing: MAINTAINED.
Charge as originally listed: MAINTAINED pending review.*

"They've kept it," said Sazare.

"They've kept it and they've updated it, which is worse." Serizawa
tapped the appended line without any particular feeling. "That's this
year's printing. Somebody in a Kiri office has read a report that says
you're wearing our headband and has gone into the file and written it
down, and then left the retrieval order standing underneath it. So
what you have now is a live foreign retrieval order on a serving Konoha
shinobi, which is a thing our normalisation people would be extremely
interested to know about if they had any reason to look at a
threat-review file, which they don't."

"And the charge."

"Still on there. *Pending review*, which in my experience means nobody
has looked at it and nobody is going to unless somebody makes them."
Serizawa handed him the pen. "You confirm the entry's accurate as a
description of what they hold — not whether it's *true*, that's not
what you're signing — and I file it, and it goes in a cabinet with
about four hundred others."

Sazare did not take the pen.

---

He sat in a small office on the first floor of the administration
building with two sheets of paper in front of him and understood, with
the total unwelcome clarity that always arrived about four seconds
after it would have been useful, exactly what he was looking at.

The reparations schedule said the Ruri had ended.

The bingo book said Sazare Ruri, alive, Crystal Release, Konoha
establishment, price boxed at the bottom.

The same government. Both current. Both printed inside the last twelve
months.

Compiled by two offices four streets apart in a city he had not seen
since he was thirteen, neither of which had ever had the faintest
reason to read the other's paper, because the office that pays out for
the dead and the office that prices the living do not correspond.

And there was no third document anywhere in the world in which those
two facts appeared together, and there was no clerk in either country
who had ever held both, and there never would be.

Except here. On this table. In front of him.

"Are you all right?" said Serizawa.

"Yes."

"You've gone a colour."

"I'm told I have a colour." Sazare put one finger on the amended line
and one on the extinct entry, forty inches apart on a desk in a
foreign country. "This one says my family are gone. This one says I'm
worth about a million and a half."

Serizawa looked at the reparations schedule for the first time.

"...Ah," he said.

"Yes."

"That's not — those are different sections."

"I know."

"I mean in Kiri. Registry and hunter-nin. They wouldn't — " He stopped.
"No. You're right. That's a mess."

"Can I correct either of them?"

Serizawa thought about it properly, which Sazare appreciated more than
he could have explained.

"To correct that one," he said, pointing at the schedule, "you'd have
to file as a surviving Ruri, which puts your name and your location
into a Kiri registry office, and about nine desks down the corridor
from that registry office is the section that maintains *that* one." He
pointed at the extract. "And to correct that one you'd need Kiri to
withdraw a standing retrieval order on a foreign shinobi, which is not
a clerical act, that's a diplomatic one. That's above me, it's above my
whole floor."

"So the answer's no."

"The answer's no unless somebody senior enough to be in a room with
Kirigakure decides it's worth putting on an agenda." He put the pen
down on the desk between them. "And I'll be straight with you, because
you've been straight with me: that only happens if it becomes
inconvenient for somebody other than you."

Sazare picked up the pen and signed the threat review, because it was
accurate as a description of what they held.

Then he asked Kudō, at the other office, an hour later, to hold the
reparations query open and not to close it and not to file a claim,
and she asked him why, and he gave her the true answer, which was:

"Because at the moment they've written down that we ended, and if I
put my name on that form the only thing that changes is that they'll
know where I am."

He went back to the ward and did the six o'clock observations.

The entry stayed open. It was still open eleven months later, when it
stopped being a clerical matter and became, exactly as Serizawa had
said it would have to, inconvenient for somebody else.
