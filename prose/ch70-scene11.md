# Chapter 70, Scene 11 — The Part of Her Brain

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the residence bath. Tsunade,
age fifty-three. POV: Tsunade. ~1050 words.*

---

The bath was perfect, which was the problem.

Not a large problem. Not, for the first hour, a problem at all:
the water at the Tarugi decimal, the lamps at the raking angle,
the boy — awake, present, restored to her for a whole
structural Thursday — soaking the field's four weeks out of
his shoulders at the far end of the cedar tub with his eyes
half shut, within reach, hers, the account balanced, the
morning's hand still warm on the curve of her memory.

That was where it started. The hand.

She was lying back against the rim with her hair pinned up in
the two lacquered sticks, boiled soft and perfectly happy,
running the morning again for the pure pleasure of the replay
— the weight of it arriving, deliberate, placed like
stonework, *staying* — and the part of her brain that never
boiled soft, the part that had kept her alive through three
wars and every card game it had ever been allowed to touch,
surfaced without being called and said, in its flat
professional voice:

*Placed like stonework. Yes. Now ask the question you skipped
at half six because you were too happy to hold it: where did
he learn the move?*

And the file opened. The whole file. In order.

*Item: a man who for two months would not let his hands
leave my shoulders without a signed order. Who budgets
looking at three seconds and pays in sinuses. Who has never
once — the register would know — improvised an escalation in
his life. And at half six this morning he runs a slow trail
down the full line of my back and sets his hand on my arse
with the confidence of a mason closing an arch. Unhurried.
Grammatical. PRACTISED — no. Not practised. Studied.*

*Item, cross-filed from January: a proposal to share a bath,
arriving out of a clear sky, from a man who then prepared the
room like a theatre. Item: a request to do my hair — out of
the blue, I checked the whole sky at the time, I wrote it in
this register in those words. Item: the sudden fluency in
teasing, which I flattered myself I had taught him — and I
did teach him the register, but the MOVES, doctor, chart the
moves: the bath, the hair, the trail, each one arriving
whole, sequenced, escalating in — say it — in curriculum
order.*

*He has a source. The source has chapters.*

The water lapped. At the far end of the tub the subject of
the file floated in perfect innocence, eyes shut, being
beautiful in the lamplight on her specific instructions.

*Think, woman. What does he DO with every problem? He gets
literature. He got Urushibara for seals and a scale for the
wood question and my own palpation papers for the ovals — he
reads METHOD SECTIONS for pleasure. Of course the courtship
has a text. Somewhere in this village there is a manual with
his notes in the margins, and the notes are numbered, and
whatever it is, chapter by chapter, it is being worked
through in order, on ME—*

And there the file hit the morning, and the morning would
not move.

Because the hand had not felt like a chapter. That was the
finding underneath the finding, and she made herself chart
it honestly, because this register did not keep two sets of
accounts either: whatever text was under it, the weight that
had settled on her at half six had arrived with eleven years
of jeweller's certainty and a keeper's mark two days old,
and it had rested there the way his marks rested — *placed,
claimed, not for the world* — and she had been handled by
enough men who learned from books to know the difference
between a man performing a page and a man who had found, in
some page somewhere, the permission to do the thing his
whole body had been asking to do since November.

*So. Two true things, held at once, the way this house holds
everything: the moves have a source, and the man is real
under every one of them. The source is a curiosity. The man
is the finding.*

She opened one eye and looked down the steam at him.

*I could ask. He would tell me — he tells me everything I
ask, it is the single most inconvenient of all his virtues,
I would have the title and the chapter numbers inside a
minute. And then this — whatever this campaign of his is,
the bath, the hair, the hand, whatever is NEXT on the
syllabus — becomes a thing we have discussed, instead of a
thing that keeps happening to me out of a clear sky, and I
have not had things happen to me out of a clear sky since I
was young enough to deserve them.*

*No. The file stays open and the investigation stays MINE.
A private study, methodology his — watch the subject,
document the pattern, name the source from the evidence.
He reverse-engineered a hundred-and-forty-year-old barrier
off the ink. Let's see what fifty-three years of reading
tells can do with a boyfriend's curriculum.*

*Besides—* and she let the eye fall shut again, boiled
soft, entirely content, one thumbnail's width from the
truth and electing, luxuriously, not to close the gap
today— *it's Thursday. The first one. He gave me all the
Thursdays, and I am not spending this one on forensics.*

*The audit is scheduled. The auditor is patient. God help
the source when I find it.*

"You've gone quiet," said the subject, from the far end,
not opening his eyes. "You only go that quiet over a
chart."

"Mm," said Tsunade, sliding a foot along the cedar until it
found his, there in the small warm country he had built
her, twice now. "Occupational habit. Somebody once told me
the eye flatters and the finger audits." She hooked her
ankle over his, and filed the whole morning, seam and hand
and mark and mystery, under the heading that had survived
every audit since a slab in a Tarugi corridor, and closed
the drawer, and left one light burning inside it. "Ignore
me, fabricator. Today nothing gets kept. Through and out."

*Through and out,* agreed the register, obediently, at the
bottom of its last Thursday page.
