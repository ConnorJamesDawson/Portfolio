# Chapter 68, Scene 5 — Out of the Blue

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the second floor. Tsunade, age
fifty-three. POV: Tsunade. ~1110 words.*

---

The chest question went into her ledger that same evening, while
the boy sat at the desk pretending the splint correspondence had
his full attention, and it filed easily, which was the mark of a
good evening's data.

*Entry. He short-circuited and a question fell out.*

That was the mechanism, whole. She had watched the sequence with
the professional pleasure of a woman who had, after all, designed
the experiment: filters down, clerk unconscious — she could see
the exact moment the internal supervision went dark, it happened
around the eyes — and then, out of the unsupervised dark, not a
grab, not a line, not any of the four hundred things fifty years
of being built like this had taught her to expect from men with
their filters down.

A question. A real one. About her *spine*.

*Thirty years of medics,* she wrote, *and the number who ever
asked about the load case, unprompted, with genuine concern for
the cantilever: one. He was worried about my BACK. He has been
worried about my back, evidently, since November, quietly, the
way he worries about retaining walls. Somewhere in that ledger of
his there is a standing file about my thoracic spine and it
predates the first kiss.*

*Filed under: short-circuit, honest contents thereof. Which is
the finding, really — you learn what's actually in a man by what
falls out when he's dropped. What falls out of this one is
structural concern and the word "wonderful" deployed as a
technical term.*

*Case closed. Best consult of the month. I said so and meant it.*

---

The hair thing did not file.

It arrived twenty minutes later with no warning on any
instrument. She was on the end of the bed with the brush, working
through the evening fifty — a ritual older than her rank, older
than most things she still did with her hands — and he got up
from the desk, crossed the room, and sat down next to her, at the
range he usually budgeted and paid for in sinuses, entirely
unbudgeted, and said:

"May I help with your hair?"

And her hands stopped.

She ran the assessment, because running the assessment was
automatic at fifty-three: face — level, present, none of the
copper, none of the white noise; this had not fallen out of him,
this had *walked* out, on purpose, in the working register with
something underneath it she could not identify at four inches.
Source — and here the assessment simply returned nothing. The
bath she had understood inside a minute: eight weeks of receiving,
a man mounting a repayment operation, sweet, catastrophic,
legible. The squid had been legible. Row one, he'd said, and
she'd read the whole column format off the phrase.

This was not on the column. Men did not arrive at hair. Hair was
not in the market lane or the bath chapter of anybody's — hair
was not a thing a boy raised in a workshop and a hospital *got
to* by inventory. In fifty-three years, through every species of
suitor, husband-hunter, chancer and genuine article the five
nations had sent her, the number who had ever asked to *help
with her hair* stood at zero. It was out of the blue in the pure
meteorological sense: she checked the whole sky and there was
nothing it could have come from.

"You may," she said, because whatever her instruments were doing,
her mouth had standards, and handed him the brush.

---

Afterwards — hours afterwards, in the dark, in the register
nobody would read — she tried to write it up, and watched
herself fail twice, and let the failures stand in the ledger,
because failed headings were data too.

*Attempted heading one: another row. Wrong. Rows are things
couples do; there was a version of this that was a row, some
gallant nonsense with a comb, and it was not what happened.*

*Attempted heading two: the study, the short-circuit, the boy
pushing his tolerances. Wrong twice over. His hands never once
shook. Whatever this was, it was not at the edge of him. It came
from somewhere in the middle, somewhere with years on it.*

She lay in the dark and looked at the ceiling, with her hair
still carrying the memory of it — sectioned, worked through knot
by knot from the ends the way you were supposed to and nobody
ever bothered, the brush in long slow passes with a fingertip
following each one, checking, the way — she had finally placed
what the fingertip reminded her of, and it had nearly undone her
on the spot — the way a jeweller follows a polish, feeling for
what the eye misses.

An hour. He had given it an hour, unhurried as the crawl had
been unhurried, and somewhere in the middle of the hour, quietly,
without stopping the brush, he had said the thing she was still
holding with both hands:

*My mother dressed hair. The women came to the shop at closing
to have their pins fitted, and a pin is fitted in finished hair,
so she dressed it first, at the bench, by the good lamp. I held
the lamp. I passed the pins, smallest first. I have not thought
about it in eleven years and I find I have not forgotten any of
it.*

His mother. Unprompted. Unpurchased. Handed over mid-brushstroke
like a pin, smallest first.

*Attempted heading three,* she wrote, *and this one holds. Same
file as the slab at Tarugi: with everything stripped away — no
docket, no programme, no me needing anything — the work is the
same. The care was never for the dockets. He sat behind me in
the lamplight doing his mother's trade with his mother's hands
and he gave me the provenance without being asked, because that
is what he does with everything he certifies.*

*Where it came from, out of that blue: from the shop over the
water, from a bench and a lamp, from before Kiri did what Kiri
did. From the one room of him I have never been allowed to cost.
He opened it an inch, for me, on a Wednesday, and passed me a
pin.*

*Filed under: gift received. Sub-filed, and I am only writing
this because no one will ever read it: I have been Hokage three
years, I have the hat, the mountain, and the seal, and the
finest thing this village has given me is a jeweller's evening
fifty.*

*The counting, incidentally: he thinks I didn't feel him doing
it. Fifty passes of the brush and he counted every one, the
lunatic, like laughs, like everything worth keeping.*

*Item for the diary that doesn't lie: whatever he asks for next,
the answer is yes.*
