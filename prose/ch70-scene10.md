# Chapter 70, Scene 10 — The First Thursday

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the second floor. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1010 words.*

---

The first Thursday began with nothing, which was the entire
specification, and holding a specification of nothing turned out
to be — he had suspected it would be, and had budgeted for it —
the hardest fabrication order of his career.

He woke at ten to six regardless, because the body was four
weeks into Jiraiya's clock and bodies do not read diaries. He lay
in the grey light itemising the day's obligations, found the
list empty, itemised it again on the assumption of clerical
error, found it empty again, and then lay very still,
experiencing an empty list at full consciousness for the first
time since Tarugi.

Beside him, the other party to the Thursday order slept on with
the serenity of a woman whose diary had been arranged by
professionals, both hands full of his shirt per standing
practice, seniority long since established.

*Entry, first Thursday, six a.m.: nothing is scheduled. I have
checked three times. The sensation is structurally identical to
standing at the edge of deep water.*

*The last time I stood at the edge of deep water was the bath
in January, and the finding of the bath was: the plans burn on
contact and the day arrives anyway. The finding of the holiday
before it was the same. The finding of the entire quarter, at
every scale, is the same, and a man who requires the same
finding a fourth time is not conducting research. He is
stalling at altitude.*

*So: in.*

And because the heart-to-heart was two days old and the fourteen
statements stood at six certified and the whole ledger of them
was running a surplus for the first time in a month; and because
the literature — consulted the previous evening for the first
time in seventeen days, four minutes, one chapter, purely as a
parts catalogue — had a page on exactly this, on the morning
after the storm, on what the hero's hands did when words had
already done the load-bearing; and mostly because he wanted to,
which was the clause the whole study had been built to make
sayable —

— when she stirred at half six and turned into him the way she
did, and his hands found her shoulders the way they had found
her shoulders every morning since November, they did not stay
at the shoulders.

They travelled. Slowly, the way the book advised and his own
craft would have specified anyway — *unhurried is the whole of
the grammar; she taught you that in a bathtub* — down the long
line of her back he had surveyed once from four feet in a
Tarugi corridor and never once touched at survey grade, over
the last vertebra his medical education could name while his
medical education still had the floor, and came to rest, with
the deliberate placed weight of a man setting a keystone, on
the curve of the finest load-bearing composition of the age.

And stayed.

The effect on the owner of the composition was immediate and
comprehensive. She came awake the rest of the way in one long
wave — he felt it travel up through the structure under his
hand, a whole spine reporting in — and made a sound against
his collarbone that no ledger he kept had a column for, and
went, for approximately four seconds, completely and
uncharacteristically still.

*Four seconds,* noted the auditor, from very far away, in a
building already on fire. *The pinwheel count. Something is
being filed. I will care later.*

"Well, good *morning*," said Tsunade, into his neck, in a
voice with the stretch still in it, and the happiness in it
was so entire, so unguarded, so exactly the sound the whole
folder of exhibits had been drafted to restore, that the four
seconds and whatever she had filed in them sank without trace
beneath it, unpursued by either party. "And what time do you
call this, fabricator."

"Thursday," said Sazare. "I call it Thursday. All of it."

---

The morning proceeded at the pace of the boat chapter, which
is to say at no pace at all, and he logged it afterward as
the highest-yield nothing since the eighth of January.

Breakfast happened at nine, scandalously, in the small room,
with the morning blend and no tray protocol whatsoever
because Shizune had — her one flourish — left the Thursday
tray at the door with a note reading *not minuted* and a
drawing of a pig. The count advanced twice before the tea
went cold: once at his description of Fukasaku's marital
ruling and Jiraiya's face above it, and once, entry
forty-four, scored deliberately, on purpose, with malice
aforethought and three days of planning, when he presented
her — deadpan, in the docket voice, sliding it across the
breakfast table like a summons — with a single sheet of
paper headed *ATTACHMENT THIRTEEN*, which she had
commissioned in January and which turned out to be, in its
entirety:

*The fabricator's report on the state of the account,
submitted per the lead model's standing request for new
attachments. Finding: paid in full. Signed—*

and the signature was not his name. It was the mark. The
keeper-knot, one stroke simplified, inked from a rubbing of
the brush handle — the first time in four hundred years the
mark of the house had gone on paper — and she looked at it
for a long time, and then folded the sheet along its rule
lines with great care, and put it inside her robe, against
the collarbone, without one word, which was the correct
filing location, and the loudest minute never taken.

"Bath," she said, eventually, when the tea was finished
and the nothing had rolled out ahead of them all the way to
the horizon. "The deep one. It's Thursday, fabricator — I'm
told we have the entirety of it, on paper, from a man who
gives whole days the way other men give flowers." She stood,
and stretched, and looked back at him from the doorway with
the morning light doing exactly what he would have specified
if light took commissions. "Come and hold the temperature
steady. You're good at that."
