# Chapter 49, Scene 6 — Ah, This One Is Easy

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,474 words.*

---

"How do I give this to her?"

He had not planned to say it. It arrived at about ten past two in the
morning in a yard, after four days and a hundred and eleven attempts
and a forearm that had gone, and it came out in the flat voice he used
for asking Kanzaki what a thing weighed.

Katsuyu did not answer immediately.

"You want to give it to Tsunade-sama," she said.

"Yes."

"Have you decided that, or have you just heard yourself say it?"

Sazare sat on the yard stone with his back against the bench leg.

"I decided it at about the sixtieth attempt on Monday," he said, "and I
have not looked at the decision since, because I did not want to find
out what was underneath it while I still had ninety attempts left."

---

"Then tell me the difficulty," said Katsuyu, "because I do not see it,
and I have found this week that when I do not see a difficulty it is
usually because it is a very large one."

"There are five."

"Then tell me the five."

He counted them off, which was the only way he could do it.

"One. It's a rose. I know that roses mean something. I do not know what
they mean and I have never known and there is no book in that library
that will tell me in a way I can use, and I have read four of them.

"Two. Her birthday was the second of this month. It was thirteen days
ago and I said nothing and did nothing, and I had known the date since
the third month and had five months of notice and decided — in
writing — not to make her anything this year, and I gave good reasons
and they were good reasons.

"Three." He stopped, and made himself do it properly. "In the fifth
month I wrote down why I could not make her anything at all, and it
was not modesty. It was that I would be making a thing that had to be
worn or kept or looked at, and I would know it was being worn or kept
or looked at, and that is not a gift, that is a claim staked in a
material I happen to be able to work. I wrote *state: refused, reason
known, not resolved*, and I have written those five words twice now and
they were not more honest the second time.

"Four. It is the first permanent object anybody in my family has made
in three hundred years and I have had it for eleven minutes.

"Five." He looked at the felt. "If I carry that up a hill and put it on
a desk, then everything I have just said is standing in the room with
me while I do it, and she will read all of it in about four seconds,
because she reads me faster than I read myself and always has."

---

The rose sang on the felt, very quietly, at the edge of hearing.

"Ah," said Katsuyu. "Yes. This one is easy."

Sazare turned his head.

"Which part of it."

"All of it." She flowed forward a little on the bench. "You have spent
eleven minutes constructing a delivery, and every single version has
the same fault in it, and the fault is not the rose or the date or the
five words you have written twice."

"Then what."

"It is *you*," said Katsuyu. "You are in it. Every route you have built
has you standing in the room at the end of it, and you are quite
correct that she will read the whole of you in four seconds, because
she will."

"So there isn't a way."

"There is an extremely obvious way," said Katsuyu, "and it is that
somebody else takes it."

---

He did not say anything for a moment.

"That's a manoeuvre," he said. "That's me getting the thing done and
not paying for it. If I send it up with an orderly then I have found a
way to hand her an object without being in the room, which is a
technique, and it's the same technique as anonymous chapter four, and I
did that in the ninth month of last year and I have four entries about
why it was cowardice."

"Yes," said Katsuyu. "If you sent it with an orderly."

"...You."

"I am not somebody you are sending," she said, mildly. "I am not a
messenger and you cannot instruct me and I would like that to be
extremely clear before I do this, because it matters and I think you
will want it to have mattered.

"I have been here for six days. I asked to come and I asked to stay on
Saturday and I have watched a hundred and eleven attempts and I have
stopped you once. I have my own opinion of what happened on that bench
tonight and it is not yours and it is not hers."

She was already moving towards the felt.

"So I am not carrying your gift up a hill," said Katsuyu. "I am going
to see Tsunade-sama, which I may do at any hour and have been able to
for forty-one years and have never once done without being called, and
I am taking a thing with me, and what I say when I get there is mine."

---

He put out his hand, which was the reflex, and stopped it halfway.

"Katsuyu."

"Yes?"

"If she asks you to take it back—"

"Then I shall take it back," said Katsuyu, "and I shall bring it here,
and I shall tell you exactly what she said including the parts you will
not want, because you gave me a no twice this week and I have had four
days to work out what that was worth and I have concluded that it was
worth this."

She was on the felt now, over the base of the stem, and the rose lifted
against her mantle and settled and went on singing.

"May I say the other thing," she said, "or would you rather I did not."

"Say it."

"You have asked me four times this week whether I was free," said
Katsuyu. "Nobody has ever asked me. I did not know until Tuesday
morning that there was anything in me that wanted to be asked."

Sazare sat on the yard stone.

"That is not a repayment," she said. "I want to be precise. I am not
doing this because I owe you a thing — that would be a poor reason and
I would not trust it. I am doing it because I would like to see how it
comes out, and I have not wanted to see how anything came out in a very
long time, and I find that I am prepared to spend something for it."

"Take it," said Sazare.

"I am."

---

And then she was gone.

There was no ceremony to it at all. There was a slug on a felt with a
nine-inch crystal rose against her mantle, and then there was a felt,
and the lamp Kanzaki had put out for her on a stool at the end of the
bench, and a yard, and the sacking over the far bench moving very
slightly in whatever was coming down off the hill at two in the
morning.

Somewhere well above him, in a room on the second floor of a tower
where a light had been on since about midnight, a slug appeared on the
edge of a desk with a flower.

He did not hear what she said.

He found out four days later, from Shizune, who had been standing in
the doorway with a folder and had not been able to leave, and who told
him in the corridor outside the records annexe in a voice she was not
entirely in control of.

What Katsuyu had said, apparently — setting the rose down on the border
reports, in the ordinary mild register she used for the fourth floor is
holding and for *you never asked* — was:

"He will be good for you, Hime."

---

Sazare sat in the yard until about three.

He had the notebook in his coat. He got it out at one point and had it
open on his knee with the pencil in his hand for some minutes, and the
page stayed empty, because there was no entry available: the article
was gone, the observer was gone, and everything else in the evening
belonged to somebody else.

He put it away.

Then he put the lamp out, and thanked a woman of seventy-two who had
gone to bed four hours earlier and would deny the stool for a week, and
went home in the dark up a hill.
