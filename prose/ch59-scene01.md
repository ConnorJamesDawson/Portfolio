# Chapter 59, Scene 1 — A Statement of Fact

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Tsunade, age fifty-three.
POV: Tsunade. ~1860 words.*

---

She let go of him and straightened up and did not step back, and she was
about to say something extremely ordinary about the delegation at nine
in order to get the room back down to a level she could stand in.

"I love you, Hime," said Sazare.

---

She did not move.

He was looking up at her from the chair with his hands still on his
knees, and there was nothing on his face at all in the way that there
had never been anything on his face, which meant the whole of it was
there, undefended, at four in the morning, at eleven inches.

"Ruri—"

"I'm going to be beside you until I die."

He said it in the same voice.

That was the thing. That was the entire thing and she had it
immediately and it went through her like cold water: **he did not
change register.** He said the second sentence exactly the way he said a
throughput figure and exactly the way he had said *it wasn't the tare*,
which was flat, and level, and with the small pause in the middle that
he put in front of a thing he had checked.

"That's not a promise," he said. "I want to be accurate. I haven't said
*I promise* and I'm not going to, because a promise is a thing that can
be broken and then you find out afterwards what it was worth.

"It's a statement of fact. It's the same class as anything else I'd put
on a page. I've checked it and I'd defend it and I'm not going to say it
again unless you ask me to, because saying a thing twice is what people
do when they don't think the first one held."

---

*Sit down.*

She did not sit down. There was nowhere to sit down that was not the
desk or the other chair and both of them were four feet away and four
feet was not available.

*Right. What has just happened.*

She made herself do it properly, in the corridor of her own head, at
speed, because the alternative was standing in a room at twenty past
four in the morning with nothing at all.

*He has declared.*

*Not hinted. Not opened a door. Not done the thing men do where they put
a sentence out at forty per cent so it can be a joke if it lands badly.
He has declared, in full, at maximum, and then attached an undertaking
to it with his own death in it, and then explained the grammar of the
undertaking so that I would not mistake it for a smaller class of
sentence.*

*In about eleven seconds.*

*From a standing start.*

*After a kiss.*

---

And then the second thing arrived, and it was worse, and it was the one
that put her hand on the back of the chair.

*There was no process.*

*There was no* process*, Tsunade.*

She had been to a great many weddings. She had watched about four
hundred people do this and she had done it herself, twice, properly,
and she knew the shape of it the way she knew the shape of a fracture.

*You look at somebody. Then at some point you contrive to be near them
and both of you pretend it is chance. Then there is a thing that is
almost an evening out and is deniable by both parties. Then there is one
that isn't. Then there is a first time somebody says something they
cannot take back, and by the time it happens it is the eleventh step and
everybody has known for months and it is a formality.*

*That is not decoration. That is not manners.*

*Every one of those steps is a place where either person can stop, and
it costs them nothing, and the other one can pretend they never noticed.*

*It is graduated on purpose. The whole thing is built so that nobody is
ever exposed further than the other one at any given moment.*

*Dan did all of it.*

She had that with complete clarity and it did not feel like a betrayal
of anything, which surprised her.

*Dan did every single step. It took eleven months. He was so careful
about it that I was three steps ahead of him for most of it and had to
manage the pace so he did not lose his nerve, and I have thought about
that with enormous affection for twenty-six years.*

*And by the time he said it, I had already agreed to it four times in
increments and neither of us had ever been standing out in the open on
our own.*

---

*This boy has skipped the entire apparatus.*

*Ten days. Ten days from* why me *to a statement of fact with his death
in it, and there was not one single point in any of it where I could
have declined at low cost, because there were no points.*

*There was no interval.*

She stood with her hand on the back of the chair.

*Oh.*

*Oh, that's what it is.*

*That is exactly what it is and Jiraiya said it to him on a windowsill
in this room on Wednesday morning and I was sitting four feet away with
a pen in my hand and I did not once think of it in this direction.*

*A seal takes time. Half a second, a second, four if it's a big one. And
that interval is the only thing on this earth between a shinobi deciding
to do something and the world finding out about it.*

*He hasn't got one.*

*He has never had one. Not in a yard, not on a bench, not with an
article, not with a rose, not with a woman.*

*He decided, and it happened, and there was no gap.*

---

"Hime," said Sazare.

"Give me a minute."

"All right."

He did.

He sat in the chair with his hands on his knees and gave her a minute,
and did not fill it, and did not qualify anything, and did not offer her
any of the four releases she could feel him having available — and she
knew he had them available, because he had spent three minutes fifty on
one earlier and had explained why it went at the end.

He did not use one.

*He's not going to soften it.*

*He gave me the escape hatch on the other thing because that one was a
question and he did not want it to be a lever. This one isn't a
question. There is nothing for me to answer, so there is nothing to let
me off.*

*He has not asked me for anything at all.*

---

"Do you understand what you've just said?" she said.

"Yes."

"Say it back to me. Not the words. What it is."

Sazare thought about it for about two seconds, which for him was a long
time.

"I've put the whole of it out at once," he said, "with nothing held
back and no way of walking it in, and I've done it after ten days, and
the ordinary version of this takes about a year and goes in steps so
that neither person is ever further out than the other one.

"So you are now standing next to somebody who has committed everything
and you have committed nothing, and that isn't a position anybody would
choose to put another person in.

"I know that. I worked it out at about half past two this morning and I
did not have a solution to it and I said it anyway."

"Why?"

"Because it's true," said Sazare, "and because the alternative was to
say a smaller version of it, and a smaller version would have been
false, and I have got one thing that is mine and it is that I do not say
things I can't support."

---

*Right.*

*Right, and now you're going to have to say something, and there are
four available and three of them are cowardice.*

*One: say it back. It's there. It has been there since some point I have
not been able to fix and I would not now be able to get it out of the
building.*

*And if I say it at twenty past four in the morning, eleven seconds
after that, in this state, in this light — he will take it, because he
takes everything, and then in about a fortnight he will do what he does
with everything, which is go back and check the conditions it was
produced under.*

*And he will find that I said it under load, immediately after, in a
room, at four in the morning, having been kissed twice.*

*And he will write down that it is not a clean reading.*

*He would never say so. He would put it in a bracket in his own book and
never mention it to me for the rest of his life, and it would sit there,
and every single time he wanted to be certain of it he would have to
step over that bracket.*

*I am not giving that boy an object he cannot weigh.*

She let go of the back of the chair.

---

"I'm not going to say it back tonight," said Tsunade.

He did not flinch. She had known he would not and she had braced for it
anyway.

"And I'm going to tell you exactly why, because if I don't you'll build
four reasons and two of them will be bad ones and you'll live in the
worst one for a fortnight.

"It isn't that it isn't there."

She saw that land. She saw the whole of it land, across a face with no
machinery on it whatsoever, and she had to look at the lamp for a
second.

"It's that if I say it now you'd have got it out of me at twenty past
four in the morning, eleven seconds after I kissed you, in a room, with
no sleep, on the worst possible run of days.

"And you'd take it. You'd take it and you'd be — " she gestured at his
entire face " — like that, for about a fortnight.

"And then you'd do what you do. You'd go back and look at the conditions
it was produced under, because you cannot help it, and you'd find that
it came off a woman under load in the middle of the night and you would
write *not a clean reading* in your own book in your own hand, and you
would never once say so to me, and you'd step over it every time you
wanted to be sure of it for the rest of your life."

Sazare sat very still.

"I'm not handing you an article you can't weigh," said Tsunade. "You've
had eleven years of people giving you things that don't survive
inspection.

"So you'll get it. You'll get it when I'm sober and slept and there's
nothing on the table and it's the middle of an ordinary afternoon and
nobody's touched anybody.

"And then you'll be able to put it on a felt."
