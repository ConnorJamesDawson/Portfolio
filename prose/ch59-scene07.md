# Chapter 59, Scene 7 — The Edge

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1760 words.*

---

He did the exercise in the yard on the Thursday because it was warmer
than his own floor and because Kanzaki had left the lamp out again.

*Ex. 1. Being bad at it, as instructed, day six.*

*Six on Monday. Nine on Tuesday and I did not chase it. Four on
Wednesday, which is the correct shape of it and which I wrote down
without comment, and I would like it recorded that not commenting took
me eleven minutes.*

He sat down on the yard stone and put his hands on his knees and went
still.

It came.

---

It came the way it had come on the eleventh, which was without any
resistance whatsoever, and he had about four seconds of holding it in a
sphere and counting before he understood that it was still coming.

*That's not the shape.*

*It comes and then it stops and then I hold what came and count it. It
has done that seventy-two times and it did it at two and it did it at
fifteen. The gather is not a tap I leave running; it fills and then it
is full and then the work is the holding.*

*This is not stopping.*

He sat on the yard stone with his eyes shut.

*Right. How long has it been coming.*

*Eleven seconds.*

*Twelve.*

*I do not know what the ceiling is. Nobody has ever told me the ceiling
and I have never once been near it — except that I have been over it,
on the fifteenth of the eighth month, at seventeen, on a bench, and I
did not feel it happen then either.*

*So the correct action here is to close.*

*I am not going to close.*

---

He was entirely clear about it afterwards and he wrote it down in those
words because softening it would have been a lie: he knew what the
correct action was, he had it in front of him, it was one movement he
had made ten million times since he was seven, and he did not make it.

*Why not.*

*Because I want to know.*

*And that is not a reason. That has never been a reason. That is the
sentence that goes at the front of every accident report I have ever
read and I have read about four hundred.*

*And I have said it to myself in this yard about a hundred times and
stopped, and I am not stopping.*

He let it come.

---

He went to about thirty seconds and then he lost the count, which he did
not notice at the time.

What he had, and what he was able to write down afterwards with some
confidence, was this:

*It does not feel like filling. I have said that before and I have never
been able to get past it. It feels like the room getting larger, and at
about the twentieth second the room stopped being a room.*

*I could feel the whole yard.*

*Not see. I want to be exact. Not sensing and not detecting and nothing
that anybody would recognise as a technique. The flags, and the frost on
the flags at the north end, and the bench, and the sacking, and the four
hundred grams of reject in the tin, and the aggregate in the far bay
which is thirty-one per cent and which I have graded eleven times, and
the tare weights that Kanzaki was pretending to work on, and the wall,
and the lamp.*

*And the hill under all of it.*

*It was not information. There was nothing in it I could have used and
nothing I could have written a line about, and I have got no measurement
of any of it.*

*It was that all of it was in the room with me, and that I was not
looking out at it.*

---

Then his left hand started to ache.

It was not much. It was a dull thing along the line at the carpus where
the work had stopped three months earlier, about a quarter of an inch
wide, and it had done that four times in the ninth month in cold weather
and he had written it up as ordinary scar behaviour and thought nothing
of it since.

He came out of it.

That took about a second and a half and it was not difficult and he did
not have to do anything clever; he simply closed, in the way he had
closed since he was seven, both acts, a tenth of a second apart, welded
together by eleven years of habit.

And then he sat on a yard stone in the cold with his heart going and his
hand aching and looked at his own palm in the lamplight.

*Unchanged.*

*No line. No opacity at the fingertips. Sensation normal to the pulp of
all five including the thumb. The patch is the patch.*

*Nothing has happened.*

He sat there for about four minutes.

Then he laughed out loud in an empty yard at half past nine at night.

---

*Right. Write it up. Properly. All of it.*

*26/11, quartermaster's yard, alone, no observer, standing arrangement.*

*Exercise commenced ordinarily. Gather did not terminate at the usual
point. Held open, deliberately, against the correct action, for a period
I estimate at thirty to forty seconds and cannot state because I lost
the count somewhere in the twenties and did not notice.*

*Findings:*

*One. The intake has a very much larger margin than the hold. I have
been at fifteen minutes of holding for a fortnight and I have assumed
that fifteen was the size of me, and it is not — fifteen is how long I
can carry a full one. The reservoir is a different quantity and I have
never measured it and neither has anybody else, and it is bigger.*

*Two. Past about twenty seconds the character changes and I cannot
describe it and I am going to stop trying tonight.*

*Three, and this is the important one.*

He stopped and made himself write it exactly.

*There is no gauge.*

*I have been asking myself for four minutes how close I got and the
answer is that I have got no way of knowing and there is not one
available. The only instrument in existence for measuring proximity to
that ceiling is the ceiling, and it reports by taking your hand.*

*You find the edge by going over it.*

*That is what happened to me at seventeen and that is what happens to
everybody it happens to, and it is why there are four of us, and it is
why the review said what it said.*

*Four. The only warning I got was the scar.*

*Line at the carpus, aching, at about thirty seconds. Three months old.
Dead tissue with no nerve supply that should not be capable of reporting
anything.*

*I do not know whether that was the ceiling talking or whether it was
cold and coincidence and I wanted it to be something.*

*But if it is real then the only proximity gauge I own is made out of
the last time.*

---

He put the pencil down and sat back against the bench and found that he
was still doing it.

*I am pleased.*

*I want that on the page and I want it in the same hand as the rest.*

*I have just sat in a yard on my own and held open the thing that
turns people into statues, past the point where I know how to stop
being able to stop, for a period I cannot state, with no observer, four
days after two separate parties told me in writing not to do anything
like it, and I got a finding out of it that is worth having.*

*And what I feel about that is pleased.*

*Not relieved. I have been relieved. Relief is what I had on the
sixteenth of the eighth month when I woke up and laid a course with an
eighty-five per cent hand and it came up in four seconds.*

*This is not that. There is no relief in it because there was never any
fear, and I have only just noticed that, sitting here, eleven minutes
after.*

He looked at the lamp on the stool.

*The stone should have been the loudest thing in that yard.*

*It was not in the yard at all. It did not arrive. I have had it in the
front of my head since Monday the sixteenth, in every single thing I
have done, it has been in the exercise and it broke the exercise and I
wrote four pages about it —*

*— and tonight it simply was not there.*

---

He sat with that for a long time and did not enjoy the end of it.

*So write the actual finding.*

*The thing that has made my work the best it has ever been is the same
thing that took the fear out of the yard tonight.*

*It is not two effects. It is one.*

*I have not got room for the watching at the moment. That is why the
articles are clean and that is why the light goes all the way in and
that is why it came up in three and a half seconds — and the watching is
also what asks* is this safe.

*I have not been checking. I have not checked anything since the
twenty-first. I checked the aggregate on Sunday out of habit and I have
not run a single risk line since, on anything, and I did not know that
until this minute.*

*The observer is gone.*

*The observer was the fault in my hands for eleven years and it was also
the only brake I had, and I have got about four hundred pages on the
first half and nothing at all on the second.*

He looked at his own writing for a while.

*I am going to have to tell her.*

*Tomorrow. At the fourth hour. All of it, including the thirty to forty
seconds and including the part where I am pleased.*

*She is not going to take it well and I am going to tell her anyway,
because the alternative is a man with a finding in a drawer, and I have
never once been that and I am not starting because I am frightened of a
conversation.*

He put the lamp out and went home over the hill and slept eleven hours,
which he had not done since he was a child.
