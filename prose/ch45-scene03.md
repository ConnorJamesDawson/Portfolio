# Chapter 45, Scene 3 — The Next Page But One

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,542 words.*

---

The next page but one was headed *EXERCISES* and had four of them, and
Tarumi Ichirō had annotated all four.

*1. Between the palms, an open sphere. No material. Hold to a count of
thirty.*

*2. The same sphere. Halve its size without changing the quantity.*

*3. Move it, palm to palm, without loss of form.*

*4. Exercise 3, walking.*

Beside the first, in soft pencil: *easy.*

Beside the fourth, four years of a child's life later by the look of
the pressure: *STILL CANT DO 4.*

Sazare cleared the low table, and put the lamp where he could see his
hands, and sat down on the floor with his back to the wall at about
half past nine on the twenty-fourth, and set out to do a thing eleven-
year-olds are given in the first term.

---

He could not do the first one.

Not badly. Not to a count of eleven, or four, or two. He put his palms
eight inches apart and reached for the thing he had done nine hundred
thousand times since he was seven years old — and found nothing there
at all, in the way that a man who has spent his life going down a
staircase finds nothing there at all when the bottom step is missing.

It was not difficulty. Difficulty he would have recognised and enjoyed.

There was no purchase.

He tried it eleven times over about forty minutes, in four different
ways, with his eyes shut and his eyes open, sitting and standing, and
on the ninth attempt he got something — a pressure, a warmth, an
approximate *there* — which held for slightly under a second and then
was not anywhere, and did not disperse so much as simply stop having
been.

He sat back against the wall.

Then he got the tin down off the shelf, and took out one grain of
seeded stock, and put it on the table between his palms, and did it
again.

It came up instantly.

Four seconds to a full course. Nested, three deep, the fine damping the
coarse, exactly as it had done every time since he was nine, without
hesitation, without effort, without any part of him having to think
about it at all — the whole operation as automatic as catching
something dropped.

He took the grain away.

Nothing.

He put it back.

Everything.

---

He did that nineteen times and wrote down every one of them, because
that was what he was for, and by the eleventh he had stopped feeling
anything about it and was simply running the test.

Then he put the pencil down and sat on the floor with his back against
the wall for a considerable while.

*I cannot hold chakra.*

*I have never held chakra. Not once, in eleven years, for one second.
There has been a material at the far end of every single operation I
have ever performed and I have never in my life done the thing without
one.*

*Exercise one is: hold it yourself, in the open, with nothing to put it
into.*

*A boy of eleven wrote "easy" beside it.*

He got up, because sitting had stopped being useful, and went to the
window, and the fifth month was warm and black outside and there was
nothing to look at.

*So the profile is: cannot perform exercise one. Can perform, at
speed, without conscious attention, a three-deep nested course into a
seeded medium, which is not on any page of this book and which
Kanzaki tells me she has audited from four countries and seen twice.*

*That is not a person who is good at this. That is a person who is
missing a limb and has spent eleven years becoming extraordinary with
the other one.*

---

He worked at exercise one for four more nights and got to a count of
two.

That was the whole of the progress and it was, he wrote, *the single
largest amount of effort I have expended for the smallest result since
I was seven and could not hold a graver.*

What he got in exchange was better than the count.

*The reason I cannot hold it is not weakness and it is not control. I
have tested both. I put nine hundred grams of load through a splint
plate on Tuesday without a wobble and I have laid a fine course inside
a coarse one for nine years, which the book calls a shape operation of
the third order and gives four pages to.*

*I cannot hold it because holding it is not a thing I have ever needed
to do. The material holds it. That is what a medium IS.*

*Ch. 41: a course is the material taking up part of the instruction.
The skill is in the handing over.*

*I wrote that four months ago and I was pleased with it and I put it in
front of the Hokage at the fourth hour of the morning.*

*It is also a description of a disability and I did not notice.*

He read that back and did not soften it, and then wrote the rest,
which took three attempts.

*I hand it over because I cannot hold it.*

*I have been calling that a philosophy.*

---

He went to the yard on the twenty-eighth and did exercise one at a
bench in daylight, badly, in front of a quartermaster of sixty-two who
watched him do it four times and said nothing.

On the fifth she said, "That's the Academy sphere."

"Yes."

"You're not doing it."

"No."

"Mm." Kanzaki went on with the day book for about a minute. "My
brother's boy could do that at ten. He's a hopeless article in every
other respect — he's thirty-one and he has been in and out of four
trades and his mother has given up — and he could hold that sphere for
a count of a hundred at ten years old and it has never once been of the
slightest use to him in his entire life."

"That's not the encouragement you think it is."

"It wasn't encouragement, it was data, and you're the one who's always
telling me the difference." She turned a page. "Do you want the
useful thing or shall I go back to the book?"

"The useful thing."

"You've come into this yard for three years and there is not one
process in it you have ever done the ordinary way." Kanzaki did not
look up. "You mill wrong. You anneal wrong. You have never used the
long tongs in your life and you do a two-man lift on your own by
splitting the load, which is not a technique, it is a thing you
invented because there was nobody else there. Every single one of those
is worse than the ordinary way in nine conditions out of ten and better
in the tenth, and the tenth is the one you keep getting."

"That isn't a compliment either."

"No," said Kanzaki. "It's an inventory. You've never had the ordinary
way, so you built round the hole, and the going round is the thing
everybody pays you for, and there is now a hole in the middle of it the
size of a schoolbook."

She closed the day book.

"Fill it in," she said. "It'll take you a year and you'll be worse at
everything for about four months and you'll hate it, and at the end of
it you will still be doing the two-man lift on your own, because that
one is actually better."

---

He wrote the night's entry sitting on the floor again with his back
against the wall and the count of two in front of him.

*Exercise 1: two.*

*Fifth night. Twenty-nine attempts. Best hold two seconds and I have
had two seconds three times out of twenty-nine, which is not a
capability, it is a frequency.*

*Yagi says first term. Ochiai says everybody in this village by twelve.
Kanzaki's nephew, who has failed at four trades, did a hundred at ten.*

*I am going to keep doing it, and I am going to keep the count on this
page every night, and I am not going to tell anybody the number.*

He stopped there.

Then he added the thing that had been standing behind all of it for
five days and had not been allowed forward, because a ledger with the
comfortable half in it was not a shorter ledger, it was a different
document.

*And the other one, which I have been walking round since the
twenty-fourth.*

*If I cannot hold chakra in the open, then there is a category of
operation I have never attempted and could not attempt, and it is not
a small category. It is everything anybody does that does not have a
material in it.*

*Which means the question I have been asking for eleven years — what is
the crystal, how does it work, what am I — has been the wrong question,
because it has always been a question about the far end.*

*The far end is nine-tenths of everything I know.*

*I have no account whatsoever of the near end, and until five days ago
I did not know there was one.*
