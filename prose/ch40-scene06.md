# Chapter 40, Scene 6 — What She Is Running From

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the tower. POV: Tsunade. ~1,508 words.*

---

She went back to the office afterwards, which she did not usually do,
and sat behind the desk in the dark without lighting the lamp.

The four faces on the cliff were straight ahead through the middle
window. She had turned the desk at an angle to it in her first fortnight
and had told Shizune it was the light and had never corrected the
record.

*Hime.*

He had cited a source. In a public room, at volume, with four
witnesses and a wine list, that boy had stood up a nine-day justificatory
argument with a document reference in it, because it had not once
occurred to him that a person might simply decide to call somebody
something.

Tsunade put her forehead in her hand and laughed for about four
seconds in an empty office.

Then she stopped, because the other thing arrived, the way it always
arrived, about a minute behind the first one.

---

*It is entirely inappropriate and I have permitted it.*

She was extremely clear with herself about this, because she had been
extremely clear with herself about all of it from the beginning, and
the one thing she was not going to permit was the comfortable version.

A provisional genin does not address the Kage of his village by an
epithet in a public room. It does not matter that it is documented. It
does not matter that the reasoning is airtight, and it was airtight, and
she had checked it twice while he was still talking. Nine people in this
village would make it a problem and two of them would enjoy it, and she
had said so out loud and then permitted it anyway.

Same as the tenth.

She had known what she was doing on the tenth as well. She had had — she
had counted it afterwards, because she was a medic and she counted
things — about a second and a half. And in that second and a half she
had run the whole of it: eleven witnesses, four of them hospital staff,
one of them married into the liaison office, one of them a market
trader; a nineteen-year-old rumour cycle that would be through the
second floor by six the following morning; and a Council that would
have it inside a fortnight and would not raise it and would remember
it.

And underneath all of that, in the same second and a half, the thing
that had actually decided it:

*If I stand here with my arms at my sides he will work it out from the
geometry inside four seconds, and he will file it as a transgression,
and he will never do anything like it again as long as he lives.*

That had been true. She stood by it. It was the correct clinical
judgement about a specific patient's specific pathology and she would
have made the same call about anybody.

*And I wanted to.*

She had not permitted herself that one for four days. She permitted it
now, alone, in an unlit office, and then sat with it, which was the
part she was good at and had been good at for thirty years and had
never once found useful.

---

The interest had been genuine. That was the part nobody would believe
and it was the part she was most certain of.

She had come back to a village that had buried a Hokage and she had
found, in a stack of establishment files, a medical result that did not
descend from her.

Forty years. Forty years of medicine, and every advance in the field
for the last two decades had come out of her hands or out of hands she
had trained, and there was a boy on the second floor of a hospital who
had built a device that stored a chakra instruction and released it into
living tissue without its author present, out of a bloodline nobody had
ever documented, alone, at seventeen, with a bad knee and no formal
education whatsoever.

*The bait was the interest,* Tsunade thought, in an empty office. *I
would like that on the record, if there were a record, because it is
true and because everything else that has happened proceeds from it and
not the other way round.*

*I came for the work.*

*The hook was him and I did not notice for about five months.*

---

She could date it.

That was the humiliating part and she had stopped being embarrassed
about it around the fourth month, which was itself a bad sign.

Third Thursday of last year. Markets are fine, it's audiences. Four
words in the middle of a sentence about sightlines at Kanezaki,
delivered as a professional observation by a boy who did not know he
had said anything, and she had sat there with a cup halfway to her
mouth and thought: *somebody put you in a crowd facing a platform and
you have never told a living soul.*

And she had filed it, and had not asked, and had turned six tables
ninety degrees eleven months later.

*That is not a colleague.*

*I have been a colleague. I know what it is. I have had four hundred of
them and I have never once rearranged a room.*

---

She got up and stood at the window with the village going dark below
it.

The problem was not the professional boundary. She wanted to be
scrupulous about that, because the professional boundary was the thing
everybody would talk about, and the professional boundary had gone out
of the window at nine minutes past the second hour on the tenth of the
first month in front of eleven people and was not coming back.

The problem was that she was doing it again.

*A man who treats his own body as an acceptable cost.*

That was the whole of it and it had taken her about six months to be
able to say it in that many words. Nineteen months of a file: eleven
self-inflicted cuts on a forearm to learn control, because there was
nobody to teach him and nothing else available. A knee walked on for
nine years without review. One hundred and eight pathways under
simultaneous load on a roof at sixteen and a chest leak and both arms
abraded, and the line in the Hyūga's report — *he maintained exclusions
inside the network* — which meant that while he was tearing himself
apart he had been holding four people out of it by hand.

A boy with a foreign retrieval order on him until six months ago, a
bounty before that, and a habit of writing down exactly what he is
worth in his own handwriting so that somebody else does not have to
estimate it.

*I know exactly what this is.*

*I know precisely which afternoon is in the way and I know which of the
two of them it is, and I have known since the fourth month, and I have
been drinking with him every Thursday for fourteen of them.*

She put one hand on the window frame.

*Dan died in about four minutes and I was holding him and I am the best
medical ninja who has ever lived and I could not do one single thing.*

*And that is the whole of it and there is no version of me that has
dealt with it, and I have had thirty years, and I am fifty-three, and
the honest position is that I am no further forward than I was at
twenty-two and I have simply got extremely good at arranging a life in
which it does not come up.*

---

She thought, in an unlit office, with the roofs going away red-brown to
the trees:

*He is running from his and I can see the whole of it. I could write it
out. I could tell him in about nine minutes what he is doing and why
and he would take it, because he takes everything I tell him, and he
would go home and write it in that notebook and he would work on it,
because that is what he does with things people tell him.*

*And I am running from mine and I have the entire vocabulary and forty
years of the literature and I have never once said it out loud to
anybody who is alive.*

*Two of us in a room with six tables. Same avoidance. Different
notation.*

*And I am the one who is supposed to know better, and the difference
between us is not that I am wiser. The difference is that he is
eighteen and has been trying for six months out of a library book, and
I am fifty-three and have not tried at all.*

She stood at the window for a while longer.

Then she lit the lamp, and pulled the border returns across, and worked
until the second hour, which was what she had come back to the office
for and was not, she was entirely aware, why she had stayed.
