# Chapter 60, Scene 1 — The Room Down From Hers

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Tsunade, age fifty-three.
POV: Tsunade. ~2240 words.*

---

She woke at about ten past six and did not know why she was awake,
because nothing had woken her.

That was the first thing and it took her some time to identify, lying
still in the grey with the shutter not quite closed. **Nothing had woken
her.** There had been no noise and no dream and no arriving thought, and
she had not surfaced at half two and lain there for forty minutes as she
had done four nights in five for a very long time.

She had gone under and stayed under and come up at ten past six.

*When did I last do that.*

She went looking for it, which was a mistake and she knew it was a
mistake about two seconds in, and the answer came back from a very long
way off and was not a place she went.

*Before I left.*

*Before I left the village. Not since. Not once in seventeen years, and
before that not since—*

*No. Stop there. That's enough of that.*

---

He was asleep on the other side of the bed with his back half to her and
his face about ten inches away.

She had gone and got him at about half past one.

She remembered every part of it, which she wanted on the record even
though there was no record and nobody would ever ask: she had been awake
since about midnight, she had been perfectly sober, she had lain there
for an hour and a half doing nothing, and then she had got up and gone
down the corridor and opened the door of the room at the end.

He had been asleep on top of the covers with his coat still on, in the
dark, in a room with a bed in it, like a man who had been shown
somewhere and had not liked to presume.

She had woken him about a third of the way and said four words and he
had come, and she was not entirely sure he had been conscious for any of
it.

*And I am not going to explain it to him, because he will ask, and if I
answer he will write it down.*

*And I am not going to explain it to myself either.*

---

She looked at him.

She did not have anything to do at ten past six on the morning of the
twenty-eighth except be in that building at nine, and there was a good
deal of grey light coming round the edge of the shutter, and so she lay
there on her side and looked at him for a while.

*He looks his age.*

That was the thing and it went into her like a splinter.

*Awake, he doesn't. Awake he could be thirty — he stands like a man of
forty and he talks like a man of sixty and he has got a face on him that
has had four years of a ward on it, and I have not once in three years
looked at that boy and thought* eighteen.

*Asleep, he's eighteen.*

*He's eighteen years old and he's got his mouth slightly open and there
is a mark on the left side of his face from Friday morning that is going
yellow at the edge, and I put it there, and he has not mentioned it
once.*

She lay there with that for a while and did not flinch off it.

*Right. Have it, then.*

*Thirty-five years. I did the whole argument with him in this office
eight days ago and he came back at me with an actuarial table and I took
his head off for it and I was right to, because a table is not the
thing.*

*This is the thing.*

*The thing is a woman of fifty-three lying in the half-dark looking at a
boy's face and knowing what it is going to look like in twenty years and
knowing exactly what she is going to look like, and knowing that one of
those is a fact and the other one is a shape she puts up.*

*And knowing that he has never once asked to see it and asked her to
show him instead, and that she could not do it, and that she has not
worked out what that means and is not going to this morning.*

She did not move.

*And here's the rest of it, and I have got about four minutes before he
wakes up.*

*I do not care.*

*I have run that arithmetic four hundred times in three weeks and I get
the same answer every time and the answer has stopped being interesting.
I decided on the Saturday morning at about half past four with a boy
coming apart in a chair in front of me and I have not moved off it since
and I am not going to.*

*So the thirty-five years is a fact about the world, like the weather,
and I am going to have to carry it, and the alternative was not carrying
it and having none of this, and I have looked at that and I would not
take it.*

---

She realised, somewhere in the middle of it, that she had two fingers
resting on the inside of his wrist.

She had no memory of putting them there.

*Fifty-eight.*

*Of course it's fifty-eight. It's been fifty-eight since he was fifteen,
he told me so in a chair on the twenty-first with a straight face, and
it did not move on a stair with his hand going to stone and I said it
was ridiculous and he wrote it down as a compliment.*

*Take your hand off him, Tsunade.*

*You are not on a ward.*

She took her hand off him and put it under her own cheek and did not
look at the wrist again for some time, and thought, with some
irritation, that he was going to work out that she had done it, probably
from something to do with the position of her arm, and would not say.

---

His eyes opened at about twenty past.

There was no transition. That was a thing she had noticed on the twelfth
and had put down to the circumstances and was now going to have to file
as ordinary: he did not surface and blink and gather himself. His eyes
opened and he was entirely present and had been for some fraction of a
second before they opened.

He looked at her from ten inches away.

"Pervert," said Sazare.

---

She got about half of a laugh out before she got hold of it.

"I beg your pardon?"

"You've been awake for some time."

"You don't know that."

"Your breathing's at fourteen and it's been at fourteen for as long as
I've been listening to it," said Sazare, "which is about ninety seconds,
and asleep you were at eleven, which I know because I woke up at
eighteen minutes past and lay still and did the count before I opened my
eyes, and it went eleven, eleven, eleven, fourteen, and the fourteen
started before I did anything.

"So you were awake first. And you weren't asleep and drifting, because
that's got a rhythm and it wanders, and yours has been level.

"And you've had two fingers on my wrist at some point in the last four
minutes, because your arm's not where an arm goes and there is exactly
one reason for it to be there."

"That," said Tsunade, "is a diagnosis."

"Yes."

"You have diagnosed me for looking at you."

"Yes," said Sazare, and then, with an air of a man doing something
mildly experimental: "*Pervert*."

---

She hit him with a pillow, which she had not done to another human being
since she was about twenty-two.

"Right," she said. "Out. Get up. There are two mountains coming into
this building at ten o'clock and I have got a delegation report to sign
and Shizune will be up at the fifth—"

"Shizune's been up," said Sazare.

She went very still.

"What?"

"About an hour ago. She came up the inner stair and got about four steps
onto the landing and stopped and went back down, and she did it quietly,
which she does not normally do, because she does not normally have to."

"You were asleep."

"I was asleep at eleven respirations," he said, "and I woke up at
eighteen past, and I know about the stair because there are four boards
on that landing and the third one goes, and it went at about twenty past
five and then it went again about nine seconds later on the way back
down.

"Nine seconds is not somebody who has changed their mind about the
errand. Nine seconds is somebody who got far enough to see both doors."

Tsunade put her hand over her eyes.

"Which door was open?" she said.

"Mine."

"...The one down the corridor."

"Yes," said Sazare. "I didn't shut it. I wasn't fully awake when I came
down here and there was no reason to."

---

She lay on her back and looked at the ceiling for a moment.

"That's this morning's first problem, then."

"It isn't a problem."

"Ruri, Shizune stood on my landing at twenty past five and looked at an
empty room with the door open and worked out where the occupant was, and
she is now downstairs making tea."

"Yes."

"And that is not a problem?"

"No," said Sazare, "because she has known since the twelfth of this
month, and she stood outside your office door for three hours and forty
minutes on the eleventh and she sent the guard home, and she has said
nothing to anybody for seventeen days.

"She did not go up that stair to find anything out. She went up it
because it was twenty past five and she takes the delegation folder up
at twenty past five, and she found something out by accident, and then
she went back down nine seconds later because she decided not to know it
where either of us could see her decide.

"That's the fourth time she's done something like that for us in
seventeen days and I've written all four down and I'm going to have to
find some way of saying thank you that doesn't make her acknowledge any
of it."

---

Tsunade lay there.

"How long have you been awake?"

"Nineteen minutes."

"And you've done all that."

"There was nothing else to do," said Sazare, "and I wasn't going to move,
because you were asleep and you were at eleven and you were flat."

"Meaning what?"

He turned over onto his back so that he was looking at the same ceiling
she was.

"Eleven respirations, no catch on the third or the sixth, no shift in
depth across at least ninety minutes and probably more, and you did not
surface once in that period," he said. "I have three years of you. I've
counted your breathing in that office about six hundred times and I did
it with my arms round you on the eleventh at twenty-six coming down to
twelve.

"That is the best set of figures I have ever taken on you.

"By a distance. It is not close and it isn't a good night, it's a
different order of thing, and I don't have any comparison for it in
anything I've got."

He said it in exactly the voice he used for a bed state at the end of a
ward round.

"So I wasn't going to move," he said.

---

She lay on her back next to him and looked at a ceiling she had looked
at for eleven hundred mornings and found that she could not immediately
say anything at all.

*He has just told me that I slept.*

*He has told me the way he would tell Yagi about a man in bed nine. He
has got a figure and a range and a comparison set of six hundred and he
has told me, on his back, at half past six in the morning, that the
numbers are the best he has ever had off me.*

*And he has no idea. None. He thinks he has passed on a clinical
observation.*

*Nobody has ever told me that I slept.*

*Twenty-six years. Dan used to say I looked tired, which was true and
which was kind and which was a completely different sentence, and this
boy has just handed me a measurement.*

She turned her head.

He was still looking at the ceiling and it was all over his face — the
thing that had been on it since Saturday, the thing four people had
remarked on and Yagi had called a lamp, and it was worse in daylight at
ten inches than it had ever been across an office.

*Right.*

*Sober. Slept — Kami, slept. Nothing on the table, nothing in this room
at all, and it is a perfectly ordinary Saturday morning.*

*I said he'd get it when he could weigh it.*

*Four of my own conditions and I have got all four and I did not
engineer one of them.*

She reached over and slapped him, lightly, on the same side of the face
she had hit on Friday.

"Ow," said Sazare, without any conviction at all.

"That's for the face."

"I can't do anything about the—"

"I know you can't," said Tsunade. "Love you too, Ruri."
