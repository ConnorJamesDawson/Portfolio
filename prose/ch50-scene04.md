# Chapter 50, Scene 4 — What It Is Called

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard, after hours. Sazare
Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,607 words.*

---

She started correcting other things about a week after the rose, and
she did it the way she did everything, which was to mention it in the
middle of a sentence about something else.

"You take about a third more than you use."

He stopped with his hand on the felt.

"Say that again."

"When you begin. You reach, and you take an amount, and then you use
rather less than the amount and the rest simply — goes." Katsuyu shifted
on the corner of the bench. "I am sorry, I do not know whether that is
useful. I assumed you knew and I have been assuming things for a
fortnight and being wrong about all of them, so I have decided to stop
assuming."

"Every time?"

"Every time I have watched. Which is now something over four thousand."

Sazare sat down on the yard stone.

*Four thousand.*

*A third.*

*Eleven years. Call it four hundred thousand courses at a third over,
which is a number I am not going to convert into anything because there
is no unit for it and I would only be doing it to feel something.*

"Why do I do that?"

"I do not know," said Katsuyu. "I can see what you do. I cannot see
why. I would guess — and it is a guess and you should mark it as one —
that it is the same reason as the other thing."

"What other thing?"

"You grip."

---

That took four days to get anywhere with, and the four days were the
worst work he had done since the twenty-second of the fifth month.

She could not explain it. He could not feel it. They went at it the way
they had gone at the close — she said *there* and he tried to find what
she meant, over and over, and for three days he found nothing at all
and got progressively worse-tempered about it in a way he wrote down
each night with some embarrassment.

On the fourth day he got it, and he got it because she stopped trying
to describe it and described the *consequence* instead.

"It is harder for you than it needs to be," she said. "That is all. I
have watched a great many things happen in four hundred years and I do
not know very much about any of them, but I know effort, and you are
using — I would say twice. Twice what the work is."

"Twice."

"You are holding it as though it will get away."

He sat very still on a stool in a yard at eleven at night.

*Oh.*

---

He knew it immediately, once she had said it that way, and he knew it
the way you know a thing about yourself that four people have told you
in different words and you have declined to assemble.

*Handa: you grip your chisel like it owes you money. Ninth month, in a
shed, two years ago. I thought it was about the chisel.*

*Yagi: your hands are hard. Fifteen. She meant on patients and she said
it once and I corrected it inside a week because it was a fault I could
see.*

*Kakashi: you resist the fall all the way down. Fourth month of this
year, eleven times, and it took him eleven falls to make me hear it.*

*Kanzaki: you do the two-man lift on your own by splitting the load,
which is not a technique, it is a thing you invented because there was
nobody else there.*

*Four people. Four trades. One fault.*

He wrote it out properly that night and it took him three attempts.

*I over-grip everything I have ever held, in every medium, since I was
about seven.*

*The reason is not nerves and it is not tension and it is not a habit I
picked up badly and could unpick.*

*I grip because nobody has ever been there to catch it.*

*That is the whole of it. If you drop a thing and somebody says stop,
you can hold it loosely. If you drop a thing and there is nobody, and
the thing is the article you have spent nine hours on, or a man's
forearm, or a wall in a corridor with people behind it — you hold it as
hard as you can, every time, and you get very good at holding, and
after eleven years you cannot feel that you are doing it because there
has never been a moment when you were not.*

*A third over on the take. Twice the effort on the hold.*

*It is not a flaw in the technique. It is eleven years of no
supervision, expressed in my hands.*

---

Loosening it was the hardest thing he had ever tried to do and he was
bad at it for a fortnight.

Not because it was difficult. Because it was *frightening*, in a way he
had no framework for and had to build a framework for on the page,
sitting on a floor at two in the morning:

*To hold it loosely I have to be prepared to lose it.*

*And to be prepared to lose it I have to believe that losing it is
survivable, which means somebody has to be there, which means I have to
rely on her being there, which is the exact thing I refused a contract
in order not to be able to do.*

He looked at that for a long time.

*So I have declined the mechanism that guarantees she is there, on
grounds I still think are correct, and I am now asking myself to do a
thing that only works if she is there.*

*I would like it on the record that I saw this coming approximately
never.*

---

It worked in the third week of the ninth month, at about the two
hundredth attempt, and it worked the way the close had worked, which
was quietly and all at once and without any effort in it at all.

He laid a course at what felt like nothing.

The chip came up on the felt, correct, dull, three deep, four seconds,
entirely ordinary — and his forearm was not tired, and his shoulder had
not gone, and he sat there with his hand still out over the felt
waiting for the thing that always happened at the end of an evening and
it did not happen.

"That was it," said Katsuyu.

"That was nothing. I didn't do anything."

"Yes."

---

He asked her what it was called on the last evening of the month.

He had been going to ask for a fortnight and had kept not asking, and
he worked out afterwards that the reason was that he had been enjoying
not knowing, which was a state he had spent his entire life avoiding
and had discovered in the ninth month of his eighteenth year that he
liked.

"Katsuyu — what is this? What we're doing. Does it have a name?"

"Does what have a name?"

"This." He gestured at the felt, the bench, the four thousand courses,
the fortnight. "You watching me and telling me when. The grip. The
third over. All of it — is there a word? Is it a thing people do?"

There was a pause.

"I do not think it is called anything," said Katsuyu, slowly. "It is
how you hold it."

"That's not an answer."

"No, I am — forgive me, I am trying." She settled. "You are asking me
what it is *called* and I am finding that I do not understand the
question, which has happened four times this month and I have begun to
enjoy it. It is not a thing that is done. It is a way of being that is
correct or is not correct, and yours was not correct, and now it is
somewhat more correct, and in about a year it will be more correct
still."

"Slugs don't have a word for it."

"Slugs do not have words for most things," said Katsuyu, "and we keep
no scrolls of any kind and never have. There is nothing to write down.
There is only somebody doing it in front of you until you are doing it
too."

Sazare wrote that on the back of a requisition form and kept it.

---

He entered the fortnight in the ledger under the only heading he had.

*Ex. 1 — assisted.*

It went at the bottom of the page, under the hours on the sword and the
reject figures and the batch numbers, in the ordinary place, in the
ordinary hand, where a count had sat every night for seventeen weeks.

*Assisted practice, 4th to 30th of the ninth month. Observer present
eleven evenings of nineteen.*

*Corrections received: the take (approx. one third over, all
occasions, eleven years); the grip (approx. twice necessary effort, all
media, eleven years). Both invisible to me. Both visible to her
immediately and she assumed I knew.*

*Cause of both, on my own analysis: no supervision, ever. Not a
technique fault. A history, in my hands.*

*Result: less effort. I do not yet have a figure for the work and
Kanzaki will have one before I do because she always does.*

*It has no name. I asked. She could not understand the question and
then said "it is how you hold it," and they do not keep books, and
there is only somebody doing it in front of you until you are doing it
too — and I have written that down, which she would find funny.*

Then, at the bottom, under all of it:

*Exercise 1: five.*
