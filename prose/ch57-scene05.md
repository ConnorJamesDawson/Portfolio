# Chapter 57, Scene 5 — For a Task Well Done

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1650 words.*

---

He came back up at twenty past five with the bundle under his arm and
saw it from the door.

Nineteen sheets. Squared, on the corner of the desk, string still
through the top corner, and it was his own hand at forty feet in bad
light and he would have known it at four times the distance.

He looked at it for perhaps a second and a half.

Then he put the bundle down on the near end of the desk and said:

"It wasn't the tare."

---

"No," said Tsunade.

"I was wrong about that. I was wrong about it for about forty minutes,
which is why it took forty minutes, and I want that in the account
because if I don't say it you'll assume the problem was hard and it
wasn't."

Jiraiya made a small noise.

"The return's right," said Sazare. "Kanzaki's return is correct in every
figure on every page and there is nothing wrong with it at all. The
schedule's wrong.

"It was amended on the fourth, after the return was compiled, and the
amendment shifted two line items out of section three into section five,
which is the correct place for them and somebody did a sensible thing.

"And they didn't date it.

"So there's an amended schedule with no date on the amendment sitting
against a return compiled on the second, and anybody comparing the two
gets a mismatch on page two and no way to see why, and there are four
people in that annexe who have now looked at it and all four have gone
looking in the return, because the return is the thing that moves and
the schedule is the thing that sits still.

"Nobody checks the thing that sits still."

---

He stopped.

"How long did it take you to see that?" said Jiraiya.

"About four minutes."

"You said forty."

"Thirty-six of it was me being wrong about the tare," said Sazare. "The
tare was the obvious answer, and the obvious answer was wrong, and I
went at it for thirty-six minutes before I put it down and started
again, and that is not a good ratio and I'd rather it was on the record
than not."

Jiraiya looked at Tsunade.

Tsunade did not look back at him.

"What?" said Sazare.

"Nothing," said Jiraiya. "Nothing at all, kid. Go on."

---

"There's a second half and it's worse than the first.

"The schedule went out. It went to Kanzaki and it went to the annexe and
it went to two ward stores, and none of the four copies has a date on
the amendment either, and the only reason it's caused one query and not
nine is that the other three haven't reconciled yet.

"So it's not a paperwork problem, it's a distribution problem, and it'll
land again in about three weeks in three places at once, and the fix
isn't this document. The fix is that amendments get dated at the point
of amendment, which is a rule, which is yours."

"Write it," said Tsunade.

"I'll have it by nine."

"Write it and put it to the Corps board over my name and not yours,
because they'll adopt it out of me and they'll argue with it out of you,
and I'd rather have the rule than the credit and so would you."

"Yes."

---

"Well, fuck me," said Tsunade, and picked up her cup. "Four days I've
had that on my desk."

Sazare stopped.

It was not a large thing. It went past in about a second and a half and
nobody in the room reacted to it in any way, and Jiraiya carried straight
on with something about the northern road, and it did not disturb the
surface of the morning by any measurable amount.

*New.*

He stood at the end of the desk with a bundle under his arm and had it
whole, immediately, the way findings came.

*That is the fourth one since I came in at four o'clock. Fourth. I have
been in this room for eleven minutes in total this morning and I have
had four.*

*And in three years and about four hundred visits to this office I have
had none.*

*None. Not one. I have gone through the book — I did it on Sunday night
for a different reason — and there is not a single instance recorded of
that woman swearing in my presence before the twenty-second of the
eighth month, and there are two in the eighth month and both of them
were the yard and both of them were pain.*

*Wednesday night there were about forty and I filed the whole of that as
drink.*

He did not move.

*It is not drink. She has had one cup in ninety minutes and she has not
touched it since Jiraiya sat down.*

*And the change is not that she has started swearing.*

*She has been doing it the entire time. She does it with Shizune —
I have heard it through a door twice and put it down as fatigue. She has
evidently done it with him for forty years. It is her ordinary speech
and I have never once heard it, in four hundred visits, because in four
hundred visits I have been a person she was in a room with.*

*The change is on my side of it.*

*She is not being coarse in front of me.*

*She has stopped assembling.*

---

He sat down, because standing had stopped being available.

*Right. What is the finding.*

*The finding is that at some point between the eleventh of the eleventh
month and this morning I was moved out of one group and into another
one, and nobody told me, and there was no event, and it did not happen
in a sentence.*

*The first group is everybody. Four hundred visits. Full voice, no
swearing, hands flat before a number, the whole apparatus running.*

*The second group has Shizune in it and it has him in it and I think
that may be the entire membership.*

*And the way I found out was that a woman said two words about a
schedule at twenty-five past five in the morning and did not think about
it first.*

*That is the largest thing that has happened to me this week and it took
one and a half seconds and nobody else in this room noticed it.*

*I am going to write it down tonight and I am not going to be able to
make it sound like anything.*

---

"Ruri," said Tsunade.

He looked up.

She had got up out of the chair. He had not registered her doing it,
which was the second time in five days, and she came round the corner of
the desk with the cup still in her left hand.

"That was good work," she said.

And she bent down and kissed him on the cheek.

It was about an inch below the bone and it took perhaps a second, and it
was the same second and the same inch, and she straightened up and went
straight back round the desk and sat down and pulled the compensation
files over.

"Nine o'clock for the rule," she said. "Not five past."

---

He sat there.

*Right.*

He was aware of the room in a way he had not been aware of a room since
Thursday morning, and he was aware that Jiraiya had not said anything,
and that Jiraiya not saying anything was itself an enormous quantity of
information which he was not going to be able to process for some hours.

*I asked her for that on Thursday morning in a kitchen.*

*I said* can I have another one, Hime *, and she did not answer, and she
stood in a doorway with her back to me and went to work, and I have had
four days of not knowing whether the not-answering was the answer.*

*And she has just given it to me for a schedule amendment.*

His pulse was at about a hundred and twenty and he did not take it.

*Now. Be careful. This is the part where I get it wrong.*

*Reading one: she has answered me. Four days late, in her own time, in
her own way, and the delay was the point and the way was the point.*

*Reading two: she has done a thing she does for work. It was good work
and it got a reward and the reward is the coin she has decided this is
paid in, and she has priced it deliberately at* good work *so that it
can be nothing on Monday, and so that neither of us ever has to say a
word about what it actually is.*

*Reading three, and I do not like it and I am going to write it down
anyway: there is a man in this room. She did it in front of Shizune on
Thursday and she has done it in front of him now, and both times there
was a witness, and there has never once been one when we were alone.*

*So it may not be for me at all.*

He put his hands flat on his knees.

*Or it is all three at once, and she does not know either, and that is
the honest position and I am not going to get any further with it.*

*I asked for a thing.*

*I have got the thing.*

*I have got the thing and I cannot read it, and I have spent eleven
years being able to read absolutely everything I have ever been given,
and there is nobody on this earth I can take it to.*

He looked up.

Jiraiya was watching him with an expression that was, on the face of the
best-informed man in four countries, unmistakably and horribly fond.

"Kid," said Jiraiya, "we're going to have a talk, you and me."
