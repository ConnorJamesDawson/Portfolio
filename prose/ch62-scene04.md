# Chapter 62, Scene 4 — Fourteen Books

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, the hall. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1820 words.*

---

Tsunade came down at twenty past ten with Shizune behind her and the Suna
folder under her arm, and stopped at the bottom of the stair.

"That's the box, is it."

"That's the box."

"Naruto."

"Hey, Granny."

"Don't." She came the rest of the way down. "Sakura, the Suna people are
at eleven and I want you in the room for the medical annex, so you have
got forty minutes and you are not spending them on a handcart."

"I've *got* forty minutes—"

"Go and eat something. Take him."

"I'm not hungry," said Naruto, who had said the words *I'm starving*
three times on the hill.

Nobody moved.

*Right.*

*She has just tried to clear the hall and it has not worked, and she is
not going to try again because trying twice is the thing that gets
noticed, and so we are going to have this conversation in front of both
of them.*

*Which is fine.*

*It is fine because on the face of it it is a conversation about
shelving.*

---

"Where's it going?" said Tsunade.

"That's the question."

"Have you got an answer?"

"I've got four and I don't like any of them."

"Give me them in order."

He put his hand flat on the lid.

"One. Upstairs. There's a room with nothing in it and a door that shuts
properly and it's the safest room in the village.

"And it is the wrong answer, and it is the wrong answer for the same
reason the twenty-seventh of the eleventh month was the wrong answer,
which we both said out loud at the time and neither of us has retracted.

"Everything is in one building now. Me, the desk, the drawer, the — " he
did not turn his head " — everything. Every additional thing that goes in
here makes the failure worse and does not make any single thing safer,
and I said that to you on the twenty-seventh and you said *I know* and
then you did it anyway."

"I did," said Tsunade.

"So that's one and it's out."

---

"Two. It goes back where it was."

"It does not go back where it was."

"No. The hasp on that door has never had a staple in it," said Sazare,
"and I have had the staple in my tool roll since the third month and I
did not fit it, and I would like it recorded that the reason I did not
fit it is that there was nothing in that room worth taking and I was
right about that until about the eighth month.

"Three. It goes in the annexe with the Corps material."

"That's a public building with a duty clerk."

"That's a public building with a duty clerk and a register that would
have my name in it four times a week and about eleven people entitled to
be in the room," said Sazare, "and every one of the eleven is fine and
none of them is the problem. The problem is the register."

"Four?"

He did not answer for a second.

"Four is that they go," he said.

The hall was quiet.

"All of it. Fourteen books and the four in use, in a stove, in about an
hour, and there is no box and no problem and I would never have to have
this conversation again with anybody for the rest of my life."

"No," said Tsunade.

"I know it's no. I've had it since the second and I have got it costed
and it is a genuine option and I am putting it in the list because a list
with the unbearable one missing is a list somebody has arranged."

---

Sakura had not moved.

Sazare did not look at her and did not need to; she had been eleven feet
away by the door with a lamp in her hands since Tsunade came down the
stair and she had not put the lamp down and she had not said one word.

*She is not listening to a conversation about shelving.*

*She has got fifty-one sheets and about nine hundred marks and she has
been on that stair once a week for three years, and she has just heard me
say* everything is in one building now *and stop before the end of a
sentence, which I have never done in front of her without her writing it
in a column.*

*And she heard the Hokage of Konohagakure say* I know *and* I did it
anyway *about a decision to move a Corps sergeant into her tower.*

*She has got it.*

*She has probably had it since about the twenty-fifth of the eleventh
month and this is the confirmation and I have just handed it to her
myself in a hall with a handcart in it.*

*And she has not said anything.*

*She is standing there holding a lamp and she is going to go on holding
the lamp until this is over, and then she is going to go to a meeting
about a medical annex, and she is not going to say anything to anybody,
ever, and I am going to have to work out what to do about the fact that
she has decided that on her own without being asked.*

*Fifth.*

---

"Right," said Tsunade. "My turn.

"You've given me four and three of them are real and one of them is you
being honest about a thing you're not going to do. Here's the one you
haven't got.

"It goes to the store."

He looked up.

"The dye-works."

"The dye-works. Double doors, a padlock, a stone floor, brick on four
sides and a yard that a cart comes into.

"And it is a *Corps facility* with your posting attached to it, effective
the first, with a budget line and a schedule, which means there is a
documented reason for you to be in it at any hour of the day or night
and nobody has to invent one.

"And that is the whole of the argument, because it is the same argument
as the book."

"Say it," said Sazare.

"If somebody takes them out of a room over a persimmon tree with no
staple in the hasp, that is a burglary of a private dwelling and it is a
matter for the civil guard and it goes nowhere.

"If somebody takes them out of a Corps facility they have committed a
documented offence against a village programme with a schedule and a
budget line, and I have got a *lever*, and I will use it and everybody
who is thinking about it knows I will use it.

"You cannot stop a thing being taken. You can pick which kind of theft
it is."

---

*She is right and it took her about eleven seconds and I have had it for
a fortnight.*

*And it is the same shape as the drawer — pick the option where the
failure mode is one you can act on — and I have got that written down
from the second of the twelfth in my own hand and I did not apply it to
my own books.*

*Because they are mine.*

*That is the whole of why I did not get there. I have been running the
security on that box as a personal problem and it is not a personal
problem, it is a facility problem, and I could not see it because the
box is the only thing in the world that is mine.*

"Agreed," he said. "And there's a fifth thing and it's mine and it's
better."

"Go on."

"The index doesn't go with them."

---

Tsunade stopped.

"Say that again."

"Fourteen books is a week's work for somebody," said Sazare. "That's the
honest figure. Fourteen books of a man's daily notation, in a hand
nobody else writes, with about two hundred pages a book — you would have
to read it. Properly. There is no way round it.

"Unless you have got the index.

"There are nine pages at the front of that box and I made them myself
over about four years and they are extremely good, and with those nine
pages you do not read fourteen books. You read the index, and then you go
to four entries, and you are done inside an hour."

He took his hand off the lid.

"I have built the most efficient possible route into my own life and I
have put it at the front for the convenience of whoever opens it.

"So the box goes to the store and the nine pages go somewhere else.

"Neither half is worth having. The books without the index are a week of
somebody's life and they would give up. The index without the books is
nine pages of dates and page references about nothing at all."

"Where do the nine pages go?"

"The drawer," said Sazare. "With the fragment and the eleven pages and
the one that isn't to be preserved.

"Which puts them in this building, which is the thing I said was wrong
four minutes ago, and I want that inconsistency on the record and I have
got an answer for it: nine sheets is not a concentration. Nine sheets
goes in a coat. Nine sheets can be burned in about four seconds by
somebody standing next to a lamp, and there is no version of this where
the box can."

---

Tsunade looked at the box for a while.

"Do it," she said. "Today. And, Ruri—"

"I know."

"Say it anyway."

"There's going to be a second index," said Sazare. "In my head. Inside
about a fortnight, because I am not going to be able to stop, and it will
be as good as the paper one and it will be in the only place in the world
that cannot be searched.

"And I have got no procedure for what happens if somebody takes *me*.

"And neither have you, and it is on the governor in the eleventh clause
and I read it four times in the ninth month, and it is the one that goes
*including if somebody takes it off my body*, and I signed it."

Nobody said anything.

"Right," said Tsunade. "Naruto."

"Yeah?"

"Can you take a box to the hospital on that cart without opening it."

Naruto looked at her, and then at Sazare, and then at the box, and his
face did about four things.

"...I mean, *now* I want to open it," he said.

"That's the honest answer," said Sazare, "and it's the one I'd have
given, and you can take it."

"Yeah, all right." Naruto got a hand under the end of the box. "Where is
it, the dye-works place?"

"I'll come."

"You've got—"

"I'll come," said Sazare. "It's fourteen books. It's not going anywhere
on a cart without me on the other end of it, and that is going to be true
for about the next forty years."
