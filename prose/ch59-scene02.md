# Chapter 59, Scene 2 — The Completion Condition

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1680 words.*

---

He sat in the chair for a while after she had finished and did not say
anything, because there was nothing available that would not have been
smaller than what she had just done.

*She has refused to give me a thing I could not weigh.*

*She has done it deliberately, at cost, at four in the morning, having
worked out in about ninety seconds what I would do with it in a
fortnight — and she is right, I would have done exactly that, I would
have gone back and looked at the conditions and I would have found the
load and I would have written it down and never mentioned it.*

*She has anticipated an entry in my own book that I have not made yet.*

*Nobody has ever done that.*

*People give me things all the time. They have been giving me things
since I was fifteen and about half of them do not survive four minutes
of looking at, and I have never once said so, and I have got a whole
column for it.*

*She has looked at a thing she wanted to hand me, found the flaw in the
provenance, and withheld it until she can produce a clean one.*

*That is not a refusal.*

*That is the single most careful thing anybody has ever done about me
and she did it in the dark with no notice.*

"Ruri," said Tsunade. "Say something or go downstairs, because it's
twenty-five past four and I have got a delegation."

"That was better," he said.

"What was?"

"What you just did. It was better than the other one would have been."

Tsunade stood there for a moment.

"Go downstairs," she said.

---

He went downstairs.

He got about four steps and stopped on the half-landing and stood there
in the dark for a considerable time, and there was nobody in the
building and nothing was happening and he did not move.

*Right.*

*Now do the thing you have been not doing for eleven minutes, which is
work out why you said it.*

*Because I did not decide to. I want that established first, because it
is the part I like least. There was no decision. I have got no record of
one. I have been in that room for a quarter of an hour and I built four
clauses and delivered them and then she took hold of my coat and after
that there is a gap and then there is me saying a sentence with my own
death in it.*

*I did not construct it. I have constructed every single thing I have
ever said to that woman and I did not construct that.*

He sat down on the half-landing step.

*So where did it come from.*

---

It took him about four minutes and he did not like where it went.

*Twelfth of the eleventh month. A kitchen in a house nobody lives in, at
about half past seven in the morning, with a woman who had been awake
all night.*

*I asked her eleven questions and she answered them and then I asked
the twelfth and she refused it, and then I asked the one underneath all
of them, which was* what am I supposed to do*, and she stopped me and
gave me the frame instead of a rule.*

*And the frame was: there is no docket. Nobody raises one. There is no
reference and no scope and no schedule of works and no line at the
bottom for a name. Nobody signs it off.*

*And there is no completion condition.*

*It does not have a state called finished. It only exists while both of
you are doing it and it stops the day one of you stops and there is
nothing to hold up at the end and say* there.

*And I sat in that kitchen and told her I did not know how to do a thing
with no end state, and she said everybody does it badly, and that is the
method, and that I would hate it more than anybody she had ever met.*

*Nine days.*

*Nine days of that.*

---

He put his hands on his knees on a cold step in the dark.

*I have never had a day without a number in it.*

*Not one, since I was seven. There has always been a completion
condition on everything. A course takes or it doesn't. A splint holds
the load or it fails. A book is finished when the column has no gaps. An
exercise is two and then it is nine and then it is fifteen.*

*And on the twelfth I was handed the largest thing in my life and told
it has no end state, and I have been living in it for nine days, and I
have handled it in the only way I have ever handled anything.*

*I supplied one.*

*I have written the completion condition myself. Unilaterally. Without
being asked. At the maximum available value, because if you are going to
set a term and nobody has given you a basis for it you take the largest
defensible one, and the largest defensible one is my own death.*

He sat with that for a while.

*So that is what that was.*

*A woman told me nine days ago that this thing has no acceptance
criteria and I have gone away and written some.*

---

*And there is a second half and it is worse.*

*She told me in that office on the eleventh — she said it in about
fourteen words and I have had it in front of me ever since —* you're
doing the thing where you solve it.

*Somebody hands you a problem, you take it away, you find the
constraint, you tell them the constraint is a good constraint and then
you tell them it doesn't bind. It's the finest bit of you and it does
not work here and you cannot see that it doesn't because it has never
not worked.*

*I did it again.*

*Twenty past four this morning. She did not hand me a problem, she
kissed me, and I took the absence of terms away and solved it and
brought her back the answer.*

*That is the same failure. It is exactly the same failure and it is the
one she named, in that room, ten days ago, and I have been carrying it
around as the most useful sentence anybody has ever given me.*

He rubbed his face.

*And it worked.*

---

*That is the part I have got no procedure for at all.*

*It worked. It is the first time. She did not stop me and she did not
tell me I was doing the thing and she did not put a hand out and move
it, and she has done all three of those about forty times in three
years.*

*She let it stand. And then she went and did an entirely separate
careful thing about not giving me a bad reading, which is not what a
person does when they have just been handed something they did not
want.*

*So: the failure mode produced the correct output.*

*Once.*

*And I do not know why, and I cannot tell whether it worked because it
was right or because it was true, and those are not the same thing and I
have got no way to separate them from inside.*

He got up off the step.

*Do not build a rule out of one instance. That is the whole of chapter
one.*

*Write it down as an anomaly and leave it alone and do not go and do it
again tomorrow because it came off once.*

*I am going to want to.*

---

He got to the bottom of the stair and stood in the dark hall for a
moment with his hand on the door.

*There's a third thing and I have only just got it and it is not
comfortable.*

*It came out at four twenty in the morning, from a standing start, with
no construction — which means it was already built.*

*Nothing comes out of me that was not built. Not one sentence in
eighteen years. If it arrived assembled then somebody assembled it and
the only candidate is me and I have no record of doing it.*

*So it has been sitting there, finished, for some time, waiting for a
door to open.*

*How long?*

He stood in the hall and went looking for it and got about as far as the
eleventh of the eleventh month, which was a hill, and eleven pages, and
a woman who took the object off the table and asked for the rest — and
then further back than that, which he had not expected, and then further
still, and he stopped himself.

*Not tonight.*

*That one is going to take an evening and a book and it is going to
change the dates on several things I have already written down, and I am
not doing it on a doorstep at half past four in the morning.*

He opened the door and went out into the cold and stood on the tower
steps.

The village was black with four lights in it. There was frost on the
handrail and a very long way off somebody was moving a cart.

*I have said it out loud.*

*It cannot be un-said. There is no version of the next fifty years in
which that was not said in that room at that hour, and if the whole
thing comes apart in a fortnight it will still have been said, and she
will have heard it, and that is now a permanent feature of the world.*

He put his hands in his pockets.

He discovered, standing on the steps of the Hokage's tower at
half past four in the morning on the twenty-first of the eleventh month,
that he was not frightened by that at all.

Not slightly. Not in any part.

*Right.*

*That is going to have to go in the book as well.*
