# Chapter 56, Scene 3 — Congratulations

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower, outer office. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1730 words.*

---

Sakura came up at about two with a folder under her arm and a bag of
something from the market, and got three steps into the outer office
and stopped.

"Why are you at a desk?"

"Good afternoon."

"Ruri, why are you at a *desk*?"

"Because I'm sitting down," said Sazare, "and there was one, and it was
that or the floor."

Sakura put the bag on Shizune's desk without looking at it.

"I came to congratulate you."

"Thank you."

"I came to congratulate you on Fabrication Research, effective the
first of the twelfth month, which I heard about from Ino who heard
about it from her father, who is on the council, and who apparently
described you in an actual sentence, out loud, at a table, as—"

"I know what he said."

"You know what he said."

"He said it to me on the fourteenth," said Sazare. "He came down to the
fabrication room to say it, which took him eleven minutes off his
afternoon, and I have written it down, and I am not going to repeat it
because it was better than I deserved and repeating it would make it
into a thing I go about saying."

Sakura stood there for a second with her mouth slightly open.

"That is *exactly* the kind of thing," she said, "that I have spent
fifty-one sessions trying to get you to say to people, and you have
just said it to me, unprompted, in a sentence with a subordinate clause
in it, and you are *doing filing*."

---

"I'm assisting Shizune," he said, "until her correspondence is caught
up."

"Why?"

"Because she asked me."

"Ruri."

"That's true."

"I know it's true," said Sakura, "everything you say is true, it's the
most annoying thing about you. It's true and it's about four per cent
of it. *Why* did she ask you?"

He put the pen down.

*Here it is.*

*And there is exactly one available move, because I am not going to lie
to her, and there is no version of the truth that is both short and
survivable, and the third option is the one I have been able to do
since I was about nine and have almost never used.*

"I'm not going to tell you," he said.

Sakura went very quiet.

"Right," she said.

"It isn't mine to tell."

"Right." She pulled the other chair out and sat down on it backwards
with her arms along the back, which was a thing she did, and which he
had seventeen entries on. "You know that's worse, don't you. You know
that *I'm not going to tell you* out of you is about nine times worse
than a lie would have been, because you don't have a face and you've
never had one and the whole village could read you off a wall at forty
feet."

"Yes."

"So?"

"So it's still what I'm doing," said Sazare.

She looked at him for a long moment.

Then she said, "Have you eaten?" and pushed the bag across the desk,
and that was the end of it, and he did not know until about eleven
hours later that it had been the end of it.

---

Shizune came in at twenty past with the temple file and stopped when
she saw who was in the chair.

"Sakura."

"I'm not staying. I'm going up in a minute. I brought her the thing
about the Suna delegation and I'm going to be told off about the
fourth-week returns."

"You are going to be told off about the fourth-week returns."

"I know I am. I've brought them." Sakura swung round on the chair. "But
before I go up — how long have you had him?"

"Two days."

"Two days." Sakura nodded slowly. "Right. Sit down, I'm going to brief
you."

"You are not going to brief me."

"I'm going to brief you," said Sakura, "because I have had fifty-one
sessions with this man on a south stair and nobody briefed *me*, and I
went in there with a sheet and a pen and about three assumptions and
all three of them were wrong inside a quarter of an hour, and I am not
letting it happen to somebody else."

Sazare picked the pen back up.

"Are you writing this down?" said Sakura.

"Yes."

"*Why?*"

"Because it's the best available data," said Sazare, "and you've never
given me any of it in a form I could keep."

---

Sakura put her head in her hands for a second and then took it out
again.

"One," she said. "He does not take breaks. That is not a figure of
speech. He does not get tired, he does not get bored, he does not need
the lavatory, and if you leave him with a stack he will still be there
at the ninth hour with his coat on the back of the chair looking
exactly as he did at the fourth, and you will feel like an animal, and
he will not understand why."

"I've had that one," said Shizune. "Yesterday."

"Two. He answers rhetorical questions. All of them. If you say *why do
I bother* he will tell you why you bother, and it will be a good answer
and it will take about forty seconds and it will be *correct*, and you
will want to put him through a wall."

"Had that one as well."

"Three. If you ask him how he is, he will tell you how he is. Not
politely. He will give you an actual state — he will give you sleep,
appetite, whether his hand's playing up and what he thinks about
something that happened at half past two — and you will realise about
nine seconds in that you asked a question you did not want the answer
to, and there is no way to stop it once it has started, and there is no
polite exit, and you will have to stand there."

"That one," said Shizune, "I would like to hear more about, actually."

"Don't encourage it."

Sazare wrote *3. Ask-how-are-you: no exit. Confirmed by second
observer* in the margin of an acknowledgement.

"Four. He reorganises things."

"Reorders," he said.

"He reorders things," said Sakura, without looking round, "and he is
always right, and that is the worst part, and you will find your own
desk better than you left it and you will not be able to say a single
word about it because it *is* better."

"That happened at forty seconds," said Shizune.

"Forty *seconds*."

"He'd done it while I was at the temple thing."

"He's getting faster," said Sakura darkly.

---

"And five," she said.

She had stopped swinging on the chair.

"Five's the one that actually matters and it's the one nobody tells
you, so listen to this one.

"He will not look at you.

"He isn't being rude. He is not shy and he is not avoiding you and he
is not one of those men. It is that when there is a thing in front of
him, the thing is the whole of the world, and you are a voice coming
out of the side of it. I sat on a stair with him for four hours in the
ninth month and he looked at me twice, and both times were because I'd
asked him a question with a number in it.

"You will be in a room with him for an entire afternoon and he will not
look up. Not at you, not at the window, not at somebody coming through
the door. Nothing gets in.

"And you'll think it's about you, for about a fortnight, and then you'll
work out that it isn't, and then you'll find it restful, and then
about a month after that you'll realise you've started saying things
out loud in front of him that you wouldn't say in front of anybody,
because it's like talking near a cat."

Sakura got up off the chair.

"That's the five. That's everything I've got. Good luck."

---

Sazare had his pen on the page and did not write the fifth one down.

He was aware that Shizune had not said anything, and he was aware that
she had not said anything for slightly too long, and he did not look up
and did not need to, because he could hear where she was standing and
she had not moved since about halfway through it.

*She has had it four times in two days.*

*Yesterday at eleven when Hime came through with the temple people and
I lost the end of a sentence about the dispatch log. Yesterday at four,
on the stair, going down. This morning at half five when Shizune came
up and I was already in the chair. And an hour ago.*

*Four. In two days. And Shizune is standing behind me at this moment
doing exactly what I would be doing, which is holding a completely true
statement about me that stopped being true on Wednesday, and working
out that she cannot say so to the person who made it.*

*She cannot say it to Sakura, because Sakura would ask what changed.*

*She cannot say it to me, because I already know.*

*And she cannot not-say it, because she is going to be in a room with
both of us at some point in the next fortnight and Sakura is the
sharpest observer in this building under thirty.*

He wrote, in the margin, in pencil, in the ordinary notation:

*5. Does not look up. Nothing gets in.*

*— S. Haruno, 13/11, in front of two witnesses, offered as the one
that actually matters.*

*Status: false since 11/11.*

*Nobody in this room is going to say so.*

---

"Right," said Sakura from the door. "I'm going up to be shouted at."

"Sakura," said Shizune.

"Mm?"

"Thank you for the briefing."

There was the smallest pause in the world.

"You're welcome," said Sakura, in the voice of somebody who has heard
something in a sentence and not identified it yet and has decided to
come back to it later, and went up the stairs two at a time.

Shizune stood in the middle of the outer office.

"Ruri," she said.

"Yes."

"Don't write that down."

"I've written it down."

"Of course you have," said Shizune, and went and sat at her own desk,
and did not do any work for about four minutes.
