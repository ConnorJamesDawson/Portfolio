# Chapter 53, Scene 4 — Fabrication Research

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the Hokage's office. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1,881 words.*

---

She was more formal than she had ever been with him and it took him
about nine seconds to work out that this was the point.

*She has gone into the register. That is a room she can be in, and it
has walls and a floor and a set of forms, and there is nothing in it
that has just happened.*

*I am not going to make her come out of it. I would be doing that for
me.*

"A fortnight," he said. "You've been trying for a fortnight."

"The board sat on the ninth. I have had it approved since the tenth and
it is now the eleventh, so that is two days and not a fortnight, and I
have wanted you in this room since about the twenty-eighth of last
month." Tsunade squared a sheet that did not need squaring. "The
difficulty was the sending."

"You couldn't send for me."

"I could send for you at any time. That is precisely the difficulty."
She looked up. "Anything from this desk is an instruction. You would
have come inside the hour, correctly, and you would have been standing
in front of me because you were *told*, and after the fourteenth of last
month I was not prepared to have that be how it happened."

"So you waited."

"I waited, and it was intolerable, and I have been extremely unpleasant
to a number of people who did not deserve it."

Sazare sat in the chair.

"I couldn't come up either," he said. "Not for nothing — I'd have had
to build a reason, and on the third of last month I built one. A
drainage seat for the third-floor stumps. Real problem, good article,
Emiko's raised it four times in two years. Three-quarters finished and
in a drawer in that yard because I couldn't put a false object on a
table."

"...You made a *pretext*."

"I made it in granite, over seven hours, and got the lip thinned at
eleven o'clock and was very pleased with myself."

Tsunade put her hand over her eyes for a moment.

"Twenty-one days," she said, "of two people refusing to manufacture an
excuse."

---

"Right," she said. "Business."

She took a folder from the left-hand stack — the one she had not put on
the floor — and she did it properly, and it took eleven minutes, and he
understood by about the third that she was going to give him every part
of it including the parts that were not to his advantage, because that
was how she did things and because it was somewhere to be.

"The board's recommendation, on the medical crystal work. Ratified,
signed, effective the first of the twelfth month.

"You are off the second floor."

He did not say anything.

"Your post goes to Tsuchida," said Tsunade. "Twenty-three, second year,
one of your six, and he sat in a treatment room for two hours in the
fifth month and did not open his mouth and then asked the only question
in that room that nobody could answer. Kaburagi put him up. Yagi
seconded it. I had nothing to do with it and I want that understood."

"He's good."

"He's better than good and he is going to be dreadful for four months
and then he will be better at that job than you were, and you are going
to have to be somewhere else while it happens."

"That's fair."

"It's not fair, it's correct." She turned a page. "You are also off the
wards."

---

There it was, and it took him about four seconds to find the shape of
it.

"Off the wards entirely?"

"Entirely. No round, no list, no rota, no dressings, no admissions
cover." She said it flatly. "You will be in that building four days a
week and you will not touch a patient."

"...Right."

"Say something."

"I'm working out what it costs," said Sazare, "and I would rather do
that accurately than quickly, and I'd rather do it out loud if you'll
let me."

"Go on."

"Yagi. Emiko. Kaburagi. Ōtaki Sue and the third floor and the six on
the study." He was going through it in order. "Four hundred and eleven
people in a building I have been inside every working day for three
years and two months, and about eleven of them are the only people who
have ever taught me anything about being a person, and every one of
those eleven is on a floor I will not be on."

"Yes."

"And the patients."

"And the patients," said Tsunade.

He sat with that one for rather longer.

*I asked for this.*

*Twenty-ninth of the ninth month, two years ago, at a low table, after
Daichi's graft: "this is not better medicine, it is medicine that does
not require a person to be in the room."*

*I have written that sentence out perhaps nine times since and quoted
it to two committees and I have been extremely pleased with it every
single time.*

*I did not once notice that I am a person, and that it is a room.*

"I've had that sentence on a page for two years," he said. "*Medicine
that doesn't require a person in the room.* I've never costed the
version where the person is me."

"No," said Tsunade. "I costed it in the ninth month and I have been
sitting on it, and I want you to know I nearly took it out of the
recommendation twice."

---

"Then what am I for?"

"Fabrication Research, Medical Corps," said Tsunade. "It is a post that
did not exist on the eighth and exists now, and I want you to hold on
to that, because a post made for one man is a thing that can be
unmade."

"Understood."

"The argument is yours and I have used it verbatim in front of eleven
people and I am not going to pretend otherwise." She read it off the
sheet. "*A recorded stone performs, at present, approximately a third
of what a medic performs on the same injury. It requires no medic in
the room, no training beyond issue instructions, and no additional
establishment. If the class can be produced at scale, the medical corps
of this village is expanded by a third at no increase in
commitment.*"

She put the sheet down.

"Four hundred and eleven staff in that hospital," she said. "A third is
a hundred and thirty-seven medics we do not have, will not get, and
cannot train. That is the whole of the case and it is a good case and
it is why fifteen people who do not like you voted for it."

"They don't have to like me."

"They don't, and about six of them are going to be extremely difficult
about the degradation and they will be right to be, because you have
just told a board that a class of article does a third of a medic's
job and the class currently dies at six days." She looked at him. "You
have four to nine months to give me a stone that lasts, and there is no
version of this post that survives you not solving that."

"I know how to solve it."

"I know you do," said Tsunade. "And it is open at both ends and it does
not go near a patient until it is one-way, and I am saying that in this
room, on the record, on the eleventh of the eleventh month, so that
neither of us can be vague about it in the ninth."

---

"And the approvals," she said.

"Through the board."

"Through *me*." She turned the last page round so he could see it.
"Head of the medical corps signs off every new class of article in
contact with a patient. That is not a thing I have invented for you, it
has been in the establishment since before I took the hat. But there
has never been a post that produced new classes as its entire function,
so in practice—"

"In practice everything I make goes through one desk."

"Everything you make goes through this desk," said Tsunade. "Every
article, every class, every trial, every protocol. Nothing you produce
reaches a person in this village without my name on the bottom of it,
and that is not a courtesy and it is not a compliment and I would like
you to be clear about what it is."

Sazare looked at the sheet.

*It is a leash.*

*I want that in the book tonight in exactly those words, because in
four months I will be describing it to somebody as an approvals
pipeline and it is not an approvals pipeline. It is a man who has been
unsupervised since he was seven being put on a line with one name at
the end of it.*

*Second floor: I was under Yagi, who supervised my medicine. Kanzaki
audits my materials. Kaburagi runs the floor.*

*Nobody has ever supervised* me. *That is the actual finding and it has
been true in four countries and eleven years and it took a board of
fifteen people two days to end it.*

*They have looked at a fabricator who walks summons through wards and
puts unexplained lights in yards and has an operator section in a
protocol, and they have decided, correctly, that what that man needs is
a name on the bottom of everything he does.*

*It is a leash and I would have recommended it myself.*

And underneath that, arriving about four seconds later, unasked and
unwelcome and entirely undeniable:

*And I am relieved.*

He sat very still.

*I have been holding the whole of it on my own since the eleventh month
of my sixteenth year and I did not know I was tired.*

---

"You've gone quiet," said Tsunade.

"I'm agreeing with it."

"That is not a thing you do quietly."

"No." He put his hands flat on his knees. "I was constructing an
objection out of habit and I couldn't find one, and then I found what I
actually felt about it and I would rather not say it out loud in this
room today."

"Is it *leash*?"

"It's leash," said Sazare. "It's also the other one and the other one
is worse."

Tsunade almost smiled and did not quite manage it.

"You'll get four months of hating it," she said, "and then about a year
where you don't notice, and then one afternoon you will put something
in front of me that would have got somebody killed and I will send it
back, and you will be extremely angry with me for a fortnight, and that
is the afternoon the whole arrangement is for."

"That's the argument I'd have made."

"It is the argument you made," said Tsunade. "In this office, in the
fourth month, about a stopping rule you couldn't hold yourself, and I
wrote it down."

She closed the folder.

"Effective the first of the twelfth month," she said. "You've three
weeks on the second floor to hand over to Tsuchida and say whatever
you're going to say to Yagi, and I would use all three of them if I
were you, because you will not get them back."

She squared the folder against the edge of the desk.

"Go home, Ruri."
