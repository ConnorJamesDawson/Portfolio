# Chapter 45, Scene 2 — What Ochiai Had Not Said

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the village library. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1,695 words.*

---

The library at three in the afternoon in the fifth month was the
coolest room in the village and everybody in it knew that, so there
were eleven people at the tables and nine of them were asleep.

Ochiai Sumiko was seventy-nine now and had given up the stool
altogether in the spring, and worked instead from a chair that Handa
had made and delivered without being asked and had refused payment for
in a manner Sazare had found technically educational. The cardigan had
been re-elbowed a second time in a third colour. His hands on the
slips were spotted and the knuckles had gone, and he still wrote the
accession numbers in a copperplate that had not degraded by a single
degree in four years, which Sazare had checked against an old card
once and had never mentioned to anybody.

"I want the beginner's material on chakra shape transformation," said
Sazare.

He had rehearsed exactly that sentence on the way down, because Yagi
had told him what he was about to do and she had been right and the
only way he had found to stop himself doing it was to fix the words in
advance.

Ochiai did not look up.

"How beginner."

"The beginning."

"Mm." He finished the number he was writing. "Define the beginning for
me, because I have four beginnings and three of them will waste your
fortnight."

"The first lecture at the Academy," said Sazare. "First term. Eleven
years old. Before they're allowed to touch a kunai."

Ochiai put the pen down.

He looked at Sazare for what was probably two seconds and felt like
considerably more, over the top of glasses that had been mended at the
hinge with a wire he had wound himself.

"Right," he said. "Sit down at the third table. I'll be some minutes,
because it's on the low shelf at the back and I shall have to get a
chair to sit on halfway."

---

It took him eleven minutes.

He came back with a single thin book with a cloth cover that had been
red once and put it on the table, and did not let go of it.

"Before I give you this," he said, "I am going to say a thing, and I
have been deciding whether to say it for about four years, and I have
decided this afternoon on the walk to the back shelf, so it will not be
elegant."

"Go on."

"I have known since the second month you came in here." Ochiai sat down
opposite, slowly. "You have taken two hundred and ninety books off me.
I know the number because I keep the cards. Monographs. Translated
foreign work. Assay tables. Four things that had not been off the shelf
since the war. A treatise on faceting that I do not believe anybody in
this country has ever read to the end except the two of us."

"And."

"And you have never once, in four years, asked me for anything that a
child gets." He set the book down flat and left his hand on it. "Not
the terms. Not the framework. Not a single one of the eleven books on
that low shelf that everybody in this village had by twelve. I noticed
in the second month, when you asked me for something on lattice
strain and I had to work out from your question that you did not have
the vocabulary underneath it, and I have gone on noticing for four
years, and I have said nothing."

Sazare sat with that.

"Why," he said.

"Because a librarian who tells a man what he has not read," said
Ochiai, "is a librarian who has been keeping a list about him."

The fan at the end of the room went round.

"I have all of your cards," Ochiai said. "Every one. I could tell you
what you were interested in in any given month for four years and I
could tell you within a fortnight when you stopped being interested in
crystal, which by the way I noticed, and did not mention either. That
is an enormous amount to know about a person who has not offered it.
The whole of my job is that I hold that and do not use it. The moment I
lean across this desk and say *you've never read the basics, dear*, I
have told you that I have been watching, and every book you take off me
after that has me in the room with it."

"So you let me go four years."

"I let you go four years," said Ochiai, "and I have thought about it in
the night more than once, and I am still not certain I was right, and
I am telling you now only because you came in and asked for it
yourself, which changes what I am allowed to say."

He took his hand off the book.

"Also," he said, "you are the only adult who has ever asked me for that
shelf and not made a joke about it first, and I would like you to know
that I noticed that too."

---

The book was a school copy.

That was the first thing about it and it was the whole of it. Cloth
cover gone from red to a dull brick along the spine and still red where
the shelf had shaded it. Academy stamp on the inside board, twice, the
second one over a struck-out first. A ruled panel for names, with
seven in it.

Seven children. Different hands, different years, four of them in
pencil and three in ink, one of them in the enormous careful
letters of somebody who had learned to write about eleven weeks
earlier.

The seventh name was a boy called Tarumi Ichirō and he had had it in a
year Sazare could date off the ink, and he had annotated the margins
throughout in a soft pencil.

Sazare read the whole book in an hour and forty minutes, and he read
Tarumi Ichirō's marginalia as well, because he could not do otherwise.

*don't get this* — page four, beside the two definitions.

*OH* — page five, beside the worked example, in a hand that had pressed
hard enough to emboss the reverse.

*ask sensei* — page nine, twice, and then on page eleven, *asked. it's
BOTH at once. that's why its hard* — and then, four pages later, in
the margin beside a diagram of a compressed sphere:

*is this how the 4th did it??*

Sazare put his finger on that line and kept it there for some time.

---

The content was not difficult. That was what he had expected from
Yagi's four sentences and it was still, somehow, worse in the having of
it.

Nature transformation: what the chakra *is*. Five of them, plus the
combinations, plus a paragraph of warnings.

Shape transformation: what the chakra *does*. Its form, its movement,
its size, its density, where it is and how much of it is there. A
diagram of a hand and a compressed sphere and an arrow.

Independent axes. Most techniques use both. Some use only one.

*I use only one,* wrote Sazare, on the second page of a fresh notebook,
sitting at the third table with nine people asleep around him and the
fan going round.

*Everything I have ever done is on the shape axis. Every course, every
seeding, every recall, every straw, the six short faces, the slab on my
own arm.*

*I have never in my life performed a nature transformation and I could
not tell you what one feels like.*

*Which means Shōton is not a nature-transformation kekkei genkai, which
is what every document about me in four countries assumes it is,
because every document about me in four countries was written by
somebody who put "bloodline" in the box where the mechanism goes.*

He looked at that for a while.

Then he wrote the rest of it, and it came out fast, in the bad
fast hand Kurihara had said was not bad.

*Page four. Two definitions. A boy of eleven wrote "don't get this" in
the margin and a page later wrote "OH."*

*I have just done the same two things in the same order on the same two
pages, at eighteen, having killed twenty-two people with one of them.*

*He got it in a page. It took me eleven years, four countries, and a
splint paper from thirty-one years ago that was rejected.*

*I want to be careful about the self-pity here because there is a
version of this that is extremely comfortable and I can feel it
available.*

*The honest version is not that nobody taught me. The honest version is
that nobody taught me AND I never asked, and I never asked because I
have never in my life gone into a room and said "I don't have the first
thing." I asked Ochiai for lattice strain in the second month. Lattice
strain. I could have asked him for the eleven-year-olds' shelf on the
same afternoon and he'd have got it down and said nothing, and I know
that with certainty, because he sat opposite me this afternoon and told
me he had been declining to say it for four years.*

*Four years of a man holding two hundred and ninety cards and not
running the measurement.*

*I ran mine on Thursday in about fifty minutes.*

---

He took the book out on his card.

"Ochiai-san."

"Mm."

"Tarumi Ichirō. Seventh name in the panel."

"I remember him. Loud boy. Terrible handwriting, good questions."
Ochiai stamped the card. "Made chūnin, went into the sealing corps,
died at the Crush along with a great many other people, and his mother
gave the books back to the Academy in the winter after, which is how it
came to be on my low shelf and not on theirs."

Sazare stood with the book in his hand.

"He asked whether it was how the Fourth did it," he said.

"They all ask that," said Ochiai. "It's on the next page but one, and
they never read that far, because the next page but one is about
homework."
