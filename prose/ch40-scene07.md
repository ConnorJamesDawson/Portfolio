# Chapter 40, Scene 7 — Patterns

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,484 words.*

---

He built the table because he built tables. It was not a decision.

He had four facts about her past on the eleventh of the third month and
by the end of that month he had nineteen observations, and they were
not facts about her past at all, they were facts about the shape of the
conversation, and he had been collecting them for fourteen months
without ever once putting them on the same page.

*Ruled sheet. Two columns. Left: subject raised. Right: what happened
next.*

*Her grandfather — she said "careful" and then answered honestly and
then closed it inside ninety seconds. Ninety seconds is the longest.*

*Her teacher — deflected to an anecdote about the Second War in which
nobody is named.*

*The necklace — permitted the object, refused the mechanism. "You may
not take my necklace apart in a bar."*

*The war — she will talk about the fisheries, the logistics, the
politics, the food, the terrain and the paperwork of three wars in any
amount of detail, at length, and with feeling.*

*Any individual person in any of them — four seconds and then a
different subject, and the different subject is always* better *than
the one we were on. It is more interesting. She hands me something
genuinely good every single time and I take it every single time.*

*Fourteen months. Nineteen recorded instances. Success rate: nineteen.*

He looked at the right-hand column for a long while.

*She has never once refused me. That is the finding. Not a single
refusal in fourteen months. There has been no door shut in my face and
no visible reluctance and nothing I could have identified in the
moment, and I have walked away from every single one of those
conversations satisfied.*

*That is not what obstruction looks like when a person is bad at it.*

*That is what it looks like when a person has had thirty years.*

---

The second table was worse and took him four evenings.

He built it out of absences, which was a thing he had learned to do at
fifteen in a rope-walk and had not done to a person before.

*Not present in fourteen months of conversation:*

*Any name of any dead person. Not one. She has referred to
approximately nine deaths in my hearing and has named none of the
people.*

*Her own family, in any form, except her grandfather, and him only as a
name on a village and never as a man in a room.*

*Any account of anything she did before she left this village. She has
told me about the Second War as a set of logistics problems and she has
told me about eleven years of gambling in four countries and there is
a gap of about a decade in the middle that I did not notice until I
went looking.*

*Any illness or injury of her own. She is a medic. Every medic I have
ever met tells you about their own injuries constantly; it is the
occupational disease of the whole trade. Yagi has told me about four.
Fujita told me about a finger for six minutes. She has told me nothing
and she is fifty-three.*

*Anything she is afraid of.*

He put the pencil down on that one.

*She has asked me, directly, three separate times, what I am afraid of.
I answered all three.*

---

The hypothesis assembled itself on the fifth evening and he wrote it
out because he wrote everything out, and it took eleven lines, and when
he had finished it he sat back from the bench and did not like any part
of what he was holding.

*Hypothesis.*

*She is not open. She is fluent, and fluency is what obstruction looks
like from the outside when the person running it is exceptional.*

*The obstruction is specific and it has a shape. It is not "the past"
and it is not "her family." It is* people who died *— named, individual,
hers — and the deflection engages on the person and never on the event.
She will give me a death. She will not give me a name.*

*Therefore: there is at least one person, and I would estimate one or
two rather than many, whose death she has not processed and cannot
approach, and everything else that looks like reticence is scaffolding
built around that one place.*

*Supporting: the necklace, which is eighty years old, which she has
worn every day for approximately forty, which has been repaired twice
by two hands decades apart, and which she has refused to have the mount
taken off by four separate people over thirty years. That is not
sentiment about an object. That is an object which is standing in for
something and which cannot be dismantled because dismantling it would
require handling the thing it stands for.*

*Also supporting: she said, in this room, in the eleventh month, that
she had been told at four separate funerals that her grandfather would
have been proud, and that she had wanted to put the speakers through a
wall. Four funerals. She gave me the number.*

*Estimated confidence: high. Higher than I am comfortable with.*

---

Then he wrote the second half, and the second half took him until the
third hour of the morning.

*And I am not going to do anything with any of it.*

*I want to set out why, because the reasoning is not obvious to me and I
have had to construct it, and I would like to be able to find it again
in a year when I have talked myself out of it.*

*One. This is a diagnostic procedure and I have run it on a person
without her knowledge or consent. I did not decide to. It assembled
itself out of fourteen months of material I had already collected and
it took four evenings, and I am aware that "it assembled itself" is
precisely the defence I would refuse from anybody else in this
building.*

*Two. I have a method. That is the actual danger and it is new. Six
months ago I could not have built this table. I can now build it about
anybody, and I built this one in four evenings out of a habit, and the
habit is a materials habit — I take a thing apart to find the fault,
and it works, and it has never once failed me.*

*She is not a piece of work with a fault in it.*

*Three, and this is the one that decides it. If the hypothesis is
correct, then the thing I have located is the worst place in a person
who has spent thirty years building a life in which nobody goes near
it. And I know exactly what that is worth, because I have one, and
because a man went into mine for twenty-two minutes at my own
invitation and came out with a sentence about a splint, and I was not
right for eleven days.*

*And she has not invited me.*

*Four. I am also almost certainly wrong about the details. I have a
shape and no content. I have no names, no dates, no relationships and
no account of what actually happened, and there are about nine
configurations that fit this evidence and I would be guessing between
them, and the difference between those nine configurations is
everything.*

He read the whole of it back at about the fourth hour.

Then he wrote the last line, and it was the shortest thing on the page:

*She will tell me or she will not.*

---

He did one more thing before he put the notebook away, and he was not
able to account for it at the time and never entirely was afterwards.

He turned to the back, where the page with no unit at the head of the
second column was, and he wrote, underneath the entry about the six
tables and the entry about a Thursday that was ordinary on every
measure at once:

*Fifth item. I have built a nineteen-line diagnostic table on a person
I have never once asked when she was born.*

*Both of those are true on the same night and I would like whoever
reads this to have them in that order.*

*I am going to ask her the birthday. Not as a drill and not on a list.
I have not worked out how yet and I am not going to plan it for nine
days, because everything I have planned for nine days has come out
wrong and everything that has ever worked arrived without a check
running.*

*And I am going to leave the other thing exactly where it is.*

He closed the notebook.

Outside, the persimmon was bare and the first grey was coming up behind
it, and he had a shift at six, and he got about two hours.
