# Chapter 71, Scene 8 — The Defense

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the residence bath. Tsunade, age
fifty-three. POV: Tsunade. ~1040 words.*

---

The shout went through the bath, off the cedar, out the door she
had left ajar for the steam, and — physics being no respecter of
anything — down two floors of a tower built with excellent
acoustics by paranoid ancestors.

In the small office below, Shizune looked up from the evening
folder. She consulted the diary, which said *Thursday — no
entries*. She consulted the ceiling, which said nothing further.
She wrote, in the margin of nothing, *not minuted*, moved the
Hokage's first Friday appointment to ten, made a note to change
the morning tea to the calming blend, and went back to the
folder, because three years had taught her exactly which fires
were hers and this one had been laid, fuel by fuel, for three
months, by both arsonists personally.

Two floors up, the accused turned around in the water to face
the court.

She watched him do it, and even through the full magnificent
head of her own outrage — and it was magnificent; forty years
of that book's collected offences against her had come up the
stairs at once — she registered, with the part of her that
never stopped charting him: *no blackout. Pink to the ears,
pulse at the throat, the noise taking him — and he is not
going down. He is going to stand here, in a bathtub, at
ground zero, and MOUNT A DEFENSE.*

"Yes," said Sazare.

Just that, first. The plain guilty plea, level, while the
steam curled between them. Then the defense, in order, on
his fingers, because of course on his fingers, the court
could have predicted the fingers:

"Finding one. It helped — didn't it, Hime?"

And he let that stand. Just stood in it, chin up, blush
blazing, and let the evidence report itself: three months of
it, the bath she had been given out of a clear sky, the
hair, the hands, the trail, the campaign, the count — the
whole restored account of them, every entry of which she
had received, catalogued, and privately called the finest
season of her late and unexpected springtime, standing in
the witness box in its slate-blue bespoke and declining,
absolutely, to testify for the prosecution.

Her mouth opened. Her mouth closed.

"Finding two." He pressed on, second finger, into the
silence he had no right to have won that easily. "The use
was not — the book was not read for its market purpose. I
do not think I have ever masturbated in my life, so it was
not for that."

Delivered exactly as he delivered kiln temperatures. She
made a sound that had intended to be a word and became,
against her entire will and forty years of statecraft,
the first crack in the courtroom wall — because there was
no defending against it, the flat clinical *audacity* of
the sentence, the complete absence of shame in a man
neck-deep in a bathtub testifying to his own — she filed
it. She had to. Register, new page, verbatim, under: *the
things this man says levelly could kill a council at
forty paces.*

"Finding three. It was read as literature is read at my
bench: as an object, for its load paths. I have two pages
of notes on what is wrong with the thing — structural,
anatomical, and in one case a plumbing cost the author
should be professionally embarrassed by. Fourteen
numbered faults. Four were accepted by—" he caught it,
one half-second, the first hesitation of the entire
defense— "by the author. For the next edition."

*Filed also. Flagged. We will be returning to THAT.*

"And finding four."

He stopped. The register, which had carried findings one
through three at full grade, stood at the edge of finding
four visibly, the way she had watched him stand at the
edge of deep water twice now — and then, because he was
who he was, because the answer to every deep end of his
entire documented life was the same single syllable, he
went in.

"I could read every page of it as an object. Every page
but one. There is one scene — chapter twelve, the bath at
the mountain inn — where the method failed. I attempted
it four times. The fourth attempt was in December, before
any of — before the towel. And the reason the method
failed, Hime, the reason a man who has read wound charts
across a decade could not get through six pages of
mediocre prose, is that I could not keep it an object. I
kept—" the blush had gone somewhere beyond copper now,
into a register the Hime line's dye department also had
no name for, and the voice held, and the voice held, and
the voice held— "I imagined us. In that. You and me, in
the bath, in the steam, in — every time, from the first
attempt, before I had any standing to imagine anything,
and I could not read past it, and I have never told
anybody that, and the incident reports on my own composure
have been wrong for three months, because they all assume
the white noise is about your body, and it was never only
that. It was that I had already been in this exact room
with you four times before you ever said *budge up*, and
no instrument I own survives the interference pattern of
a memory that hasn't happened yet meeting the real thing
arriving."

The steam rose. The lamps burned at the raking angle.

The Hokage of the Land of Fire stood hip-deep in the
warm water of her own bathroom, in receipt of the single
most catastrophically honest declaration in the history
of a courtship conducted entirely in catastrophically
honest declarations, her prosecution in ruins around her
ankles, her fury somewhere back down the hall with the
steam, her face — she could feel it — doing something it
had not done since she was considerably younger than her
own apprentice, and managed, at last, from the wreckage,
in a voice that came out half its intended size:

"...Another deep-end dive. You absolute — you couldn't
have led with findings one through three and STOPPED—"

"The record gets the whole of it," said Sazare, hoarse,
pink, upright, eighteen years old and entirely unbowed,
"or the record is worth nothing. Your rule, Hime. I
learned it in this room."
