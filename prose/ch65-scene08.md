# Chapter 65, Scene 8 — A Holiday

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1920 words.*

---

He did it on the first of the first month at about half past eight in the
morning, because she was working, because she was always working, and
because Shizune was at the end of the desk with the year-end returns and
he had decided at some point on the walk up that having a witness was
correct.

He put it on the desk.

Tsunade looked at it.

She had been mid-sentence about the returns and she stopped, and the pen
went down, and she did not touch the paper.

"Right," she said.

"I'd like to use it."

"...Now?"

"Now. This morning. In front of Shizune, and I want her here, and I will
say why at the end if you want it."

Tsunade sat back.

At the end of the desk, Shizune had put her own pen down very quietly and
had gone completely still in the way of somebody who has decided that
leaving the room would be worse.

"Go on, then," said Tsunade.

---

*Right.*

*Say it plainly and do not build a run-up, because a run-up is a lever
and I have spent eleven days working out how not to have one.*

"I want a day," said Sazare.

There was a silence of a particular quality.

"A what?"

"A day. One. You and me, somewhere that is not this building, with
nothing on the table — no docket, no article, no patient, no study,
nothing anybody has to sign and nothing either of us has to think about.

"We get up. We go somewhere. We come back in the evening.

"That's it. That's the whole of the ask and there is not a second part to
it."

---

Tsunade did not say anything for about four seconds.

"You have got," she said, "a piece of paper on that desk with my name at
the bottom of it and no reference number on it, which obliges the Hokage
of Konohagakure to do one thing of your choosing, unconditionally,
without asking why and without the right to refuse.

"You have carried it for eleven days.

"There is not one person in four countries who would not know what to do
with that. There are men on that council who would sell a child for it.

"And you want a *day out*."

"Yes."

"Ruri—"

"I know what it's worth," said Sazare. "I said so in a corridor on the
twentieth about eleven seconds after you handed it to me and I said it
was worth more than this building and I have not revised the figure."

"Then *use* it."

"I'm using it."

---

"On a—" She got up. "Do you want the paper? Say a thing. The Kiri claim.
Kudō has been at that for two years and there is a determination board
that is not going to move in your lifetime, and I could move it in a
morning.

"Do you want the *post* fixed? There is a Corps board chaired by a man
who voted against you and I could have him off it by Thursday and never
have to explain myself.

"Do you want out of the correspondence?"

Sazare did not move.

"That one," said Tsunade. "I saw that one land. That is a thing you have
been carrying since the eleventh of the twelfth month and I could take it
off you with that piece of paper before lunch and neither Ibiki nor
Jiraiya could say one word about it."

"Yes," said Sazare. "You could."

"Then—"

"And in about four years I would be a man who got out of an arrangement
he had agreed to in writing, by using a debt, on a woman who could not
refuse," he said. "And I would have to live in the same head as that for
the rest of my life, and so would you, and neither of us would ever
mention it."

---

Tsunade stood behind the desk.

"Give me the reasoning," she said. "The real one. Not the answer, the
*reasoning*, because you have got one and you have been sitting on it and
I can see the shape of it from here."

*Right.*

*Give her the honest half. She will find the other one anyway and it will
be worse coming from her.*

"It's a contract," said Sazare.

Shizune's head came up.

"Tenth month, in a yard," he said. "I asked Katsuyu how the first
contract came about and then I thought about signing one, and I decided
against it, and the reason was eleven words and I have said them out
loud to two mountains and a toad and to you.

"*If she is contracted, she comes because she is contracted, and then I
would never be able to tell the difference again.*

"That is what is on that desk. It has got one clause in it and it runs
the other way, and I have been carrying it for eleven days and I only got
it on the thirty-first.

"The moment I ask you for something you would not have given me, whatever
you do about it is a thing you were obliged to do. And I will never be
able to tell.

"And if I do not use it, that is worse, because then it sits there for
forty years and every single thing you ever do for me for the rest of
both our lives is done by a woman who owes me an unconditional favour.
You would forget it inside a year. I have got four hundred pages that say
I would not."

"So you burn it."

"Burning it is me deciding afterwards that it did not count," said
Sazare, "and you said the terms out loud on the Wednesday in front of a
witness specifically so that *you* could not do that, and I am not going
to do it on your behalf on the thirty-first of the twelfth month."

---

"So," he said.

He put his hand flat on the desk about four inches from the paper.

"The corruption is not the paper. It's the *gap* — between what I ask for
and what you would have done anyway. Everything in that gap is a thing I
have taken and cannot verify.

"So close the gap.

"Spend it on something you would have given for nothing. Then there is no
gap. Then the paper is worth exactly nothing the instant it is used and
it stops existing and nobody has taken anything off anybody.

"And it has got to be a thing I would never have asked for on my own,
because if I would have asked for it then I do not need the paper and it
stays in my coat and we are back where we started."

He took his hand off the desk.

"There is one of those," he said. "There is exactly one and I found it at
about ten past nine on the thirty-first and it took me eleven days to get
there.

"Three years. Four hundred visits up this hill. Six hundred hours in this
office and eleven weeks of the fourth hour and twenty-four nights on the
second floor.

"And there has not been one day that was not about a thing.

"Not one. Not ever. An article, a docket, a study, a patient, a council, a
report, a fight or a wound, every single time, including the good ones,
*especially* the good ones — the hill was about eleven pages, the
twenty-first was about a question I had built for four days, the second
of the twelfth was about nought point one one grams.

"I have never once been anywhere with you for no reason.

"And I would never have asked. Not in forty years. It would not have
occurred to me that it was a thing a person could ask for, in exactly the
way it did not occur to me for two years and three months that a chair
was a thing a person could have in a room."

---

Nobody said anything.

Tsunade stood behind her own desk on the first morning of the year with
her hands at her sides and did not say anything at all for about eleven
seconds, and Sazare stood on the other side of it and watched a face that
he had four hundred pages on stop being managed.

"You absolute—" she said.

And then did not finish it.

"I'd like it on the record," said Sazare, "that half of it is a
manoeuvre. It is in my book in ink from last night and it says so. It is
the only way I found to discharge an instrument that should never have
existed without either using it against you or carrying it between us for
forty years, and I built it as a solution.

"And it is also the thing I want.

"I have tried to separate those since half past nine last night and they
do not come apart, and I have decided that a thing being useful does not
stop it being true."

---

"You could have anything," said Tsunade.

"I have got everything I want," said Sazare. "That's the whole of the
problem. That is why it took me eleven days — I kept looking for a thing
to spend it on and there isn't one, because I have got a room the size of
a barn and four hundred impossible things on a shelf and a slug who comes
on Fridays and a man who is going to break me in a field four mornings a
week from the second week of this month.

"And I have got you.

"There is nothing left over. I went looking for a want and I have not got
one."

---

At the end of the desk, quite distinctly, Shizune made a noise.

It was not a large noise. It was the noise a person makes when something
has gone down the wrong way, and it was followed by about a second and a
half of her attempting to convert it into a cough, and it did not
convert.

Both of them looked at her.

"Sorry," said Shizune. "Sorry. No, that was — carry on."

"Shizune."

"It's the *year-end returns*, I've been at them since six—"

"*Shizune.*"

"That was disgusting," said Shizune. "I want that minuted. I have taken
the minute of four rooms in this building that I would rather did not
exist and I have never once had to write down anything as revolting as
what has just happened at half past eight in the morning on the first of
the first month, and I am going to have to go and stand somewhere."

She got up.

She got to the door and stopped with her hand on it.

"Take the day," she said, without turning round. "I'll do the diary. It
will take me about eleven minutes and I have been waiting three years to
do it and I am not going to explain that either."

And went out.

---

Tsunade looked at the door for a moment.

Then she picked up the piece of paper off her own desk, and read her own
four lines on it, and put it back down.

"Where?" she said.

"I don't know," said Sazare. "That's yours. I have got no idea where
anybody goes and I have never been anywhere, and if I picked it I would
research it for a fortnight and produce a schedule.

"So you pick. And do not tell me, because if you tell me I will look it
up."
