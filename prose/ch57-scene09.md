# Chapter 57, Scene 9 — Be There

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower roof. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1800 words.*

---

"What's your hold?" said Jiraiya.

"Sorry?"

"The count. Slugs teach capacity, so there'll be a count. What is it?"

"Fifteen."

Jiraiya turned round.

"Fifteen what?"

"It's a sphere," said Sazare. "You gather and you hold it in one and you
count. I was at two for seventeen weeks. Nine at the end of the ninth
month, eleven in the tenth, fifteen on the eleventh of this month, and
past about eleven it stops feeling like holding and starts feeling like
something else and I have not been able to write down what."

"Fifteen minutes."

"Yes."

Jiraiya sat down on the parapet very slowly.

"When did you last do it?"

And there it was.

"The eleventh," said Sazare.

"That's five days."

"Yes."

"You've just told me you went from eleven to fifteen in five weeks and
then stopped for five days."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"I forgot," said Sazare.

---

Jiraiya did not say anything at all for about twenty seconds.

When he did, the voice had changed, and it had not got louder.

"Right," he said. "Now I'm going to be unpleasant. I told you I would.
Sit there."

"You don't have to warn me."

"I do, because you're going to want to take it apart while I'm talking
and I want you listening instead."

He put his hands on his knees.

"You've stopped."

"It's five days."

"It's three days of an exercise you have done every day since you were
ten," said Jiraiya, "and it's twelve days on a slug, and it is — how
long since you asked the question? Not did the work. Asked the
question. The actual one. *How does it hold, why does it go.*"

Sazare sat on a cold roof.

"Tuesday," he said.

"The eleventh."

"Yes."

"So five days on all three, and you did not notice any of the three, and
you found out about two of them by accident."

"I found out about the exercise on Saturday night at nine o'clock when I
got to the bottom of a page," said Sazare. "The other two are this
morning. From you."

"And how do you feel?"

"...Fine."

"Say the true one."

"I've been happy," said Sazare. "Three days. Four. I have got no prior
entry for that word in fourteen books and I have looked, and I cannot
tell whether it's a recovery or the start of something going badly
wrong, because they look identical from inside, and there is nobody I
can ask."

---

"I've seen her do it," said Jiraiya.

Sazare looked up.

"Not the same. I want to be careful, because you'll take the analogy and
run it too far and it doesn't hold all the way. But the mechanism's the
same and I have watched it at four feet.

"Everybody in this village thinks Tsunade broke and left. That's the
story — Dan died, she saw the blood, she couldn't be a medic any more,
she walked out and gambled for twenty years.

"That is not what happened.

"What happened is that she went on working. Months of it. She was in
that hospital every day and she was excellent and nobody could have
faulted a single thing she did, and somewhere in about the second week
of it she stopped *asking*.

"That's all. She kept doing and she stopped asking. She'd had a
question since she was nineteen — she was going to fix the thing that
killed her brother, and then the thing that killed Dan — and it went
out, quietly, in about a fortnight, and there was no announcement and
nobody noticed and I do not think she noticed.

"By the time any of us could name it, it was two years gone and she was
in Tanzaku with a debt."

He looked out at the roofs.

"It did not start with a decision, kid. Nothing like that ever does.

"It started with about three days."

---

"So you're telling me to be careful of her."

"No," said Jiraiya.

"Then—"

"*No.* Get that out of your head right now, because it's the obvious
reading and it is completely wrong and I'd rather be rude than let you
walk off with it."

He turned round.

"I am delighted about her. I want that said out loud on this roof at six
in the morning. That woman has been alone inside her own head since I
was about twenty-five and I have watched it and I could not do a single
thing about it, and I got here on Sunday night and found out that
somebody had, and I have been in an extremely good mood ever since and
it has been very hard to conceal.

"You are the best thing that has happened to her in thirty years and I
am not going to say that twice.

"What I'm telling you is about *you*."

---

"You were made in the dark," said Jiraiya.

"I don't—"

"Eleven years on your own in a series of rooms with nobody watching. No
teacher, no clan, no adult, nothing. Everything you are came out of
that, and it is why you are the only person in this village who could
have written those nineteen pages, because you had to derive every
single thing yourself from first principles like a lunatic, and nobody
ever told you what was impossible.

"And you have just been given people.

"You've got her, and you've got Shizune stood between you and two
procedures, and you've got a slug who comes for free, and you've got me
now whether you like it or not.

"That's better, kid. I want to be clear. That is *better* and I am not
being wise about the loneliness having been valuable, because it wasn't,
it was just what there was.

"But you have never in your life had to find out whether you're anything
with the light on.

"And you're going to. Starting about now. And nobody can do that part
for you and there is no procedure and I have not got one either."

---

Sazare sat with it.

*He is right and it is the fourth correct unpleasant thing anybody has
said to me this week and the third of them was a woman in a kitchen.*

*And there is no answer available. There is no answer available because
he has not asked a question — he has described a state and told me it is
mine, and the only thing that could possibly settle it is time, and I
have never once in my life been able to wait for anything.*

*Right.*

*I will do the exercise tonight.*

*And I will go up the hill on Friday and I will not be able to give her
a reason, because the true one is that I forgot, and I am going to have
to say that out loud to something that has never asked me for anything.*

He got up.

And then, because the sky had started and because he was not going to
get another morning like it, he said the other thing.

"Can I check something with you."

"Go on."

"The mark on her forehead."

Jiraiya's head came round.

---

"It's a seal," said Sazare. "I want to say what I've got and you tell me
if I'm wrong, and I'm not asking you to add anything, and if you say
nothing at all I'll take that as the answer and never raise it again.

"It's a store. Boundary, closing stroke, density sat toward the centre
and not the edge. It's got a way in and it hasn't got a way out.

"It's been open a long time — decades, and I'd defend decades. And it
isn't drawn on, because a store that gets used doesn't sit at that
density.

"So it's one demand, and it's fixed, and it's enormous, and she knows
what it is, because you cannot size a store without knowing the load.

"And it's not for a patient. Nobody spends thirty years of their own
capacity on a patient and she's a doctor and she knows that better than
I do.

"It's for her.

"She decided a long time ago that there would be one day when the thing
in front of her was too big, and that on that day she would be able to
pay for it, and she has been putting it aside ever since, in her own
body, where nobody can take it off her.

"She has spent thirty years getting ready to spend herself."

---

Jiraiya sat on the parapet with the light coming up behind him and did
not say anything for a long time.

"Who told you that?" he said.

"Nobody."

"Shizune—"

"Shizune gave me the name and refused the rest and told me that asking
Hime would be the most expensive question available to me, and she was
right, and I'm not going to ask her." He stood on the roof. "I did the
rest on Thursday morning in a kitchen in about four minutes off the
density distribution.

"You're the only other person who knows. So you're the only place I can
check it."

"You've got it right," said Jiraiya.

"All of it?"

"All of it. Word for word. And there are three people alive who know
that and you have just made four, and you got there off looking at a
woman's forehead for four minutes while she was asleep."

He did not say it unkindly.

Then he stood up.

"Kid. I want one thing off you and then I'll leave you alone, and it's
not a favour and you can say no."

"Say it."

"Be there," said Jiraiya.

---

"When she spends it."

He picked the nineteen pages up off the parapet and took the stone off
them and put it back where it had been.

"I've known about that seal for thirty years. I have known for thirty
years that one day that woman is going to open it, and that it will be
the last thing she does, and that when it happens I will almost
certainly be four hundred miles away or dead, because that is the shape
of my life and I chose it.

"I'm not handing you anything I'm still carrying. I want that clear, and
I'll only say it once — I shot my shot forty-one years ago and I'm fine.

"This is a watch. That's all. It's a watch I have kept on my own since I
was about twenty-four and I cannot keep it any more, and you turn up at
four o'clock in the morning."

He put the pages under his arm.

"So be there," said Jiraiya. "That's the whole of it. Don't stop it —
you won't, and you shouldn't, and she'd never forgive either of us.

"Just don't let her do it on her own."
