# Chapter 47, Scene 6 — Twelve Roses and Nobody

*Act 4, part D. Konoha. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,600
words.*

---

He sent word up the same evening, properly, through Shizune, in four
lines that said nothing except that the thing the Hokage had authorised
had happened and that he would like twenty minutes at her convenience.

The reply came back on the Friday morning.

*She left for the capital on Wednesday night. Border commission — the
Land of Rivers thing. Back the seventeenth at the earliest and I would
not put money on the seventeenth. Is it a fire? If it is a fire tell me
now and I will send. — S.*

He stood in the second-floor corridor with the slip in his hand for
about eleven seconds.

*Is it a fire.*

*No. Nobody is bleeding and nothing is on the table and there is not
one thing in it that will be different on the seventeenth than it is
today.*

He wrote *not a fire* on the back and sent it down.

Then he went and did a shift, and another one, and it was the ninth of
the eighth month and she would be back on the seventeenth at the
earliest and he had twelve roses in a box under his bed.

---

He charted them, because charting them was a thing he could do.

*Rose 1. Day 0: 11.14 g. Day 1: 10.02. Day 2: 8.87. Day 3: 7.9.*

*Drying at the ordinary rate for cut green material of this section. No
anomaly. It is a rose and it is doing what roses do.*

*Day 4: 7.11. Day 6: 6.02. Day 9: 5.4 and slowing.*

*Colour: as expected. The margin fault on the second leaf went first
and the whole leaf followed it, which is the ordinary sequence.*

*The other thing: unchanged.*

He wrote that on day one and wrote it again on day three and again on
day six, and on day nine he sat and looked at the page and made himself
write out the whole of what he meant by it, because *the other thing*
was a phrase and not an observation and he had been letting himself
use it for a week.

*Held rose 1 with eyes shut alongside four cut roses of the same
variety bought from the stall at the west end of the market for eleven
yen. Repeated eleven times with the four decoys reshuffled by Emiko,
who did not ask what I was doing, which I have entered in the ledger
because it is remarkable.*

*Eleven out of eleven.*

*I can find it blind. It is not smell — I did it holding my breath. It
is not temperature and it is not weight and it is not texture, and the
dead one is as loud as the live one, and it has been nine days and it
has not moved.*

*Mine goes at six days.*

*His does not go at nine years.*

---

He tried to tell people and could not, and the not being able to was
the shape of the whole nine days.

Kanzaki got half. She got the provenance half, which was hers — the
weighing, the sack scale, sixty-eight point nine before and after, a
hundred and thirty-four grams on a table — and she took it standing up
and then sat down on the stool and said nothing for about a minute and
then said, "So it's not just you," and Sazare said, "It's not just me,"
and she said, "Right," and picked up her pen and wrote *unknown* in the
new column eleven more times without another word.

She did not get the second half, because the second half was a sentence
about kekkei genkai and he had been told one question, one person, no
paper, and he was not going to hand a quartermaster a thing he had been
forbidden to write down.

Yagi got the medical half, which was the six days against nine years,
and Yagi said *then find out what he's doing to it that you aren't* and
went to her round, and that was correct and useful and about a fifth of
the size of the thing.

Kakashi could not have any of it. He had been extremely clear about
that at the gate — *whatever it is, it's between you and the Hokage and
I've done my part* — and Sazare had watched him mean it.

Handa would have listened for two hours out of pure friendship and been
bored inside four minutes and not said so.

Ashida would have written it in a folder.

Saeko was at the sealing office on Tuesdays and Thursdays and would be
in the yard for four days in the seventh month, and it was the eighth
month.

---

He wrote the whole of it out on the eleventh, in one go, at the low
table, and it took four hours and it was eleven pages and it was the
best technical writing he had done since a letter in the third month
that had gone into a fishmonger's cart under a till board.

Then he read it back and could not do anything with it.

*Eleven pages. Nobody to give it to until the seventeenth at the
earliest and Shizune would not put money on the seventeenth.*

*This is the second time.*

*The third month: eleven hundred words in a drawer with nobody to send
them to, and a man four hundred miles away who would simply have been
interested, and I priced the whole business out in two columns and
found the cost side reading zero and worked out that a cost that
appears to be zero is a cost that has not been entered yet.*

*I did not send it because I could not send it.*

*I sent it in the fifth month because Ibiki told me to, under an
arrangement, with a fee named in advance and agreed to in writing, and
I have not had a word back and I am not owed one until the ninth month.*

*And here I am again, in the eighth, with eleven pages and one person
in the world who can take the whole of it, and she is at the capital
arguing about a border.*

He looked at that for a while and then wrote the honest half.

*I want to be accurate about what this is, because there is a version
where it is loneliness and that version is true and is also not the
useful one.*

*The useful one is: I have built a life in which exactly one person can
hold the whole of a thing I do, and that person is the Hokage of a
village at war-adjacent peace, and her availability is not a function
of anything I can affect.*

*That is not a fact about her. It is a fact about the number one.*

*Sakura can hold the human half and is fifteen. Kanzaki can hold the
material half and will not take the rest. Yagi holds the clinical half.
Kurihara can hold the sealwright half and has arranged her next two
months so that she does not have to be in a room with me except by
contract, and I did that, in a yard, on the twenty-second of the fifth
month, by answering a direct question with the word "no."*

*Four halves and no whole.*

*I wrote in the third month that I would like there to be more than
two. It is now the eighth month and the number has gone down.*

---

He went to the invasion streets on the fifteenth, on a rest day,
because Tenzō had left him with a question and it was the only end of
it he could pull on alone.

*What's in the ground?*

They had rebuilt most of it. The admissions frontage was two years
finished and the shutters on the east side had gone up in the spring
and there were children in the lane where the staggered blocks had
been, playing a game with a stone and four chalked squares, and nobody
looked at him twice.

He crouched at the corner where the flat plate under the admissions
window had failed, and put his palm flat on the road, and asked for
about a gram.

It came up at about the fourth attempt, gritty, wrong, hard work.

He did it again at the top of the lane where the camber ran fast and it
came up cleaner.

He did it eleven times across four hundred yards and wrote every one of
them down and got a map of nothing he could interpret, except that the
worst readings were where the fighting had been worst, and the best
were under the old cedars at the wall, which had been there before the
village and had not been touched.

*Old growth easy. Burnt ground dragging.*

*He said that about a forest four hundred miles from here and I have
just measured it in a lane with a chalk game going on nine feet away.*

*So it is in the ground and it is in the air and it is in a rose on a
table in my room and it is in every article I have made since I was
seven, and it is thicker in some places than in others, and the places
where it is thin are the places where something has been destroyed.*

*I do not have a word for it.*

*I have eleven pages and no word and nobody until the seventeenth.*

He walked home past the old cedars.

*Two days.*

*I can wait two days.*
