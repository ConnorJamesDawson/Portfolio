# Chapter 46, Scene 2 — The Tap Test That Did Not Stop

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard, after hours. Sazare
Ruri, age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,500 words.*

---

He knew what it was before he knew what it meant, which was unusual and
was entirely because of the trade.

Every craftsman who has ever worked cast or drawn stock tests it by
tapping. You hold the article loosely, at a node if you know where the
node is, and you strike it with something hard and small, and you
listen. Sound stock rings and goes on ringing for a count. Cracked
stock gives you a shorter, deader thing with no tail on it, and you can
hear a fault in a casting that you cannot see and could not find with
any instrument in the workshop, and the ear that does it takes about
four years to build and never leaves you.

He had tapped perhaps forty thousand articles since he was seven.

What the rod was doing, on the twenty-eighth night, at half past
midnight, with his palm flat over the seat end and the rain coming off
the awning, was a tap test.

It was simply not stopping.

---

It was very quiet.

That was the first thing he wrote down and he wrote it four times in
different words over the following week trying to be accurate about it,
because the temptation to make it louder in the record than it had been
in the yard was enormous and he could feel it operating.

*Barely audible. At the edge. I could not hear it standing up and I
could hear it with my head at about nine inches. It is under the rain
and it is under the awning drip and it is not under Kanzaki's pigeons,
which is the only reason I have any measurement of it at all, because
the pigeons stop at dark.*

*It is not a ring and it is not a hum. It is a struck note that does
not decay. Pitch: somewhere around the top of a man's speaking voice,
which I have checked against my own by humming at it, alone, in a yard,
at one in the morning, and which I am recording because it happened.*

He took his hand off.

It stopped, immediately, with no tail on it at all — which was itself
a piece of information and he caught it — and the yard was just rain.

He put his hand back.

It came up.

He did that eleven times.

---

Then he stopped doing it eleven times and started listening properly,
and that was where the evening turned over.

*It is not one note.*

He sat on the yard stone with his head down at nine inches and his palm
on the seat and did nothing else for about twenty minutes, and by the
end of it he had four.

*Four voices. Not harmonics — I know what harmonics sound like off a
bell and off a saw blade under tension and these are not that, they are
not in any ratio I can name and two of them are close enough together
to beat against each other about twice a second.*

*Four branches in the architecture, counted under a triple lens on the
fifteenth.*

*Four voices.*

He put a hand out for the notebook without lifting his head.

*The article is telling me its own structure.*

*Not how much I am putting in. WHAT IT IS DOING WITH IT. The pitch of a
run is a function of the run — its length, its width, its seat, what is
in it — and there is a way at the third branch that I have measured
seven times with a good triple and could not resolve against my own
error, and it is sitting in the air of this yard at a frequency I can
hum.*

*I have been trying to see the ways.*

*I can hear them.*

---

He did not sleep. There was a shift at six and he took it and was
competent throughout and remembered almost none of it, which had
happened once before in the fifth month and which Yagi noticed both
times and mentioned neither.

That night he built the measurement.

It was crude and he knew it was crude and he wrote *crude* at the top
of it. He had no instrument for pitch, so he used the only calibrated
tone source in the village that he could get at after ten at night,
which was the three-note bell on the tower that rang the hours and had
rung them, according to Ochiai, since well before the war.

He learned the three notes. He hummed the rod against them.

*Method: hold the seat, take the note, hum it, walk to the tower
approach, wait for the hour, compare. Nine minutes per reading.
Ridiculous. Works.*

*Reading 1, 108 hours: about a tone and a half above the tower's
middle.*

Then he went back and got out the log, and did the thing he had been
unable to do for a month, which was to put a number against the work.

Because the pitch was a function of the way.

And the way had been getting wider, invisibly, at a rate below his
measurement error, for a hundred and eight hours.

*If the pitch drops as the ways widen, then every hour I have put into
this article since the sixth of the month is recorded in the pitch, and
I have been sitting in a wet yard writing "nothing to report" against
work that was reporting itself the entire time and had no voice to do
it with until tonight.*

He read that back and struck out the last clause and wrote it again
properly, because the first version had been about him.

*— which had no voice until tonight because the ways were not yet wide
enough to carry the amount of me it takes to make a sound.*

*That is what the last month was. Not four hundred hours to make it
strong. A hundred and eight hours to make it audible.*

---

He got the rest of it on the walk home at about half past two and it
was the part he did not want.

*Two hundred and nine dishes.*

*Every failed article I have made in three years, in a back room, in
Kanzaki's copperplate, and I went through all of them with a borrowed
lens in the fifth month and found bulbs and breaks and called it
burnout and was pleased with myself.*

*Not one of them ever made a sound.*

*Not because they were not carrying. They were carrying — that is the
whole of the finding, they were carrying and they burned out doing it.*

*They were too fine to sing.*

He stopped on the hill in the rain.

*So the four seconds was never silent.*

*A wall goes up in a corridor and holds and comes apart, and every one
of those seconds it is doing exactly what this rod is doing on a bench,
and there is nothing in the air because the ways are a fraction of the
width and whatever note is in them is above anything a person could
hear or below anything a person could hear or simply too small to move
the air at all.*

*Eleven years. Forty thousand tap tests. Nine hundred articles.*

*I have made one thing that can tell me what is happening to it and I
made it by accident, by going slowly, for four months, at the only
point in my life when nobody was waiting.*

---

He wrote the last part at the low table with his coat still wet and the
lamp low.

*I want to be careful about a sentence I can feel coming, which is that
the article is talking to me.*

*It is not. It is a physical structure under load and a structure under
load has a frequency, and I have known that since I was eleven and
tested a casting with a hammer, and there is nothing in this that a
smith would find remarkable except the not stopping.*

*What is remarkable is entirely on my side and it is this:*

*I have spent eleven years unable to tell whether a thing I made was
good until it failed. That is the actual condition. Every article I
have made has been an article I found out about afterwards, from the
wreckage, in a dish, with a date on it in somebody else's hand.*

*Tonight I found out that a hundred and eight hours had done something,
while it was still doing it, before anything broke.*

*I do not have a word for what that is and I am not going to invent
one at three in the morning.*

He put the pencil down.

Then he picked it up.

*Exercise 1: two.*

*Hummed at a piece of crystal in a yard for twenty minutes to establish
a pitch, and could not hold a sphere between my palms for a count of
three afterwards, and both of those are the same evening.*
