# Chapter 32, Scene 3 — Four Attempts

*Act 4, part C. Konoha. Sazare Ruri, age seventeen. POV: Sazare.
~1,575 words.*

---

He kept a record, because he kept a record of everything, and it did not
occur to him until the fourth entry that keeping a record of this
particular thing might itself be part of the problem.

*Attempt 1. Nurse Tomoe. Ward, 09.40.*

Tomoe had come back onto full duties in the spring with two fingers of
the left hand gone below the second joint, and had spent nine months
rebuilding a one-and-a-half-handed technique for every task on that
floor, and had got so good at it that new orderlies did not notice for
a week.

She was re-tying a compression bandage on bed four when Sazare arrived
at the foot of the bed with the intention of saying something.

"That's a good bandage," he said.

Tomoe did not look up. "It's a bandage."

"It's a spiral reverse with the turns even to about a sixteenth, done
one-handed, in under forty seconds, and if I did it with both hands it
would be worse."

There was a pause. Bed four looked between the two of them.

"Are you telling me off?" said Tomoe.

"No."

"Because it sounded like the front half of telling me off."

"It wasn't."

"Right." She finished the tie and straightened up, and now she did look
at him, with an expression of frank suspicion. "Is there something
wrong with the trolley?"

*Result: unsuccessful. Recipient believed she was about to be
criticised. Note: I described the technique. I did not say anything
about her.*

---

*Attempt 2. O-Shige. Market row, 16.10.*

The dumpling stall had been rebuilt properly in the spring with the
public works money and a good deal of arguing, and O-Shige had put a
new counter in it, which she had made herself out of a single wide
board of something Sazare had not been able to identify from four feet
away and had been thinking about for a fortnight.

"That's a nice counter," he said.

He had rehearsed the sentence. It came out flat and about a tone too
loud, in the manner of a man delivering a line rather than a remark.

O-Shige stopped with the ladle halfway to a bowl.

"What's wrong with it?"

"Nothing's wrong with it."

"Then why've you said that."

"Because it's a nice counter."

"Boy." She put the ladle down. "You have looked at that counter every
single day for six weeks with your face doing arithmetic and you have
never said one word about it, and today you have marched up here and
told me it's *nice*, and I want to know what you've worked out about
it, because if that board's going to split I'd rather hear it now."

"It isn't going to split."

"Then say the actual thing."

So he said the actual thing, which took about ninety seconds and
involved the fact that a single board that wide was probably forty
years in the drying, that it had been cut through-and-through rather
than quarter-sawn and should have cupped and hadn't, that whoever had
laid it had run the heart side down which was correct and which about
half the joiners in that village would have got wrong, and that the
end grain had been sealed with something before it went in, which is a
thing people only do when they intend the object to outlive them.

O-Shige listened to the whole of it with her arms folded.

"My husband cut that board," she said, when he had finished. "In the
year we were married. It sat under our bed for thirty-one years."

"...Oh."

"Sealed the ends himself with the same stuff he did the boat with." She
picked the ladle back up. "You're the first person who's noticed it
wasn't bought."

She gave him a bowl he had not ordered and would not let him pay for
it.

*Result: partially successful, by accident, and only after abandoning
the attempt. Note: the useful thing was the ninety seconds. The
sentence I planned was worse than saying nothing.*

---

*Attempt 3. Mitarashi Anko. Hospital rear yard, 12.25.*

She had taken to intercepting him at the yard wall roughly once a
fortnight, always at a time that suggested she had checked the roster
and always in a manner that suggested she had not, and she had come
that day in a coat he had not seen before.

Dark red, three-quarter length, wool, wrong for the weather. New — or
not new; new *to her*. The cut was ten or twelve years out of fashion
and it had been made for somebody about two inches shorter in the arm,
and somebody competent had let the cuffs down and pressed the old fold
line out well enough that you had to be looking for it.

He had, at that point, been carrying Kakashi's instruction round for
five weeks.

"That coat looks nice," said Sazare.

Anko's eyebrows went up about a quarter of an inch, which for her was
a considerable reaction.

"Does it," she said.

"Yes."

"Go on then."

"...Go on what?"

"You've got more. You've always got more, it's the only genuinely
restful thing about you." She spread her arms. "Do the coat."

And he did the coat, because she had asked, and because the alternative
was standing in a yard with nothing further to say.

The cuffs, first. Then the fact that the cloth was better than anything
being woven now and had come from a mill that had probably stopped
existing, and that the lining was newer than the shell by about a
decade and had been put in by a different hand, and that there was a
repair under the left arm done with thread that did not match and had
been done in a hurry by somebody who was not going to take the coat
off to do it properly.

"And it was made for a woman two inches shorter in the arm than you,"
he finished, "and about the same in the shoulder, so it fits you
everywhere except across the back, which is why you keep it open."

Anko was silent for slightly longer than was comfortable.

"That was my mother's," she said.

"I'm sorry—"

"Don't be sorry, it's the first time in eleven years anybody's said a
word about it." She pulled the front of it straight with one hand.
"Everyone in this village either doesn't notice it or notices it and
doesn't say, because I'm the one with the mark on my neck and people
have decided everything about me is a topic." Her mouth moved. "You
looked at a coat and saw a coat. Do you know how restful that is?"

"You've said restful twice."

"I'm aware. Don't get used to it." She pushed off the wall. "Same time
in a fortnight. Bring something better than a coat."

*Result: unclear. Recipient appeared pleased. I did not say one thing
about her.*

---

*Attempt 4. Mori Suzu. Hospital gate, 17.50.*

The girl from the culvert exercise had turned up twice at the hospital
in six weeks on errands that did not require her to come through the
second floor, and had come through the second floor both times.

He caught her at the gate on the second occasion, going out.

"Mori."

"Ruri-san." She stopped, and stood the way she had stood in the square,
half a pace forward of where she needed to be.

He had a sentence ready. He had had it ready for two days. It was about
the fact that she had put herself between a marker and an immobile
casualty without being told to, twice, at fourteen, and that this is
not a thing that can be taught and about one shinobi in nine has it.

What came out was:

"Your pouch is still in the same place."

Mori looked down at it. "Yes?"

"Ito's moved his four times since the exercise. I've been watching. His
strap's got seven creases in it now."

"...Right."

"Yours hasn't moved at all."

She waited, visibly, for the rest of it. The rest of it did not arrive,
because the rest of it required him to say the second half out loud and
the second half was about *her* and not about a strap, and the gap
between the two halves was approximately the width of everything
Kakashi had been trying to tell him for five weeks.

"Is that bad?" said Mori, eventually.

"No," said Sazare. "It's the opposite of bad."

"Oh." She thought about it. "Thank you?"

She went out through the gate looking, he thought, mildly bewildered,
and it took him until the following morning to work out that he had
handed a fourteen-year-old a compliment in a code she had no key to,
and that she had gone home and probably thought about her pouch.

*Result: failure. Note — and I want this written down before I stop
being angry about it —*

*I have now made four attempts. In all four I described an object.
Three times the object belonged to somebody and the somebody was
pleased, which I did not do on purpose and cannot reproduce. Once the
object belonged to nobody and it did nothing.*

*In none of the four did I say a single word about the person.*

*I do not appear to be able to do it. It is not that I am choosing not
to. I get to the place in the sentence where the person goes and there
is nothing there to put in.*
