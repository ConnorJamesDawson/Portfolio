# Chapter 39, Scene 3 — Nineteenth of the First

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the east side. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen. POV:
Sazare. ~1,684 words.*

---

The present defeated him for eleven days and the reason was that the
category was empty.

He knew what to make for a joiner, a quartermaster, a deaf woman, a
market trader and a woman who wore her mother's coat. He had made all
five and four of them had worked.

He did not know one single thing about what a man of thirty wants for
his birthday.

He established, on the fourth day, that this was not ignorance of
Ashida but ignorance of the category — that he had never in his life
been inside a household on such a day, had never watched anybody
receive one, and had no reference case whatsoever. Chapter four was
about gifts in general and had two pages on occasions that assumed the
reader had been to one.

On the eighth day he did the only thing left, which was to go back
through his own file on the man and look for a want.

Ashida Kōhei: two children; a wife called Miyuki who corrected his
spelling when it mattered; a flak jacket buckled wrong across one loop
for eleven months; boots worn at the outside heel on the right; a
folder, quarter-bound, that he had put *WATER* on the label of; ninety-
one clippings and counting; a pencil he took out and put away at gates
out of a habit that had outlived its form.

Nothing. Not one want in three years of observation. The man appeared,
as far as the file went, to want nothing at all.

And then on the ninth day Sazare found it, four months old, in the
margin of an entry about a gate conversation, in his own hand:

*Kiyomi asks about me. She has got it into her head that I am the man
who makes the blue stones and she wants one and he has told her about
nine times that they are government property.*

---

Kanzaki did not look up.

"You want to requisition four-tenths of a gram of audited substrate."

"Yes."

"For a birthday present."

"For a four-year-old."

"Which four-year-old?"

"Ashida Kōhei's daughter. Kiyomi. She was four on the twenty-third of
the eleventh month and she has been asking her father for a blue stone
for about a year and he has told her nine times that they're government
property, which is correct, which is why I'm standing in your yard."

Kanzaki put the pen down and looked at him over the glasses.

"You could have done this in about four seconds," she said.

"How?"

"By growing one at your own bench out of your own aggregate on your own
evening and putting it in your pocket and never telling anybody, which
is what nine people out of ten in this village would have done and
which I would never have found out about and could not have proved if I
had."

"Everything I grow is audited."

"Everything you grow *under quota* is audited, and the distinction has
been on the form since the fourteenth month and you have never once
used it." She got the requisition book out. "I have wondered for about
two years whether you knew it was there."

"I knew it was there."

"And?"

"And an audit that I use when it suits me isn't an audit," said Sazare.
"It's an argument I can make later. I'd rather have the book."

Kanzaki Uta wrote out the chit.

*One (1) unit fabricated substrate, non-quota, 0.4 g, for private
disposal. Purpose stated: gift. Recipient: minor, civilian, non-
establishment. Fabricator: RURI, S. Auditor: K.U.*

She stamped it, and blotted it, and then, at the bottom, in the column
where she wrote nothing except weights, she added four words in her own
hand:

*Approved without reservation. — K.U.*

"That's not a standard entry," said Sazare.

"No, it isn't." She closed the book. "Go away."

---

The engineering took him six evenings and none of it was the crystal.

*Constraint 1: she is four.* Four-year-olds put things in their mouths.
He had pulled two objects out of two children's throats in that
hospital in two years and had been the fastest pair of hands in the
building on both occasions, and there was no version of him handing a
child a small hard object.

So: too large to swallow. He worked it out properly, off the ward's own
sizing gauge, and then went up by half again because the gauge was a
minimum and not a target.

*Constraint 2: it must not come off.* Which ruled out a cord, a chain, a
pin, a clasp, and anything with a joint in it, because everything with
a joint in it eventually has the joint fail and a joint that fails in a
four-year-old's hand is the whole problem again.

So: not a thing she wears. A thing she *has*.

Which required him to know what she already had, which required him to
ask her father a question about his daughter, which he did at a gate on
a Tuesday and which took him two attempts.

"What does she carry?"

"...Carry?"

"Everywhere. Children have one. There's an object that goes where they
go and if you take it away for a wash there's an incident."

Ashida stared at him.

"How do you know that?"

"I work on a ward with a children's end. There are eleven of them in
there on a bad week and every single one arrives holding something and
the something is the first thing the mothers ask about." Sazare had the
notebook out. "What is it?"

"It's a *bear*," said Ashida, "and it's disgusting, and it's been
through the wash about four hundred times, and it's grey now and it
started out orange, and if you are about to tell me you're going to do
something to that bear I want it on record that Miyuki has views."

---

He did not do anything to the bear.

What he did — and it took the six evenings, and about eleven attempts,
and a piece of birch he got off Handa's offcut pile — was make a small
wooden box with a sliding lid, about the size of two matchboxes, with a
recess cut in the top face and the substrate set flush into it, filled
from behind and bedded so it could not be prised out by a
four-year-old's fingernail. He tested that himself for about twenty
minutes with a fingernail.

The blue was in the top of the lid. When you slid the lid open the blue
went with it.

Inside, it was empty, which was the entire design.

*Chapter four, page nine: "A gift to a child ought to be a container
rather than a thing. The child will supply the thing."*

He gave it to her on the nineteenth in a house on the east side with
eleven people in it and both children awake past their bedtime and a
one-year-old throwing a wooden spoon at approximately shoulder height.

Kiyomi Ashida took the box in both hands, opened it, closed it, opened
it, looked at the blue, said "It's the *stone*," and then went and put
the bear's left ear in it, which did not fit, and shut the lid on the
ear, and carried the whole assembly around for the rest of the evening
with the bear hanging off the side of the box.

Nobody in that room laughed at her. Sazare noticed that and did not
know what to do with it.

---

Miyuki Ashida was thirty-four, four inches shorter than her husband,
and had the flattened forefinger of somebody who sewed, and she caught
Sazare in the passage by the kitchen at about the ninth hour with a
bowl in each hand and no free ones.

"You're Ruri."

"Yes."

"I wrote you a note about a shipping page."

"*Kōhei — I think this is the big one.* You wrote it in the margin of
clipping forty and I read it about eleven times and I did not know it
was you for a year and a half."

Miyuki Ashida stood in a passage with two bowls.

"He said you'd know which one," she said.

"He was right."

"He's right about you about a third of the time, which is a much better
rate than he manages with anybody else, and he has been trying to work
out how to be your friend for three years and he thinks he's been
terrible at it." She shifted the bowls. "I would like to know whether
he has been terrible at it, and I would like an honest answer and not a
kind one, and if you give me the kind one I will know."

Sazare stood in the passage.

"He kept ninety-one pieces of newspaper about my dead country in a desk
drawer for ten months without telling me," he said, "because he thought
handing them over would be a thing he was doing *to* me. He was wrong
about that and it cost me nothing and it cost him ten months of
carrying it. And he was the first person in this village who talked to
me like a person and he did it on the second day and he has not stopped
since, and he has never once asked me a question I did not want to
answer."

Miyuki did not say anything.

"He hasn't been terrible at it," said Sazare. "He's been better at it
than I have, by a very considerable margin, for the entire three years,
and I only worked that out in the ninth month of last year out of a
book I got from a library."

"A book."

"There's a book. It has six chapters. I'm on five."

Miyuki Ashida looked at him for a moment with an expression he would
spend some time failing to classify, and then handed him one of the two
bowls, which he had not offered to take and had not known how to offer
to take.

"Go and sit down," she said. "You've been standing up for two hours
like a man waiting to be told he can leave."
