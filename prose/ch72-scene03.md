# Chapter 72, Scene 3 — Not My Table

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the tower, the second floor. Shizune, age
thirty-one. POV: Shizune. ~1390 words.*

---

Shizune had planned the first family dinner the way she planned
border summits, and by half past five on the Sunday she had
conceded that the plan was dead and that this was, on review,
probably the correct outcome for a family.

The casualty list ran as follows. The seating plan: dead on
arrival, because Naruto had been added at one day's notice by a
fabricator who informed the diary rather than consulting it,
which — she had noted in the margin, with a small amount of
professional pride — meant the boy had finally learned how power
actually worked in this tower. The menu: amended twice, once for
volume (Naruto), once for a covered dish of live moths (the
toads, RSVP'd by scroll, attending "in spirit and in Fukasaku's
person if the rain holds"). The rain had held. The moths were in
the pantry, in a jar, and Tonton had positioned herself outside
the pantry door with the air of an officer containing an
incident.

*Not my table,* Shizune reminded the margin, and went down to
receive the guests.

What the evening actually became, once it stopped being a plan,
was the thing she spent the rest of it quietly cataloguing,
because somebody in this family should keep the record of the
first one, and it was never going to be any of the four people
having it.

Jiraiya arrived early, groomed, bearing wine of a quality that
constituted an apology in bottle form, and behaved — behaved
*conspicuously*, like a man serving the first evening of a long
sentence in front of his parole officer, which was accurate on
both counts. Fukasaku arrived on his shoulder and took the seat
by the warm brick with the covered dish, and proved to be, of
all the table's population, the one with actual table manners,
four hundred years of them, terrifying in their completeness.

Naruto arrived at six exactly — *exactly*; she'd have bet the
good sake he'd been outside the door since five-fifty — in a
jacket she recognised as his formal one because the zip was
done up, carrying, with extreme care, a cloth-wrapped pot.

"I brought the hotpot," he announced to the room, to the
Hokage of the Land of Fire, to the Sannin, to the elder of
Mount Myōboku, with the composure of a man who had decided
that if he only had one move he was going to play it at full
strength everywhere forever. "Page sixty-one. It's amended.
The egg goes in at the end — there was a trial."

And Tsunade — who had been informed of the guest list, as
promised, by the time he knocked; who had said only, in the
privacy of the small office, *of course he invited him*, in a
voice Shizune had minuted under *fond, terminal* — looked at
the pot, and at the boy holding it, and said, "Then it goes
on the table with the rest," and made a space for it at the
centre with her own hands, and Shizune watched Naruto watch
the pot take the centre of the Hokage's table and added a
line to the record: *he will not remember what else happened
tonight. That was the whole night, for him, right there, at
six-oh-one.*

---

The craft, she noted across the courses, was flawless.

She was the only person at the table positioned to grade it —
the only one who held both books, the diary and the truth —
and she graded it through the evening with rising admiration:
the working register worn like good tailoring, the *fabricator*
and the *Tsunade-sama* deployed at exactly the public depth,
the eleven kinds of ease between them all throttled to
colleague-warm, nothing false anywhere — they had simply,
artfully, turned the amplitude down to what the room could
hold. December's artists, back in practice. If she had not
known, she would not have known.

Which was precisely when Naruto, three helpings deep,
mid-thought, entirely happy, lowered his chopsticks and
said:

"Hey, Baa-chan. You should marry somebody like Sazare."

The table achieved a stillness that Shizune would treasure,
privately, for the rest of her professional life.

"Because think about it," Naruto went on, oblivious,
gesturing with the chopsticks at his own excellent logic
while four of the five most controlled people in the Land of
Fire sat in perfect silence around him, "he does paperwork
for FUN. You hate paperwork. He never yells, you always
yell, that's — that's balance, that's like a team comp. He
can cook eggs. He'd remember all the — the dates and stuff.
And he already lives in the tower anyway, so, logistics:
solved." He retrieved a piece of pork, pleased with the
architecture of his case. "Obviously not Sazare-Sazare.
He's eighteen, that'd be weird, you're like a hundred.
Somebody LIKE him. A Sazare-type guy. They should make
more."

The stillness held one further, perfect second.

"They should," said Tsunade, with a cup completing its arc
to her mouth at regulation speed, her voice a masterwork of
dry, her eyes not moving anywhere near the fabricator, "but
the production run was limited. Eat your dinner, brat."

"Limited edition," agreed Naruto, comfortably, already back
in the pot, and the table breathed, and Jiraiya performed a
seamless and heroic redirection onto the subject of
Fukasaku's opinions regarding the wine, and Fukasaku, who
had watched the entire exchange with four hundred years of
eyes and the covered dish untouched before him, said that
the wine was adequate and that *some vintages, in his
experience, were simply sold before the public ever heard
there had been a harvest* — and ate a moth, with perfect
manners, into the silence of a table where three people
were now not looking at each other with tremendous skill.

*The toad knows,* Shizune wrote in the margin of nothing.
*The toad has known since the frost, per the marriage
comment in the field minutes that nobody asked me to
transcribe and I transcribed anyway. Present at this table:
one secret, four keepers, one boy made entirely of daylight
who keeps setting it off like a wind chime and hearing
nothing.*

*Not minuted. None of it. My hand's tired from all the
not-minuting. Best dinner this tower has ever held.*

---

They lost the thread of the evening somewhere around the
third hour, in the best way — Jiraiya telling a war story
with the classified parts hummed instead of spoken; Naruto
retelling the fourth step from inside, on his feet, playing
both parts, to an audience that included both the medic who
had rebuilt the leg and the man who had grown the crystal,
neither of whom corrected a single glorious exaggeration;
Fukasaku and Tsunade discovering a shared and highly
technical contempt for a particular Land of Rivers
tavern-keeper of forty years ago; the fabricator saying
little, at the corner of the table, in the seat with the
sightlines, doing the thing Shizune had finally learned to
read after three years — not detachment. *Inventory.* A man
counting the room, slowly, over and over, the way other
people counted savings.

At the door, at the end of it, with the empty pot rewrapped
in its cloth and pressed back into Naruto's arms over his
objection ("the vessel returns full or the debt compounds —
tower rule," said Tsunade, straight-faced, over a pot she
had personally directed the kitchen to refill), Naruto
stopped, half into his jacket, and looked back at the
wrecked and glowing table, and said, in the voice of
somebody double-checking an arithmetic that had come out
suspiciously well:

"So this is — monthly. This happens monthly. And I'm — like,
I'm invited-invited? Standing?"

"The chair is yours," said Tsunade, before anybody else
could answer, in a register Shizune filed with great care
under *the other one, public depth, load-bearing.* "It was
yours before you sat in it, brat. Ask the fabricator to
show you the drawings sometime — he plans furniture forty
years out. Now go home before I find paperwork for you."

And Shizune, closing the diary on a day it had recorded as
*private — no entries*, appended the evening's last line in
the shorthand only she could read, and it said:

*First family dinner. Attendance: six, one in spirit, one
containing an incident by the pantry. Casualties: one
seating plan, one jar of moths, the last of my composure at
six-oh-one. The account is in surplus. Same time next
month. — S.*
