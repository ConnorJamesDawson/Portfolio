# Chapter 74, Scene 1 — The Morning After

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the north fields. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1,860 words.*

---

The frost had gone out of the north fields over the weekend and left
the ground soft and stupid underfoot, and he arrived at six sharp on
the Monday to find Jiraiya already there, boots caked, considering the
churn with the expression of a man reading a hostile document.

"Ground's ruined," said Jiraiya, happily. "Two months of frozen and
now it's soup. Everything you learned about footwork you learned on a
floor that pushed back. This doesn't." He scraped a heel through it,
watched the trench fill from the sides. "Congratulations, you're a
beginner again. It'll be like this until the fourth month."

"I fought on ground like this on Saturday."

"I know you did." Jiraiya straightened up. "Fukasaku-sama's on the
cloth, the swords are staying in the case, and you've been carrying
something up that lane for four hundred yards with your hand on your
coat like a man with a summons in it. Sit down and deliver it."

He sat down on the cold stone at the field's edge, and got the
notebook out, and did not open it, because he had written the findings
on the Sunday evening precisely so that he would not have to read them
off a page in front of the two people he was reporting to.

"Three findings," said Sazare. "Assessment conducted Saturday
afternoon, informal, unwitnessed, in the garden of a private house.
Opponent fought at an undeclared fraction. Result three to one against
me."

"Three to one," said Fukasaku, from the cloth, with his arms folded.
"To *her*, boy, or to you?"

"To her. The one was mine."

The old toad's head came round about four degrees. It was, Sazare had
learned across nine weeks, the entire range of Fukasaku's astonishment,
and it was worth more than most men's shouting.

"Finding one," he said. "The touch I scored was purchased. It was not
speed and it was not skill. It cost eleven minutes of prepared ground,
a ridge laid across the fall of the land, a knuckle at a tree root
where her left foot had gone twice, nine yards of seeded path, and
four inches of deliberate bait — I offered her the hilt of my own
sword, she took it off my body, and clause eleven of the governor
fired and locked the article in her hand. It cost her a quarter of a
second. I used the quarter second. I am reporting it as an engineering
result and not a combat one, because that is what it was, and I would
rather have it in the record correctly than flatteringly."

"Noted and rejected," said Jiraiya.

Sazare looked up.

"You heard me. Noted, and rejected, and I'll tell you why in a
minute. Finding two."

"Finding two is yours and I am giving it to you before you ask for it.
The footwork held. Soft deep ground, roots at the surface, grass to
the shin, terrain I have never trained on and did not survey in
advance — and my feet found a root before I saw it. Twice. That is
eight weeks of you, it is the only unambiguous return on the whole
programme so far, and I wanted it said before you had to fish for it."

Jiraiya's face did something quick and pleased and put it away.

"Finding three," said Sazare.

And then he did not say anything for a moment, because finding three
had been easy to write at a desk two floors above a woman who had
made it possible, and was turning out to be a different proposition in
a wet field at six in the morning.

*Say it in the register. The register is a load path. That is the
whole technology and you taught it to somebody on a Wednesday.*

"I found the decision," he said.

The field was very quiet. Behind him, somewhere down the hedge, the
crow that had opinions in January had opinions still.

"Immediately," he said. "Completely. Inside the first ninety seconds,
without hesitation of any kind, and I want to state the shape of it
precisely because the shape is the finding. For nine weeks we have been
looking for a component that chooses to do harm, cold, and I have gone
in along every approach I own and it is not there. It is still not
there. I went and looked again on Saturday while she was hitting me
and it is *not there*.

"But the room contains a different one. It contains: decide to take.

"And I could. Instantly. On her ground, on her terms, with the paper
on the step and the assessment at two and her standing in the middle
of my garden telling me what I had done wrong — I could choose, cold
and fast and entire, and there was no hesitation anywhere in me at
all. Because there was no possibility of damaging her, and the whole
brake in me is a brake against damage, and she had built an afternoon
with the brake removed and handed me the question with nothing else
attached to it.

"So the conclusion, offered for correction: the yes does not live
where we have been digging. It lives inside trust. I can choose,
coldly, exactly as far as I trust the ground I am choosing on. Which
means the training is not the decision. The training is building
enough certainty about my own limits that a stranger's ground can be
trusted the way I trusted hers."

He stopped.

Jiraiya took his time. He walked four paces out into the churn and
back, hands behind him, looking at the soup, and Sazare sat on the
cold stone and discovered that his heart was going the way it went
before a board.

"That's the best piece of work you've done since I met you," said
Jiraiya, "and the last third of it is wrong, and I'm going to show
you where, and then we're going to do something else entirely.

"Findings one and two: accepted, and one is rejected on the grounds
that you filed it under the wrong department. You keep doing it. She
told you off for it on Saturday and you did it again this morning
before breakfast. When a man arranges eleven minutes of ground so
precisely that the strongest woman alive has to put her foot where he
wants it, and then hands her his own sword because he has correctly
predicted that forty years of taking bait will make her take it — that
is not an *engineering result*, kid, that is a *fight*. You won an
exchange against a Sannin by knowing her better than she was
defending. There is no discipline on this continent where that gets
recorded as masonry."

"The governor did the—"

"The governor did what you built it to do, at a price you paid, in a
window you made. Stop. You'll hand the credit to a clause, the clause
to a dead sealmaker, the sealmaker to your mother, and by the time
you've finished nobody in the chain is you. I've watched you do it
four times and I'm going to keep saying it until it stops working."
He came back and stood over him. "Now finding three. Say the last
sentence again."

"The training is building enough certainty about my own limits that a
stranger's ground can be trusted the way I trusted hers."

"There it is." Jiraiya crouched down, so they were level, which he had
done perhaps three times in nine weeks and never for a small thing.
"You trusted the *ground*?"

"I trusted the conditions. The walls, the terms, the—"

"You trusted that you could not damage her."

Sazare opened his mouth and shut it.

"Say it back," said Jiraiya.

"...I trusted that I could not damage her."

"So the thing you trusted wasn't the garden. It was the *impossibility
of the harm*. And on a road, against a stranger, at a range where it
matters — the harm is not impossible. The harm is the entire point of
the transaction. You cannot manufacture that condition anywhere except
in a walled garden belonging to a woman who can take anything you have
got." He let that sit for exactly as long as it needed. "Which means
your remediation is: survey the ground until it's safe. And there is
no such ground. You've written yourself a year of work that ends in a
place that doesn't exist."

The crow said something. The soup ticked and drained.

*He is right,* Sazare thought, with the specific cold clarity of an
audit finding its own error. *I generalised from one instance. I had
one instance, with a confound in it the size of a house, and I
generalised, and I know better, I have written four hundred pages
about exactly this failure and I did it anyway because the instance
was the best day of my life and I wanted it to be load-bearing.*

"Then where does it live," he said.

"No."

"...Jiraiya-sama—"

"No. You get worse when you're told and better when you work it out,
it's in your file, I wrote it, and I am not going to hand you the one
thing this whole year is for. You get half. Here's the half." He
stood up, and the register changed, and it was the bathhouse one, the
one with no jokes anywhere in the load path. "You didn't trust the
ground and you didn't trust the terms. You trusted the man holding
the decision, because for one afternoon you knew exactly what he was
and exactly what he could not do, and that made him safe to be.

"The year isn't about ground, kid. It's about becoming somebody you'd
trust with a stranger."

Sazare sat on the stone with the notebook shut in his hands.

"That is not a small piece of work," he said.

"It's the biggest one there is and most people die without starting
it." Jiraiya went and got his coat off the fence post, and did not
put it on, and stood holding it. "Right. Swords stay in the case.
We're not doing footwork this morning and we're not doing the two-feet
band, and you can have that back on Tuesday.

"Say the creed."

Something in Sazare's chest went perfectly still.

"...Now?"

"Now. Out loud, on your feet, in a field, in front of the witness who
has been sitting on that cloth for nine weeks waiting for exactly this
morning. I told you in January that a creed revised in week one is a
slogan and that the date would come the morning after you first chose
the yes and held it, and no earlier." Jiraiya looked at him steadily,
with the coat over his arm and the ruined ground between them and the
sun not properly up. "You chose it on Saturday. You held it for three
hours and then you did something else with your evening that is none
of my business and I want that on the record as the single greatest
act of restraint of my life.

"It's Monday. It's the morning after.

"On your feet, Sazare. Say the creed."
