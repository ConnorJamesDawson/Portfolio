# Chapter 14, Scene 1 — Known Associate

*Act 2, part D. Kirigakure. Jirō, age fifteen. POV: Jirō. ~2,400 words.*

---

The third time they interviewed him about Sazare Ruri, Jirō had the answers
in order before the door had finished shutting, which was the whole trick of
those rooms, and his mother had taught it to him at eleven without either of
them knowing it was a curriculum.

*Say it straight. Say it the same. They write it down and they come back in
a fortnight and ask it again, and what they're checking isn't the answer,
it's the distance between the answers.*

So: yes, he had known the subject. From age seven; Academy intake of the
same year; the association was daily and continued until the subject's
household was detained. Yes, the subject had been in his mother's house;
his mother had fed him; this was on record, volunteered at the time. No,
no contact since the twelfth day of the fifth month three years ago. Yes,
he was certain of the date. The date was the day before the subject's
family was produced in the fish market square, and the subject had come to
their street looking for lodging, and had been refused, and Jirō had
followed him to the corner and given him eight ryō and the rice out of the
cloth, and told him to keep his head down, and that was in the record too,
all of it, in his mother's statement and his own, said straight, said the
same, ever since.

The officer across the table was new — they were always new; that was
another part of the trick — and went down the file with a dry finger, and
found what the last two had found, which was nothing, because there was
nothing, because the whole family had done the one thing available to
people in their position, which was to be exactly as guilty as they were
and not one grain more.

Fed him. Schooled beside him. Liked him.

Cost, to date, of having liked Sazare Ruri: the barracks laundry contract,
withdrawn from his mother in the month after the square, *pending review*,
reviewed ever since; his sister's betrothal, quietly returned by
the boy's family with the porcelain still wrapped; two postings his
brother had been passed over for, in writing, with no reason given,
because the reason did not go in writing.

His brother had got the third posting. Everyone had been very pleased
about the third posting.

The officer set the family file aside and opened the other one — the fat
one, the one with the wave-stamp on the board — and went through it aloud
in the passionless inventory voice they all used, as though reading back
a delivery manifest.

"Two years listed, near enough. Correct me where your information's
better. Year one: subject vanishes off the face of the earth out of a square with
four hundred witnesses in it — into the smuggler channels, the assessors
wrote, *if he lived*, and half of them underlined the if. A starved
child with a destroyed arm, in a winter crossing, in wartime. This file
carried him at even odds dead by spring." A leaf turned. "The year after:
nothing. No sighting, no whisper, no coin. And I'll save you the
question you're too well trained to ask: no, nobody went looking.
There's a war on, Chūnin. Retrieval is not leak-plugging — the boy holds
no ciphers, no signals, no doctrine. He cannot hurt us by existing; he
can only fail to be recovered. Every specialist this office could field
was spent on men who *can* hurt us, and an asset at even odds dead does
not outbid a live defector with a codebook in his head. That's not
neglect. That's arithmetic, and I'd do it again."

Another leaf.

"Then this spring. A mender in the Hot Water spring towns who works
island-fashion. Trade chatter, low grade — it would have sat in the file
another season, except that it arrived alongside something with more
iron in it." He looked up for the first time. "Other parties asking up
that coast. Moneyed. Patient. Professionally polite. Interested in
precisely one commodity this office holds title to. You're streamed for
tracing — so trace it for me. An asset carried as probably dead draws a
rival bid. What does the office learn?"

"That he's alive," said Jirō. "Rival bids don't form on corpses.
Somebody with better information than ours has done their own assessment
and priced him worth collecting."

"Worth collecting," the officer agreed, "and findable. And nothing on
this earth wakes an office like another office reaching for its
property. Hence the corridor. Hence — at last — the budget for it.

"You've requested the external corridor," he went on, turning the last
leaf. "Retrieval support, coastal detail. That's a strange request from
a man whose file reads like yours — war-list chūnin at fifteen, tracker
rating underlined twice by your own assessors, a clean career in front
of you if you keep your sleeve clean. And you've asked for the one file
that can dirty it."

"It's not strange, sir."

"Enlighten me."

Jirō had built the answer months ago, the way you build anything you'll
have to stand on: plain materials, no ornament, every joint true enough
to hold under a second interview and a third.

"The corridor's tracing work, sir. Tracing's what I'm streamed for and
what I'm rated at, and my rate's the best in my intake, and that's in the
file too. And the Ruri retrieval sits in that corridor, and every tracker
you send who never knew him starts from the paper. I start from the
person. I know how he walks. I know what he eats. I know that he reads
every notice on every board he passes because he can't not, and that he
counts things when he's frightened, and that if you show his picture in a
town and he's been there, the person who'll remember him isn't the
innkeeper, it's the mender, the chandler, the man with the broken thing —
because wherever he stops, sir, things get fixed." He kept his eyes on
the middle distance, regulation. "You can spend three more years walking
the paper up and down that coast. Or you can send the one asset in this
building who can walk into a market and know inside an hour whether
Sazare Ruri has ever bought food in it."

The officer looked at him for a while, with the particular flat attention
they trained into them, which Jirō could feel and name and stand under,
because standing under it was also in the curriculum.

"And the association?"

"The association is the asset, sir."

"That's the recruiter's answer. Give me yours."

And here was the gate, and Jirō went through it the way he had rehearsed,
which was: straight, and the same, and true — because the true thing,
said plainly, is the best cover ever built, and he had learned that from
watching a whole neighbourhood fail to learn it.

"He was my friend, sir. The file says the retrieval's flagged alive-only,
premium terms, no damage. If that flag ever changes hands to people in a
hurry, it stops meaning anything — I've seen the corridor reports; I know
what *resisted recovery* covers. If it's me who finds him, the flag
holds. I'll bring him back whole because I want him back whole, and the
village gets its asset undamaged, and my family gets its name back, and
every part of that is in the village's interest, and not one word of it
is a lie."

Silence, of the assessing kind.

"You're aware of what he did."

"I was four streets away when he did it, sir. I heard it." He had. He
heard it still, some nights: a sound like a struck kettle the size of a
district, and then four hundred people making one noise. "Nobody has to
tell me what he is. I knew him before he was it."

The officer closed the file, and squared it, and for one narrow moment
was a person — tired, provincial, up since the fourth hour, with a
war eating the village's men out from under him island by island.

"My predecessor marked your request *refused, conflict of interest*," he
said. "I'm marking it *approved, exploitable interest*. I'd carry both
of those sentences with you, Chūnin. They'll take turns being the true
one." He stamped it, in the way of that whole country: one clean strike,
no flourish. "Corridor detail musters on the eighth. You'll draw kit,
and you'll draw a mask. Dismissed."

---

His mother did not speak to him for nine days.

She did it the way she did everything, at volume, with the whole street
informed — not of the reason, never the reason; only of the silence,
which she wore out-facing, like washing hung inside-out to make a point
of what wasn't being shown. His sister told him to wait it out. His
sister had told him to wait everything out for sixteen years and had
been right at a rate no oracle could touch.

On the ninth day his mother put his dinner in front of him and sat down
opposite with her arms folded and said:

"They pulled the contract in one afternoon. Fifteen years of sheets,
gone by supper. You remember."

"I remember."

"And your idea is you'll go and fetch him, and they'll give it back."

"That's part of it."

"Don't dress the other part for my benefit," said his mother. "I fed
that boy four hundred times. I'd have fed him four hundred more. When
they came round with the list I said it straight and I've said it
straight every fortnight since, and I'll tell you what I've learned in
three years of rooms, Jirō, because it's the only thing there is to
learn: they don't ever want *him*. They want the shape of the hole he
leaves. They'll take him whole, like the paper says, all kindness, and
they'll stand him somewhere and grow things out of him till there's
nothing left in the middle, because that's what that village does with
what it loves, it did it to your brother with a flag on it—"

"Mum."

"—and if it's you that finds him," she went on, over him, straight, the
same, true, "then you bring him to me before you bring him to them. You
sit him at this table one hour. I'll feed him, and I'll look at him, and
then—"

She stopped. She folded the sentence up the way she folded sheets,
corners in, and put it away wherever the last three years of sentences
had gone.

"Eat," she said. "It's getting cold."

He ate. It was the best thing he'd had in a month, which was how she
argued, and they never spoke of it again, and on the eighth he mustered
with the corridor detail and drew his kit, and drew his mask.

It was the standard pattern, white, with the wave-lines of the village
over the cheeks in dull blue, and two eye-slits that made the world
smaller and simpler and further away, and he stood in the stores with it
in both hands for slightly too long — long enough that the storesman, an
old fellow with one arm, said, not unkindly:

"First mask?"

"First mask."

"It gets lighter," the storesman said, which Jirō filed at the time as
kit advice, and understood a year later to have been the other thing.
