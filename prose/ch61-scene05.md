# Chapter 61, Scene 5 — A Room and a Desk

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the hospital, the north store. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1920 words.*

---

The post had started on the first and nobody had told him, because on
the first he had been on the floor of the long room at midnight watching
a man's hand get warm.

He found out on the third from a clerk.

She caught him on the second-floor corridor with a folder and said, "Oh
— you're not on the ward list any more," in the voice of somebody
raising a filing query, and then went off down the corridor, and that
was the whole of it.

He stood there for a moment.

*Four years.*

*Second floor, from the ninth month of my fifteenth year. Four years and
about four thousand bed states and eleven hundred articles and about
nine hundred handovers, and I have come up that stair at the seventh
hour on four hundred and some mornings.*

*And it ended two days ago and nobody said anything and there was no
reason for anybody to, and the notification is a clerk in a corridor
mentioning it as an aside about a list.*

*That is completely correct and it is how it should have gone.*

He went and found the room.

---

They had given him the north store.

It was at the back, past the laundry and the second bathhouse, on the
cold side, and it had been a dye-works before the hospital took the
block and had been a store for about eleven years and had held nothing
in particular for the last four.

It was thirty feet by eighteen.

Stone floor with a fall on it and a drain in the corner. Two windows the
whole length of the north wall, high, with the glass still in them.
Brick. A double door onto the yard that a cart could come to. A copper
sink at the far end that the dye-works had put in and nobody had ever
taken out.

He stood in the middle of it for about four minutes.

*This is the best room I have ever been in.*

*North light, all day, no sun on the bench at any point. Stone floor,
which means I can drop a thing and it does not bounce and it does not
mark. A drain. A cart door. A sink with a proper run on it. Eighteen
feet of clear wall for racking and a ceiling I cannot reach.*

*The fabrication room was nine by nine and had a west window that was
useless after two and a wooden floor and no water, and I took it because
it was cold and nobody else would have it, and I have done every single
thing I have made in there since I was sixteen.*

*This is four times the size and it is better in every particular and I
would have wept for it at seventeen.*

*And there is nobody in it.*

---

That was the whole of the afternoon, really.

He walked the racking out with a piece of chalk. He worked out where the
bench went and then changed it twice, because the second one had him
with his back to the cart door and the third one had him lit from behind
at the sink. He established that the drain took water at a good rate and
that the copper was sound and that two of the high windows would open
and four would not and one had been painted shut by somebody in a hurry.

It was, on any reasonable measure, an extremely good day.

He came out at about six and stood in the yard and put the padlock on
and understood that he had not spoken to another human being since the
clerk in the corridor at nine.

*Nine hours.*

*Which is not remarkable. I have had nine-hour days on a bench about four
hundred times.*

*But there were people through the wall. That is the difference and it
took me until the yard to find it. There has never once been a day where
I did not know that the ward was on the other side of that partition,
and that Emiko was going to come through it at some point with a trolley
and ask whether I had eaten, and that Yagi would put her head round at
about four with something.*

*Nobody is coming through that wall. There is no wall. There is a
laundry and a bathhouse and a yard.*

*This is what I asked for.*

*I want that written down plainly, because in about a fortnight I am
going to be tempted to feel put-upon about it and I would like the entry
to be waiting for me.*

*Everything through one desk. No ward. No patients. No colleagues. I
argued for that, in a room, in front of fifteen people, with Ibiki's
notice on the front of it, and the argument was that a fabricator with a
foreign name who is producing medical articles should not also be at a
bedside, and it was a good argument and I still believe it.*

*They gave me exactly the thing I asked for.*

---

The budget line came up from the annexe on the fourth in an envelope.

He opened it standing in the yard because he had not got a desk yet.

He read it twice.

Then he went and sat on the step of the cart door for a bit.

*That is more than I expected.*

*I had put it at about a third of that. I had done the arithmetic in the
tenth month off the surplus figures and the splint volumes and I had a
number, and I have been carrying that number around for six weeks and
using it, and the number is wrong by a factor of about three.*

*And a budget is not money. That is the whole of it and it took me four
minutes to get to and I did not like it.*

*A budget is a statement of what somebody expects.*

*Kanzaki gives me forty grams of reject because she expects splints.
Nobody has ever given me anything without an expectation attached and
the expectation has always been legible off the size of it.*

*Somebody in this village has looked at me and written down a figure
three times larger than the one I would have written, and I do not know
who, and I do not know what they are expecting for it, and the number is
the only thing I have got to read it off.*

He looked at the sheet.

*Eight to seven.*

*Somebody who voted for the post has decided what it is worth, and the
figure is in a hand I do not recognise, and it went through a board
chaired by a man who voted against.*

*So either the man who voted against did not fight it, or he did fight
it and lost, and I would very much like to know which and there is no
way on this earth to find out that would not cost more than the answer.*

He put the sheet in his coat.

*I will spend it properly and I will file every single line of it and
somebody will read those files.*

*That is the whole of my answer and it is not much of one.*

---

Yagi came on the fifth.

She came round through the laundry yard at about eleven with her bag and
a roll of paper under her arm, and she stopped in the double door and
looked the length of the room.

"Well," she said. "They've given you the dye-works."

"They have."

"I trained in a room like this. Not this one — one in the Land of Hot
Water, in the war, and it had been a tannery and you could still smell
it in the wet."

She came in.

"You've chalked the bench in the wrong place."

"It's the third position."

"It's still wrong. You've got the sink behind you and you'll be turning
your back to the door every time you rinse, and in about four months
somebody will come through that door while you are holding something,
and you will jump, and you will drop it."

He looked at the chalk.

"That's right," he said, and rubbed it out with his boot.

---

She put the roll of paper on the windowsill, which was the only surface
in the room.

"Right," she said. "That's what I came for and then I'll go.

"That is the list."

"Which list?"

"Every article the second floor has needed in the last eleven years and
could not get."

He did not move.

"It isn't tidy," said Yagi. "It is not a requisition and it is not
costed and about a fifth of it is impossible and I have not taken the
impossible ones out because I do not know which fifth.

"Some of it is mine. Some of it is Emiko's and there are four pages in a
hand you will not know because he died in the ninth year and I have
never been able to throw it out.

"It goes back to before you were born."

"How long is it?"

"About four hundred items," said Yagi. "I have not counted properly and
I started to and stopped, because when you get past about two hundred
you start to notice what it *is*, and I did not want to be sitting in my
office at nine at night noticing that."

---

He unrolled about two feet of it on the sill.

*A splint that grows with a child's leg.*

*A clamp that does not have to be removed.*

*Something for the fourth-week suture on a burn that does not tear when
it is taken out.*

*A frame for a crushed hand that lets the fingers work while it holds
the bones — from the ninth year, in a hand I do not know, with three
lines under it and the word* please *at the end, which is not a word
anybody puts in a requisition.*

He stopped reading.

"Yagi."

"Mm."

"Some of these are four years old."

"Some of them are thirty."

"I've been in that building for four years."

"Yes."

"Why has nobody—"

"Because you were a ward assistant with a bench in a cold room," said
Yagi, "and then you were a fabricator with a docket queue and a quota
and about nine hundred splints a year to make, and nobody in this
village had any business handing you a list of impossible things,
because you would have *tried to do them*, and you would have done it
in your own evenings, and it would have killed you by about twenty."

She picked her bag up.

"And now you have got a post that exists for exactly this and a room the
size of a barn and a budget line I have seen the figure on.

"So now it is your business."

She got to the door.

"Yagi," he said.

"Don't."

"I haven't said anything."

"You were going to say something," said Yagi, "and it was going to be
about the man in the ninth year, and I would rather you did not, because
I have carried that roll of paper from three different offices and I am
going to go and have a very ordinary afternoon."

She went out through the laundry yard.

He stood in the middle of thirty feet by eighteen with four hundred
impossible things on the windowsill and the north light coming in, and
had, for the first time in about eleven days, an entirely uncomplicated
thought.

*Right.*

*Good.*
