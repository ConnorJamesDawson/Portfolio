# Chapter 69, Scene 1 — Interface Failure

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~780 words.*

---

The docket arrived on the Monday of week two, through the one named
channel like everything arrived, and it was, on its face, a routine
performance query on splint 44-B, and it was, by the second page,
nothing of the kind.

Tachibana Rei, chūnin, twenty-four. Cracked seventh rib on a courier
run, ordinary fall, ordinary fracture. Splint 44-B seated at the
hospital, correctly, by a practitioner he had trained, per a
protocol he had written. And then the follow-up, eleven days later:
seating compromised, the recorded instruction discharging into a
gap, healing running four days behind the table.

The querying practitioner had flagged it as possible article fault
and requested inspection, which was proper.

The article was not at fault. He had the fault inside four minutes,
off the seating diagram and one line in the intake notes that the
querying practitioner — a man — had recorded without reading:
*patient resumed standard chest bindings against advice, day three,
citing operational requirements.*

He sat back from the bench with the diagram in his hand.

*The splint seats against the rib line. The bindings compress the
whole structure the splint is trying to hold in its natural
position. Two devices, occupying the same span, fighting each other
— and mine lost, because hers is cinched tighter, because hers has
to do its job at a full sprint under a courier bag.*

*And "against advice" is doing an enormous amount of work in that
sentence, because the advice was: go without. To a working kunoichi.
Whose alternative is running a delivery route with an unsupported
structure that the whole of ch— that the anatomy lecture priced for
me twelve days ago: a real load, on real anchors, taxed daily, and
nobody takes the tax seriously because the cause embarrasses
people.*

*She did not fail to comply. She was given a choice between two
injuries and picked the familiar one.*

He pulled the ward data he was still cleared for, because the
question had gone structural and structural questions got surveyed.
It took the afternoon, and the afternoon rearranged him.

*Binding-related presentations, this hospital, three years:
sixty-one that say so, and an unknowable number filed as "rib
strain," "shoulder groove abrasion," "thoracic pain, cause
unstated." Standard-issue field wear for kunoichi: unchanged in
forty years. Support engineering: nil. The standing solution is
sixteen feet of cloth wound tight enough to fight in, which is to
say tight enough to restrict breathing, abrade skin, and turn a
cracked rib into a compression injury, and it is so completely the
water everybody swims in that the intake form does not have a box
for it.*

*Half the shinobi force. Forty years. No box on the form.*

---

He did the honest entry that evening, because the study had rules
and the first rule was that the book got the whole of it.

*Project note, new file. The load problem is real, documented, and
unengineered — a standing injury tax on half the force, paid daily,
filed under embarrassment. I can build the fix. Open articles at
the load points, self-drawing, no wearer cost; graded seals to
spread what the anchors carry. The medical case writes itself and
Tachibana Rei is its first line.*

*And the second motive, entered here in full because this book does
not keep two sets of accounts:*

*The work requires familiarity with the structure I would be
engineering for, and the reference structure I have closest access
to belongs to Hime, and a fitting conducted in the working register
is exposure my systems might actually survive — the theory being
that an occupied instrument overloads slower. I am, in other words,
proposing to spend professional hours in the near vicinity of the
exact anatomy that has been shorting me out since November, with a
clipboard as insulation.*

*I have read this entry back twice and it does not come apart. Half
of it is a manoeuvre and half of it is the thing I want, and they
do not come apart, and a thing being useful does not stop it being
true. Established the thirtieth of the twelfth, behind a bathhouse,
about a different document entirely.*

*Tomorrow: the keepers, because it touches the open-article
principle and I do not move on that axis without the field's
blessing. Then the docket. Then—*

He looked at the last line for a while before he wrote it.

*Then I get to solve a real problem for several hundred people as
cover for holding a measuring tape near one of them, and if
Urushibara were alive he would charge me a fortnight of afternoons
for the privilege of laughing at me, and he would be right to.*
