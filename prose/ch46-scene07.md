# Chapter 46, Scene 7 — Four Hours

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the room over the persimmon tree. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1,579 words.*

---

He got home at twenty past two and did not light the lamp for a while.

*Bring it to me before you write it anywhere.*

He sat at the low table in the dark with the window open and the
persimmon making its small noises, and he understood the instruction
perfectly and agreed with it entirely and had every intention of
obeying it, and he was aware that all three of those were true and that
none of them were the relevant fact.

The relevant fact was that he could not think without a pencil.

He had known that since he was about nine. He did not have an inner
process that arrived at conclusions and then recorded them; the writing
*was* the process, the page was where the reasoning happened, and a
thought he had not written was not a thought he had had — it was a
pressure, a direction, a thing pushing at the inside of his head that
would go on pushing until it was on paper and could be looked at.

He sat in the dark for about forty minutes doing it the other way.

It did not work. He went round the same four sentences eleven times and
got no further on the eleventh than he had on the second, and twice he
lost the middle of it entirely and had to rebuild from the start, and
at about ten past three he understood that he was not obeying an
instruction, he was simply failing to think, and that at the end of it
he would take her a worse version of the same thing three days later.

He lit the lamp.

---

He wrote it in about twenty minutes and it was four steps long.

*One. The article I made on the eleventh contains seven point one one
grams of matter which did not exist before I made it, and nothing on
that bench weighs less. Tested against air, ground, operator and seed.
Total to date, by Kanzaki's ledger, in excess of eleven hundred and
forty kilograms.*

*Two. Therefore the crystal is not made out of anything I own or
anything I brought. I am not manufacturing it. I am gathering something
that is already present, from somewhere I cannot locate, and giving it
a shape.*

*Three. The same must be true of Wood Release, and nobody has ever
asked, and I have this from the Hokage of this village standing in a
yard at two in the morning saying that she has three of the four in her
family tree and has never once wondered where the wood comes from.*

He stopped there and looked at the three of them for a while.

Then he wrote the fourth, and it took him four attempts, and the first
three were all versions in which he was cleverer than the evidence.

*Four. If two unrelated kekkei genkai both produce matter out of an
unaccounted source, then the source is not inside either bloodline —
because two bloodlines with no common ancestor cannot both contain the
same reservoir. It is outside. It is shared. It is available to both of
us and neither of us put it there.*

*Which means the bloodline is not the source of the material.*

*The bloodline is only the shape.*

He put the pencil down.

*And a shape is a technique.*

---

He read that back twice and then did the thing he had spent a year
learning to do, which was to go looking for the ways it was wrong
before he went looking for the ways it was interesting.

*Objections, honestly, in the order they arrive.*

*a) Two is not a sample. I have one case I have measured and one case I
have inferred from a sentence somebody said in a yard, and I have never
in my life seen Wood Release performed and could not tell you whether
it produces matter or reorganises it. I am one careless step away from
a general claim about four hundred families off a sample of one and a
half, and I know exactly what she would say about that because she said
it in the third month with a cup in her hand.*

*b) "Shared reservoir" is doing an enormous amount of work and I have
no evidence for it beyond arithmetic. It could be four separate
reservoirs. It could be that Wood Release is nothing like this at all
and the wood comes off a seed the way I thought my crystal came off a
seed, which I believed for eleven years and was wrong about for eleven
years.*

*c) I want this to be true. That is the most dangerous line on this
page and it goes on it. I want it to be true because if the material is
outside me then the thing that got my family killed was never in our
blood, and I have wanted that to be the answer since I was thirteen and
a half, and a man who has wanted an answer for five years is the worst
possible instrument for weighing the evidence for it.*

He looked at (c) for some time.

*So the correct next step is not a paper and it is not a theory. It is
one question, put to one person, and the question is:*

*Where does the wood come from — and does anything weigh less
afterwards?*

*If a Wood Release user tells me that the timber comes out of the
ground, or out of a seed, or that he is four kilos lighter at the end
of an afternoon, the whole of this collapses in about nine seconds and
I will have spent a fortnight and learned something.*

*And if he tells me he does not know, and has never asked, and that
nobody has ever asked him—*

He did not finish that sentence on the page.

---

Then he turned back to the top of the entry, and ruled a line above the
whole of it, and wrote in the space:

*I have violated an explicit instruction inside four hours and this is
the record of it.*

*She said: bring it to me before you write it anywhere. Not after. Not
the tidy version.*

*I sat in the dark and tried for forty minutes and I could not do it. I
do not think in my head. I have never thought in my head. The page is
where it happens and a thought I have not written is a pressure and not
a thought, and at the end of forty minutes I had lost the middle of it
twice and would have taken her something worse in three days.*

*That is a true account and it is also an excuse, and both of those are
the same sentence, and I am not going to spend the rest of tonight
deciding which one it is because I have watched myself do that
manoeuvre four times this year.*

*What I am going to do is the thing that worked in the third month.*

*I am going to take her the violation first. Before the finding. Not
"here is a hypothesis and by the way I wrote it down" — the other order.
The first sentence out of my mouth is that she gave me an instruction
at the gate and I broke it before three in the morning and here is
exactly how and exactly why, and then, if she still wants it, the four
steps.*

*And if she says the four steps go in the fire, they go in the fire,
and I do not argue, and I do not keep a copy, and I want that written
down now while it is cheap, because at eleven o'clock tomorrow it will
be expensive and I will have four reasons.*

---

He put the notebook in his coat rather than in the drawer, which was
new, and sat back.

The lamp had burnt down to almost nothing and the first grey was coming
up behind the persimmon and there was a shift at six.

*Exercise 1: two.*

*Hours on the article: 132.*

*Position: the six seconds is gone and it was never mine. Eleven
hundred and forty kilos of something is in this village and nobody
knows what it is including me. A joiner of seventy-one can hear three
of four voices and lost the fourth at fifty and never told anybody why
he took the yard job. There are thirty-one people who think there is a
sword and none of them have seen it. Kanzaki has opened a column and is
going to write "unknown" in it in ink three hundred and eleven times in
her own evenings.*

*And I have a question that takes one afternoon to ask and could end
the whole of it, and I do not know a single person who can do Wood
Release, and I have never heard of one who was not the First Hokage,
who has been dead for fifty years.*

He looked at that.

Then he wrote the last line, because it was four in the morning and it
had been sitting behind everything since a woman had said *I have three
of the four in my family tree* in a wet yard and had not appeared to
notice she had said it.

*Somebody in this village knows whether there is one.*

*And she has already told me to come and see her.*
