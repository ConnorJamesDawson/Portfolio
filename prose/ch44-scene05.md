# Chapter 44, Scene 5 — The Second Thursday

*Act 4, part D. Konoha, the west market. Sazare Ruri, age eighteen.
POV: Sazare. ~1,286 words.*

---

The second Thursday of the fifth month was the twenty-first, and it was
the hottest day of the year so far.

Fish in the cold is one smell. Fish at twenty-nine degrees under an
awning with the ice going faster than the trade is a different one
altogether, and by nine in the morning the whole of the west end of the
market had it — meltwater running down the camber in three channels,
cedar, brine, and underneath all of it the sweet edge that comes off a
cart in its fourth hour when the top layer has started to go.

He had the envelope inside his coat, which was the wrong coat for the
weather and was therefore itself a thing somebody could notice, and he
had been told about that on the Tuesday and had not been able to solve
it, because the alternative was carrying it in his hand.

Eleven hundred words. The four media and the reject rates. The source
column and the fourteen months of his own handwriting. The seventy per
cent and why it was not a loss. The sentence about the handing over.

Unaltered. Not one word touched since the third month.

*If you go back to it now you'll write it for me, and he'll read it and
know.*

---

There were four Division people in the market and he could not find any
of them, which he had been told would be the case and had spent the
walk down failing to accept.

He had made a game of it and lost. A woman with two children who was
too unhurried. A man mending a shutter on the second morning of a job
that should have taken one. A pair at the tea stall who were the wrong
distance apart. He had eliminated all three by the time he reached the
cedars and had no idea whether he had eliminated them correctly or had
simply been shown three ordinary people and one careful one, and the
not knowing sat in him the entire time like something swallowed whole.

The fourth he never even guessed at.

---

The fishmonger was between fifty and sixty and brown to the elbow on
both arms and pale above.

The blue jacket had gone — it was the fifth month — and what was under
it was a sleeveless thing gone grey in the wash, and the arms had the
same line on them they had had in the frost, sharp as a tide mark. The
army boots. The left heel worn down the outside. The nicks across the
backs of the fingers, dozens, all old, all running the same way.

He was arguing pleasantly with a woman about the price of a bream and
losing on purpose.

Sazare waited.

When it was his turn he bought two mackerel, because two mackerel was
what he had bought in the third month and a man who buys the same thing
twice is not a thing anybody remembers.

Then he put the envelope on the boards next to the coin.

The fishmonger looked at it.

"That's the Thursday one," he said.

"Yes."

"Right." He wrapped the mackerel first, unhurried, and then picked the
envelope up between two fingers and put it under the till board in the
cart without looking at it, in a movement so worn-in and so completely
unremarkable that Sazare understood, watching it, that the man had done
this some hundreds of times for some number of people and had never
once been curious about any of it. "Goes down Tuesday. It's four days
because of the road, not because of me."

"That's fine."

"They're better tomorrow than today, those." He counted the change into
Sazare's palm without looking at it. "Keep 'em cold. Not in the
window."

He said the same four words to the man behind.

---

Sazare walked up the hill with two mackerel under his arm.

It took about six minutes and he did not notice any of them, and he was
most of the way up before the thing arrived, and when it arrived it
arrived complete.

*He does not know what he is carrying.*

*He has a wife or he does not, and children or he does not, and thirty
years of a knife against the outside of his right index finger, and he
has just put a document into a cart under a till board that will make a
man four hundred miles away considerably better at something, and if
that ever goes wrong the second thing anybody will look at is the
route, and the route is him.*

*I did not put him there. Ibiki did not put him there; the man who
wrote to me did, two months ago, in a courteous hand, and he chose a
fishmonger precisely because a fishmonger is the sort of person nobody
counts.*

*And I have just used him.*

He stopped at the corner by the dye-works, in the sun, with the fish
going warm under his arm.

*I agreed to the fee in advance and in writing. I have that sentence in
a book at home in my own hand and I was pleased with it at the time.
I wrote it down so that whoever read it would have the fee and the
agreement in the same entry and in that order.*

*It has done nothing.*

*I have been assuming, without ever once checking it, that a cost you
have priced beforehand is a cost you have paid beforehand. It is not.
It is a cost you have merely described. The description does not
discharge anything at all — it is a note in a ledger about a debt, and
the debt falls due at the counter, in the heat, when the man says the
same four words to you that he says to everybody.*

He made himself walk again.

*Second finding, and it is worse.*

*I priced the fee wrong. I priced it as: this will help him. That was
the fee Ibiki named and I accepted it and both of us thought that was
the whole of it.*

*Nobody costed the fishmonger.*

---

He wrote it up that night in a hot room with the window open and the
persimmon making no sound at all, and cooked the mackerel badly, on the
wrong heat, as always.

*Envelope handed over 09:40, west market, second Thursday. Unaltered
from the third-month draft, per instruction. Four Division present, all
four unidentified by me; three false positives, recorded overleaf so
that I can find out later how wrong I was.*

*Carrier: male, mid-fifties, fishmonger, thirty years in the trade,
army-issue boots he did not buy, unaware of content, incurious about
content, paid presumably by a third party he has never met. Says the
same four words to every customer. Advises against the window.*

*He is not in anybody's assessment. I have checked mine and he is not
in it, and I would take a considerable amount of money that he is not
in Ibiki's either, because Ibiki's remit is four hundred miles and this
man is nine minutes from my door.*

He looked at that.

Then he wrote the honest half, because the ledger got both or it got
neither, and this one he did not have to attempt three times.

*I have spent two years building a doctrine that says nobody in a
building has a thing done to them without knowing.*

*I stood in a yard eleven days ago and told Kanzaki that the doctrine
was about patients and she asked me to read it back and tell her where
it said patients.*

*It does not say patients.*

*It does not say this hospital either.*
