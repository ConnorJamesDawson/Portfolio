# Chapter 29, Scene 3 — Not in the Room

*Act 4, part C. Konoha General. Sazare Ruri, age sixteen. POV: Sazare.
~1,235 words.*

---

Fujita signed the eligibility line without looking up from the chart
he was already reading.

"Adult, conscious, stable, already on successful conventional
treatment, in a bed close enough that failure costs nothing but a
delay." He initialled it. "That's Daichi. Next."

"That's all the assessment there is?"

"That's all the assessment there needs to be. The interesting part
isn't whether he qualifies. It's whether he says yes." He handed the
form back and returned to the chart, which Sazare understood, after a
moment, to be the whole of the conversation Fujita intended to have
about it.

The stamped approval from the Hokage's office arrived two days later,
carried up by Kaburagi herself rather than a runner, which he took to
mean something about how closely it had been read even though she
offered no comment on the contents beyond setting it on the desk and
saying, "That's the last signature. Everything after this one is
yours to be careful with."

---

He brought the device to Daichi's bedside on the twenty-ninth day, and
Emiko was already there when he arrived, which he had not expected and
should have.

"I asked her to be present," Daichi said, before Sazare could say
anything about it. "Ashida told me that's how it works with you lot.
Somebody who isn't invested explains the risk, then you explain the
mechanism, and I get to hear both before I sign anything."

"I can explain the mechanism now, if that's useful."

"Do."

Sazare set the chip on the tray between them — small, blue-grey,
unremarkable, the four-tenths of a gram that had cost twenty-nine days
and five failed attempts to become boring enough to trust. He
explained it the way he had explained everything since the fifth
month: plainly, in the order it had actually happened, without
softening the parts that had gone wrong on the way to the part that
hadn't.

"It stores a pattern Yagi already used on you successfully, six times.
It doesn't improve on it, doesn't adapt to anything the wound does
differently, and doesn't think. It repeats exactly what she already
did, once, for as long as the store lasts, and then it's finished and
becomes ordinary mineral. It's keyed to your chakra specifically —
Urushibara's work, not mine — which means it will not answer anyone
else's hand, including my own, once it's loaded."

"And if it goes wrong."

"Then it does nothing at all. The trigger either recognises you or it
doesn't. There isn't a version where it recognises you badly."

Daichi turned the chip over with one finger, the burned hand held
carefully out of the way of the other. "How do I get it off, if I
change my mind."

"There's a tab under the dressing. Pull it and the crystal separates
from the wound before the dressing itself needs to move. No
skill required. It works whether I'm in the building or not."

"And if I want it off after it's fired?"

"Then it's already spent. There's nothing left to remove but a piece
of mineral, and any nurse can lift it the way they'd lift a used
dressing."

Emiko had said nothing through any of this, watching Daichi's
questions rather than Sazare's answers, and Sazare understood, without
being told, that this was the part he was meant to leave.

"I'll wait outside," he said.

Daichi looked faintly surprised. "You don't have to."

"I know. I'd rather."

He went, and did not listen at the door, which cost him more
discipline than he expected it to, standing in a corridor he had
walked four hundred times, unable to hear a conversation about a thing
he had spent twenty-nine days building, because the one condition
Yagi had set was the one condition he understood, better than anyone
in that building, the actual weight of.

*A person who wants a thing to work is the last person who should be
the one telling somebody else it's safe to try.*

He had never once, in his own life, been asked first. He was not going
to be the reason Daichi wasn't.

---

Emiko came out after eleven minutes, which he had counted without
meaning to, and did not tell him what had been said before she was
ready to.

"He has questions for you," she said. "Technical ones. I told him I
wasn't equipped to answer whether the trigger could fire while he was
asleep, and he said that was exactly the sort of question that made
him want to ask it of the person who'd know."

"What did he decide."

"That's not mine to tell you either. Go and hear it from him."

Daichi was sitting up when Sazare came back in, the chip still on the
tray, untouched, which told him nothing either way.

"Three things," Daichi said. "Can it fire while I'm asleep?"

"Yes. It doesn't know the difference. If the graft opens, it opens
whether you're awake to notice or not."

"Can you feel my chakra through it? From wherever you are?"

"Only that the seed's intact. Not what you're doing, not where you
are, not anything about you as a person. It's a route, not a window."

"And if it works — if it buys the eight seconds it's supposed to —
does the crystal stay in me, or does somebody have to come and take it
out?"

"It slumps to grit on its own once it's spent. A nurse wipes it away
with the next dressing change. Nothing to remove, nothing left inside
you."

Daichi considered all three answers with the same flat attention he'd
given the road below the hospital on the day they'd fought over it
together, weighing something Sazare could not see the shape of from
outside.

"Emiko told me this won't save my life," he said. "That my life isn't
in danger. That we're testing whether it repeats eight seconds
already proven to work, nothing more."

"That's accurate."

"I believe her. I want that clear before I say yes — I'm not doing
this because I think I need saving. I'm doing it because you built
something out of the eight seconds everybody in this building keeps
having to spend on me, and I'd rather be the one who finds out if it
works than have the next person be somebody it actually matters for."

"You don't have to frame it as a favour to anyone."

"I'm not. I'm telling you why, because you'll want it in your ledger
eventually and I'd rather you had the honest reason than have to guess
at it." Daichi held out the burned hand, palm up, the graft's edge
visible along the inside of the wrist. "Load it, then. Properly, with
Yagi doing the pattern and you doing the growing, the way it's meant
to be done."

"Now?"

"You've spent twenty-nine days getting here. I don't see the use in
either of us sleeping on it another night."

Sazare looked at the chip on the tray, at the tab already sewn into
the spare dressing Yagi had left ready in its own sealed paper, at
Daichi's hand held out with the particular steadiness of a man who had
decided something and did not intend to revisit the decision out of
politeness.

"I'll get Yagi," he said.

"Good," said Daichi. "And Ruri."

"Yes?"

"Thank you for leaving the room. I know what that cost you. Ashida
told me that too."
