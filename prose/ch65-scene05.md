# Chapter 65, Scene 5 — Three Questions

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~2080 words.*

---

She came on the Friday and she came late, which she had never done, and
she said so before he had got the lamp lit.

"I am four hours late and I want to say at the front that it was not
travel and it was not anything at this end. I have been arguing since
Tuesday."

"With whom?"

"With everybody," said Katsuyu.

---

He got the felt down and did not ask anything else for about eleven
minutes, because she had come off the arrangement of the second and had
never once been late in fifteen Fridays and there was going to be a
reason and she was going to give it to him in order.

She gave it to him in order.

"You won the wager," she said.

"That's not—"

"Ruri, I am not going to have the argument you had with the Hokage and I
am not going to have the one you had with Jiraiya. There was a wager. It
was on the twentieth and it was between the mountains and it was
proposed by Myōboku and accepted by us, and the terms were that the loser
answers three questions in person, truthfully, at length, at the winner's
choosing.

"Fukasaku wrote on the twenty-first. Four lines. The third one says
*Shikkotsu holds three.*

"He does not think it was a match either. He has said so. He has said it
in writing and it is not going to make one particle of difference,
because the terms were about the two of you and not about a result, and
the boy was on that ground and you were not."

"All right."

"So we have got three questions of Mount Myōboku," said Katsuyu, "and it
is the first thing of any kind that has passed between the mountains in
four hundred years, and my elders have spent five days deciding what to
ask."

---

*Right.*

*She said* arguing *and she said* since Tuesday *and she has never once
used the word about herself.*

*She was outvoted about the bet and she was told she was being modern.*

"What did they want to ask?" he said.

"I am going to tell you all three," said Katsuyu, "because you are going
to hear the third one and understand what it cost, and I would rather you
had it from me than found out in a year.

"The first was about the dry seasons. Myōboku is on a mountain and we are
in a forest, and there is a thing that happens to us in a dry year that
does not happen to them and nobody knows why, and it has been happening
for as long as there have been records and it takes about a tenth of us
each time.

"That is a serious question. I want that understood. It is not moss and
it is not curiosity. It is a tenth of my kind, every dry year, for four
hundred years."

He put the pencil down.

"And the second?"

"The second was about how they count time."

"...How they—"

"It sounds like nothing," said Katsuyu. "It is not nothing. We do not
count it and they do, and it is the reason they have got records and we
have got telling, and the fourth elder has wanted to know how it is done
since before your village had a wall, and she is the one who read your
book aloud to eleven of us over four evenings."

---

"And the third," she said.

She was quiet for a moment.

"The third was: why did we stop speaking."

The yard was very quiet.

"Four hundred years," said Katsuyu. "There is no record. There is no
telling. Nobody at Shikkotsu knows and I have asked, and the oldest
creature on that mountain was not alive for it and neither was anything
she was told it by.

"There was contact and then there was not, and there is not one word
anywhere about why, and we have gone four centuries without a mechanism
for finding out.

"And on the twentieth of the twelfth month a human being fell over on a
training ground and we suddenly had three questions in our hand, and the
oldest of us — who has declined to answer four times in two hundred years
— asked for that one.

"And I argued against it."

---

Sazare sat on the yard stone.

"Say what you argued for," he said.

"All three about how to train a sage," said Katsuyu.

"Say the rest of it."

"There is no rest of it. That is the whole of my position and I made it
on Tuesday and I made it again on Wednesday and I made it four times on
Thursday, and it did not get any better with repetition and I did not
have a second argument to fall back on."

"Give it to me the way you gave it to them."

---

"I cannot teach you," said Katsuyu.

"I know."

"I told you so in your own store on the seventeenth and I told you why,
and the reason has not changed and will not: we cannot lose, so there has
never been a stake, so there is no art. I have got holding and I have got
healing and there is no third thing and four days did not produce one.

"And on the twentieth you went into a state that I have never seen, that
nobody on my mountain has seen, and that I told you about on a step on
the Thursday and let you laugh at.

"You cannot enter it. You will not be able to. Jiraiya has told you and he
is right and I would have told you the same thing if I had known the word
existed.

"And the only creatures in the world who know how to take a human across
that line are on the other mountain.

"They have done it. They have done it repeatedly, over centuries, on
purpose, with a method, and there are two of them who have personally
stood next to a human being and said *there, that is the line, you have
crossed it*.

"That is what is in Myōboku that is not in Shikkotsu.

"And we have got three questions."

---

*Right.*

*Say the cost out loud before she does, because she is going to say it
flatly and I would rather have had it first.*

"You have spent the dry seasons," he said.

"Yes."

"A tenth of your kind. Every dry year. Four hundred years and no
mechanism and no way to ask anybody, and the first time there has ever
been a way to ask, you have argued it off the sheet."

"Yes."

"And you have spent the fourth elder's question, which she has wanted
since before there was a wall on this village."

"Yes."

"And you have spent the third one."

"I have spent the third one," said Katsuyu, "and I want to say what it
was, plainly, once: I have argued my mountain out of the only opportunity
it will ever have to find out why it is alone.

"And I did it on Wednesday afternoon and I have not slept since and I do
not sleep."

---

He sat on the yard stone in the cold.

"How did you win?" he said.

"Badly."

"Katsuyu."

"I want to be accurate," she said. "I did not win an argument. I have
never won an argument on that mountain and I did not win this one.

"I said one thing on Thursday evening and the room stopped, and about
four hours later the oldest of them said *very well* and went away, and
nobody has said anything about it since and nobody is going to.

"So I do not know whether I persuaded her or whether she decided
something."

"What did you say?"

Katsuyu was quiet for a moment.

"Fukasaku asked in a long room on the second of the twelfth month what
the boy had given Shikkotsu," she said. "And you answered *nothing*, and
you were right in every particular, and I said so at the time and then I
said the rest of it.

"So on Thursday I said: the toads asked us what he had given us and we
had to answer *nothing*, and we were correct, and it was the truest thing
said in that room all day.

"And in about a month they are going to find out that we spent
everything we have ever had on him.

"Four hundred years of not speaking, and the first thing we do with it is
give the whole of it away to a human who owes us nothing, has signed
nothing, and will never owe us anything, and who asked me on eleven
Fridays and got yes eleven times.

"And I said: I would like Myōboku to have to sit with that."

---

Sazare did not say anything for a considerable time.

*She has taken his own architecture and turned it round on the mountain
that built it.*

*Fukasaku minuted two things on the second of the twelfth month and made
Shizune write the second one in his own words so that somebody in ninety
years would know: nothing today is under a Myōboku contract, and* I do
not know what it is under instead.

*And in about a month he is going to find out what it is under, and the
answer is going to be three questions spent on a boy with no contract.*

*That is not an argument about a sage. That is not what she said to them
at all.*

*She has told her elders that the most valuable thing Shikkotsu will ever
own is worth spending on proving a point to Myōboku about obligation.*

*And a four-hundred-year-old who declines to answer said* very well *and
went away.*

"You didn't argue for me," he said.

"No," said Katsuyu.

"You argued for the mountain."

"I argued for the mountain," she said, "and the thing I argued *for* is
that we are right about something and they are wrong, and the only way to
demonstrate it costs us everything, and I have been sitting with the fact
that those are not the same reason since about four o'clock on Thursday
afternoon.

"I would like to say that I did it for you and I have gone over it eleven
times and I did not.

"I did it because a toad asked me a question in a long room in front of
five other creatures and I have not been able to put it down."

---

"That's better," said Sazare.

"...I beg your pardon."

"That's better than the other one and I want to say why, because you have
come here four hours late and you have been carrying it since Wednesday
and I do not want you carrying it into next Friday.

"If you had spent your mountain's only leverage in four hundred years on
me because you like me, then in about eleven years somebody at Shikkotsu
is going to say so out loud, and they will be right, and it will have
been a bad decision made for a good reason and you would have to defend
it for the rest of your life.

"You have spent it on a position. On something you believe about how
teaching ought to work, which you have believed since before you met me
and which you found out you believed in a room with a toad in it.

"That is a thing a person is allowed to spend four hundred years on.

"And I happen to be standing at the end of it, and that is my luck and
not your judgement, and it is going to keep both of us out of a
considerable amount of trouble in about a decade."

---

Katsuyu did not say anything for about eleven seconds.

"You have got better at this," she said.

"At what?"

"At the thing in your own book. Chapter two."

"That's the one they've broken."

"They have broken chapter two," said Katsuyu, "and I have not corrected
it and I am not going to, and I will tell you now that when I read your
book aloud over four evenings, the elder who has declined to answer four
times in two hundred years asked me to read chapter two twice.

"I did not think anything of it at the time.

"I have thought a great deal about it since Thursday afternoon."
