# Chapter 63, Scene 3 — Waiting To Be Told

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the yard behind the north store. Sazare Ruri,
age eighteen. POV: Sazare. ~1930 words.*

---

She walked at him.

That was the whole of it. She did not run and she did not do anything
with her hands and she was in ordinary clothes with the haori off, and
she came across the yard at the pace of a woman crossing a yard.

He backed up.

She got a hand in his collar at about the fourth step and stopped.

"Again," said Katsuyu.

---

They did it nine times.

She walked at him from nine feet, at a walk, and he backed up, and she
got hold of him — collar, wrist, collar, wrist, collar, the front of the
coat, collar, wrist, collar — and each time it took between three and five
paces and each time he had done absolutely nothing at all.

On the seventh he got as far as putting his hand out.

On the ninth he did not.

Tsunade let go of his coat and stepped back and said nothing whatsoever,
which was worse than anything she could have said, and went and stood at
the far end of the yard with her arms folded.

Katsuyu said, "Sit down."

---

He sat down on the step of the cart door.

*Say it before she does.*

*Nine times.*

*I had nine feet and a walking woman and about two and a half seconds and
I could have put a wall between us in under a second on any one of them.
Any one. I could have put four inches of graded article across that yard
at ankle height on the first step and it would have cost me almost
nothing, and I have got the range and I have got the material and I do
not need the material, and I did none of it.*

*Nine out of nine.*

*And I was not frightened. That is the part I do not understand. I have
been frightened about four times in my life and I know what it is and it
was not in that yard — I was standing in a yard behind my own store on a
Wednesday morning with a woman I sleep next to walking at me at about
four miles an hour, and there was nothing in it, and I did nothing nine
times.*

"Why?" he said.

"That is the second fault," said Katsuyu, "and I have had it since Monday
evening and I said I would give it to you today."

"Give it to me."

"You are waiting to be told what to make."

---

He sat on the step and did not say anything for some time.

"Say the whole of it," he said.

"There is not very much of it and that is what makes it bad," said
Katsuyu. "It is one sentence and it explains all nine.

"You cannot begin. There is nothing wrong with your hands and nothing
wrong with your speed and nothing wrong with your judgement of what would
have worked — I watched you and you had the answer on at least six of
the nine, I could see it arrive, and on the fourth one you had it in
about a third of a second and then you did not do it.

"You were waiting for somebody to say what the article was."

"Nobody was going to say anything."

"No," said Katsuyu. "Nobody has ever *not* said anything. That is the
whole of it."

---

*Right.*

*Do it properly.*

*Everything I have ever made in my life has arrived with a specification
attached to the front of it by another person.*

*Yagi hands me a patient. That is a specification and it is a better one
than I could write, and it says what the article has to survive and what
it does to somebody if it fails, and it comes with an argument.*

*Kanzaki hands me a grade and a quantity.*

*The board hands me a quota. The study handed me a question. Ibiki handed
me a fee and a form of words and both were in writing and I read them
four times.*

*Even the exercise. She gave me a sphere and a count and I have never once
asked what the number was for and I did not find out until Monday of this
week.*

*And the one object in my life that came with nobody at the front of it
has got a blank panel on the guard and one word on it in my own hand and
the word is* unknown*, and it has been on a shelf where I can see it from
the bench for eleven days.*

*It is the same fault.*

*It is not two faults. It is one fault and it has been in me since I was
nine years old and it has never cost me a single thing, because there has
never once been a situation in my life where nobody was standing at the
front holding the paper.*

He looked up.

"It's the panel," he said.

"Yes," said Katsuyu. "I did not know what the panel was until yesterday
and I have been sitting on that bench putting it together, and I am not
going to pretend I had it before you did."

---

"Do it again," said Tsunade, from the far end of the yard.

"Hime—"

"Do it again, and this time I am going to tell you what to make."

She came across the yard at the same walk.

"Wall," she said, at nine feet.

There was a wall.

It went up in about a third of a second and it was four feet high and
eleven feet across and about three inches thick, and it was graded
properly and it had a batter on the yard side because a wall with a
batter on it does not go over, and he had not thought about a single part
of it.

Tsunade stopped in front of it.

"Right," she said.

---

They did it eleven more times and he got every single one.

*Spike* — a spike, under her lead foot, blunt, at four inches, which was
the height that lifts a heel and does not break an ankle, and he had
chosen blunt without deciding to.

*Trip* — a bar at ankle height with a rounded top.

*Bind* — and he did not have an article for *bind* and made one, in
about a second and a half, out of nothing at all: a collar of graded
crystal at the ankle with the closing act deliberately incomplete so that
it would come apart under about eleven kilos of pull, because a thing
that holds a person's ankle in a yard is a thing that breaks a leg if
they go over.

He did eleven and every one was right and every one was safe and three of
them were better than anything he could have designed at a bench with a
week.

Then she said, "Now do one."

And he did not do one.

---

Nobody said anything for a while.

Tsunade came and sat down on the step next to him, which she had not done
in the whole of the three years he had known her.

"You've got it," she said.

"I've got it."

"Say it out loud anyway."

"It isn't ability and it isn't nerve," said Sazare, "and I would like
that established at the front because both of those are more comfortable
and both of them are wrong.

"I can do the thing. I have just done eleven of them and three were good.
The gap is not between me and the article.

"It is between me and the *decision*.

"There is a step at the front of every operation that everybody else in
this village has been doing since they were about six years old, which
is deciding what the operation is going to be, and it has been done for
me every single time for eleven years by somebody standing in front of me
with a piece of paper.

"And I am extremely good at everything downstream of it.

"And I have got no practice at all at the step itself, and I am
eighteen, and on Friday morning there will not be anybody in front of me
with a piece of paper."

---

"Can it be fixed in three days?" said Tsunade.

"No," said Katsuyu.

"I wasn't asking you."

"I know," said Katsuyu, "and I am answering because he is going to say
*probably not* and then work until two in the morning for three nights,
and I would rather the no came from me.

"It cannot be fixed in three days. It is eleven years old. It can be
*worked on* in three days and he will get better at it and he will get
better at it very fast, because he gets better at everything very fast,
and on Friday morning he will still hesitate the first four times and
possibly the first nine."

Tsunade did not answer.

"May I say the other thing," said Katsuyu.

"Go on."

"It is not a finding and I want it clear that I have no framework for it
and that I am reporting it because it is what I saw and the two of you
are better placed to use it than I am."

---

"He moved away from you nine times out of nine," said Katsuyu.

The yard was quiet.

"Not backwards nine times. Away. On the third he went left and on the
sixth he turned his shoulder and stepped through and behind, and both of
those were good — the sixth was very good, and it was faster than
anything else he did all morning, and it was not a thought, it came off
him the way the close comes off him.

"And on none of the nine did he move toward you.

"Not once. Not by an inch. There is no instance in the whole of the
morning of him closing the distance to another person by any amount at
all, and when I gave him the eleven and told him what to build, four of
the eleven were things that put something *between* the two of you and
seven of them were things that stopped you moving.

"None of the eleven touched you.

"I have watched his hands for eleven weeks and this is the first thing I
have ever seen that I could not put into the fault column, because I do
not know whether it is a fault or a method or something else entirely,
and I have got no word for it and neither has anybody on my mountain."

---

Sazare sat on the step and did not say anything.

*She is right and I have got no answer.*

*And I am not going to have one this morning, because I have just gone
through it and there is nothing there — I did not decide to go left on
the third and I did not decide to turn my shoulder on the sixth and I
have got no report of either of them.*

*They came off me the way the close comes off me.*

*Which means they are the same age.*

He looked at his own hands.

Beside him, Tsunade had gone completely still.

He noticed it and did not look at her, because he had spent three years
learning exactly how much of a thing he was entitled to see, and because
whatever was on her face at that moment was not being managed and she did
not know it was showing.

"Right," she said, after a while, and her voice was entirely ordinary.
"Again. Nine feet."
