# Chapter 36, Scene 1 — First Consignment

*Act 4, part C. Konoha, the quartermaster's yard. Sazare Ruri, age
seventeen. POV: Sazare. ~1,389 words.*

---

The first consignment under the Kirigakure clause went out on a
Thursday in the fifth month, which was appropriate, because everything
in Sazare Ruri's professional life had gone out on a Thursday since he
was sixteen.

He had expected — he established this afterwards, and was embarrassed
about it — that it would feel like something.

It did not. It was crating.

Kanzaki had the yard doors open and two of her own people and a
carpenter from public works who had been requisitioned for the day and
was extremely put out about it, and the whole operation took four
hours and consisted almost entirely of straw.

"Straw," said Kanzaki, "is the entire problem, and I have thought about
nothing else for a fortnight. Your material is dimensionally stable and
chemically inert and it does not care about damp, and it will crack if
a cart goes into a rut at speed, and there is one hundred and forty
miles of road between this yard and the coast, most of which I am told
is *adequate*."

"Adequate?"

"That was the word in the transit assessment. I have written back."

She had built a case system. Sazare read it in about eleven seconds and
then read it properly for four minutes because it was very good: a
double crate, the inner one suspended off the outer on four leather
straps that could be tensioned from outside without opening anything,
and the straw packed in courses rather than stuffed.

"Courses," he said.

"Yes, and I want you to notice that I stole that off you and I am not
going to pretend otherwise." She was doing three things at once and
did not look up. "One layer laid flat, next layer crossed, third
crossed again. Stuffed straw settles into one mass and transmits every
shock across the whole of it. Crossed courses argue with each other."

"That's exactly the mechanism."

"I know it's exactly the mechanism, I have listened to you explain it
eleven times." She straightened. "It also means that when a case
arrives cracked I will be able to tell them precisely which layer
failed, and they will not be able to tell me it was the material,
because the material will be sitting there intact next to the piece
that isn't."

---

The manifest was the part he had not been braced for.

It was a single sheet, and it was ruled, and Kanzaki filled it in
standing at the trestle with the brass balance beside her the way she
had filled in three years of ruled sheets standing at trestles.

*Consignment 1. Fabricated substrate, seal blank grade, unfaulted.*

*Units: 210. Aggregate mass: 4.13 kg. Rejected at audit: 61 units,
retained failure tin.*

*Fabricator: RURI, S.*

*Auditor: K.U.*

And then, on the line below, in a printed heading he had never seen on
any of the three hundred sheets that had preceded it:

*Consigned under: Konoha–Kiri Normalisation Instrument, Sched. 4, cl.
9. Treaty ref. attached.*

He looked at that for a while.

"It's the same form," he said.

"It is *not* the same form, it's got two extra lines and a different
stock number and it took the stationery office nine weeks."

"It's the same form with two extra lines."

"Yes," said Kanzaki, and stopped writing, and looked at him properly
for the first time that morning. "Is that a problem?"

Sazare considered it honestly, because she had asked honestly and
because she was the one person in the village who had never once asked
him a question she did not want the true answer to.

"No," he said. "I thought it was going to be."

"So did I. I had a speech."

"You had a speech?"

"I had about four sentences, and they were good, and I have been
carrying them round since the fourteenth in case you came into this
yard and went grey." Kanzaki pushed her glasses up onto her forehead.
"You've not gone grey. You've gone *interested*, which I did not
predict and which is going to annoy me for a week."

"I'm interested in the crate."

"Everybody is interested in the crate, it is a *very good crate*, that
is not the point." She put the pen down. "Ruri. A year ago I
weighed nineteen grains of your work on that balance and entered them
at four-tenths of a gram apiece. Today I have consigned four kilos of
it to a foreign government under a treaty. If there is a version of you
that has an objection to that, I would rather it came out in this yard
than in a year."

---

He walked round the crate once, slowly, the way he went round
everything.

"Three years ago," he said, "the audit was a condition of my amnesty. I
was a foreign national under a residence order and the reason you
weighed everything I made was that the village needed to know I wasn't
putting crystal in the walls."

"Correct."

"And I signed that. Under duress, in the sense that the alternative was
not having anywhere to live, but I read it and I understood it and I
signed it."

"Also correct."

"This one I read twice, and I argued four clauses through Kōji, and I
got the medical exemption on disposal and the ownership schedule on
the reverse recall and the one about combat output not counting against
quota." He put a hand flat on the outer case. "And then I stood against
a wall while two governments agreed it out loud and I did not object,
because I did not object. It's a good agreement. It's the best terms
anybody in my position has ever been offered and I have looked, and
there isn't a second example."

"But."

"But nothing. That's what I've been waiting for. I've been waiting a
fortnight for the *but* to arrive and it hasn't." He looked at the
manifest. "Somebody in Kirigakure is going to open that case and take
out a seal blank I made on a Tuesday and use it for something I will
never know about, and I have turned that over about four hundred times
and I cannot make it hurt."

Kanzaki wrote something down.

"Why not?" she said.

"Because it's a seal blank." He said it slowly, working it out as it
came. "It's not me. It's four kilos of stone that came out of a bucket
of aggregate from the yard behind the laundry. I made it and it's good
and it's exactly to spec and I'm proud of it, and it is not a piece of
my family and it is not a piece of my sister's network and it is not a
thing anybody has taken off me. It's a job. I did the job and they're
paying for it."

"And three years ago?"

"Three years ago I'd have been counting who owned it."

"Yes," said Kanzaki. "You would. I have the sheets."

---

She closed the manifest at the eleventh hour and the cart went at
noon.

Sazare watched it out of the yard because he had never watched anything
he made leave a building before — it had always been handed across a
counter, or put on a step, or left where somebody would find it — and
he stood in the gateway with his hands at his sides while four hundred
and thirteen kilos of crate and straw and stone went down the hill on a
public works cart with a driver who did not know what was in it.

Kanzaki came and stood next to him for the last of it.

"Right," she said. "Next month's quota's the same, and there's an
increment in the fourth quarter that I have already told them is
optimistic."

"How optimistic?"

"Eleven per cent. Which is achievable and which will cost you your
evenings, and I have written *achievable with adverse effect on
fabricator* on the schedule, which they will ignore, and which will be
extremely useful in about nine months when it turns out I was right."

"You put that in writing?"

"I put everything in writing," said Kanzaki Uta, "and I have been doing
it about you since you were sixteen years old, and one day somebody
above me is going to read three years of it in one sitting and get a
considerable surprise."
