# Chapter 71, Scene 2 — Incoming

*Act 5, part A. Konoha, the Hokage's tower. Tsunade, age
fifty-three. POV: Tsunade. ~740 words.*

---

The register got a new section that week, because the old
sections had no infrastructure for what was arriving.

*Situation report. The boy has opened a second front. I have
spent three months as the artillery in this relationship and I
am now, for the first time since the Second War, UNDER fire,
and the after-action reviews are not flattering.*

*Engagement one, Saturday: "beautiful in the mornings before
you are beautiful on purpose." Eleven seconds of total system
failure over BREAKFAST. I lost the line I was reading. I have
chaired executions with a steadier pulse. And the worst of it —
chart it, doctor, the chart is the discipline — the worst of it
was watching him TAKE the recoil: pink to his ears, pulse
visibly climbing at the throat, and the cup never wavered.
The blush used to be the fuse that saved me. The fuse is now
apparently decorative.*

*Engagement two, Sunday, the yard: he took a hair off my
shoulder in passing. Didn't look. Didn't break stride. I
signed a manifest wrong and had to initial the correction and
Kanzaki's office will keep that initial for thirty years.*

*Engagement three: I have no notes for engagement three. I was
combatant, not observer. Shizune was present and has minuted it
as weather.*

*Assessment: he fights exactly like he fabricates — patient,
graded, nothing false, everything placed — and he has correctly
identified that I have no incoming doctrine whatsoever. Forty
years of being the one who leans in. My entire kit is outgoing.
When it comes back at me, level, with citations, from a man
whose face pays the cost of every round in copper and fires
anyway—*

*The register will simply record that the woman who wrote "an
embarrassment to the profession" three weeks ago has revised
her position. It is a MARVELLOUS embarrassment. I have not
been this off balance since I learned to walk on water, and
the sensation is the same: certain drowning, technically
survived, again and again, by something that has no business
holding.*

---

The investigation, meanwhile, was going badly, which was its
own species of enjoyable.

She ran it in the gaps of the week, methodology his, and the
file refused to close. *The source has chapters* — that
finding stood; the curriculum order was too clean for
improvisation. But the physical evidence would not surface. A
man who owned eighteen books, all catalogued, none orange. No
purchases — she had, in a low moment she declined to chart,
had Shizune pull his ryō ledger, which was pristine and
slightly heartbreaking: tools, lamp oil, eleven ryō of squid.
No library record. No bookshelf, because the man had never in
his life owned a shelf's worth of anything that was not
notation.

*Where does a man with no shelf keep a manual?*

*On his person,* answered the investigator, who had been
married to the method for three months. *Where he keeps
everything load-bearing. The coat. It has been in the COAT,
in this tower, at this table, in that BATH—*

And there the file sat, one search of one coat from closure,
and she did not search the coat, on principle — the same
principle three weeks standing: the clear sky was worth more
than the forecast. Things happened to her out of that sky
now. A woman would be a fool to shoot down her own weather.

*The auditor is patient,* she wrote, closing the section, on
the Tuesday night, with the boy asleep beside her at the
correct depth for the first time in a month, her hands at
their post, the campaign's fourth engagement (unlogged,
his mouth, her jaw, the pause before the lamp went out)
still humming somewhere at the base of her spine.

*The auditor is patient, and the auditor is losing on
points, and the auditor has never enjoyed an investigation
less or a war more in her entire preposterous life.*

*Item, appended, small hours: we are getting careless. I
know we are. I took the salute off his shoulder-touch in
front of the memo clerk today and it was worth it and it
was CARELESS, and the part of me that ran a village through
a war winter knows exactly what carelessness costs and
files this anyway under: worth it. The village isn't
watching. Nobody watches the ordinary.*

*The Hyūga appointment is Monday. Clan garment business.
Routine.*

*Note to self: be Hokage on Monday. It's only an hour.*
