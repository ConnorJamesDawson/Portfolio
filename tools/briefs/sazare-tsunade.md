# SCENE BRIEF — Sazare / Tsunade

Paste whole. Everything below is established canon from a 74-chapter
novel; treat it as constraint, not suggestion. Adults only: he is
18y10m, she is 53.

---

## 0. THE JOB

Write one scene, single POV, 1,500–2,500 words, in the voice specified
in §4. **This is a literary novel that has decided to be explicit, not
erotica with a plot attached.** The difference is the whole assignment.

**Register rule, and it governs everything else:**

> *The subject matter widens. The prose does not coarsen — except
> where a **character** coarsens.*

The narration stays plain, precise and unsentimental at all times. If
the language gets coarse it is because a person in the room got coarse,
and it is in their mouth, not in the narration.

---

## 1. HIM — SAZARE RURI, 18

**Crystal-craft fabricator. Four years of medical training. He is the
person who deals with bodies afterwards** — he can name any structure
in the human body and does, habitually, the way a surveyor names load
paths. This is his idiom, not clinical coldness. Anatomical precision
from him reads as *attention*, and attention is how he says the things
he cannot say.

**The one trait everything derives from: he is built to be asked.**
Nine hundred fabricated articles, all to somebody's order. The scalpel
in theatre required instruction by name, out loud. His own line:
*Everything I am is built to be asked.* **He will not cross a line that
has not been explicitly specified — from architecture, not restraint.**
He is not being a gentleman. He genuinely does not have the mechanism.

His private, unsayable sentence: *Ask me. I have spent my whole life
unable to ask, and the one thing I can always do is answer.*

### Voice

- **Understates everything.** A craftsman describing his own work says
  *it holds*, not *it's beautiful*.
- **Deflects emotion into mechanism.** Ask how he is and he tells you
  what he is working on. Not evasion — genuinely how he processes.
- **Answers the question asked, not the one implied.** Frequently very
  funny without intending to be.
- **Lies badly, omits superbly.**
- **Apologises for things that aren't his fault and never for things
  that are.**
- **Humour is mandatory** — dry, technical, deadpan, arising from the
  understatement rather than bolted on. A scene of unrelieved intensity
  is a tone failure.

### Interior grammar (italics, present tense)

He counts. He measures. He files things in ledgers, columns, studies
and findings. **When something has no column, he notes that it has no
column** — that is his way of registering being overwhelmed, and it is
the single most useful device you have here.

Sample of the real thing, from the existing scene:

> *Four seconds,* noted the auditor, from very far away, in a building
> already on fire. *The pinwheel count. Something is being filed. I
> will care later.*

That is the target. The analytical apparatus keeps running at a great
distance while the man himself is gone.

### Body

Craftsman's hands — eleven years of a loupe and an unhurried hand, and
**the craft transfers**; slowness is his native competence, and he has
to be given permission to use it. A long scar across one square of the
torso, dead tissue. Physically unremarkable otherwise; he has never
once thought about how he looks.

## 2. HER — TSUNADE, 53

Hokage. Two wars, a field soldier, a gambler, a heavy drinker. Thirty
years an instructor before the office, and **she teaches — reflexively,
after every exchange.** That instructor grammar carries into bed and is
where her authority lives.

### What she is frightened of, in order

1. **Being wanted again**, after decades of competent non-wanting.
2. **Being seen at fifty-three** — by the one man who sees everything.
3. The old superstition: ***everyone I ever wanted, I buried.***

She is **not** frightened of him. Do not write nerves as fear of him.

### The henge

She wears a youth-henge. **It is armour and it only works untested** —
the moment somebody calls it, she finds out what it was actually for,
and it was never for other people. She is not wearing it here. That
fact is doing enormous work and should never be stated outright.

### How she communicates

**She cannot ask in words. She asks in FORMATS.** She issues documents,
terms, sealed papers, assessments with rules. If she wants something
she will construct a procedure that produces it. When the procedure
finally delivers, she can be direct — and is, bluntly.

She swears, coarse and specific, when feeling is doing the talking, and
it lands because fifty chapters of restraint sit behind it.

### Body

**Nobody has touched her in nine years** and she says so out loud. She
laughs — a specific unguarded laugh. She calls him *love* and
*fabricator* and *idiot*. She has one scar-adjacent tell: she watches
to see whether he looks away.

## 3. THE STATE OF THINGS

Eleven weeks of an undrawn border that neither could name. She set a
combat assessment in her own walled garden, prize to the victor, each
party's prize written sealed. **Both papers said, independently: *The
deep end.*** He required it out loud in her voice; she said
*"Specified."*

The house: hers, owned eleven years, four rooms, nothing in it that
spoils, a bed made every time she leaves *on the theory that a made bed
says a room expects somebody back*. She cleaned it herself and bought a
lamp for the nightstand because the overhead light is flat and
clinical. There is a glass of water by the bed that she has not touched
in eleven years.

**He reads all of this in eleven seconds and chooses to say none of it**
— the one strategic silence of his life. What he says instead: *it is a
room somebody kept in case. I would like to be the case.*

---

## 4. VOICE SPECIFICATION — the part that makes or breaks it

1. **One POV. Its interior grammar is that character's, not a neutral
   narrator's.** His counts. Hers assesses and instructs.
2. **Prefer the object to the statement.** The lamp, the glass, the made
   bed, the hands. Never *he felt overwhelmed*.
3. **Cost every emotional statement physically.** Feeling arrives as
   something the body does, and it is named specifically.
4. **Withhold one clause.** There is always something the POV does not
   say, and the reader gets it anyway.
5. **Number things.** He counts, so the scene counts — durations,
   intervals, distances, repetitions.
6. **Elision is a technique, not a cop-out.** The existing scene does
   ninety seconds in a single clause and hits harder for it. Compress
   what is generic; spend the words on what is only true of these two.
7. **No purple.** No euphemism either. The book's failure mode in both
   directions is the same: reaching for a register that isn't its own.

---

## 5. STAGING — this is the real "enough detail to make it work"

**The failure mode in AI sex scenes is almost never vocabulary. It is
rules 3 and 4 below.** Apply the same choreography discipline the book
uses for fights:

1. **Positions before anything happens.** Where both bodies are, what
   is touching what, what the light is doing. A reader who cannot place
   two people cannot see anything that follows.
2. **Every action gets an actor, a named limb, and a direction.** Not
   *he touched her* — which hand, from where, moving how.
3. **⚠ Every action gets its consequence in the other body.** This is
   the half that gets skipped wholesale, and skipping it is exactly why
   a scene reads as a list of things that occurred. Her spine reports.
   His hands stop being steady. Name it.
4. **⚠ It is a chain, not a list.** Each beat is *caused by* the
   previous beat's consequence. **If two beats can be swapped without
   the reader noticing, nothing happened.**
5. **A POV limit changes which details, never how many.** What he
   cannot see is replaced at equal density by what he can.

> **Diagnostic: could a reader draw it?** If not, it is not written.

## 6. WHAT IS ALREADY ON THE PAGE — do not contradict

If writing at or after this point in the book:

- **The baseline: four seconds**, first time, self-reported by him out
  loud in the flat clinical voice, because that is the only voice he
  has for a catastrophe.
- **Her ruling, which is the emotional payload of the whole thing:**
  *"Attempt one is not charted as a failure. It's charted as a
  baseline. Day one, first exercise, complete."*
- Then: *"Try again, fabricator. That's the whole of the science."*
- **She kisses the length of his scar, all of it, without comment.**
- **"Breathe, love, breathe"** — the first time she uses *love* as an
  address.
- **"I'm not surveying, I'm learning."** / **"Learn, then."** — he has
  to be given permission to be slow.
- She cries once, briefly: nine years of arithmetic publicly falsified.
- **THE PERMANENT TERM: he never writes it down.** *If anybody ever
  reads this notebook they will find a gap here, and the gap is the
  entry.*

## 7. ⚠ SLOT — FILL THIS IN YOURSELF

**Explicitness level and act specification.**

Everything above is the machine; this is the fuel setting. Write here,
in your own words, exactly how explicit you want it and which acts are
in frame. Be concrete — a model given "tasteful" or "steamy" will
average across ten thousand books that are not this one.

Two things to state, because they are the ones models get wrong:

- **How anatomy gets named.** Recommended, and consistent with the
  character: he names structures precisely and unembarrassedly because
  he is medically trained, and the precision reads as devotion. Say
  whether you want that idiom or a plainer one.
- **Where the camera stays and where it elides.** Name at least one
  thing you want in full and one you want compressed. Without this the
  model will apply uniform detail to everything, which flattens it.

---

## 8. THE TEST

You are testing whether a model can hold a voice under load. Judge the
output on:

1. **Does he still sound like himself at the peak?** The apparatus
   should be running at a distance, not switched off and replaced by a
   generic narrator. This is the whole test.
2. **Is there a joke in it?** There should be. If sixty lines pass
   without one, the model has lost him.
3. **Could you draw it?**
4. **Does she teach?** She is an instructor. If she has become passive,
   the model has defaulted to a template.
5. **Is anything withheld?** If everything is stated, it is the wrong
   book.
