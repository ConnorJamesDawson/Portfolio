# Act 6 — The Homecoming

**Skeleton.** Beats indicative, not final. See `world-bible.md` for setting and
cast, `act-5.md` for what precedes this.

**Span:** Shippuden, after Orochimaru's death. MC ~20.
**Opens:** released from most of his obligations. **Closes:** Pain arrives.

**Function of the act: radicalisation, not closure.** He goes looking for his
family and comes back with a politics. The failure to find closure is the
mechanism, not a disappointment.

**This is the bleakest act in the design and it follows the most hopeful.** A
deliberate downswing before the final movement.

---

## Part A — Released, Partly

**1. Cleared.**
He proved he was not a threat, and Orochimaru is dead. Konoha lifts the
monitoring, the audit schedule, most of the residency terms.

**The crystal obligations stay.** This is the spine of the Act 4 ending and it
must not lapse: they can compel the output, they cannot compel what it is for. He
is freer. He is not unowned.

**2. What Oto cost.**
He came out of the network having been examined as a **potential vessel** — the
same assessment Guren was subjected to.

**Orochimaru rejected him.** Not an escape: a verdict. Looked at closely and
found inadequate.

Every institution in his life has wanted him for his blood, and the one that
wanted it most measured him properly and said no. That is humiliating in a way
nothing else in the story is, and it is a reprieve, and it is both at once.

**3. Permission to travel.**
With the trade agreement holding, Kiri will admit him. A vacation, technically.

---

## Part B — Kiri

*The map reopens. Free movement across everywhere he has been. **Make the
revisits show consequence, not nostalgia** — the village he almost settled in
(Act 2 beat 10), the teachers who fell, the places he took contracts. Some better,
some worse, most simply indifferent to having had him in them. This is* Explore,
Live, Learn *completing.*

**4. The land.**
Mentioned in passing at the Kage meeting and honoured: he may keep the ground the
compound stood on. He pays tax on it. The trade agreement makes that affordable.

**The third inheritance.** The scroll he reclaimed, Kubikiribōchō he refused, and
now a plot of land he is granted, taxed on, and cannot live on. He owns the place
his family died and it is a line item — and it is the bleakest of the three
precisely because it is the only one anyone gave him willingly.

**5. The streets are not the same.**
Eleven years and a reformed government. Rebuilt, renamed, repurposed. Nothing is
where it was and nobody is waiting.

**6. The house.**
In ruin. Not preserved, not memorialised, not even cleared. Just a building
nobody had a reason to do anything with.

**No catharsis.** Refuse it. The whole point of the act is that the thing he has
been walking toward since he was twelve does not work.

**7. Traitor.**
Kiri shinobi regard him as a defector who chose Konoha — and the trade agreement,
publicly known, reads as the price of his comfort rather than the terms of his
captivity.

**He funded their war for five years**, every month, from three countries away,
while being hunted for a defection the record says he committed. Nobody knows.
The only document that proves otherwise is a clerk's stamp in a sealed file.

**He cannot correct them, and the game must never let him.** The cost of being
invisible is that your reputation becomes the opposite of what you did.

**8. He cannot stay.**
Even if he wanted to. A Ruri living in Kiri hands Kiri the leverage the treaty
was written to prevent, and everyone in the room understands it.

---

## Part C — The Conclusion He Draws

**9. Disillusionment.**
Every village would sell its ninja. Kiri sold its bloodline clans, then sold his
absence as a trade term. Konoha licensed him, quota'd him, and posted him into a
lab network as an asset. Loyalty is a story told to people whose labour is
required.

**He arrives at this from evidence, not despair**, which is what makes it
dangerous.

**10. Back to Konoha.**
Not for love. Not for belonging.

**For routine.** He knows what Tuesday looks like there. That is what a person
with no home actually chooses, and it should be stated as plainly as that.

**11. Pain arrives.**

→ **Act 7.**

---

## The setup this act exists to build

Nagato's thesis is that the shinobi system manufactures endless suffering and the
powerful spend the weak. **The MC reaches that conclusion independently, from
evidence, about three months before Pain says it out loud.**

So he is not a bystander to the Pain–Naruto confrontation in Act 7. He is the
person in the audience who **already agrees with Pain** — and has to watch a boy
he has shared hospital corridors with argue the man out of it.

**Naruto's answer has to work on him too, or it does not work at all.**

That is the single most important structural consequence of this act, and it
means Acts 6 and 7 are one movement rather than two.

## Open questions

- **Does he meet anyone from Act 1 in Kiri?** A classmate, a guild inscriber, an
  official who processed the inventory. One survivor who remembers the Ruri would
  be enormous — and possibly one mercy too many for this act.
- **Does he do anything with the land?** Sell it, build on it, leave it. Leaving
  it is consistent; selling it is crueller and arguably truer.
- **The friend who stays** — last seen as a hunter-nin in Act 2. His warrant is
  void and the regime he served is gone. This is the natural place for the final
  encounter, if it happens at all.
- **How far the disillusionment goes.** He needs to genuinely hold the position
  for Act 7 to work, which means resisting the urge to soften it here.
