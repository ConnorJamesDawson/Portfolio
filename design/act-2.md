# Act 2 — The Road

**Revised for the novel.** The original version of this file predated the prose
and assumed the game's Act 1 (rebel HQ recruitment, formal deployment, staged
missing-nin listing). The prose broke those assumptions; this version matches
what is now on the page.

**Span:** ages 13 to 17 — four years, and his formative ones. He enters it
malnourished, mute by choice, with a ruined right arm and nightmares; he leaves
it solvent, capable, hunted, and compromised.
**Opens:** a boat in the fog. **Closes:** the floor, and the road to Waves.

**Function of the act:** *Explore, Live, Learn.* He recovers a body, then a
craft, then almost a life — and pays for every increase in capability with a
piece of himself. The act must be genuinely warm in its middle or the fall at
the end lands on nothing.

---

## Corrections carried in from the prose

**He is not a deserter and cannot be one.** He never graduated and never served.
What he is wanted for is real: the killing of a state executioner, mid-sentence,
in front of four hundred witnesses, with a kekkei genkai. **Kiri knows exactly
who he is** — Schedule B, four names, and a district clerk's note that the
youngest was better than his grade suggested. The state deduced nothing. It has
paperwork.

**No badge.** He never received a headband, so there is nothing to slash and
nothing to wear. No outlaw iconography anywhere on him. His camouflage is not a
disguise — it is the truth: a scarred craftsman boy with no affiliation. The
bingo book entry exists; his body does not announce it.

**The crowd wound is specific.** Crowds were his camouflage for fourteen months
and remain so. What he cannot be in is **a crowd facing one thing** — an
audience, an announcement, a stage, anything with a platform and people arranged
toward it. Markets are fine. Gatherings are not.

**The craft is radioactive.** The last thing he grew came out through his own
arm and — in his own accounting — killed his family a hundred and forty seconds
early. Rehabilitating the craft is a major arc of this act, not an establishing
beat.

## Part A — Triage (age 13)

**1. The crossing.**
The boat, the fog, fourteen silent people. He answers the only question anyone
asks him with *I make earrings*.

**2. The sorting station.**
Not the main HQ — a station abroad where the resistance-in-exile triages its
own. Fighters stay to fight. Runners carry. Refugees work where they can.

He is the one thing the triage has no column for: thirteen, one-armed,
starving, mute, and the last of a bloodline half the room feels some way about.
He is also *Kohaku's* — which is worth exactly as much as it costs.

**3. Pointed at Hot Water.**
He cannot help anyone as he is. There is a medic in the Land of Hot Water — one
of theirs, out of the life now — who can restore the movement in his arm.

**The route is Kohaku's road.** "Nagihama first, then over to the Land of Hot
Water." He leaves the country on his sister's escape route, the one his mother
refused to take.

The warnings travel with him, from Ryōsuke, informal and unadorned — including
the one about the people who collect bloodline orphans, who will find him
eventually, whose offer will be good, and which is not an offer.

## Part B — The Body (ages 13–14)

**4. The medic.**
The Land of Hot Water: a country from Mizuho's list of "the nine that don't
matter," full of hot springs, tourists, and people whose lives turn out to
matter entirely — the first lesson falsified, ambiently, without anyone arguing.

The medic restores his hands **slowly**. Months. Guided treatment, the rubber
ball, daily work — the boat medic's "every day for a year" was true, and this
completes it rather than overriding it. The skill is knowing how; the body does
the labour. **Function returns fully. The scar stays** — wrist to elbow, three
exits. It is the only record of the square he carries on his body.

*Constraint, load-bearing:* medical restoration in this story is slow and
knowledge-bound, never miraculous. If old damage could be waved away, every
injury in the back half loses its teeth and Tsunade stops being special.

**5. The two recoveries.**
Malnutrition first — the medic feeds him before she treats him. The nightmares
second, and slower, and never entirely.

**This is the first real medical ninjutsu he ever sees, and it is performed on
him, for months, by the first adult since the square to do him a kindness that
costs something.** Nothing is said about it. The root of everything he becomes
in Act 4 goes in here.

**6. The hands, and what they won't do.**
Restored function, and he still cannot put them over a medium. The block is not
physical any more and everyone involved knows it.

## Part C — The Craft (ages 14–15)

**7. The first piece.**
The rehabilitation of the craft, earned over a chapter — refusal, failure, a
crooked earring grown badly in a rented room. The first thing he has made since
the spike. The first meal bought with work his mother taught him.

**8. Buying a teacher.**
No academy will touch an unaffiliated foreign boy with a wanted bloodline. His
instructors are the fallen — a disgraced jōnin, a retired mercenary, a drunk
with one good technique left. **Every teacher is a preview of what he might
become.** Training costs money; money comes from work; the economy of the act.

**9. Grasping in the dark.**
The experiments, and what they cost. He develops **workshop crystal** — fluent
at structures, barriers, traps, precision, anything prepared; hopeless at
speed, armour, anything reactive. **A fortifier, not a duelist** — the culvert
lesson formalised: he wins by having been somewhere first.

**10. The bingo book.**
His own name. Not a sketch of a stranger — his *name*, his clan, his technique,
because Kiri never had to guess. The entry is the Schedule B file wearing a
price. Somebody assessed him and wrote down what he is worth: the fourth ledger
of his life.

**11. Courier again, and the money goes home.**
He picks up carrying work because he is unusually, uncomfortably good at it —
the code she taught him as a game, now used in the direction she never let him
carry anything real. Through the same channel, his earnings start going back to
the network. Nobody asked him to. It is the only relationship with home he has
left.

## Part D — The Almost-Life and the Fall (ages 15–17)

**12. The place he almost stays.** *(Whole chapter. Protected. Do not cut for
pace.)*
A town, a workshop, steady work, someone kind with no agenda. Months pass. He
is nearly happy and nearly ordinary. This is the warm centre the entire act
exists to earn — and he leaves it, because the hunter is close and the money
has to move.

**13. Jirō.** *(POV chapter — his side.)*
Reconnaissance stream, out and about, searching: exactly what he was built for.
He knows Sazare never served; what he believes is his own account of the square
and the people Sazare left with. Never wrong in his own frame. How he got the
file, and why he wanted it, is his chapter to answer.

**14. Survived, not won.**
The encounter, at ~16. Sazare lives through it by prepared ground — a trap laid
two days early, a wall where a wall should not be — and wins nothing. He
understands with total clarity that he cannot survive the second visit as he
is. **Four years** after the street parting, and the mask is exactly where the
prose promised it would be.

**15. The hinge.** *(Act midpoint by weight, whatever the chapter number.)*
He needs training he cannot afford, urgently. Fabrication will not cover it in
time. **He takes the killing contract deliberately**, knowing what he is buying
and what he is paying with. He can name the reason. That is worse than
drifting.

**16. The work changes.**
Tier-three becomes normal. Craft skills persist, repurposed — the same
precision, aimed differently. Growing power reads as loss, never reward.
**Guren's first approach** lands here: she buys him a meal, admires his work,
asks for nothing, and makes sure he knows that somewhere in the world is a
person who could teach him properly. **The scroll rumour** surfaces through the
courier network: sacked compounds get looted, looted things get sold.

**17. The floor.**
A contract on another bloodline user. Whether he knows before he takes it is
the darkest dial in the design — still deliberately unresolved. He does it. He
is very good at this now.

**Close:** solvent, capable, hunted, alone, seventeen, and the money still goes
home every month to a war he has not seen in four years.

→ **Act 3, the Land of Waves.**

## Keeping the war alive

The civil war does not resolve until much later, so it runs as background for
this whole act. Seed it at intervals — a rumour the Mizukage is not himself, a
setback that makes his remittance matter, a courier who doesn't come back.
Jirō's POV chapter doubles as a war-state carrier from the hunter's side.

## Open questions

- **Does he know the floor contract is a bloodline user before taking it?**
- **The person in the almost-stay town** — a friend carries the beat; anything
  more changes the act's register and should be chosen on purpose.
- **What Jirō does at the end of the encounter** — presses, withdraws, or
  reports something other than what he found.
