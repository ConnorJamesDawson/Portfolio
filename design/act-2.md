# Act 2 — The Road

First draft beat sheet. See `world-bible.md` for setting, cast, and canon
anchors; `act-1.md` for what precedes this.

**Span:** MC ~12 at the opening, ~15–16 by the close.
**Opens:** rebel HQ, immediately after the execution.
**Closes:** competent, solvent, and compromised — with the floor reached.

**Function of the act:** the game's title theme — *Explore, Live, Learn*. He sees
the world, is briefly happy in it, and pays for every increase in capability with
a piece of himself. The act must be genuinely warm in its middle or the fall at
the end lands on nothing.

---

## Part A — The Briefing

**1. The last Ruri.**
Rebel HQ. Someone runs the names for him. There is no one. Not missing, not
detained — no one. This is the fact that pushes him out of the country, not the
grief: grief has somewhere to go, and this doesn't.

**2. He can't walk the streets.**
Specific, not atmospheric: **he cannot be in crowds.** Markets, gatherings,
anywhere with a raised platform. Derived directly from the execution. It shapes
where he can work for the rest of the game and makes him a road character by
default.

**3. The war costs money.**
He overhears, then asks about, the shinobi leaving to work abroad and send
earnings back. A civil war bleeds manpower and coin at the same rate. This is a
job, and it is the only one he can do.

**4. The briefing, and the warnings.**
He is **deployed, not deserting** — and he is told so plainly.

- The missing-nin listing is a cover. **The listing is also real.** The regime
  doesn't know it's staged, so the bingo book entry is genuine and the hunters
  sent after him are genuine.
- No contact with the village. Ever. If he's taken, he was never theirs.
- **Orochimaru's people will find him.** They find everyone like him. The offer
  will be good. It is not an offer, it is an acquisition.

He is twelve and he says yes, because there is nothing else in the building for
him.

**5. The crossing.**
First border. Patrols, papers he doesn't have, a night spent in a culvert. The
world starts one field further than he has ever been.

## Part B — Live

The warm middle. Without this the act is a misery march and the fall at the end
means nothing.

**6. The first job.**
Fabrication. A jeweller, or a quartermaster, or a farmer who needs a seal
re-cut. He is good at it. He is *paid* for it. He eats a meal he bought with
work his mother taught him, and it is the first uncomplicated thing that has
happened to him in a year.

**7. The first lie falls.**
He meets people from a country the Academy taught him to despise, and they are
neither soft nor treacherous nor grasping — they are just people, in a place
with different weather. This is the deprogramming engine: **every region
personally falsifies a lesson he was taught by someone he trusted.**

Run this all act. Each falsification should be small, specific, and delivered by
someone with no idea they are doing it.

**8. Buying a teacher.**
No academy will take a missing-nin. His instructors are the fallen — disgraced
jōnin, retired mercenaries, deserters, drunks with one good technique left.
**Every person who makes him stronger is a preview of what he might become**, and
some are further down that road than he wants to look at.

Training costs money. Money comes from work. This is the economy the whole act
runs on.

**9. Grasping in the dark.**
He experiments with the bloodline alone, badly, and arrives somewhere nobody
would have sent him.

He is fluent in everything resembling craft — structures, barriers, traps,
precision growth, anything set up in advance. He is hopeless at everything
resembling combat — speed, clones, armour, anything reactive.

**He is a fortifier, not a duelist.** He wins fights by having been somewhere
first and loses every fight he didn't choose. This is not a power-level fudge; it
is a childhood spent at a bench instead of a training ground, and it holds all
the way to the last scene of the game.

**The theory obsession, running.** He is not only experimenting — he is trying to
build a working model of chakra with no source material. He buys books he can
barely afford, copies scrolls he isn't allowed to keep, and interrogates every
teacher he pays for far more than he bought. Five years of reconstructing a
discipline from scraps and secondhand rumour.

This is what makes Act 4 beat 5 land, and it is why medical ninjutsu is
reachable for him later: sustained precision chakra control plus an appetite for
theory is exactly the profile the discipline demands.

**10. The place he almost stays.**
A village, a workshop, steady work, someone kind to him with no agenda. Months
pass. He is nearly happy and he is nearly ordinary.

He leaves it, because the money has to go home and because the hunters are
closer than he told anyone. **This is the beat the entire act exists to earn** —
he could have had a life, he was offered one, and the war he is funding took it
instead.

**11. The bingo book.**
He sees his own face, his own name, and a number. Not a threat — a *price*.
Somebody assessed him and wrote down what he is worth.

From here his notoriety is the world's difficulty dial. Regions never lock; they
get more dangerous.

**12. Kohaku's hands.**
He picks up courier work — dead drops, sealed crystal, the code she taught him
as a game. He is unusually, uncomfortably good at it. **He survives on a skill
she gave him without telling him what it was for.**

She stays present in the game without ever appearing in it.

**13. Guren, first approach.**
No pressure. She buys him a meal, admires his work, and does not ask him for
anything. She can see in ten seconds that he is self-taught, and says so kindly.

The tell is that she doesn't make an offer. She just makes sure he knows that
somewhere in the world there is a person who could teach him properly.

**14. The scroll surfaces.**
A rumour, third-hand: sacked compounds get looted, looted things get sold. The
Ruri combat scroll — *when you graduate* — is somewhere out there on a
collector's shelf.

Start of a long, cold, optional trail across the whole map. Not on the main
path. This is the thread that makes a continuous world worth having.

## Part C — The Fall

**15. The friend.**
The Academy classmate who told him to keep his head down arrives as a
hunter-nin. He is not cruel and he is not wrong in his own frame; he believes
the MC deserted, because that is what the record says, because the cover works.

**16. Survived, not won.**
The MC lives through it by preparation — a prepared ground, a trap laid two days
early, a wall where a wall shouldn't be. He wins nothing. He escapes.

And he understands, clearly, that he cannot survive the second encounter with
what he currently is.

**17. The hinge — the first killing contract.**
*Author's call, since this was left open: the fall should be purchased
deliberately, not drifted into.*

He needs training he cannot afford, urgently, to survive a man who is coming
back. Fabrication will not cover it in time. So he takes the other kind of work,
knowing exactly what he is buying and what he is paying with.

**This lands at the midpoint of the act.** Everything before it is a craftsman
abroad. Everything after it is something else. He can name the reason, which is
worse than drifting — drifting is something that happens to you.

**18. The work changes.**
Tier-three contracts become normal. He gets better at them. The noticeboard in
each village still offers fabrication, and the player watches themselves stop
picking it.

Craft skills persist and get *repurposed* — the same precision, aimed
differently. Growing power reads as loss throughout. Never let a technique
unlock feel like a reward.

**19. The floor.**
A contract on another bloodline user. Whether he knows before he takes it is the
darkest dial in the design and worth choosing deliberately.

He does it. He is fifteen. He is very good at this now.

**20. Close.**
He is solvent, capable, hunted, and alone, and the money still goes home every
month to a war he has not seen in three years.

→ **Act 3.**

### Keeping the war alive

The civil war does not end until Act 4, which means it runs as background for the
whole of this act. If it is inert the whole time, the call home lands on nothing.

Seed it at intervals: a rumour that the Mizukage is not himself, a rebel setback
that makes his remittance matter, a courier who doesn't come back, news that the
Kaguya-style reprisals have stopped or worsened. The war should be visibly moving
while he is not in it.

## Open questions

- **Does he know the bloodline-user contract is a bloodline user before he takes
  it?** Knowing is bleaker and more honest; not knowing is more forgivable and
  keeps him sympathetic for Act 3. Currently unresolved.
- **The person in beat 10** — how close does that get? A friend is enough for the
  beat to work; anything more romantic changes the register of the whole act and
  wants deciding on purpose.
- **How many falsified lessons.** One per region is the natural rate, but the
  Academy scenes in Act 1 need to plant exactly that many specific claims for
  them to land.
