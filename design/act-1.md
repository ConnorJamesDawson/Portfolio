# Act 1 — The Workshop

First draft beat sheet. See `world-bible.md` for setting, cast, and canon
anchors.

**Span:** MC aged ~7 at the opening to ~11–12 at the break.
**Ends:** arrival at rebel HQ. This is where the game proper starts.

**Function of the act:** establish a warm childhood worth losing, drift the
politics underneath it without ever stating them, and take everything — with the
final blow delivered by the MC's own hand.

---

## Part A — The Workshop

**1. Waking.**
Morning in the Ruri compound. Small family, shared rooms, the sound of crystal
being grown in the next room. The MC's own ability established immediately:
hollow, small, cloudy. He makes an earring — a gift for Kohaku. The praise he
gets for it is half a beat too long.

**2. The scroll.**
The family head keeps the combat techniques sealed. Shows it, does not open it.
*When you graduate.* Kohaku has already learned them and won't teach him — same
words, same promise, doubled. This is what the world is going to break.

**3. The delivery.**
The MC goes with a parent: raw crystal to the fūinjutsu guild for inscription,
finished pieces to a shinobi quartermaster. He sees who wears their work. First
ANBU sighting — a mask, a gauntlet, a small blue stone set into it. The player
understands what that means. The child is just proud.

**4. Kohaku.**
Establish the register: busy, admired, obviously important, makes time anyway.
She eats with him. She talks about missions vividly and without specifics.

Two things planted here that pay off in Part C:
- **She teaches him to read inscribed crystal as a game** — a private code
  between them, framed as a puzzle for a clever little brother.
- **She asks him to carry a present to a friend** across the district. He does it
  happily. This recurs, casually, across years.

---

## Part B — The Academy

Spread across four years. Warmth stays constant; the world moves underneath it.

**5. Enrolment.**
Chakra-control tutorial. Warm instructor. World-history lesson one: the Five
Great Nations, delivered with unmistakable Kiri bias. The player learns the world
and learns it is being sold to them in the same breath.

**6. Zabuza.**
Glimpsed at a food stall. An older boy everyone gives room to. A classmate
whispers what he did. The instructor changes the subject. The MC's interest lasts
eleven seconds.

**7. Drift, lesson one.**
Same instructor, later term. New unit: *clan-based risks to village stability.*
Read in a flat textbook voice. Nothing else happens that day.

**8. The Kaguya.**
Felt as aftermath, never shown. The village on alert; a bloodline clan
annihilated in the streets overnight; public mood hardened by morning. A boy the
MC's own age carried past in a cage. No dialogue.

Every bloodline family in the country wakes up more hated for something they did
not do.

**9. Purity, argument one.**
In the Ruri kitchen: the uncle says the clan weakened itself by marrying out,
that they should have kept the blood clean. **Kohaku argues him down.** Her
politics become visible. Her network does not.

**10. The quiet street.**
Washing left on a line three days. An adult steers the MC a different way. A
shopkeeper who used to know his name.

**11. The Inventory.**
Officials arrive — not a census, an audit of *output*. Yields, grades, who
produces what and how fast. The elders cooperate enthusiastically; being valuable
has worked so far.

**Kohaku is unnervingly smooth with them.** She has been managing this
relationship for some time. The MC reads it as his sister being impressive.

He is asked to demonstrate. He does well. He is praised. He is written down.

**12. Drift, lesson two — final year.**
The textbook now lists bloodline clans as a security category. The instructor
reads it without conviction. Two classmates have moved desks.

At home, the errands get heavier and more frequent, and Kohaku stops eating with
him as often. She is tense in a way he has no framework for.

---

## Part C — The Break

**13. Round-ups.**
ANBU take other clans. The Ruri are untouched — and someone at dinner says so
*with relief*. The bargain gets stated out loud for the first time, and it is
ugly.

**14. The family's own civil war.**
Run, stay useful, or close ranks. It resolves nothing. **Kohaku leaves that
night** to join the uprising.

**15. The trail.**
He goes after his sister — not after a rebellion. The dead-drops are sealed
crystal and he can read them **because she taught him**. Four or five stops
through a Kiri he has never seen at night.

Mid-trail he recognises a drop point as somewhere she used to send him with
presents. The realisation lands on the player before it lands on him.

**16. The gate.**
He finds the meeting and is turned away: he has not graduated. **The examiner
fight — unwinnable, scripted loss.** He loses not because he is weak but because
he is twelve and makes earrings. This is where the player feels the withheld
scroll as a physical absence.

**17. Home.**
The compound stands open. Doors ajar, growing-frames smashed, the workshop
wrecked. No blood, no bodies, nobody. *Taken* is worse than *killed*, and he does
not know which.

**18. The streets.**
Weeks compressed. Hunger, hiding the bloodline, small ugly compromises. He stops
making crystal entirely.

**19. The coup.**
Zabuza moves against the Mizukage. The MC is caught at the edge of it and nearly
killed. Zabuza pulls him out — irritably, without explanation, *go home, idiot* —
and then flees the country. The one thing that looked like hope leaves, and it
was never for him.

**20. The empty workshop.**
A rumour reaches him that bloodline families are not being killed but *put to
work*. He chases it and finds a state facility: Ruri growing-frames, Ruri tools,
half-finished pieces on the benches, and not one person. The equipment is still
warm. He is days late, or they were never there.

It is the closest he gets to an answer in months, and it is worse than silence.

**21. The execution.**
A public event staged as reassurance. He goes because he has to know.

His family are on the scaffold. **Kohaku sees him in the crowd** and shakes her
head — *don't* — the same gesture she used when he asked her to teach him
something she wasn't going to teach him.

**22. The spike.**
First offensive use of the bloodline. It **costs him**: the crystal tears out
through his own hand and forearm on the way. He is scarred and partly crippled on
that side for the rest of the game — the player carries this scene in every
subsequent cutscene.

The executioner dies.

**23. The ambush, early.**
The insurgents were already in the crowd, waiting on a signal that had not come.
He forces their hand. It goes badly.

**Kohaku dies in the crossfire he started.** The remaining Ruri on the scaffold
die with her, in the fighting or under the executioner's replacement.

He killed his sister trying to save her, and he is the last of his clan.

**24. Extraction.**
He is dragged out — not rescued, *taken*, by people who have just paid for him in
blood. Rebel HQ.

Act 1 ends with someone asking him his name, and him not answering.

## Open questions

- **The bridge between 19 and 20** is thin. Beat 20 covers it thematically but the
  passage of months needs a shape — currently it is a montage with one location
  in it.
- **The friend who stays** (see world bible) is not yet placed in this act. He
  should appear in Part B — probably around beats 10 and 12 — so his turn in Act 2
  has a childhood behind it.
- **Does the MC ever learn what Kohaku was doing?** Current position: he learns
  enough to suspect, never enough to be sure. She dies before she can explain, and
  the ambiguity about whether the regime needed a reason or merely had one is
  more corrosive left open.
- **Where the entrance-exam examiner recurs.** A scripted loss this heavy wants a
  rematch somewhere in Act 3 or 4.
