# Chapter 16, Scene 5 — The Drover

*Act 2, year 1, month 4, the sixth. Below the stone bridge at Sarugaya.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~2,000 words.*

---

He did not go into Sarugaya, because he had been told not to, and the way
you do not go into Sarugaya is that you come off the road at the top of
the meadows and go down and under the stone bridge and along the water, and
the track picks up on the far side about a quarter of a mile on.

It took an hour and a half and it was slower than the town would have been
by about forty minutes and he did not think about that at all.

There was a man under the bridge.

---

He was on the shingle at the near end with a trap upside down across his
knees and a knife, and he was doing the thing you do to a trap, which is
cutting out the bad withies and putting new ones in, and he had a bundle of
new ones in the water beside him to keep them soft.

He did not get up.

He looked at the mule and then at the frame and then said, without any
weight on it:

"You'll be the one with the ninety kan."

"That's right."

"Mm."

And he went back to the trap.

---

Tadayoshi stood on the shingle about eight feet off and waited, and the
waiting was about a minute, and he used it.

*He is about fifty. He is not a trapper — the knife is right and the hands
are wrong, because a man who does that all winter has a shine on the inside
of the first two fingers and he has not got one.*

*So it is a trap he has borrowed to be sitting behind.*

*Which is good work, because I have been looking at him for a minute and
the only reason I know is the fingers.*

The man cut a withy out and put it on the shingle beside him.

"There's a drover," he said, "takes about forty head over the second ford
below Hane."

---

Tadayoshi did not say anything, and that was correct, and the man went on
in the same voice he had been using about the trap.

"Ninth, about an hour after light. Depends on him. He's been doing it the
same day for eleven years and he starts when he starts, but it's inside the
hour."

He bent a new withy into the frame with his thumbs.

"It's a bad crossing while he's in it."

"Is it."

"Forty head and a dog. Water goes to nothing and comes back. You wouldn't
want to be taking an animal through it and you wouldn't want to be standing
in it."

He looked at the trap and turned it round on his knees.

"Water's low on the near side this time of year. There's willows about
sixty, seventy yards up from the crossing on that bank and the ground's
firm behind them. You'd want to be there and not in the open, if you were
minded to wait for it to clear."

"How long does it take to clear."

"Four minutes," said the man. "Nobody hears anything and nobody looks at
anything for about four minutes. Then he's out the other side and it's a
ford again."

He put the knife down and picked another withy out of the water.

"That's all I've got about it," he said, "and it's not much."

---

Tadayoshi went at it while he was still standing on the shingle and he had
the whole of it inside about eleven seconds.

*Second ford. There are four below Hane and he has said the second, and he
has said it once, and he has not said it twice, which means he thinks I can
count.*

*Ninth. An hour after light, and it is not a time, it is a window, and he
has told me the window is the drover's and not his.*

*Behind the willows, sixty to seventy up, near bank, firm ground. That is
a position and he has given me the ground condition with it.*

*Four minutes.*

*And the signal is forty head going into the water, which I will hear from
six hundred yards and cannot mistake for anything else in the world.*

He stood there with his hand on the headcollar.

*That is a brief.*

*That is the best brief I have ever been given and there is not one word in
it that anybody could repeat to a magistrate.*

*He has told me a ford, an hour, a position, a window and a signal, and if
you wrote down every word he has said you would have a man talking about
cows.*

He was, for about four seconds, more purely admiring of another person's
work than he had been since the twelfth month.

*Somebody taught him that. That is not a thing you invent under a bridge.*

*And he is doing it with a trap on his knees that he cannot mend.*

---

There was one other thing in those eleven minutes and it went past him.

The man said, at some point, cutting: "It's not goods."

Then he said, "Anyway," and went on to the near-bank ground and the
willows, and it did not come up again.

Tadayoshi heard it, filed it, and did not act on it, and the reason he did
not is the reason he never acted on anything of that kind: he had nothing
to do with it. It was not a ford, an hour, a position, a window or a
signal. It was three words about a category, and a category is not a
question, and a thing that is not a question does not go anywhere.

He did not write it down.

---

The whole of it took eleven minutes and he noticed that too, and he
noticed it correctly, and got nothing out of it.

*He said what he had to say in about six.*

*He has been sitting here for five more doing a trap he cannot do.*

*And he has not asked me one thing. Not my name, not the mule, not the
case, not whether I have done this before, not what I am going to do about
the near bank if the water is up.*

*A man arranging a job asks questions. This is a man who has decided in
advance not to have any.*

He filed that with the trap and the fingers, in the place where things
about people went, and there were nine items in that place and he had
never once got anything out of it, because a fact about a person is only
useful if you have a second one about the same person, and he never did.

---

Then the man turned to Uzuki.

He had not looked at her once in eleven minutes.

"And you'll do the fetching," he said.

She did not say anything. Tadayoshi looked at her and she was standing
about four feet behind him and to the left, which is where she stood, with
the hat on, and her face did not do anything at all.

"Fetching what," said Tadayoshi.

"Whatever's small enough."

---

He objected once.

He has gone back over it since — not often, and never for long, because
going back over a thing you cannot change is not a use of an evening — and
what he has never been able to fix is that the objection came out wrong,
and that he knew it was coming out wrong while it was happening, and that
he could not find the other version in the four seconds available.

What he said was:

"She's carrying. If she's fetching, the pack's on the ground somewhere and
somebody's got to be with it."

Which was true. It was operationally true and it was a real problem and any
man in the trade would have granted it.

It was not what he meant.

The man put a withy in the water and did not look up.

"She'll do the fetching," he said.

And that was it, and it was not an argument that he lost, because it was
not an argument. It was a thing said twice, and the second time it was said
the way you say a thing that has already been decided by somebody who is
not in the conversation.

"Right," said Tadayoshi.

---

They went up off the shingle and along the water and got the track on the
far side and did about six miles before dark.

He did the book that night and put in five lines and every one of them was
correct.

> *6/4. Sarugaya. Contact under the br. 2nd ford below Hane, 9th, ab. 1
> hr after lt.*
>
> *Drover, ab. 40 head. Signal = cattle in the water.*
>
> *Position: willows, near bank, 60–70 up. Firm.*
>
> *Window ab. 4 min.*
>
> *U to fetch.*

He looked at the last line for a bit.

It was three words and it was the only line on the page that was not a
measurement, and there was nowhere else to put it, and he was not going to
leave it out because leaving a thing out of a book is the only lie a book
can tell.

He did not put the mark beside it.

*That is not a thing I have made. That is a thing I was told.*

He shut the book.

---

And she was on the other side of the fire with the hat still on, doing
nothing, which she did not do.

He looked at her for about two seconds and got the whole of the following,
which is more than he usually got and was still the outside of it.

*She is pleased.*

*She has not said anything, and she says everything, so she is going to say
it in about a minute and she is working out how.*

He was right. It took her about ninety seconds.

"Nobody's ever given me a job," she said.

"No."

"That's a job."

"It is."

She put a stick on the fire and did not say anything else about it, and he
went back over the eleven minutes on the shingle looking for the version of
his objection that would have worked, and did not find one, and put the
book away.
