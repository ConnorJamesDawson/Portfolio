# Chapter 15, Scene 2 — Warm

*Act 2. A room eleven paces by seven. A boy, age thirteen. POV: the boy.
~1,700 words.*

---

Three things happened in nine days and he had all three of them and could
do nothing with any of them, and he knew that he could do nothing with
them, which is a different thing from not trying.

He put them in the order they came, because that was the only order he
had.

---

The first was the water.

It came on a day he could not name, in the ordinary way, which was that
the door opened and a thing happened and the door shut, and he did not
resist it because resisting had never once been an option that was
offered.

Two of them came in. One had a bucket and one had a cloth and they did it
standing up in the middle of the floor with him out of his shirt, and it
took about four minutes, and they did his arms and his chest and his neck
and his hair, and one of them did behind his ears with a thumb inside the
cloth, and neither of them said anything to him at any point.

The water was warm.

*That is warm water.*

He stood in the middle of the floor with his arms out where they had put
them and he had that thought and then he had the next one, and the next
one took longer.

*I have not had warm water since the sixth month of the year I was
eleven. That was a tub in a yard and there were four of us and it was
warm because it was summer and it had been standing.*

*This is warm because somebody has heated it.*

He put the shirt back on when they gave it to him and sat down against the
wall in the place he sat and worked at it for a long time and got exactly
one step.

*Somebody has heated water and carried it down here.*

*That is work. Somebody has done work.*

And that was the step, and there was not a second one, because the second
one would have had to be about why a person does work, and he had nothing
whatever to put in that.

It happened again nine days later.

---

The second was the blanket.

There was one and then there were two, and the second one arrived on the
floor by the door while he was asleep, which he knew because it was not
there and then it was.

It was better than his. It was thicker and it was not damp at the corner
and it did not have the smell.

He looked at it for a while from where he was.

Then he got up and put it on the floor under the first one, because the
floor was the cold part and always had been, and lay down on the two of
them, and it worked, and he was warmer that night than he had been since
the ninth month.

*That is better.*

He did not have a thought after that one either.

He noticed, some days later, that he had not once considered putting it
over himself, and that if he had it would have been about the same, and
that he had put it underneath because the floor was the problem, and that
he had solved the problem correctly and instantly and without thinking
about it, and he was pleased about that in the small way he was pleased
about things.

---

The third was the gap, and the third is the one that cost him something,
and he did not find out what for two days.

The gap was high on the west wall and it was about four thumbs by eleven
and he could not reach it and had never been able to.

It had failed as a clock in the ninth month. He had gone at it for eleven
days — the light coming up the wall, where the edge of it got to, how long
it took to cross a course of the stone — and it had failed for three
reasons which he had written in his head where things went, and the third
of the three was the one that killed it, which was that on a grey day
there was no edge at all and a grey day looks exactly like the middle of
the night from the far side of a room.

So it was a failed instrument and he had stopped using it in the ninth
month of the year before.

He came back from the water on the second time and it was boarded.

---

He could not reach it, so he did it the way he had done the ceiling, which
was to throw pieces of the floor at it and listen.

It took two days and about sixty goes and it was the best work he had done
since the clock.

*Board does not sound like stone. Board is a hollow and stone is a nothing
and I can hear the difference from here every single time.*

He worked the edges. Left first, going up: hollow, hollow, hollow, then
nothing, and he threw at the same place four more times to be sure, and it
was nothing four more times. Then the right, going up, and the same. Then
across the top, which took the longest because he was throwing high and
tired and had to stop twice.

By the end of the second day he had it, and he did not want it.

*Three boards, across, on the outside.*

*And they stop where the stone starts. Both ends. Top and bottom.*

*That gap is four thumbs by eleven, and there is no board sticking out
past it anywhere I can find, and I have been round the whole of it.*

*If you cut a board long and put it up, you have a board sticking out.
These do not stick out.*

*So they were cut to it. And to cut a thing to fit, you have to have the
measurement before you cut, and to have the measurement you have to have
measured.*

He sat down on the floor.

*And they are on the outside, so they went on from a passage, so there is
a passage on the other side of that wall and it is high enough for a man
to stand up in and work above his head.*

He put that in with the things about the building, which was a short list
and had not had anything added to it since the eleventh month.

*Somebody has measured this room.*

---

He did not sleep that night and he was not frightened, and it is important
that he was not, because he did not have it.

What he had was a thing that would not sit down.

*I know this room.*

*It is eleven paces by seven and the pace is mine and I have done it four
times and got the same answer four times. The floor goes down about a
thumb across the width of it toward the north corner and the drain is
there. The door is one and a half paces from the east corner and it opens
inward and the hinge is on the side away from the drain. The ceiling is a
foot above my hand and I got that by throwing.*

*And somebody else knows it too.*

He turned that over and could not get it to be anything.

*I thought I was the only one who knew it. I have never once thought that
before, because I have never once had a reason to think about who else
knows anything.*

*And it is not that they came in and measured it. They did not. I would
have had that.*

*It is that a person who has never been in here has got a number off
this room and I do not know which number or when or what for.*

He got up and stood in the middle of the floor, which he did not do, and
which he had now done twice in about two months, and both times it had
been the same thing underneath and he did not connect them.

---

Then there was the doorway, and it is the fourth thing and he did not
count it as one, and it is the only one of the four that was about him.

Somebody stood in it.

It was open, which happened, and there was a man in it, which happened,
and the man did not come in and did not put anything down and did not say
anything and did not do anything at all.

Kimimaro counted it, because counting was what he had.

*Forty. Forty-one. Forty-two.*

He got to about forty seconds and then the man went and the door shut.

It happened again two days later and it was about the same and he counted
that one too and got thirty-eight, and he was pleased with the two numbers
and did not know what to do with them, and put them in with the rest.

---

He did the whole of it once more before he stopped, in order, the way you
do a series.

*Water that somebody heated. Twice, nine days apart.*

*A blanket that is better than the one I have.*

*Boards cut to a gap by a person who has the measurement.*

*And a man in the doorway for forty seconds, twice, who did not want
anything.*

He sat against the wall with his hand on the back of his other hand, and
the sixth piece was under it, at seventy-and-something out of about two
hundred, and he could feel the place where it was going to come through
and it was not sore yet.

*Something is going to happen.*

*I had that in the twelfth month off the bowl and I was right.*

*It has taken this long.*

And that is where it stopped, and it stopped there for the same reason it
had stopped every time for two years, which is that the next question is
*what*, and *what* is a question about people, and he had never once in
his life been given anything to answer a question about people with.

So he had four facts and a conclusion and no fifth step, and it was
correct as far as it went, and it went four fifths of the way, and the
last fifth was the only part that would have been any use to him.

He was thirteen.

He went to sleep warm, on two blankets, under a boarded wall, in a room
that somebody upstairs had the dimensions of.
