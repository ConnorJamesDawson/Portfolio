# Chapter 15, Scene 5 — Nothing You Would Remember

*Act 2, year 1, month 3, the eleventh to the fourteenth. Ōtoma. Tadayoshi
Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~2,000 words.*

---

He found it on the eleventh and he was not looking for it.

That has to be said first, because he had given the other thing up on the
ninth and he had given it up properly and he did not go back to it, and if
he had gone back to it he would not have found this, and he never did work
out that the two were the same object.

What he was doing on the eleventh was the ordinary thing.

---

The ordinary thing was this, and he had done it before every job he had
ever taken.

*Reduce it.*

*A sheet is paper and paper gets wet and paper gets taken off you. So
before you go you take the sheet and you get it down to what you would
have to have if there were no sheet, and you carry that, and then the
sheet is a convenience and not a requirement.*

He had done it forty times. It took about two minutes. You read the thing
and you strike out everything that is furniture and what is left is the
job.

He sat down with the Sarugaya sheet on the twelfth morning and struck it
out.

> *Ōtoma to Sarugaya, thence to the crossing below Hane.*
>
> *1 case. Ab. 90 kan gross. Sealed at origin.*
>
> *11 days. 4,000, of which 1,000 on acceptance.*
>
> *Consignor seals. No weighing required at transfer. Hand over against
> the counterpart, which will be produced.*

It came down to nine words.

*Sarugaya, then the crossing below Hane. One case. Eleven days.*

He looked at the nine words.

*That is the job.*

*That is the whole job and I have not lost one thing off it. I could do
that with the sheet in a river.*

---

He was pleased for about four seconds and then he was not, and the reason
he was not is the only reason that ever stopped him being pleased, which
is that a thing had come out too clean.

*That is never nine words.*

He put the sheet down.

*The Kanaya one was four crates and a roll and eighteen days and it came
down to about twenty, and the twenty had **Ōtsuka's counterpart at the
Kanaya end and not the yard's** in it, and if I had lost that I would
have had to walk back.*

*The Hazaki one had a name in it.*

*Every single one of them has had one thing in it that had to be there,
and it is not always the same thing and that is the point of doing it.*

He got the frame back out.

---

He did nine of them on the twelfth afternoon and it took him about fifty
minutes and he did them the same way every time: read it, strike it,
look at what was left, and then take one more thing out and see whether
the job broke.

It broke nine times out of nine.

> *Anazawa, 26/9. Take out **the west lane** and there are four shutter-
> makers in Anazawa.*
>
> *Kurihama, 9/1. Take out **before the tide** and the crossing is not
> there.*
>
> *The eleventh-month one. Take out **the second man** and I go in
> expecting one.*
>
> *Sakabe, 11/1. Take out **upstairs** and it is a different building.*

He sat on the boards with nine dockets round him in an arc.

*Every one of them has got a load-bearing item. One. Sometimes two. Take
it out and you have to go back and ask, and going back and asking is the
thing the whole document exists to stop.*

*That is not a style. That is what a job sheet is.*

Then he picked up the Sarugaya one again and took things out of it until
there was nothing left, and the job did not break at any point.

---

He took *thence to the crossing below Hane* out.

*I would have to ask where it goes.*

Except that he would not, because *hand over against the counterpart,
which will be produced* meant somebody would produce a counterpart and a
counterpart says where, and the sheet said so on its own face.

He took *one case* out.

*I would not know what I was carrying.*

Except that it was ninety kan gross and sealed at origin, so it was one
thing or it was crated as one thing, and either way it went on a frame and
he would find out by looking at it.

He took *eleven days* out.

And that one nearly worked, and he sat with it for four minutes, and then
it did not work either, because four thousand of which a thousand on
acceptance is a fee with a shape and the shape says how long, and any
carrier in the country would read it the same.

He went round it four times and could not make it break.

---

He sat back.

*Every part of this refers to another part.*

*There is nothing in it that is only in it once.*

He said it out loud, on the boards, in an empty room, which he did about
twice a year:

"There's nothing in it you'd have to remember."

---

Then he did the thing that took the day and a half, and it is the best
work he did in the whole of the third month and it went nowhere at all.

*Test it the other way.*

*If it is on purpose, I ought not to be able to do it.*

He got a blank page and he wrote the Kanaya job out as a docket, from
memory, as if he were the clerk, and he tried to write it with nothing in
it you would remember.

It took him forty minutes.

He read it back.

> *Ōtoma to the Kanaya. 4 crates + 1 roll. 18 days. 1,400. Counterpart at
> the receiving end.*

*Ōtsuka.*

He had not written the name. He had written *the receiving end*, and it
was still Ōtsuka, because the Kanaya has one yard and everybody knows
whose it is, and a person reading that sheet in a room in another country
would get to Ōtsuka in about a day.

He tried again and took the Kanaya out and put *a river yard, eleven days
east*, and read it back, and there are four river yards eleven days east
and one of them takes crates.

He tried a third time and produced something that had nothing in it at
all, and read it back, and it was not a docket. You could not do the job
off it. He had not made it forgettable, he had made it useless, and those
are different and he could see exactly where the line was and could not
walk along it.

He put the pencil down at about eight in the evening on the thirteenth.

*I cannot do it.*

*I can write a sheet that tells you everything and I can write a sheet
that tells you nothing and I cannot write the one in the middle, and the
one in the middle is the only one that is any good, and somebody has done
it and it is sitting on the floor.*

---

He sat with that for a long time and what he arrived at is four sentences
and he wrote all four of them down.

> *14/3. The Sarugaya sheet.*
>
> *It is complete. I can do the whole job off it and I cannot take one
> thing out of it.*
>
> *And there is no item in it that is in it once. Nothing to carry.
> Nothing you would remember.*
>
> *A badly written one leaves the thing out by accident and you go back
> and ask. I do not have to go back and ask. So it is not badly written.*

And then the fifth, which was not part of the finding and which he wrote
anyway because it was true:

> *That is good work.*

He looked at it on the page.

*Somebody sat down and did that on purpose and it took them a while,
because I have just spent a day and a half failing at it and I am the best
person I have ever met at a piece of paper.*

*That is a skill. Somebody has that skill and uses it on freight
paperwork.*

He was not uneasy. It is important that he was not, and the reason he was
not is that he was pleased, and he was pleased in the specific way he was
pleased about three times a year, which was the way you are pleased when
you meet the work of somebody who is properly good at the thing you are
good at.

---

There is one more line and it is the one that sends him to the frame on
the fifteenth, and it is entirely correct and it is the beginning of five
days of looking in the wrong place.

*A person does not learn to do that on one sheet.*

*If somebody can write like that, they have written like that before, and
they have written like that more than once, and the ones before this are
in yards.*

*And I have got seventy-two of them under the boards.*

He shut the book.

---

He had now done this three times.

He had watched a man route a child through four hands in the twelfth month
and thought *he is doing what I do.* He had stood in a yard on the sixth
of the second month and watched a man describe an animal for money and
thought *this is a man who knows a thing and is describing it accurately.*
And he had sat on the boards on the fourteenth of the third month and
written *that is good work* under a sheet of freight paperwork.

Three men, three skills, three correct assessments.

And not once, in any of the three, the other half of it — which is that a
skill is for something, and that the thing it is for is a fact about the
person and not about the skill, and that he had been standing in front of
the answer to that question all three times and had never once asked it,
because his eye went where it went and it had been going there since he
was eight years old in a workshop being told his own work was two
thousandths out.

Nobody said it to him.

There was nobody in a position to.
