# Chapter 16, Scene 7 — Eleven

*Act 2. A box on a cart, and water on the left. A boy, age thirteen. POV:
the boy. ~1,600 words.*

---

On the tenth day he did the thing he had been putting off since the fourth,
and he had been putting it off honestly, which is to say he knew he was and
knew why.

*The nine hundred and three is dead.*

He had made that number in a room over five months, on his fingers, in
fives, in the dark, and it was the best piece of work he had ever done and
he had loved it in the only way he loved things, which was to check it.

*Nine hundred and three bowls. Seven more times. A half each time.*

*And bowls stopped being a clock in the twelfth month and I have been
carrying a number in a unit that does not exist for four months and I have
known that for four months.*

He lay on his side in the dark with his hands in front of his chest.

*So I have got the shape and I have not got the size.*

*The shape is right. Seven more, a half each time, and I have watched four
of them do it and it has held every single time and I am not throwing that
away because I lost a unit.*

*What I have not got is how long a half is in anything a person could
count.*

---

And then the part that made the tenth day different from the four hundred
days before it.

*I have got days now.*

He went at it properly and it took most of the grey and he enjoyed all of
it.

*The sixth one came through on the fifth day of the box.*

*And I had it at seventy-and-something out of about two hundred when the
man with the book put two fingers on it, and the man with the book was
before the box, and I do not know how long before, and that is the hole.*

He turned it over.

*So I cannot do the sixth in days. I have got the end of it in days and I
have not got the start.*

*Right.*

He was not disappointed, because being disappointed requires having
expected something, and what he had was an arithmetic that had come out
short and a reason why.

*Then it is the seventh.*

---

He set it up the way he set everything up, and it took him about ten
minutes, and it is the coldest piece of work in this book and there is
nothing in his head about that at all.

*The seventh starts when it starts and I will feel it start, because I felt
the fifth start and I felt the sixth start and both times it is the same
and it is about four days before you can find it with a thumb.*

*I will have the day it starts. I will have the day it comes through. That
is a half in days.*

*And a half in days gives me the eighth, because the eighth is a half of
the seventh, and the ninth is a half of the eighth, and I have got seven of
them to go, and if I have got one of them in days I have got all of them,
because it is halves and halves are the easiest thing there is.*

He put his hand over the back of his other hand where the sixth one was
through and hard and slightly the wrong colour.

*So the seventh is the one that tells me how long.*

He lay in the dark for a while with that.

*I want it to start.*

He had it plainly and there was nothing under it and nothing on top of it.
He was not frightened of the seventh and he was not looking forward to
dying and neither of those things was in the room. What he had was an
instrument he could not finish and one missing reading, and the reading
was in his own hand, and it was going to arrive on its own, and he wanted
it to hurry up.

*Two is any line you like. One is not even that.*

*So I have got nothing until the seventh, and then I have got the whole of
it.*

**And he did not once, in ten minutes of correct and careful arithmetic,
have the thought that what he was building was a date.**

He would not have known what to do with it if he had. Nobody had ever given
him a reason to want more days than he had, and the days themselves were
four days old.

---

On the eleventh they stopped early and it was not a feeding stop.

He had it off the light — grey, and still grey, and the road out of the
floor, and nobody came.

Then the water.

*That is a river.*

He put his ear on the boards.

*It is on the left and it is close and it is not deep, because a deep one
is a nothing and this one has got a noise on it, and a noise means it is
going over something.*

*So we are next to a crossing and we have stopped before it.*

He worked at it while the grey went.

*You stop before a crossing at night because you are going over it in the
morning.*

*And you do not stop before a crossing at night unless you want to go over
it at a particular time, because a ford in the dark is a bad ford and a
ford in the morning is a ford.*

*So there is a time.*

He put it with the things about them.

*They have had a time the whole way and I have not been able to see it and
this is the first time I have been able to see the edge of one.*

---

The one who checked came that night, and it was the third time, and it was
the first time Kimimaro got anything new.

The lid came off with no lamp at all, which had not happened, so there was
a shape and no face and nothing to see, and the hand came in and took his
shoulder and turned him a quarter over the way it did.

And then it stopped.

It did not let go and it did not put him back. It stayed on his shoulder,
and the shape above him did not move, and Kimimaro counted, because
counting was what he had.

*Nine. Ten. Eleven.*

At eleven the hand came off and the lid went on.

He lay in the dark and did it.

*Four before. Four, four, four and a half, four. I have had that hand four
times and every one of them has been about four.*

*That was eleven.*

He went at it the way he went at everything, which is that he asked what
had changed and did not ask what it meant.

*The other four were in a lamp. This one was not.*

*So he could not see anything.*

*And he stayed anyway, for eleven, with his hand on a thing he could not
see.*

He turned it over four ways and got the same answer every time and did not
recognise it, because the answer had a word on it that he did not have.

*He was not looking.*

*He was there.*

That is as far as he got, and it is further than he had ever got about a
person, and he filed it with the six men and the five who were one thing,
and there were now three items in the place where things about people went
that had come out of the same fortnight, and he had had nine in it before
and none of them had ever joined up.

---

The last thing on the night of the eleventh is one line and he did not
work at it at all.

He had been on his side in a box for eleven days and he had a clock and a
map of a road made of surfaces and six men of whom five were one thing and
a hand that had stayed for eleven with nothing to see, and a piece of bone
that was going to arrive and tell him how long he had, and he was lying
next to a river he could hear and could not see.

And what he thought, before he went to sleep in nine seconds the way he
always did, was:

*Eleven.*

*Twelve tomorrow.*

And then, because it was true and he had no reason not to think it and
nowhere to put it and nobody to say it to:

*This is the most I have ever had.*
