# Chapter 17, Scene 9 — Four Minutes

*Act 2, year 1, month 4, the twenty-fourth. An upper room in Kadono. A
woman, age twenty-six. POV: her, once. ~1,700 words.*

---

She had nine that morning and this was the fourth of them and it was not
the hardest.

The hardest had been the second, which was a man called Ryūen who had been
theirs for six years and had been taking money from a shipping house at
Sarugaya since the eighth month and had a wife and two children in a house
that had been paid for out of it. She had done that one at about half past
seven, in nine minutes, and had signed it, and had eaten afterwards
because she had learned at twenty-two that not eating afterwards helps
nobody and is a kind of vanity.

So by the time the fourth came up the stairs on a sheet she had the morning
in her hands and had had four cups of tea and was not troubled about
anything at all.

---

The sheet was the ordinary two pages.

She read them the way she read everything, which was fast, twice, and the
second time from the bottom.

> *Ruri. Carrier, ab. 11. Fifteen months. Freight and nine contracts. No
> reprimands. No losses. Two refusals, both documented, both reasonable.*
>
> *Nozaki. Ab. 11. Attached from 12th mo. Not on the books.*
>
> *Kaguya. Ab. 13. Recovered from the Hane consignment, 9th of this month,
> per instruction.*

She put the first page down and read the second one and the second one was
the boy's own account, taken down in a yard forty minutes earlier by a man
called Ide who had come out for some air.

She read that twice as well.

---

**She was not upset by it.** *She had read four hundred of that kind and
this one was better written than most, because the man had been careful to
put down what the boy had actually said instead of what he had made of it,
which is rarer than it ought to be.*

What she thought about it, in order, over about forty seconds, was:

*He gave it voluntarily.*

*Nobody has written **under questioning** on this and Ide would have
written it, because Ide writes everything.*

*So the boy told a stranger in a yard what he is, unprompted, in three
minutes, having been standing there for twenty.*

She turned the page over and looked at the back of it, which was blank.

*Which is the whole of the matter and the rest is arithmetic.*

---

She did the arithmetic and it took her about four minutes and it is
reproduced here in full because it is correct and because nobody in this
book ever hears it.

**One. The carrier.**

*He is good. That is not in doubt and it is on the sheet — fifteen months,
no losses, two refusals both documented and both right, and a hand-over at
Hane on the ninth that went the way hand-overs are supposed to go and
almost never do.*

*And he pulls teams.*

*Four in fifteen months that we know of, two of them off records we
shouldn't have, and the rate is going one way. That is not his fault. It is
arithmetic about a state that has a file open, and a file that is open gets
worked on.*

*So the question is not is he good. He is good. The question is what he
costs a side that is losing.*

**Two. And a team that comes for him is a team that is not in Kirigakure.**

*Which is the argument we have been making since the eighth month of last
year and it is a good argument and I have made it myself in front of people
who did not want to hear it.*

She sat with that one properly, because it was the one that cut against
her.

*And it was a good argument when the teams were coming to a boy on a road
with nothing on him.*

*It is a different argument when the boy is standing in a yard in Kadono
with two other children, and one of them is a Kaguya, and the Kaguya has
just told a man he had known for four minutes what he is.*

**Three. The girl.**

*Not on the books, which means nobody has ever been paid for her and nobody
can be asked about her, and she is the cheapest thing on this sheet and the
only one I would keep if I could keep one.*

*And you cannot keep one.*

**Four. And the boy.**

She read the second page a third time.

*He is worth a very great deal to somebody and I do not have to know who to
know that.*

*Somebody moved him under a boilerplate docket with a real consignment and
a paid carrier and eleven days of road, which is what you do with a thing
you cannot afford to have looked at, and we took it off them on the ninth
because it was there and because a boy of thirteen who does that is a
weapon and we are losing.*

*And on the fourth day of having him, he stood in a yard and gave his
clan, his condition and his remaining time to a man with a bad knee,
because the man was nice to him.*

She put the sheet down.

> ## *We cannot hold that.*

---

**And here is the part that took the longest, and it was about ninety
seconds, and it was not about any of the three of them.**

*What happens if I keep them.*

*I put a Kaguya into a war. He is worth four men on a field and everybody
who sees him knows what he is within a week, because that is what a Kaguya
is, and then the thing that is hunting the carrier and the thing that is
hunting the Kaguya are the same thing and it comes here.*

*And I have got eleven hundred people in three provinces and about nine
months of money.*

*And it comes here.*

She looked out of the window, which faced the wrong way for the yard.

*So it is: a boy who draws teams, plus a boy who draws worse and cannot
keep his own name in his mouth, against a war I am currently losing at a
rate I can put a date on.*

*And they are eleven, eleven and thirteen, and they have not done one thing
wrong.*

**She wrote the decision at the bottom of the second page in one line and
her hand did not do anything different while she wrote it.**

> *Not to be retained. Pay to the ninth and clear. No prejudice, no
> notice to the boards, no follow-up.*

---

There is a last paragraph and it is the one that matters and nobody in the
building would have recognised it as anything.

*No prejudice.*

She wrote that on purpose. It cost her nothing and it did nothing and she
knew it did nothing, and she put it on because a boy of eleven who has
worked fifteen months without a loss should not have a sheet with a mark on
it, and she was not going to be the person who put one there.

*It is the only thing on this page I have any choice about.*

She signed it and put it on the pile of the four she had done and picked up
the fifth, which was about grain.

---

**She never saw any of them.**

The room she was in was on the first floor at the town end, four rooms and
a staircase from the one they were sitting in, and she did not go down,
because there was no reason to go down and there were five left.

She did not learn his name that day and did not learn it afterwards. There
is a *Ruri* on a sheet in a box in a building that burned in the ninth
month of the following year, and she had read it twice, and by about
eleven o'clock she could not have produced it.

She was right about all of it.

**That is the point of this scene and it is not a consolation. She was
right about the teams, right about the boy, right about the nine months of
money, right that she could not keep one of the three, and right that it
would come to Kadono if she tried** — *and she was so nearly right about the
war that four years later she ran the country.*

And the whole of it took four minutes, in a room he never entered, by a
woman he never met, and it ended the only arrangement he had ever had.

She had five more to do before noon.
