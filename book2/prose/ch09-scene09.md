# Chapter 9, Scene 9 — I Don't Know Him

*Act 1, month 9, the eighteenth to the twenty-fourth. The Ōgi road, and
Ōgi. Nozaki Uzuki, age ten. POV: Uzuki. ~2,620 words.*

---

The first day she did about seven miles and it took her the whole of it.

That was the number and she had it exactly, because she counted the
milestones on the Ōgi road the way she counted everything, and there were
seven of them between the fork and the place she stopped, and she went
past the seventh at about four in the afternoon and did not get to the
eighth.

Seven miles is a morning. Her father did seven miles to Ōgi market and back
in a day with a mule and a load and thought nothing of it.

She did seven miles in eleven hours and stopped because of her feet.

---

The feet were the whole of the first three days and there is no other way
to put it.

She got them into water on the first morning at a beck about a mile up
from the fork and sat with them in it for a long time, and it hurt and
then it stopped hurting and then, when she took them out, it was much
worse than it had been before, and that was a thing nobody had told her
and she was extremely angry about it for about a mile.

She tore two strips off the bottom of her shift on the second day and put
them round the balls of both feet and they were off inside a mile because
there was nothing to tie them to.

On the third day she worked out that the verge is better than the road and
that the wet verge is better than the dry one, and that the whole of it is
better in the morning and unbearable at about three in the afternoon, and
she got nine miles on the third day, which she was pleased about, and then
could not stand up for about twenty minutes when she stopped.

*Right.*

*Seven, eight, nine.*

*So it's four days for a person with shoes and it's five for me and it's
five if the feet don't get worse.*

---

She ate on the first day and the second day and did not eat much on the
third.

The half ryō was the whole of the estate and she had decided on the bank
under the thorn what it was for, and she had decided it properly, in
steps, sitting there with it in the twist of cloth on her knee.

*It is not for food.*

*It is for the first month at Ōgi.*

*Because when I get there I am going to be a girl at a door with nothing,
and a girl at a door with nothing is a girl who is asking, and a girl at a
door with half a ryō is a girl who can pay for four weeks of somewhere to
sleep while she starts, and those are two different girls, and one of them
gets taken on and the other one gets fed and sent on.*

So she ate about a fifth of a ryō in five days and she was hungry from
about the middle of the second day and she did not spend it, and that was
the single most difficult sustained thing she did that year and nobody ever
knew about it.

She got two turnips out of a clamp on the fourth day.

She wrote it on the back of the bag with the value, so that it could be
paid later if it ever could be, which was a thing her father had done about
a chicken when she was seven and had made a whole evening out of.

---

She did the second and the third and the fourth days as numbers because
there was nothing else in them.

*18th. 7. Beck at 1. Feet bad. Bread, a twentieth.*

*19th. 8. Feet worse. Bread. Slept in a byre and got out at first light
before anybody.*

*20th. 9. Verge. Rain in the afternoon, about two hours.*

She wrote them on the back of the flour bag with the eleven questions and
the nine no's and the gaps in a column, in the last of the space, very
small.

The rain on the twentieth was the first proper rain since the eleventh and
she sat under a wall for two hours of it, and at the end of it she put her
hand up to the front of her hair on the left where it always went patchy
and looked at her fingers.

There was nothing on them. It does not come off like that; it does not
come off at all, it just stops being there, slowly, from the roots out,
over about ten weeks.

She looked at her fingers for a bit anyway.

*Nine days.*

*It gets done every two months and it was done nine days ago, so I have got
about seven weeks before anybody could tell by looking.*

*And after that it goes from the roots and there is nobody to do it.*

She put her hand down and did not think about it again for eleven days.

---

She came into Ōgi on the afternoon of the twenty-second.

It was four times Nakadō and it had a bridge in the middle of it and the
road came down into it off the north side so that you saw the whole thing
at once, and she stood at the top of the hill and looked at it for a long
time, which was the first thing she had done for pleasure since the
seventeenth.

*Right.*

*Behind the corn chandler's, up from the bridge.*

*So: find the bridge, find the chandler, go up.*

She found the bridge in about a minute and the chandler's in about six,
because a corn chandler has sacks outside it and she had spent eleven
nights of the eighth month with her hands in about forty of them and could
have found one blindfolded.

She went up.

---

She found the house at about four o'clock and then she did not go to it for
two hours.

She sat on a wall on the other side and about thirty yards down and looked
at it, because she had walked five days to get to a door and she was not
going to knock on it in the state she was in without having a look first,
and because — she was honest about this later — she was frightened.

It was a house like the other houses. It had a yard on the side with lines
in it and the lines had things on them, and there was a lot on them, more
than one household's worth, which was the first thing that told her she had
the right house.

At about half four a woman came out and took some of it in.

*That's her.*

*That's got to be her. Forty-something and Ine's face, sort of, round the
mouth.*

And then at about five two girls came round the corner of the house with a
basket between them and put it down by the door and went back for another
one.

Uzuki sat on the wall.

*Two girls.*

*He said. He said it on the fourteenth and I heard it and I put it away
because I was busy hearing the other thing.*

*She's got two girls in.*

She looked at them properly, which took about a minute, because they went
back and forth four times.

*Fourteen. Fifteen, maybe. And the other one's sixteen or seventeen and
she's the one who carries the heavy end.*

*They're strong and they're fast and they know where everything is and
they've been doing it for years, and one of them said something and the
other one laughed, so they get on.*

She sat on the wall and did the sum, because she could not stop herself
doing sums and had never once been able to.

*Three inns. Two girls and her. That's three of them on three inns and
they've been doing it nine years.*

*So she doesn't need anybody.*

*And if she did need somebody she would want somebody who's fifteen, and
what is standing on this wall is ten, with no shoes on, and feet that
aren't right, and about a fifth of a ryō gone out of a half, and nobody's
name.*

---

She went and knocked anyway.

She thought about not doing it for about four minutes and then she got off
the wall and went across the road and up the path and knocked on the door,
because she was ten and she had walked five days and she had never once in
her life not asked a thing she had come to ask.

*And because if I don't knock I'll be a person who walked five days and
sat on a wall.*

---

Kiku opened it and looked down at her.

She looked at her for about two seconds, which Uzuki counted, and then at
her feet, which took about one, and then back up.

"Yes, love?"

"I've come about work," said Uzuki. "I do laundry. I'm strong and I don't
stop and I'll do the first month for my food and nothing else, and if I'm
no good at the end of the month you can tell me and I'll go and I won't
argue about it."

She had had that ready since the second day and she had said it about
sixty times and it came out exactly as she had built it.

Kiku stood in the door.

"How old are you?"

"Ten and four months."

"Where've you come from?"

"Nakadō."

"That's five days."

"It's four," said Uzuki. "It took me five."

---

Kiku looked at her for a while.

"I've got two in," she said. "I've had Miya four years and the other one
two, and there's three of us and three houses and there isn't a fourth
house."

"I know," said Uzuki. "I've been sat on that wall since four."

Something went across Kiku's face at that and Uzuki did not know what it
was.

"Then you know," said Kiku.

"Yes."

"Then why'd you knock?"

*Because I walked five days.*

"Because you might have said yes," said Uzuki.

---

Kiku leaned on the door frame.

"Who sent you?"

*There.*

*That's it. That's the one.*

She got it out properly, the way she had built it, with the whole of it in
the right order so that it would land:

"Kuriya, at Nakadō. He's got houses. He's had one to my mother for four
years and I've done for him three times a week since the seventh month and
he told me about you on the fourteenth — he said you'd held that house
eleven years and you'd never been a day late and you did the beds for
three inns and everybody in Ōgi knows who does the beds."

She stopped.

Kiku's face did not change at all.

"I don't know him," she said.

---

Uzuki stood on the path.

"You—"

"I don't know any Kuriya," said Kiku. "Nakadō's four hours and I've been
there twice in my life and both times it was for my sister. Is he Ine's?"

"He's — he's got houses. Ine knows him."

"Then that's Ine, then," said Kiku, not unkindly. "Ine'll have told him
about me. Ine tells everybody about me, she's proud of it."

---

*Ine told him about me.*

*He told me about her.*

*And she has never heard of him.*

She stood on the path in front of a house at Ōgi at about seven in the
evening on the twenty-second of the ninth month and understood the whole
of it at once, and it was the cleanest and the worst piece of arithmetic
she ever did.

*It went the wrong way.*

*He didn't send me. He can't have sent me. He told me a thing he had heard
about a woman in another town, and I have carried it for five days like it
was worth something, and it is not a recommendation, it is a piece of
gossip going backwards.*

**One person says your name to somebody else. That is the whole of it and
that is what it has to be, and what I have got is somebody saying HER name
to ME.**

*And I knew that. I knew that on the fourteenth. He told me on the
twenty-third of the eighth month in his own kitchen that he would like to
send me to about six people and that he was not going to, and I sat in
that kitchen and I said all right.*

*And then he gave me a name and I walked five days on it.*

---

Kiku gave her a bowl of something on the step and did not ask her in, which
Uzuki understood perfectly and was not hurt by, and told her that the
almshouse behind the shrine took people until the tenth month and that
there was work at the Kanaya for a fortnight in the eleventh when the road
trade came through and that they took anybody then.

She said, at the end, "How long's your mother had you on the road?"

And Uzuki said, "She hasn't."

And Kiku looked at her for a bit longer and did not ask anything else, and
shut the door, and Uzuki sat on the step and ate the whole of it and went
back down to the bridge.

---

She got the bag out under the bridge that night.

The first column had a hundred and fifteen numbers in it and stopped on
the twenty-sixth of the eighth month.

The second column had about eleven things in it and finished at a half and
a bit.

The third column said 1,621, and she took one off, and it said 1,620.

And the fourth column had marks in it up to the seventeenth of the ninth
month and would not be having any more, because the thing it was counting
was five days behind her in a brick house.

She looked at the four of them for a long time.

*So that's it. That's what a bag is.*

*Two of them are dead and one of them's a number I'm not going to reach for
four and a half years and one of them's got a half a ryō in it.*

She turned it over and there was no room on the back either, because the
back had the eleven questions on it and the nine no's and the gaps in a
column and five days of miles.

She did not rule a fifth column.

She sat under the bridge at Ōgi with her back against the stone and her
feet out in front of her and did the last one, and it was not on the bag
and never went on the bag.

*Nobody knows where I am.*

She turned that over.

*Nobody. Not one person in the world. Mum doesn't and Kuriya doesn't and
Ine doesn't and Kiku knows there's a girl but she doesn't know a name, I
never gave her a name, she never asked and I never gave it.*

*If I stopped, nobody would come.*

*If I went in the river tonight there is not one single person who would
find out, and there is nowhere I am written down, and nobody in the whole
of this country is going to say my name to anybody for any reason ever.*

She sat with that.

*And that's the worst thing that's ever happened to me.*

*And it's the safest I've ever been.*

*And they're the same fact.*

She put the bag inside her shift and put her arms round her knees.
