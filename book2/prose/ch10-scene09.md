# Chapter 10, Scene 9 — The Afternoon

*Act 1, month 10, the tenth to the twelfth. Ōtoma. Tadayoshi Ruri, age
ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,600 words.*

---

He got back on the tenth and had two days with nothing in them, and used
them the way he used everything, which was to go through the numbers.

He had three now. Three is enough to have a shape.

*Anazawa: two hours eleven of setting and eleven minutes of work.*

*Sakabe: forty minutes of setting and twelve minutes.*

*Nunobiki: eleven days, of which four were the client's and two were mine
finding out the four were the client's, and nine minutes.*

He laid them out on the boards with a pencil and did what he always did,
which was to look for the part that was not doing any work.

---

*The setting is coming down.*

*Two hours eleven, then forty minutes, then — Nunobiki I cannot use,
because the four days corrupt it, but the actual setting on Nunobiki was
about thirty.*

*So it is a hundred and thirty-one, forty, thirty.*

*And that is not me getting better at setting.*

He sat with it.

*It is me needing less of it.*

*At Anazawa I did not know what I needed to know, so I got everything.
Four nights, the routine, the dog, the drain, the woman with the pot, the
brazier. Half of it I never used.*

*At Sakabe I knew what I was going to need before I got there, so I got
that and stopped.*

**And what I know now that I did not know on the twenty-second of the
ninth month is which things matter, and I did not learn that from anybody,
and I could not write it down if I tried.**

---

He tried to write it down. That took most of the eleventh.

He got about nine items and none of them was the thing, and he knew none
of them was the thing while he was writing them, and did it anyway because
the writing was how he found out what he had.

At the end of it he had a page of true statements and an absence in the
middle of them, and he put a line round the absence and wrote in the
middle of the line:

> *There is a thing here that I have and cannot state.*

He had never written that sentence before either.

*I do not like it.*

*I do not like it because a thing I cannot state is a thing I cannot check
and cannot teach and cannot correct if it is wrong, and I do not know
whether it is wrong, and the only test I have got for it is whether the
work comes out, and the work has come out three times, which is nothing.*

---

The other thing he did on the eleventh was to sit down and go at the
question properly, because it had been sitting there since Nunobiki and he
did not leave things sitting.

*What has changed in me in fourteen days?*

He went at it honestly and got four answers, and three of them were
nothing.

*I am not frightened. — I was not frightened before. That has not moved.*

*I do not enjoy it. — Checked four times. No.*

*I do not think about them. — True, and it was true after the riverbed as
well, so it is not new.*

Then the fourth one, which took him until about midnight.

---

*The part that got easier was not the killing.*

He put the pencil down.

*The killing was never hard. That is the thing I have been circling for a
fortnight and could not get at, and it is the wrong end of it entirely.
The killing was eleven minutes at Anazawa and twelve at Sakabe and nine at
Nunobiki, and it was not hard at any point, and it was not hard on the
riverbed either when I was ten years and one month old and had never done
it.*

*So there is no *got easier* about the killing. There was nothing there to
get easier.*

He looked at the wall for a bit.

*What got easier is the afternoon.*

---

He wrote it out because writing it out was the only way he had of holding
a thing at arm's length.

*On the twenty-sixth of the ninth month I did the job at nine forty and I
walked five hours and I sat on a milestone at three in the morning and I
did the corrections. And then I was awake until it got light, and I could
not have told anybody what I was doing with the hours, and I did not
sleep.*

*On the second of the tenth I did the job at four fifty-two in the
afternoon and I ate at seven.*

*And on the ninth I did the job and I was asleep by half past eleven and I
slept the whole night, and I woke up on the tenth and the first thing I
thought about was whether the north road would be wet.*

He read that back.

**The first thing I thought about was whether the north road would be
wet.**

---

He went at it for another hour and could not make it into a fault.

*Is it worse work? — No. It is better work. Sakabe was better than
Anazawa and Nunobiki was better than Sakabe.*

*Am I less careful? — No. The corrections column has got fewer things in
it because there are fewer things to put in it, and I have checked that
that is what happened and not the other thing.*

*Is anybody worse off? — The people I was sent to are dead and they would
have been dead if I had not slept.*

*So on every measure I have got, it is an improvement.*

He sat with that for a long time, on the boards, in the dark, at ten years
and five months old.

*And I do not think it is an improvement.*

*And I cannot say why, and I have got no instrument that says why, and
every single instrument I have got says the opposite.*

---

He went and got the sheets.

He had ruled them on the third of the seventh month, in a plantation
outside Hanase, in the wet, out of eleven leaves of the harbour office's
spoiled paper that Nunome had let him take because they were going on the
fire anyway, and they had five columns, and he had had to invent the
fifth one standing there with the pen in his hand.

*What it cost.*

There were two lines on them.

The first was the third of the seventh month, and three of the columns
were a dash, because the whole of it was a question asked politely
through a floor by a man he never saw, and the last column said *one
coat*.

The second was the left-hander.

He looked at that one for a while, because it was the best page he had
ever made and he knew it — the feet that were wrong for a swap, the two
goes at his hands, the forty yards, the three seconds spent looking at
the ground he had opened — and then he made himself look at the top of it
instead of the middle.

*17/9.*

He turned the sheet over. Nothing on the back. He turned it back and read
the date again because he did not believe it.

*Seventeenth of the ninth month.*

*And today is the eleventh of the tenth.*

*That is twenty-four days.*

And then, because he could not stop himself doing it:

*And the docket came on the twentieth.*

*Three days.*

---

He sat with the sheets open on his knee.

*I have done three contracts and stood in a room with Kadoya and been
paid eleven thousand two hundred ryō, and the last thing I put on these
sheets I put there before any of it started.*

*And I did not decide to stop.*

He went looking for the decision, because that was what he did with any
outcome, and there was no decision anywhere, and he recognised the shape
of that immediately because he had seen it before, in the eighth month,
about something else.

*Things do not get decided. They get decided once, by accident, and then
they are what there is.*

He put his hand flat on the open sheet.

*So either nothing has come for me in twenty-four days —*

He stopped there, because the second half of the sentence was standing in
front of him and he did not want to write it down, and he noticed that he
did not want to, and wrote it down.

> ***— or something has, and I have stopped being the kind of person who
> notices.***

---

He did not resolve it.

That is the important part and it should be said flatly: he did not
resolve it, and he did not put a third line on the sheets, and he did not
decide anything at all.

What he did was rule a line under the left-hander, and date it the
eleventh of the tenth month, and write one more line under that, which
was an instruction to himself and which he kept for the rest of his life
and never once obeyed:

> *Check these on the first of every month. If there is nothing to add,
> that is the entry.*

Then he folded them, and put them at the bottom of the pack under the
throwables, which is where they stayed, and did the six minutes on the
roll, and went to sleep at about one, and slept the whole night.
