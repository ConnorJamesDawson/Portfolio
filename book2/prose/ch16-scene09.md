# Chapter 16, Scene 9 — Forty Yards

*Act 2, year 1, month 4, the ninth. The second ford below Hane. Nozaki
Uzuki, age eleven. POV: Uzuki. ~1,900 words.*

---

She stood up when the dog changed.

That was the thing she had decided on two days before and she had decided
on it because she could not decide on the cattle. The cattle were the
signal he had been given and the cattle went in and she made herself sit
through it, because going when the cattle went in meant walking into a ford
with forty head in it, and nobody had thought about that, and she had.

So she gave it the dog.

The dog changed at about the count of ninety and she stood up.

---

She put the pack down flat first.

She had worked that out on the seventh. A pack stood on its end is a pack
somebody is coming back for and a pack lying flat is a thing on the ground,
and she was not going to leave anything behind her that made a person ask a
question.

Then she walked past him.

She did not look at him. She had decided on that too, and it had been the
hardest of the four decisions and she had made it on the eighth in about
two minutes, and the reasoning had been cold and correct.

*If I look at him I am a girl checking with somebody.*

*And a girl checking with somebody is a girl who has been sent.*

She went past him at four feet with the hat on and did not turn her head,
and the whole of what she had of him was that he did not put a hand out,
and she had four days of walking in which to decide what that meant and
she used all of them.

---

**Forty yards, and it took a minute and forty seconds.**

She counted it, and the counting was not for the count. The counting was
because she had worked out on the eighth that a person walking without
counting walks at whatever speed she is frightened at, and she was not
going to arrive at a cart at a speed that told anybody anything.

*One. Two. Three.*

**Down the near bank to the shingle at eleven.** The bank is cut back and
there is a way down at the upstream end of the willows where the cattle had
been coming up for eleven years, and she took it, because a way that is
worn is a way nobody looks at.

**Into the water at nineteen.** It was to the middle of her calf and it was
cold enough to be a thing and she had thought about that too and had done
nothing about it because there was nothing to do about it.

**Past the tail of the drove at about thirty.**

That was the part she had not been able to plan and it turned out to be the
easiest thing in the whole of the ninth. Forty head of cattle in a ford is
not an obstacle, it is a wall of a different kind: it is a place where
nobody can see anything, and she went along behind the last four of them
with her hand on nothing and the noise sitting on top of her like a roof.

**Out the far side at fifty-one.**

---

And then the open, and it was thirty yards of it, and this is the part.

There were eleven people on the far bank doing something to each other and
she went up the drove track through the middle of them at a walk.

**She counted the ones who did not look at her.**

*One.* A man on his knees by the front wheel of the cart with both hands
on his own leg, four feet from her as she went past, and he looked up and
straight through her and back down at his leg.

*Two.* A man standing with his back to her about eleven feet off, not
fighting, watching two other people fight and waiting to go in.

*Three and four.* The two who were fighting.

*Five.* The one with the bow, on the bank above the track, who was looking
for a shot and swept his eyes across the whole of the ground she was
standing on and did not stop on her, and she was the only person on it who
was not moving fast.

*Six.* The dog.

She got to eleven before she got to the cart and the eleventh was a man
who did look at her — properly, for about a second, from twenty feet — and
then went back to what he was doing, and she has thought about him more
than the other ten put together.

*He saw me.*

*And he decided I was nothing.*

*And he was right, and he is going to be right about that for the rest of
my life, and I have just found out what it is worth.*

---

**The cart.**

Two wheels, a mule down in the shafts and dead, and the cover half off it
at the back where somebody had already been in.

There were four things on it.

Two sacks. A long box, about six feet, roped down the length of it. And a
crate.

She got up on the tailboard and stood there and did the whole of it in
about eleven seconds, and it is the best eleven seconds of her life and
nobody has ever known about it.

*Whatever's small enough.*

*The sacks are grain and I am not carrying grain forty yards for anybody.*

*The long one is six feet and I cannot lift it and if I could I would be a
girl carrying a six-foot box.*

*So it is the crate.*

And then, because she was already on the tailboard and it took no extra
time at all, she looked at how the crate was tied.

---

**And the lashings went over and not round.**

She had her hand on the rope before she understood why her hand was on the
rope.

*That is wrong.*

*You put a rope round a thing to stop it moving about. Round the girth,
round the ends, through and back — that is a rope holding a thing still.*

*This one goes over the top and down both sides and into the bed, and there
are two of them, crossed, and there is nothing round it at all.*

*That is not stopping it moving about.*

*That is holding it **down**.*

She stood on the tailboard of a dead man's cart in the middle of a fight
with her hand on a rope.

*You hold a thing down when the thing can push.*

*Grain does not push. Cloth does not push. Dye does not push.*

And then she had it, and she had it the way she had everything, which is
all at once and with nothing underneath it.

**There is a person in there.**

---

She did not open it.

She has been asked about that twice in her life and both times she gave the
same answer and both times it was the true one and neither person believed
it was the whole of it.

*Because it is four feet by two and it has been shut and I do not know how
long, and if I take the lid off in the middle of a field with eleven people
on it then whatever is inside it is in the middle of a field with eleven
people on it.*

*And I have got no way of putting it back.*

She put both hands on it and rocked it about an inch and it came back
against her, once, softly, the way a thing comes back that is not fixed to
the floor.

That was the whole of the confirmation and she did not need it and she took
it anyway.

---

Then she did the loudest thing anybody did on that field.

She got down off the tailboard, turned round, and said it.

Not to anybody. She did not pick a person and she did not go to anybody and
she did not lower her voice, and she said it at the volume you say a thing
at when there are cattle in a river forty yards away, which is very loud
indeed.

**"THERE'S SOMEBODY IN THE CRATE."**

Four people stopped.

The man on his knees by the wheel looked up at her, and the man who had
decided she was nothing looked at her again and this time he did not stop
looking, and somebody thirty feet up the track said something she did not
catch, and the one with the bow came down off the bank.

And she stood by the tailboard with her hands at her sides and did not say
it again, because she had said it, and saying a thing twice is for people
who are not sure.

---

**And she never once considered not saying it.**

That is the part she cannot make anybody understand and she stopped trying
at about fifteen.

Everybody who has ever asked her about the ninth of the fourth month has
asked it the same way — *weren't you frightened, why did you say it out
loud, why not go and tell him quietly* — and every one of them has been
asking about a decision, and there was not one.

She has thought about it since. She has gone at it properly, the way she
goes at everything, and the closest she has ever got is this:

*I did not know whose he was.*

*I had a crate with a person in it in a field where two lots of people were
killing each other and I did not know which lot the crate was for or which
lot had come to get it, and the only thing I knew for certain was that
nobody standing there knew what I knew.*

*So I gave it to all of them at once.*

*Because a thing everybody has been told is a thing nobody can quietly
decide about.*

She was eleven.

She had been in that field for a minute and fifty seconds and she had
walked into it as a girl fetching something small, and she came out of it
as the only person on either bank who had known, out loud, in front of
witnesses, what the cargo was.

---

There is one more thing and it happened while she was walking back and it
took about four seconds.

She heard a note come up.

She had no name for it and she did not turn round. It came up out of
nothing, upstream, behind her, and went high and stayed there for about two
seconds, and then it came down.

She kept walking.

*It came down.*

She had heard that once before, in a hall in the twelfth month, and that
one had taken four minutes to come down and she had watched the light go
out of a room while it did.

This one was under two seconds and there were cattle over the top of it and
it was the smallest sound in the valley.

She put it in the place where the things went that there was no paper for.

It was the fourth item in there.

It was eleven months before she worked out that it was the same series as
the first.
