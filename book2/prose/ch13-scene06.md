# Chapter 13, Scene 6 — Four Hundred Yards

*Act 2, year 1, month 1, the twelfth to the seventeenth. Ōtoma to Hazaki.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,950 words.*

---

He built the list on the twelfth and thirteenth and it took him about
nine hours across the two days, and the whole of it was possible for one
reason, which he noticed on the second morning and did not enjoy.

*In the eleventh month I had four days and a boy who had seen my face at
forty feet and I could not ask anybody anything, because a person asking
where you put a child four days after a household came off a mill ground
is a person who has just told the whole valley what he did.*

*And now I have got a girl who came off a mountain nobody has heard about
yet, and time, and eighteen thousand ryō, and I can walk into a town and
ask.*

He sat with that.

*So it was never that there was nothing. It was that I could not look.*

He put it in the operational column and it came out at nine words and no
comfort in it at all.

> *The eleventh month failed on cover, not on the world.*

---

There were four and he did them in order.

**One — a religious house, two days south-east, that took children.**

He went and looked at it from a bank above the road for most of a morning
and did not go in.

*There is a hall and a long building and about a third of an acre of
ground cut into a slope, and there are nine or ten people in the long
building who are not the people who run it, and there are four men and
they are all over forty, and the ground has been dug by somebody who is
not one of the four.*

*And there is a store at the north end.*

He got up off the bank at about eleven and walked away from it and did not
go back, and he wrote the reason down that night and could not make the
reason be a reason.

> *Same shape. Not going.*

*That is not an argument. That is a building being like another building,
and every temple in this country is like every other temple, and if I
struck out every place that looked like that one I would have struck out
the whole of the list before I started.*

He looked at the line.

*And I am not going back.*

He left it in the book as it was, with no case under it, which he had done
about three times in his life.

---

**Two — a family at Nunobiki who would take a girl for money.**

He got the name off a woman at a market and went and had the conversation
and it took eleven minutes and he was out of it inside six.

The man named a figure in the first four sentences.

*He has not asked her age. He has not asked whether she is well. He has
not asked whether she can do anything, and a person taking a child in to
work asks that one first because it is the only one that matters to him.*

*He asked how long, and then he said a number.*

*So what he is buying is not a girl, it is a payment, and the girl is what
he has to have in the house to go on getting it, and the day the payment
is late she is a cost.*

He said he would think about it and did not think about it.

> *Named the price before he asked her age. No.*

---

**Three — an indenture, at a dyer's in Ōgi.**

That one was real and it was the one he nearly took, and it took him a
whole evening to work out why he was not going to.

*Seven years. Keep, and a trade at the end of it, and the trade is worth
something and the man is not a bad man and I asked four people.*

*And the paper says seven years.*

He sat with the paper in his hand in a lodging with a lamp.

*Seven years is not a door. A door is a thing you go through and then it
is over. This is a thing she goes into and cannot come out of for seven
years, and the person who decides whether it was a good idea is a man with
a dye-house, every day, for seven years.*

*And on the twenty-eighth of the tenth month a man in a hall said **not
before the eleventh month** about her and everybody in that room heard it
and she was standing in it.*

*I am not signing a paper that says a length of time about her.*

He put it down and did not put a case under that one either, and the case
was there and he could have made it and did not know it.

> *Seven years on a paper. No.*

---

**Four was Sen, at Hazaki, and it was a rope-walk.**

He got it the way he had got everything useful in his life, which was by
asking three people what a fourth person was like and listening to the
order they said things in.

She was about forty. Her husband had been dead five years and the walk was
his and she had run it since. She had two of her own, a boy of nine and a
girl of thirteen, and she had had a third pair of hands until the eighth
month, when the girl who had them went home to a village that had sent for
her, and she had not replaced her because you cannot get anybody in the
twelfth month.

She wanted hands.

She said so in the first minute, before anything else, and then asked how
old, and then asked whether she was strong, and then asked, on the fourth
question, whether she was any good.

**That was the order.** *Hands, age, strength, quality, and money fifth.*

He knew inside those four questions and made himself take another two
days.

---

He did the two days properly.

**Day one and day two: four hundred yards, from a rise on the north side,
morning and afternoon.**

The rope-walk was a long open shed and about a hundred and forty yards of
laid ground, and he watched three people work it for two days and learned
four things.

*The boy of nine does about a fifth of the work and is not made to do
more.*

*The girl of thirteen does about the same as the woman, and the woman lets
her, and twice the woman went and did a thing the girl had started and did
not tell her she had done it wrong.*

*They eat outside at noon and they eat the same thing and it is on the same
plates.*

*And on the second afternoon the boy stopped and sat down for about twenty
minutes and nobody came over.*

He wrote all four down and looked at the fourth one longest, because it
was the one that could not be arranged in advance for anybody's benefit.

---

**And then the town.**

He asked three people and he asked them the question he had learned to ask
in the sixth month, which was not *what is she like* but *what does she
pay*.

The first said she paid on the day and had never once not.

The second said she had had a girl before who went home and that the girl
had cried when she went, and volunteered that without being asked, which
made it worth about four times as much as if he had asked for it.

The third said she was hard about the work and did not talk much and that
her husband had been a nicer man and a worse one to work for.

He wrote that third one down twice.

> *Says she is hard and says she pays. Says the dead man was nicer and
> worse. That is a person telling the truth about somebody they like.*

---

He did the arithmetic on the evening of the sixteenth on a wall above
Hazaki and it came out at yes, and it came out at yes on every column he
had.

*Real work, which she is good at and likes and does not know she likes.*

*A woman who lets a child sit down.*

*Four days from Ōtoma, so I can look at it from a rise twice a year and
never go in.*

*And no paper with a length of time on it, because it is an arrangement
and an arrangement stops when either party stops it.*

He sat with the whole of it and could not find the fault, and looked for
it for about an hour because he did not trust a thing he could not find
the fault in, and there was not one.

*It is better than anything I have got and better than anything I am ever
going to have.*

---

He paid on the seventeenth in the morning and he paid a year in advance,
and that was deliberate and he could have defended it.

*A thing that is paid for is a thing that does not depend on me. If I am
dead in the third month she is still fed until the first month next year
and that is twelve months in which somebody could send for her from
somewhere.*

Sen asked six thousand for the year, which was fair and was under what he
had costed.

He paid eight.

He went up the hill afterwards and sat down and made himself find out why,
because two thousand ryō is two thousand ryō and he had just given it away
against a price he had already agreed.

It took him about ten minutes and he got two answers and did not like
either.

*One. Because I wanted it and she knew I wanted it, and I have written
that down about myself once already, in Ōtoma, on the twenty-eighth of the
tenth month, about a pair of boots.*

*Two. Because it is not a fee. A fee buys a thing that is done. This buys
a thing that goes on, and the whole of the year is in front of it, and I
have got no way at all to check any of it after the fourth day, and two
thousand ryō is what I paid to not have to.*

He put the second one in the book because it was the true one and it had a
shape.

> *Paid 8 against 6. The extra is not for her. It is so that I can stop
> looking.*

---

He told her on the seventeenth at about noon, walking up the lane to the
rope-walk, and it took him eleven words.

"There's a woman here wants hands. It's a year. It's paid."

Uzuki said, "All right."

That was the whole of it. She said it the way she said everything, which
was flat and fast and without any question in it, and she went up the lane
beside him and did not slow down.

He noticed afterwards — much later, on a road, with about six miles of not
saying anything behind him — that in nine days of looking, and four
candidates, and two days on a rise, and three people asked, and eight
thousand ryō, **he had not once asked her.**

He had not thought of it.

It had not been on the list of things to do, because it had not been on
any list, because a list is a set of things that occur to you.
