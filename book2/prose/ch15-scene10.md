# Chapter 15, Scene 10 — Get the Cargo and Go

*Act 2, year 1, month 3, the twenty-seventh and the twenty-eighth. Ōtoma.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,700 words.*

---

The briefing was on the twenty-seventh at four in the afternoon and it
took nine minutes and he had been waiting twenty-five days for it.

That is not a complaint and he did not make one. Twenty-five days between
acceptance and briefing is long and it is not unheard of and there are
four ordinary reasons for it, and he had all four of them and had not
troubled to pick.

He went in at four. Ōtsuka had the sheet on the counter and a second one
under it and did not pick either of them up.

---

"Right," Ōtsuka said. "The mill yard at Ochiai. That's two hours north on
the west track, past the second ford, you'll see the wheel."

"I know it."

"First light on the twenty-ninth. It'll be out in the yard on a pallet.
Man there called Ide. He'll have the counterpart and he'll want yours."

"Mine's this."

"That's it. Don't lose it."

Tadayoshi put it away.

"Sarugaya by the ninth day at the outside. Don't go in — there's a stone
bridge on the south side and you go under it and along the water, and the
track picks up on the far side. Then the crossing below Hane, and the
other counterpart will be produced."

"By who."

"Doesn't say. It'll be produced."

"Right."

---

He asked four and he had them ready and he had had them ready for
twenty-five days, and every one of them got an answer that was better than
he had expected, which is the thing he noticed afterwards and could not do
anything with.

"How's it crated."

"Case. Bound. It'll be on a pallet and it goes on your frame off the
pallet and you don't take it off the frame again."

"Ninety kan gross — is that with the case or without."

"With."

*Good. That is a real answer and it is the one I wanted, because ninety
with the case is about seventy without and seventy is a load and ninety is
an argument.*

"If the river road's down."

"Take the long way and you're still inside eleven. That's why it's
eleven."

*And that is what I worked out on the fourth and I worked it out off the
sheet, which means whoever wrote the sheet did the same arithmetic I did
and did it first.*

"What's in it."

Ōtsuka looked at him.

"Dye," he said. "Says on the sheet."

It did not say on the sheet. It said *1 case* and it said *ab. 90 kan
gross* and it said *sealed at origin*, and it did not say dye anywhere,
and Tadayoshi had it word for word and knew it did not.

And he did not say so.

He has gone over that afterwards, more than once, over years, and he
never found a version of it where he had a reason. He was not being
careful. He was not letting a man save face. He had asked four questions
and got four answers and the fifth had come back with an extra word on it,
and the extra word was ordinary, and he took it, and the whole of it took
about a second and a half.

*Dye.*

*Right.*

---

There was one line left and it is the one this chapter is about, and it
had been on the sheet since the second and he had read it perhaps forty
times.

> *No weighing required at transfer.*

He had a thing about it and he had had the thing about it for weeks and he
had never once got it up as far as a question, and standing at the counter
on the twenty-seventh he got closest, and what he got to was this:

*I always weigh.*

*I have weighed at transfer on every single freight job I have done,
because a man at the far end who signs for ninety and gets eighty-four is
a man with a problem and the problem is me.*

He opened his mouth.

And then he answered it himself before it got out, and the answer was
correct, and it is the correct answer, and there is nothing wrong with any
part of it.

*The fee is flat.*

*Four thousand for the case. Not four thousand for ninety kan. There is
no rate in it, so there is nothing to weigh against, so a weight is not
evidence of anything to anybody.*

*And they have said so on the sheet, on the front, in advance, which is
the opposite of hiding it, and it saves me an hour at each end and a set
of scales I have not got.*

*That is not a hole. That is a clerk who has thought about the job.*

He shut his mouth.

He wrote *no wgh* in the operational column that evening with nothing
under it, because it was a fact about a job and facts about jobs went in
the book, and it sat there for eleven days and then it sat there for
years.

---

Ōtsuka squared the sheet on the counter and put a finger on it.

"Get the cargo and go," he said. "Don't stop at Sarugaya. Don't be
anywhere twice."

"Right."

"Three thousand on the counterpart at Hane."

"Right."

And that was the briefing, and it was nine minutes, and there was one
person in it who had a name.

He got out into the yard and stood there for a second in the way he did
when he was putting a thing in order, and then he went and got the mule.

---

He did the book that night and it is short and it is the last thing in
the third month.

> *WHAT IS LEFT*
>
> *6,988.*
>
> *(1,000 against.)*
>
> *2/3 – 27/3, feed, food, the shoe: −411.*
>
> **6,577.**

He looked at it.

*It went up once.*

*And it has been going down for twenty-five days and it will go down for
eleven more and then it goes up by three thousand, and three thousand
against four hundred and eleven is the best twenty-five days I have had
since the tenth month.*

He sat with that and it was fine and it stayed fine, and there is nothing
in this book about him that is more ordinary than the next thing he did,
which was to turn to the front of the operational column and read the four
entries of the third month in order, because he did that at the end of
every month and had since he was nine.

> *9/3. The Sarugaya sheet rings. Read before. Cannot place it.*
>
> *14/3. It is complete. Nothing in it is in it once. Nothing you would
> remember. That is good work.*
>
> *18/3. Checked the nine + the two. Nothing like it. It is not one of
> mine.*
>
> *27/3. Briefing. Ochiai, first light 29th. Ide. No wgh.*

Four lines. Three of them with the made-and-not-counted mark beside them,
which he had put on three things in a month that were not numbers, and had
stopped seeing.

He read them down and then up.

*There is a thing in that.*

He waited for it.

Nothing came, and he was not surprised, and he did not go after it,
because he had gone after it for four days on the fifth to the ninth and
had come away with a rule about feelings and findings, and the rule was
good and he applied it.

He shut the book.

---

She was on the other side of the fire with the hat on, which she had
started doing in the evenings for no reason he had asked about, and she
said:

"Eleven days."

"Eleven."

"What's the place at the end called?"

"The crossing below Hane."

"That's not a name. That's a description."

He looked up.

*That is true.*

*Ochiai is a name. Sarugaya is a name. Hane is a name and it is a town and
we are not going to it, we are going to a crossing below it, and there are
four crossings below Hane and the sheet does not say which and I have not
asked because the counterpart will be produced.*

He had that whole and it took about two seconds and it went nowhere,
because the counterpart *would* be produced, and a counterpart is a name
and a place and a hand, and the sheet had said so on its face, and the
sheet had been right about everything else for twenty-five days.

"It'll be on the counterpart," he said.

"Right," she said.

She put a stick on the fire.

They left at half past three in the morning on the twenty-eighth to be at
Ochiai for first light on the twenty-ninth, and the mule went well and the
road was dry and the weather held, and it was, by every measure he had and
every measure she had, an ordinary start to an ordinary job.
