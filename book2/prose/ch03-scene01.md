# Chapter 3, Scene 1 — The Message

*Act 1, month 4, the third. The Academy at Kirigakure, and the ferry
stair. Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,730 words.*

---

Aoyagi was four sentences into the difference between a substitution and
a reversal when somebody put their head round the door of the hall.

Tadayoshi got the whole of it because he was at the end of the row by the
wall and the door was on that side, and because he had been at the end of
the row by the wall for a year and had learned in that time that the end
of the row saw everything.

It was a woman in a grey overcoat and she did not come in.

She said something with about six words in it. Aoyagi said something with
about three. She went.

Then Aoyagi came back to the front of the hall and picked up his sentence
in the wrong place.

---

*He has not finished it.*

*He said "the reversal wants you to have already decided where you are
going, which is why —" and then the door, and now he has started again at
"the reversal wants you to have," and he is going to go round it a second
time and come out somewhere else.*

*He has never done that. Not once in a year. He finishes sentences the way
he closes doors.*

Tadayoshi wrote the date in the corner of his slate, which was a thing he
had started doing about a month ago and had not yet worked out why.

*Third of the fourth month. Grey coat. Four minutes.*

---

It was four minutes.

He counted them off the eave drip, which was going at eleven to the
minute because the rain had settled in overnight and had that flat sound
it got when it intended to stay, and he got to forty-three and then
Aoyagi said:

"Ruri."

"Sensei."

"Do me a favour and run down to the ferry stair."

---

The hall did not do anything. Twenty-five people went on copying out the
difference between a substitution and a reversal, because a boy being
sent on an errand is the single least interesting thing that happens in a
schoolroom.

"Sensei?"

"There's a man called Kōsuke who does the second boat. Long face, no
hat, he'll be at the bottom of the stair or he'll be in the shelter."
Aoyagi was already looking back at the board. "Tell him the fourth is
confirmed."

"The fourth."

"The fourth is confirmed."

"Is there a — " Tadayoshi looked at the desk, which had a slate on it and
chalk and no paper of any kind on it at all. "Do you want me to take
something?"

"No. Just tell him."

"Sensei, if it is a — "

"Ruri," said Aoyagi, "it's four words and it's raining and if you leave
now you'll be back before the bell and if you stand here asking me
questions you won't. Go on."

---

He went.

He got his coat off the hook in the passage and went out through the yard
and down the eleven steps and along the wall, and he was three streets
away before he stopped being annoyed enough to think.

*He did not write it down.*

*He has never once sent anybody anywhere without writing it down. He
wrote a chit for Ryūta to take four staves across the yard. He wrote a
chit for a bucket.*

*And it is nothing. "The fourth is confirmed" is not a message, it is a
fragment of one — there is no subject in it and no place in it and I do
not know what the fourth is.*

He walked down the hill towards the water with his collar up.

*So it is not a message. It is an instruction with a message painted on
the front of it, and the instruction is: be somewhere else.*

*Which means —*

---

*The note.*

He stopped in the street and stood there for a second, and then went on,
faster, because standing still in the rain achieved nothing.

*The note went up eleven days ago. He told me himself, in the corridor,
in four words, in the same voice he has just used.*

*And a woman in a grey overcoat came to the door of a hall and said six
words to him about a boy in that hall, and four minutes later I am out
of the building.*

*They are moving me again.*

He got about a hundred yards on that and found, distantly, that he was
extremely angry.

*Last year it was a form with "aptitude" on it and I was moved a year up
and I was not told until I turned up on the Monday and there were
different people in the room.*

*This year it is a note that a kind man put up because I did four seals
instead of five, and there is a woman in a grey coat, and somebody has
decided something, and the way I am going to be told is that I will be
sent out of a room while they say it.*

*I would like, once, to be in the room.*

He went down the last of the hill and along the wharf and got to the
ferry stair at about twenty past eleven.

---

Kōsuke had a long face and no hat and he was in the shelter, sitting on a
crate with his boots up on a bollard, doing something to a rope.

"Kōsuke-san?"

"That's me."

"Aoyagi-sensei says the fourth is confirmed."

Kōsuke looked up.

"The fourth what?"

---

Tadayoshi stood in the rain at the bottom of the ferry stair.

"That is the message."

"That's the whole of it?"

"That is the whole of it. He said: tell him the fourth is confirmed."

"Aoyagi." Kōsuke put the rope down. "Little fella, glasses, teaches the
seals?"

"He does not wear glasses."

"No. No, he doesn't." Kōsuke scratched the side of his neck. "The fourth
of what, though. The fourth of the month's gone, that was Tuesday. The
fourth boat's not a thing, there's three. I've not got anything on the
fourth."

"I do not know."

"Well," said Kōsuke, and looked at him for a moment with an expression
that Tadayoshi read at the time as mild irritation at having his morning
interrupted by a child with half a sentence.

"You want to get out of that rain," he said.

"I am going back."

"Aye," said Kōsuke. "Well."

---

He went back up the stair.

He was, he estimated afterwards — and he did estimate it, four months
afterwards, sitting somewhere else entirely with a piece of chalk — about
eleven minutes at the bottom of that stair, all told, including the
walking down and the standing about, and there was no possible way to get
any of them back.

At the top he stopped and turned round and looked at the water.

*He does not know what the fourth is either.*

*So it is not a thing they arranged. It is not a code between them,
because a code has to be agreed and Kōsuke has not agreed anything, he is
sitting on a crate with a rope wondering why a wet child has interrupted
him.*

*Aoyagi picked a name off the top of his head. He picked a man he knows by
sight at the bottom of a hill, and a form of words with no content in it,
and sent a boy fourteen hundred yards.*

Tadayoshi stood at the top of the ferry stair in the rain with his hands
in his sleeves.

He got to the edge of it.

He got to the exact edge of it — he could feel the shape of the thing
sitting there the way you can feel a step in the dark before your foot
finds it — and then the bell went, up the hill, thin through the weather,
and he thought: *the bell,* and then he thought: *I will be late,* and
the shape went away.

---

He was late.

Aoyagi did not say anything about it, which he noticed, and filed, and
assigned to the wrong drawer.

The rest of the morning was reversals and the middle of the afternoon was
the wrist drill, and Aoyagi walked the line behind them with his weight
back and the boards clicking, and Tadayoshi did four seals and put his
palm on the floor and swapped with a bench, and Aoyagi walked past him
without putting two fingers anywhere near his wrist, and did not stop,
and did not look at him.

He filed that too.

At the end Aoyagi said, "Right, off you go," to the room, and started
collecting the slates, and Tadayoshi got his coat.

At the door he stopped.

"Sensei."

"Mm."

"The message did not mean anything."

Aoyagi went on stacking slates.

"Didn't it," he said.

"Kōsuke-san said *the fourth what*. He did not know. He was not expecting
it and he did not understand it and he asked me twice."

"Mm."

Tadayoshi stood in the doorway with his coat half on.

*Ask him.*

*He is standing four feet away with his back to you stacking slates and
he has not looked at you all afternoon and he did not tap your wrist and
he is not going to say anything unless you ask, and if you ask he will
have to answer, because he is the sort of man who answers.*

*Ask him what the woman in the grey coat said.*

"Ruri," said Aoyagi, to the slates. "Go home."

---

So he went home.

It was an hour and ten minutes on the coast road at a walk and he did it
in fifty-five because the rain was coming across sideways off the water
and there was no point in being reasonable about it, and he spent about
forty of the fifty-five composing, in his head, in order, with the
objection written in, the case for being told when a decision was made
about him.

It was quite a good case.

He had got as far as the third pass by the time he came up the long
shoulder above the inlet, and he was thinking about the narrow column,
and about what would go in it, and whether *I would like, once, to be in
the room* was a finding or a complaint, and whether there was a
difference.

Then he came over the top and saw the hill.
