# Chapter 10, Scene 7 — Two More

*Act 1, month 10, the second to the ninth. Sakabe, and the Nunobiki road.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,310 words.*

---

The second one was on the second of the tenth month at Sakabe and the
whole of it is four lines in the book.

> *2/10. Sakabe. Ide's man on the wharf, the one with the ledger.*
>
> *In at 4:40, out at 4:52. Twelve minutes and eight of them were the
> water.*
>
> *2,900 due.*
>
> *No corrections.*

**No corrections** was the part he sat with. He had never written it
before. There had been corrections on every single thing he had ever done
since he was eight years old, including the ones that had gone perfectly,
because a thing that goes perfectly has usually got a piece of luck in it
and the luck is a correction.

He went through it four times looking for one and there wasn't one, and he
wrote *no corrections* and then wrote, underneath it, in the operational
column:

> *Which is either the truth or the beginning of not being able to see
> them.*

---

The third was on the ninth and it took eleven days to arrive at, and four
of the eleven days were spent sitting in Nunobiki doing nothing at all.

The docket said the sixth. He was in position on the fifth.

On the morning of the sixth a man he had never seen before came to where
he was staying and said, in the manner of somebody delivering a message
he did not understand either, that it was to be the ninth.

"Why?"

"That's what I've got."

"Is the man moving?"

"That's what I've got," the man said again, and went.

---

He did the work on it, because he did the work on everything.

*If the target is moving, the ninth is because he will be somewhere on the
ninth that he is not now. That is operational and it is the only good
reason.*

*So: is he moving?*

He spent the sixth and the seventh establishing that the man was not
moving. He was where he had been. He kept the same hours. There was no
cart, no packing, nobody new in the house, no change of any kind, and by
the evening of the seventh Tadayoshi had it flat.

*He is not moving.*

*So the ninth is not about him.*

He turned that over on the eighth and did not get to the bottom of it and
wrote down where he had stopped.

*The ninth is about something else that happens on the ninth, or about
somebody who will hear about it on the ninth, or about a day of the week,
or about a date somebody wants it to be on.*

*I do not know which and I cannot find out from here.*

**And I have been paid for eleven days and the work is one night.**

---

He did it on the ninth.

He did it well. It took nine minutes and six of them were on a stair, and
there is nothing else to say about it, and he wrote the four lines and the
fee and *no corrections* for the second time running and sat looking at
the second *no corrections* for longer than he had looked at the first.

Then he did the accounting on the four days, because a cost is a cost.

> *4 days waiting, Nunobiki. Reason unknown and not operational.*

And under it, because he had a rule about entries with *unknown* in them
and the rule was that you write what you would need to close it:

> *Closes when I find out what happened on the ninth.*

He never did.

---

**What had happened on the ninth was that a man in a room forty miles away
had wanted a second man to be told a thing at a particular dinner**, and
the thing he wanted him told was not *Ide's man on the wharf is dead* but
*I can have a man dead on a day of my choosing*, and the day had been
chosen for the dinner and not for the man.

Tadayoshi had been the sentence.

He had no way of knowing that and he did not find out and it does not
matter that he did not find out, because the point of it is that it worked
perfectly and cost the man who arranged it four days of a boy's time,
which was cheap.

---

What he did notice, on the road back, was the shape of the three of them
side by side, which was the first time he had had three of anything.

*Anazawa: 3,600. Two hours eleven and the eleven minutes.*

*Sakabe: 2,900. Twelve minutes.*

*Nunobiki: 4,100, and four days of nothing.*

He did the division, walking.

*So the fee is not for the time.*

*It cannot be for the time. Sakabe was twelve minutes and Nunobiki was
eleven days and Nunobiki is four thousand one hundred.*

*And it is not for the difficulty, because Sakabe was the hardest of the
three by a distance — there was water on three sides of that wharf and I
had one way off it — and Sakabe is the cheapest.*

He stopped in the road.

*So what is it for?*

---

He got about half of it and wrote the half down.

*It is for who the man was.*

*Not who he was to me. Who he was to somebody.*

*And I do not know any of these three men. I did not know their trades
until I got there and I did not know their names until the docket. I do
not know what any of the three of them had done and it has not once
occurred to me to ask, and I have just noticed that, standing in a road,
on the ninth of the tenth month.*

He turned it over for about a mile.

*Would I want to know?*

He examined that properly, because he examined everything.

*No.*

*And I have checked that four ways and it is still no, and the reason it
is no is not that I would not like the answer. It is that the answer would
not change anything I did, so it is not information, and I do not collect
things that are not information.*

He wrote that in the book that night at the ordinary time, in the
ordinary column, in his ordinary hand.

**And under it he wrote, because it had arrived while he was writing the
first part and he wrote things down as they arrived:**

> *Except that I have started wondering what the fee is for, which means I
> have started wondering who they were, which is the same question coming
> in through a different door.*

He looked at that for a while.

Then he ruled it off and did the throwables, which took six minutes, and
went to sleep.

---

He had killed three men for money in fourteen days and had made
eleven thousand two hundred ryō, which was more than nine months of
careful living, and he had four *no corrections* out of a possible three
and one line in the book about a woman with a covered pot.

He had not been frightened at any point.

He had not enjoyed any of it at any point either, and he checked that as
carefully as he checked everything, and it was true, and it stayed true
for the rest of his life.

*It is work.*

*It is the first work I have ever been given that uses the whole of what
I am, and there is nobody in this organisation who can do it as well as I
can, and I have been doing it for a fortnight.*

**That was the sentence. That was the one that should have frightened him
and he wrote it down like a weight.**
