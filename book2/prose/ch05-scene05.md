# Chapter 5, Scene 5 — Alive If Possible

*Act 1, month 5, the eighth. A dry riverbed on the coast road, a day
south of Nezu. Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,600 words.*

---

Forty-one miles had taken him twelve days.

That was on purpose and he had costed it: he had come off the coast road
on the second morning out of Sunaba and had gone parallel to it for four
days on the landward side, and had crossed it twice at night, and had
spent one entire day sitting in gorse above a village watching who went
in and out of it before he went down for food.

*It is slow and it costs four days and I do not know whether it is
necessary.*

*I will never know whether it is necessary. That is the whole trade of
being on a road.*

He had eaten badly and slept worse and he was a day out from Nezu.

He came down into the riverbed at about eleven in the morning because the
road went round the head of it and the bed went straight through, and it
was dry, and it saved a mile and a half.

---

The bed was about sixty yards wide and it had not had water in it since
the spring.

It was stones the size of a fist down the middle where the channel had
been, and then a long flat shoulder of grit on the seaward side —
grey-brown, coarse, sieved by four hundred winters, the best material he
had put his hand into since Shioiri.

He stopped in the middle of it with the tin in his hand before he had
thought about it at all.

*That is better than the third bar down.*

*That is better than anything on our beach and it is lying about in the
open and nobody within eleven miles of here has any use for it
whatsoever.*

He crouched and filled the tin, which took about two minutes, and put the
lid on it and put it back inside his coat, and stood up, and went on.

He got about ninety yards.

---

There were two of them at the far end of the bed where it came out onto
the road again.

They were not hiding. That was the first thing, and it was the thing he
got wrong for about four seconds: they were standing in the open at the
top of the shingle in the light, side by side, in the way that people
stand when they want to be seen from a distance so that the person seeing
them does not do anything sudden.

He stopped.

*Two.*

*Which means four.*

He did not know why he knew that. He had never in his life had anything
to do with anybody's corps, and the arithmetic arrived out of nowhere and
was correct, and he found out four seconds later that it was correct by
looking behind him, where the other two were coming down off the landward
bank about a hundred yards back, unhurried, spread wide.

*They have had the top and the bottom of this bed since before I came
down into it.*

*Which is the shoulder and the inlet at Shioiri. It is the same
arrangement. Somebody teaches it.*

He stood in the middle of sixty yards of open stone with a tin of grit
inside his coat.

---

"Ruri Tadayoshi?"

The one who called it was a girl of about thirteen on the seaward side of
the two at the top, and she called it in the flat carrying voice you use
across water, and she put a hand up when she said it, palm out, which is
not a thing you do to somebody you are about to fight.

He did not answer.

"That's him," said the other one at the top, not to Tadayoshi.

"Hokuto, don't — "

"I'm not doing anything."

*Hokuto.*

*Filed.*

---

They came down in a way that took a long time.

It was, he understood at about the fourth step, a *procedure*: the two at
the top came down the shingle at a walk and the two behind came up the
bed at a walk and all four of them arrived at about the same distance,
which was roughly fifteen yards, and stopped.

Four of them. None of them older than about thirteen.

And they were *new*.

He knew that from the kit. Everything on all four of them was new — the
webbing was pale where it had not gone grey yet and the flak plate on the
girl at the top had the packing crease still in it and one of the ones
behind him had a pouch on the wrong side and had clearly been told about
it and had not moved it.

*They graduated in the spring.*

*They have been in the corps for about eight weeks.*

---

"Right," said the girl. "You know why we're here."

"No."

"You — " She stopped. "You don't?"

"I know that four people have taken both ends of a riverbed to speak to a
boy of ten," said Tadayoshi, "and I know that you have my name. I do not
know what you have been told, and I would like to, and I am aware that
this is not a normal thing to ask."

The four of them looked at each other.

That was the moment. He knew it while it was happening and could do
nothing whatever with it: four children looked at each other across
fifteen yards of stone because the thing in front of them had said a
sentence, and the sentence did not match what was on the paper.

"We're to bring you in," said the girl.

"To where?"

"Kiri."

"For what?"

"That's not — " She glanced at Hokuto. "That's not on the tasking. It
says bring you in."

"May I see it?"

"*No*," said Hokuto.

---

"Miyo," said one of the ones behind him. "Just do the thing."

*Miyo.*

*Two of four. I have two of four and I have had them eleven seconds.*

"I'm doing the thing," said the girl. "Ruri Tadayoshi. You're to come
with us to Kirigakure. You're not charged with anything and nobody's
going to hurt you and you can walk it, it's four days, and we've got food
and we'll not tie you unless you make us."

She had learned it. You could hear the shape of the learning in it, the
way you can hear a boy in a schoolroom saying the parts of a seal.

"And if I decline?"

"You can't decline."

"That is not what I asked."

---

The one behind him — the fourth one, the one whose name he never got —
said, "Oh, for — " and started forward.

And Miyo said, "*Wait*," and he stopped.

He stopped. That was the thing. A boy of thirteen with about eight weeks
in the corps started to move on a ten-year-old on open stone and a girl
said *wait* and he **stopped**, and Tadayoshi stood in the middle of a
dry river and understood the entire remainder of his life in about a
second and a half.

---

*They will not hurt me.*

*That is not a hope and it is not a guess. Look at it.*

*Four of them and they took both ends and then they came in at a walk to
fifteen yards, which is a distance you choose when you want to be able to
talk. She put a hand up. She said nobody is going to hurt you before I
had said one word, which means she was told to say it, which means it is
on the paper.*

*He started forward and she stopped him and he stopped.*

*It is not that they are kind. Two of them are frightened of me and one
of them is bored and the fourth one wants to be somewhere else. It is
that they have been given an instruction with a word in it, and the word
is doing all the work, and the word is going to go on doing all the work
for as long as they believe it applies.*

*They are here to close a hand on something.*

*And you cannot close a hand and swing it at the same time.*

---

*So.*

*I cannot outrun four. I cannot fight anybody, I have never fought
anybody, I do not know the first thing about it and I am four foot ten.*

*What I have is a tin of the best grit I have ever handled, sixty yards
of open bed with nothing on it, four seals at a second and a quarter, and
four people who are not allowed to break me.*

*That is not nothing.*

*That is a boy who cannot be hit standing on the only material in the
Land of Water and none of them knows what I do.*

*None of them knows what I do.*

*It is not on the tasking. She said non-combatant — she has not said it,
but she has stood at fifteen yards to talk to me and she has come in at a
walk and she has told her own man to wait, and you do not do any of that
to somebody whose file says he can do anything at all.*

*They have been sent to collect a boy.*

---

"All right," said Tadayoshi.

Miyo let a breath out.

"Right. Good. That's — good." She actually smiled, briefly, which he
thought about for a very long time afterwards. "It's four days and you'll
be fed and if your feet go we'll slow down. Have you got anything you're
carrying?"

"Yes."

"Can I see it?"

"Yes," said Tadayoshi, and put his hand inside his coat.
