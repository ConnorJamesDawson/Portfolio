# Chapter 16, Scene 8 — The Second Ford

*Act 2, year 1, month 4, the ninth. The second ford below Hane. Tadayoshi
Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~2,200 words.*

---

The handover was at first light and it took nine minutes and there was
nothing wrong with any of it.

A man on the far side with a lamp and a counterpart. The case off the frame
onto a hand-cart with two of them lifting and Tadayoshi on the ends. The
seals read out loud, both, and agreed. The counterpart signed and dated and
folded and put away.

Three thousand, counted onto the tailboard of the hand-cart in the dark,
which he counted again himself because you always do.

"That's you," the man said.

"That's me."

And it was. At about twenty past five on the ninth of the fourth month
Tadayoshi Ruri had no obligation to anybody in the world, and he walked
back across the ford with an empty frame on a mule and went and stood
behind the willows because a man under a bridge had told him to.

---

**Set the ground, because everything that happens is about the ground.**

The ford is a wide one and shallow, forty feet across, running left to
right as he faced it, with the drove track coming down to it on the far
side and going up on the near.

Sixty-odd yards upstream of the crossing there are willows on the near
bank — nine of them, in a line, on a bank about four feet high and cut back
underneath, so the water runs against the foot of it and there is a lip.

He put the mule fifteen feet back from the lip, in the willows, tied, with
the empty frame still on it because taking a frame off is ten minutes.

He stood four feet from the lip with the water below him on his left and
the ford downstream on his left and the whole of the near bank at his back.

She was behind him and to the right, sitting, with the pack off and beside
her and the hat on.

---

He had an hour and he spent it on the one thing he had not been given.

*He told me a ford and an hour and a position and a window and a signal.*

*He did not tell me what to do.*

He went at that properly, standing still, for about forty minutes, and got
there, and it was the best piece of work he did that day and it arrived too
late to be any use.

*A position is not a task.*

*You put a man in a position because of what is in front of him or because
of what is behind him. In front of me is water and a bank. Behind me is
sixty yards of near bank and the top of the drove track.*

*So I am not looking at anything. I am standing between the water and the
way out.*

He turned it over once.

*And she is going somewhere.*

*She has been given a fetching and I have been given a place to stand, and
the place I am standing is on the near bank sixty yards up, and if she is
crossing anywhere she is crossing at the ford, and the ford is the only
place a person can cross, and the ford is downstream on my left.*

*So I am not in her way and I am not with her.*

*I am on the side she comes back to.*

He stood in the willows with his hand on nothing.

*I have not been given a job. I have been given a corner to stand in while
somebody else does one.*

And there was nowhere to put that, and no argument available, and a man
under a bridge four days away who had said a thing twice.

---

The drover came at about ten past six and he was exactly what he had been
described as.

Forty-odd head, a dog, a man on foot at the back and a boy at the front,
and they came down the far track loose and slow and stopped at the water
the way cattle stop at water, which is all at once and for no reason.

Then they went in.

The noise is the thing and the man under the bridge had been right about it
and had understated it. Forty head in eighteen inches of water is not a
sound with a shape. It is the whole of the air being used up, and the dog
on top of it, and the man shouting at the dog, and forty animals doing the
thing cattle do in water, which is to stop being in a line.

He counted from the moment the first one went in.

*One.*

---

At about eight it went off on the far bank and he did not see the start of
it either.

What he had was that the boy at the front of the drove turned round, and
turned round the wrong way — not at the cattle, past them, up the far track
— and then the dog changed what it was barking at.

Then a horse went down. He heard it.

*That is it.*

*That is forty yards of covered cart and six men on the far track and it
is going off in the noise, which is what the noise is for, and I have been
stood in a corner on the wrong side of a river for an hour and I have not
been told one thing about it.*

And then, behind him and to the right, a girl of eleven stood up, put her
pack down flat, and walked past him down the bank towards the ford.

---

He did not stop her, and he has been over that more than once.

The reasons are three and they are all real and they all arrived inside
about a second and a half. She had been given the job by a man with
standing to give it and had been given it twice. She was already four
paces past him and moving at a walk and stopping her meant a hand on her
and a voice, and both of those are things people look at. And the third
one, which is the one he keeps:

*She is not going to stop.*

He turned back to the water, which is where he had been put.

---

The two came out of the ford twenty yards downstream of him at about the
count of ninety.

**Positions.** They came out of the water on the near side, below him and
to his left, into the cut under the bank. The bank there is four feet and
undercut, so it is not a slope, it is a step with a lip on it, and a man
coming up it does it with a hand.

**The first man** was ahead by about four paces. He came at the bank fast,
put his left hand flat on the top of it, and got his right knee up onto the
lip.

**And he was not looking at Tadayoshi.** He was looking past him and up the
bank, at fifteen feet, at a mule with a frame on it.

*He wants the animal.*

---

Tadayoshi went **down** the bank and not out.

His left foot stayed on the top edge where it was. His right foot went out
and down into the cut, onto the lip, and he put the sole of it flat on the
back of the man's left hand and stepped through it with his weight.

**The hand went.** The wrist folded outward over the lip and the elbow came
after it, and the whole of the man's left side dropped about eight inches
in the time it takes to drop eight inches, and his right knee came off the
lip because the knee had been holding nothing and the hand had been holding
everything.

He went back off the bank the way a man goes off a bank, which is sideways
and with his shoulder first, and there was four feet of it, and he landed
in eighteen inches of water on his right side with his own weight coming
down after him.

He made a noise. He did not get up in the next four seconds and the next
four seconds were the whole of it.

---

**The second man was still in the water and he stopped.**

That is the thing about him and it is why he nearly did it: he did not come
on. He was four paces back and to the right and he saw the first one go off
the bank and he **stopped**, and put the point up.

*Spear.*

*Boar spear. Seven feet, cross-piece behind the head. That is not a
soldier's thing, that is a thing a man brings.*

**And he backed.** Two steps, out of the cut and back into the middle of
the ford, where the water is nine inches instead of eighteen and the bottom
is gravel instead of mud, and he stood there with seven feet of it between
him and the bank and the point at Tadayoshi's chest.

*He is right.*

*He is completely right. He has got firm ground and reach and he is
standing where I have to come to him, and if he holds that he wins, and he
is going to hold it, and he knows he is going to hold it.*

---

Tadayoshi went into the water.

Not at him. He came off the lip and down into the cut and went out into
the ford at about forty degrees to the man's left, at a walk, with his left
hand down.

**The man tracked him.** He had to. A point that is not on you is not a
point, so as Tadayoshi went left the man turned with him, and turning with
somebody in water means moving the back foot, and the man's back foot was
his right.

He put it down about nine inches from where it had been.

**And it came down on something that had not been in the river four seconds
earlier.**

His right foot went out from under him to the outside. Not far — six
inches, maybe eight. The ankle rolled and the knee followed it and his hips
came round after the knee, and **the point came down about a foot and went
off line to Tadayoshi's right by about the same.**

That was the whole of the window and it was about half a second.

---

Tadayoshi took the shaft with his left hand, about two feet behind the
head, and pulled it past himself and kept walking up it.

**The man did not let go**, which was the correct thing to do with a spear
and the wrong thing to do with that one, because letting go was the only
thing that would have put distance back in it. He held on and came forward
with it and that closed the last four feet himself.

And Tadayoshi's right hand came up under the jaw.

*Heel of the hand, under and back —*

---

He did not do that.

He knows what he was going to do because he was in the middle of doing it,
and he knows what happened because the man was on his back in the water
with the spear still in both hands and there was a great deal of blood
going downstream in the shape the current made of it.

And there were about two seconds between those, and he has never had them.

He stood in nine inches of water with the sword in his right hand.

*I did not have that.*

He turned it over, which took about a second, and he was completely calm
about it and there was nothing in his chest doing anything.

*I had my hand open. I was going to put the heel of it under his jaw. I
have done that eleven times and I know exactly what it feels like from the
inside and I was doing it.*

*And it is in my right hand and it has been out for some part of the last
four seconds and I do not know which part.*

He looked at the man in the water.

*And that is not the first time.*

*The riverbed was the first time and I called it not knowing and I did not
put a number on it because there was nobody left to ask.*

He put it in the place where things he had no account of went, which had
had four items in it for a year, and it was five now, and he did not
enlarge on it because a man was getting up out of the water twenty yards
downstream and had to be dealt with.

---

---

He got the first one out of the water because a man face down in eighteen
inches of it drowns, and he did not want a body he had not decided on.

He got him up onto the shingle on his front with his head turned, and the
man's left wrist was the wrong shape and his shoulder had gone and he was
not going to do anything to anybody for a long time.

Then he stood on the shingle in the middle of a ford with cattle going past
forty yards downstream and looked at what he had.

*One down and not dead. One dead and I did not decide it.*

*And a boy of eleven standing in a river with three thousand ryō on him and
no obligation to anybody.*

He went and got the mule.

And the last thing he did before he went down the bank after her was to
look, once, at the water where the second man was — at the gravel round
him, at about the place where a right foot had gone out from under
somebody — and he did not go over and he did not put his hand in it and he
did not look for very long.

*There is something in that riverbed.*

*I put it there.*

He turned round and went to find her.
