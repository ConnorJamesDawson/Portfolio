# Chapter 17, Scene 7 — Outside

*Act 2, year 1, month 4, the twenty-third and twenty-fourth. The Kadono
road, and a rope walk. Nozaki Uzuki, age eleven. POV: Uzuki. ~1,900
words.*

---

She got it on the road on the twenty-third and it took her about four miles
and she had had the pieces of it for seven days.

*All three of us.*

*Somebody has asked for all three of us by name, and I have been thinking
about that since yesterday afternoon like it is a good thing.*

She walked.

*It is not a good thing. It is not a bad thing either. It is a room with
people in it and one of us cannot be in a room with people in it.*

---

**And then the finding, and it arrived whole, and she stopped walking.**

*He does not have a no.*

*I have known that since the sixteenth. I said it to myself behind a bank
and then I said it was not a finding because there was nothing to do about
it.*

She stood in the road with the mule going past her.

*There is now.*

---

She gave it to him at the top of a rise about two hours later and she gave
it the way she had learned to give him things in the third month, which was
the hole first.

"I've got something and there's a hole in it and the hole is that I got it
by doing something I shouldn't have."

He said, "All right."

"On the sixteenth I asked him four questions and I made each one worse on
purpose to see where he'd stop. He didn't stop. There isn't a place where
he stops."

He walked about three paces.

"Go on."

"He'll answer anything. Not to me — to anyone. Somebody at a door being
nice to him. He'd tell them what he is, what his people did, and how long
he's got, and he'd give them the working, and he wouldn't know he'd done
anything and he'd be right that he hadn't, because they asked."

---

He stopped in the road.

She had prepared four things for what she thought would come next and she
did not need any of them, because what he did was stand still for about six
seconds and then say:

"That's a real thing."

"It's a real thing."

"Then he's not in the room."

---

**It took under a minute in total and she has been over it a great many
times.**

*He did not argue.*

*He did not ask how I knew, which would have been fair, and he did not ask
what the four questions were, which would have been fairer, and I had an
answer ready for both and I have never once had to use it.*

She walked beside him with the hat on.

*He heard **that's a real thing** and then he acted on it and there was
nothing in between.*

*Which is what I do with him.*

**And that is the first time in a hundred and thirty-two days that
information has gone the other way and been taken the same way, and neither
of them said anything about it, and she noticed and he did not.**

---

They did the rest of the plan on the twenty-third night and it took eleven
minutes and it was good and it was thorough and it was correct.

> *He waits outside.*
>
> *He does not go in.*
>
> *If anybody asks him to come in he says he was told to wait, which is
> true, and it is the only sentence in the world he can say that stops a
> conversation, because it is a fact and he has it.*

And then Tadayoshi, who prices everything, did the thing he does, which was
to look for the hole in his own arrangement.

"What if somebody comes out to him."

"Then he's on his own with them."

"Yes."

She thought about it.

"He can't not answer," she said. "That's the whole thing. So it isn't about
teaching him a sentence, it's about there not being anybody there."

"Right."

"So he waits somewhere there isn't anybody."

"Right."

---

**And that is the arrangement, and both of them were satisfied with it, and
it is watertight, and it failed for a reason neither of them had any way of
knowing.**

*She had never been in that building.*

*He had never been in that building.*

*Neither of them had ever in their lives been inside a building where a
room has a corridor outside it and the corridor has people in it who are
waiting to go into the same room.*

**She had spent eleven years in a village, a monastery, a stable and a road,
and in every one of those, outside is a yard.**

---

They came into Kadono at about seven on the twenty-fourth and she did the
building the way she does buildings, which is fast and complete and mostly
right.

*Rope walk on the north side. Long, low, three doors on the town side and
one at the end.*

*The yard is behind it and the yard has got a gate and a wall and one way
in from the lane, and there are eleven people in it and four of them are
waiting for something.*

*And the building behind the yard is not the rope walk. It is a second
building and it is stone at the bottom and it is where they are going.*

She got the whole of that in about ninety seconds and it was accurate.

What she did not get, because she could not see it and had never had cause
to imagine it, was the inside of the second building, which is one room at
the far end and one passage running to it, with a bench down one side of
the passage, because that is what you put in a place where people wait.

---

She put him in the yard.

She picked the spot herself and she picked it well: the north corner, by
the gate, against the wall, where a person is visible and not in anybody's
way and nobody has to go past him to get anywhere.

"Stay here," she said.

"Yes."

"If anybody wants you inside, you were told to wait."

"Yes."

She looked at him for a second and then said the last part, which was not
in the plan and which she added because she was eleven and could not help
it:

"And don't answer anything."

And he said, "Yes."

**And he meant it, and he would have kept it, and it would have held for
the whole of that morning, if the instruction had been given by somebody
who was there when the question was asked, or if the man who came out into
that yard at about twenty past nine had been anything other than
completely kind.**

---

She went in at nine with Tadayoshi.

The last thing she did before the door was look back across the yard, and
he was where she had put him, against the wall by the gate, with his hands
in front of him, not doing anything, which is what he does.

*He is fine.*

*Nobody is going to bother a boy standing against a wall.*

She went in.

---

**And here is the count, because she made it afterwards and made it for
years.**

*Eleven minutes from putting him against that wall to going through that
door.*

*Four things I said to him and every one of them was right.*

*And the one I did not say is: **if a person comes out to you, walk into
the lane**, and I did not say it because there was no lane in the plan,
because the plan had a yard in it and a room in it and I had drawn the
whole thing in my head and there is not one building in the world that is
only a yard and a room.*

She has been asked, twice, by people who were not being unkind, whether she
blames herself for the twenty-fourth.

Both times she said no, and both times it was true, and both times the
person went away satisfied and had not asked the right question.

**What she has never said to anybody is that she does not blame herself and
that she also cannot walk past a bench in a corridor, and that she has
known those are the same fact since she was about fifteen, and that she has
never been able to get them to sit next to each other in a way that
resolves.**
