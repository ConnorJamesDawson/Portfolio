# Chapter 2, Scene 7 — The Thing Nobody Says

*Act 1, month 3. Nakadō, the back room. Nozaki Uzuki, age ten. POV:
Uzuki. ~1,720 words.*

---

Her father waited eleven days.

She had thought at first that he was not going to do anything, and had
spent about four of the eleven being quietly and comprehensively furious
with him, which was a thing she had never once been before and did not
enjoy. Then on the twelfth evening he said, from the back room, in an
ordinary voice, "Sae. Come and sit down a minute."

And then: "Uzuki. You as well."

---

She came in and stood by the end of the bed, because there were only two
chairs and her mother had one and the other had the basket on it.

"Sit down," said her father.

"I'm all right."

"Sit on the floor then, I'm not talking up at somebody."

She sat on the floor.

Her mother sat with her hands in her lap and her back straight and did
not say anything at all, and Uzuki understood, with a small cold drop
somewhere under her ribs, that her mother had known this was coming for
eleven days as well.

---

"Right," said her father. "Kayano."

"Kiyoshi — "

"I know. I know all of it. I've had eleven days of hearing you not say it
in about nine different ways, and I'd like you to say it properly first,
because you've got the good arguments and I'm not going to sit here and
beat the bad version of you."

Her mother looked at him.

"All right," she said.

And she said it properly.

---

It took her about two minutes and it was, Uzuki thought afterwards, the
worst two minutes of the whole month, because every single thing in it
was true.

She was ten. The north road out of Ōgi went through eleven miles of
nothing with a wood in the middle of it and there were people on that
road who were not carters. She had never slept anywhere that was not
this house. She would be four days away, and a person four days away
cannot be sent for; if something happened at either end there was no
arrangement in the world that got word between them in under four days,
and four days was long enough for everything.

And there was the last one.

"And when she gets there," said her mother, "she is going to walk up to a
strange door in a town where she does not know one person and ask a
stranger to give her something for nothing. And she is very good, and
she's got it all worked out, and she is *ten*, and the answer is going to
be no, and she is going to be standing in the street in Kayano four days
from home having been told no."

She stopped.

"That's mine," she said. "That's all of it. I've not got a better one."

---

Her father was quiet for a bit.

"Every one of those is true," he said.

"I know it is."

"The road one's the true one. The rest of them you'd get over and the
road one you wouldn't." He shifted himself up the pillow. "And I want
her to go."

"Kiyoshi."

"I want her to go," he said, "and I'm going to tell you why, and I'm
going to do it in front of her, because if I do it after she's in bed
then she gets told the outcome and not the reason, and I've been on the
wrong end of that in my own family and I'm not doing it to mine."

Uzuki, on the floor, went absolutely still.

---

"I'm not getting better," said her father.

The room did not change. That was the thing she could not get over
afterwards — there was no noise and nothing fell and the fire went on
doing what it was doing.

"Don't," said her mother.

"Sae — "

"Don't do that in front of her."

"I'm going to do it in front of her because she's been in this house for
five months watching the pair of us say *winter*," said her father, "and
she's not stupid, and every day we say *winter* she has to do a little
bit of extra work in her own head to keep believing us, and I've watched
her do it, and I'd rather she stopped."

He looked at Uzuki.

"I'm not getting better," he said. "It's not a cold and it hasn't been a
cold since about the harvest before last. Tazu doesn't know what it is
and I don't think a medic at Kayano is going to know either and I'd like
that said out loud once in this house so that nobody has to work at it
any more."

Uzuki heard herself say, "All right."

"All right," said her father.

Her mother put one hand over her mouth and took it away again.

---

"Now here's the part I actually want to argue," he said, "and it's not
about the road, so you'll have to let me get to the end of it.

"Everything she said is right and it's all about the going. And I'd take
the other side of every one of them and lose. So I'm not going to."

"Then — "

"I'm going to talk about the coming back."

He put his hand flat on the blanket.

"It's going to get worse and there's going to be a stretch of it. Might
be a long stretch. And in that stretch there is going to be a great deal
of sitting in this room with me at three in the morning, and it is going
to fall on the pair of you, and there is nothing on this earth I can do
about that except be sorry about it, which is worth nothing.

"And I would rather — " and here his voice went, for the first time, and
he took it back — "I would rather she came into that room with a pair of
hands that could do something.

"That's it. That's the whole of my side. Not because it'll fix me. It
won't. Because there is a difference between a child who sat in the dark
for a year being no use, and a child who sat in the dark for a year and
was *doing something*, and that difference doesn't finish when I do. She
carries that one for sixty years."

---

Her mother said, "And if it goes bad while she's gone."

"Then it goes bad while she's gone."

"Kiyoshi — "

"Then it goes bad while she's gone," he said, "and she'll have been doing
something, and that's the best answer I've got and I know exactly how bad
it is."

Uzuki sat on the floor by the end of the bed and looked at the boards.

"There isn't going to be a Kayano," said her mother.

"What?"

"There isn't going to be a Kayano," she said again, and stopped, and
started something else. "It doesn't — the money doesn't — "

"She's not asking for money. That's the whole of her plan, that's the
clever bit, she's not — "

"I know what her plan is." Her mother's voice came out sharp and then
came back down. "I heard her plan. Her plan is better than anything I've
had."

*She's saying it again,* thought Uzuki. *She said that in the kitchen.
She said it's a good plan and then said no in four seconds.*

*And she's just started a sentence about money twice and stopped it
twice.*

---

"Sae," said her father, gently. "What is it."

Her mother sat with her hands in her lap.

"Nothing," she said. "It's nothing. It's the road."

And Uzuki, who was ten, and who had spent eleven days being furious with
the wrong parent, took that entirely at face value.

---

It went on a bit after that but the shape of it was over.

Her mother said seven days and her father said nine and they settled on
nine. Her mother said she would not go past Kayano for anything and
Uzuki said she would not go past Kayano for anything. Her mother said if
the road at the wood was wet she was to wait for a cart and go with it
and Uzuki said yes, and meant it, and was told she did not mean it, and
said yes again.

Her father said, "And you take the mill's two parcels and you get the
sixty up front, and you ask for it up front in front of somebody else so
he can't go back on it."

"That's what I was going to do."

"I know it is," he said. "I'm saying it so your mother can hear that
you'd thought of it."

Her mother made a noise that was not quite anything.

"Nine days," she said. "And if you're eleven I'm coming to get you."

"You wouldn't get four miles," said her father.

"I'd get to Ōgi," said her mother, "and I'd stand in the road at Ōgi and
shout, and it would be extremely undignified and I would do it, so don't
be eleven days."

---

Uzuki went out into the passage afterwards with her ears ringing.

She stood in the dark bit by the rack for a moment, the way she had stood
there twelve days before, and then she reached up and took her bokken off
the bottom peg, because she was going to be on a road for nine days and
there were going to be evenings, and she had a form with fourteen counts
in it and the eighth one was still not reliable.

She stood there with it in her hand.

The rack had two pegs.

Hers was empty now and her father's was not, because his was still up
there where it had been every evening of her life, on the top, put back
by him, and it had not come down since the afternoon on the flat bit by
the barrow when she had got the eighth count on the eighteenth try and he
had sat down and had not got up.

She looked at that for a while in the dark.

Then she took hers upstairs, and did not say anything to anybody about
it, and put it under the bed where she could reach it.
