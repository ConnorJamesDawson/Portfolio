# Chapter 13, Scene 7 — The Entry She Could Not Close

*Act 2, year 1, month 1, the seventeenth to the twenty-first. Hazaki.
Nozaki Uzuki, age eleven. POV: Uzuki. ~2,050 words.*

---

It was good.

She was clear about that at the time and she was clear about it
afterwards and she never once, at any point in her life, told the story
in a way that made it less good than it was.

The bed was a real bed with a frame and it was in a room with the girl of
thirteen, whose name was Nao and who talked for about forty minutes on
the first night and then went to sleep in the middle of a sentence about a
cat. There was food twice and it was on a plate that was the same plate
everybody else had. The work was hemp and it was hard on the hands and she
had the first two blisters of her life that were not from a bucket.

And Sen explained everything once and expected you to have it, and Uzuki
had it, and Sen did not say anything about that and did not need to.

*She has not thanked me for anything and she has not been pleased with me
in front of anybody and I have been here four days.*

*And she gave me the far end of the walk on the second morning, which is
the end you give somebody when you have stopped watching them.*

---

She read Sen in about a day and a half, and reading her was easy, and that
should have been the end of it.

*She wants hands.*

*She said it in the first minute and she has done nothing since that
contradicts it. She is not kind and she is not cruel and if I am no good
at this in a month she will say so and it will not be a betrayal, it will
be a fact about rope.*

*Her boy sits down for twenty minutes and nobody goes over. Her girl gets
things wrong and she fixes them without saying. She pays on the day; three
people in that town said so; and the last one who worked here cried when
she went home, and the woman who told me that was not asked.*

She sat on the end of the walk on the third evening with her hands stinging
and did the whole thing out.

*I am worth a pair of hands to her, and that is what she is getting, and
she has told me so, and there is nothing underneath it.*

*This is the best place I have ever been and it is the only one where I
knew the number on the first day.*

---

The trouble was not Sen.

She found that out on the fourth morning and she found it out in a yard,
standing still, halfway through carrying something, with both arms full.

She had been going fast.

Not fast for the work — fast on purpose. She had done the water without
being asked on the second morning and had found a better way to do the
hanks on the third and had told Nao about it because she could not not
tell people things, and on the fourth morning she had got up before the
others and started, and she was carrying an armful across a yard at speed
at about eight o'clock and she stopped dead in the middle of it.

*I am doing it again.*

She stood there with her arms full.

*Eight trips instead of eleven. Four ranks under the eave. Six hours of
floors. Fifteen days, fast, on purpose, so that they would want to keep
me.*

*And it did not come off the number. It was not in the number. The number
was the blood and the other one and none of it was anything I did.*

She put the armful down on the wall and sat on it.

---

She went at it properly for the rest of the morning, because it was not
the same and she could see it was not the same and she needed to know
where the difference was.

*At the Kadono-dera I was working fast because I was frightened, and I was
frightened because there was a number on me somewhere I could not see.*

*Here there is a number on me and I can see it and it is a pair of hands.*

*So the fear should not be here.*

She turned it over.

*And it is not here. That is the thing. I am not frightened. I have not
been frightened once in four days.*

*So why am I going fast.*

---

She got it in the middle of the afternoon and it took her the rest of the
day to believe it, and when she believed it she did not like it at all.

*Because I am still doing the sums about somebody.*

*And it is not her.*

---

She made the list that night, in the dark, in a bed with a frame, with Nao
asleep four feet away, and she made it in the order she always made lists,
which was oldest first.

*My mother's house after she married him. — I was a thing that made the
house cheaper to run and then a thing that made it harder, and the number
changed in the seventh month and I felt it change and I could not say
when.*

*Shimoda's yard. — I was the person holding the halter and the mule was
what was being priced and I found out afterwards that I had been in the
speech the whole time.*

*The almshouse behind the shrine at Ōgi. — I was a number of days. Nine
of them, and then nothing until the tenth month of next year.*

*The Kadono-dera. — I was for the blood and then a second thing, and both
of them are written down somewhere in a book by a man with ink on his
finger, and the second one had a number and I heard him say it.*

*Hazaki. — I am a pair of hands and it is six thousand for the year and I
worked it out off what Nao said about the last one.*

She stopped, because that was five and there was a sixth.

---

*And him.*

She lay on her back in the dark and went at the sixth one and got nowhere,
and went at it again, and got nowhere again, and did that for about two
hours, which was the longest she had ever failed at anything.

*Thirty-six days.*

*He killed six people in a room and did not look at me twice, and he took
me because refusing cost more than allowing and he said so with his face
and I could read it and that was a number and it was fine.*

*And then it stopped.*

*He bought me four things and did not say what they cost. He bought me a
hat and did not ask what it was for. He paid two thousand ryō over the
asking price for this and I know he did because Nao's mother said the
figure out loud at supper and it was not the figure she asked for.*

*And I have gone over the whole of thirty-six days twice and I cannot find
what I am for.*

She put her hands flat on the blanket.

*Everybody has a number. That is not a fear, it is a finding, and I have
had it since the sixth month and it has never once been wrong.*

*And there is a person walking about with mine and I cannot read it, and
he is four days from here going the other way, and in a year I will not be
able to find him.*

---

She got up on the fifth morning.

She did not decide the night before. She woke at the ordinary time, which
was before the others, and lay there for about a minute, and then got up
and put her boots on and rolled the blanket and put it in the pack, and
the whole of it took four minutes because everything she owned was hers
and fitted and had been bought new in an afternoon eighteen days earlier.

She stood in the room with the pack on and the hat in her hand and looked
at Nao asleep with one arm off the side of the bed.

*She was decent to me.*

*And I am going to be a thing that happened to her, and in about a month
she will say to somebody that a girl came and went and she does not know
why, and that is what I am going to be, and I am not going to be able to
do anything about it.*

She waited to feel worse about that than she did.

She did not, and she noticed that she did not, and she made herself put it
down properly rather than walk away from it, standing in a doorway with a
pack on.

*I am sorry about her and it is small.*

*And I am not sorry about the woman, and the woman has eight thousand ryō
of somebody else's money and a year in which to not have a girl in it, and
that is the fairest arrangement anybody has ever made about me and I am
walking out of it in the fifth day of it.*

*And I have not got a reason I could say out loud to her face.*

---

She went out the front, at the ordinary time, past the walk, and Sen was
already at the far end of it with her back to the yard, and Uzuki stopped
for about three seconds at the gate.

*If she turns round I will tell her.*

*I have got nothing to tell her and I will tell her anyway, because she
asked me four questions in the right order and she deserves the whole of
it.*

Sen did not turn round.

Uzuki went out of the gate and down the lane and did not look back at the
house, and she was on the Ōtoma road inside twenty minutes with the hat on
and four days of walking in front of her and no plan whatsoever for what
she was going to say at the end of it.

---

She worked at that for most of the first day and got about as far as she
had got with everything else.

*He is going to ask why.*

She turned that over for eleven miles and then, somewhere on the second
afternoon, she stopped and stood in the road for a moment, because it had
just come apart in her hands.

*No he isn't.*

She stood there.

*He has not asked me one question in thirty-six days that was not
operational. Not why I sat by the door. Not why I want a hat. Not why I
did not say anything on the mountain. Not once.*

*So he is not going to ask me why I have come back, and I am walking to
Ōtoma with four days of an answer that nobody is going to want.*

She started walking again.

And what she thought then was the coldest thing she had thought about
anybody, and it was completely accurate, and she was eleven.

*Which means I am not going back to tell him anything.*

*I am going back to watch him for longer.*

*Because I have got an entry I cannot close, and I have never once closed
one from a distance, and I did the bowl in four days off a chip in the
rim, and the only way I have ever found out what a thing is worth is to be
where it is and count.*
