# Chapter 13, Scene 10 — The Kanō Ford

*Act 2, year 1, month 1, the twenty-sixth to the thirtieth. The Kanō ford
and west. Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,750 words.*

---

He saw him at four hundred yards, coming down through the trees on the
east side, and he stopped walking before he had decided to.

*Somebody at the crossing.*

*And he is not crossing.*

He put his hand out backwards without looking round and she stopped, and
he registered afterwards that she had stopped instantly and without a
sound, and did not have time for it.

---

The Kanō is not much of a river in the first month and the ford is about
thirty yards of shingle and slack water with a bar down the middle of it,
and there is a road on each side and nothing else for two miles either
way.

There was a man standing in it.

Not at it. In it. About eight yards out from the near bank, on the
upstream side of the bar, with the water at about his knees, doing nothing
at all.

Tadayoshi went down on one knee behind a stump and looked at him for
eleven minutes.

---

*He is not fishing. There is nothing in his hands and nothing on the bank.*

*He is not washing anything.*

*He is standing where you stand.*

That was the first thing and it arrived whole and he did not have to build
it.

*Eight yards out puts him where he can see both roads and the whole of the
bar, and it puts twenty yards of open water between him and anybody coming
down either side, and there is nothing behind him but the far bank and
that is a slope he could take at a run.*

*If I wanted to hold this ford on my own that is where I would be
standing, and it took me four seconds to find it and he was already
in it.*

He went on looking, because the first reading is the cheap one.

*He has not moved his head.*

*I have been here eleven minutes and he has looked left once and right
once and both of those were at the start and neither of them was for me.*

---

And then the other thing, and he was never able to say afterwards how he
got it, and he tried.

*There is something wrong about the right one.*

He put everything he had on it for about two minutes and could not close
it.

*It does not match. That is all I have got. The left one is doing
something and the right one is doing something and they are not the same
something, and at four hundred yards I have got no business seeing that at
all and I am seeing it.*

*And I cannot say what it is.*

He wrote that down afterwards exactly as it stood, and it stayed in his
sheets in that form for six years, and the first person who could have
told him what it meant was four hundred miles away and eleven years old
and had not been born when it happened.

---

He went round.

It cost him three hours and about five miles and he had to do the last of
it in daylight because the detour came out on an open shoulder with no
cover on it, and daylight was the part he minded.

He did not consider crossing. That should be said plainly, because it was
not a near thing and there was no debate in it: he had a girl of eleven
with him, and a man standing in a river in the first month is a man who
does not feel the first month, and there is nothing at the end of that
sum.

*He has not come for me.*

*He is standing in a ford on a road that goes to four places and he is
looking at all of them, and if he had come for me he would be somewhere
else, because a man who has come for you does not stand in the one place
on this road that everybody can see.*

*So he is looking for anybody.*

*And that is worse, because a man looking for one person can be avoided
once.*

---

She said it at about four in the afternoon, on the shoulder, in the open,
walking.

"He wasn't cold."

Tadayoshi stopped.

"What?"

"He wasn't cold." She was looking at the road and not at him and her voice
was doing the thing it did, which was to go flat when the content went up.
"He'd been in it a while. His trousers were wet above the water line, so
he'd been in deeper at some point, and it had dried on him at the top and
not at the bottom. That's an hour. And he was stood straight."

"So?"

"So I've been in a river in the first month," she said. "Twice. You don't
stand straight. You stand like this."

She did it, in the road, for about a second and a half — shoulders up,
elbows in, chin down — and then stopped doing it, and it was so exactly
the shape of a person in cold water that he had a moment of not being able
to get the picture of the man back.

"He was stood like a man in a doorway," she said. "For an hour. In the
first month."

---

He walked about two hundred yards without saying anything and then said,
"How long have you had that?"

"Since about the second minute."

"Why didn't you say?"

"You were working."

He turned that over and could not do anything with it, and put it down,
and it stayed down for a fortnight and then came back up in a different
shape entirely and cost him a great deal.

---

He did the sheets that night in a stand of trees with no fire, because the
shoulder had been open and he did not want one.

He unfolded them on his knee. There were eleven leaves of the harbour
office's spoiled paper, ruled in five columns in the sixth month of the
year before, and there were two lines on them and a rule under the second
one with *Nothing. Checked.* in the middle of the white.

He wrote the third.

> *26/1. The Kanō ford. About noon.*
>
> *Who: —*
>
> *What they tried: nothing. Stood 8 yds out on the upstream side of the
> bar for at least an hour before I saw him and 11 min after. Did not move
> his head. Did not cross.*
>
> *How long: 11 min observed, 1 hr+ actual.*
>
> *What it cost: 3 hrs and 5 miles, and the last two of them in daylight
> on an open shoulder.*

Then he sat with it and wrote two more lines under it because they were
the whole of it and there was nowhere else for them to go.

> *Something wrong about the right one. Cannot say what.*
>
> *And he was not cold. That is hers and I did not have it.*

---

He looked at the first column for a while, which had a dash in it, the way
the first line had a dash in it.

*Three lines and two of them have got nothing in the name column.*

*And the one that has got something in it has not got a name in it either,
it has got left-handed and about twenty-eight to thirty, and I have been
carrying that for four months and calling it a person.*

*So I have got a file with three entries and no names.*

He put it away.

---

There is one more thing about the twenty-sixth of the first month and he
did not think it that night or that year.

**He had been seen.**

Not by the man in the ford — the man in the ford never turned his head and
never crossed and never, as far as anybody was ever able to establish,
made any report about anything. But four hundred yards is four hundred
yards in both directions, and the road on the east side of the Kanō was
not empty at noon on the twenty-sixth, and a boy of eleven going round a
ford in daylight with a girl in a wide hat behind him is not a thing you
forget.

He had written the rule himself, in the eighth month, in the operational
column, after an afternoon with Hōki in a yard.

> *A boy walking is nobody. Walk.*

It had been true for nine months and he had built four hundred miles on
it, and it had stopped being true at about twenty past eleven in the
morning on the seventeenth of the twelfth month, when a girl came out of a
hall behind him with nothing on her and got on a road.

**Two children on a road are not nobody. Two children on a road are two
children on a road**, and there is exactly one of that in this country,
and he was walking about in it in daylight with eighteen thousand ryō and
a file with three entries and no names in it, and he had four days left in
the first month and nothing whatsoever on the boards.
