# Chapter 12, Scene 1 — Eighteen Days

*Act 1, month 11 the twenty-seventh to month 12 the seventh. Ōtoma.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,750 words.*

---

He took it down to the yard on the twenty-seventh and found Ōtsuka doing
something to a hoop, and read the whole docket out loud to him, which he
had never done with anything.

> *9 crates. Kadono-dera. Return required.*
>
> *9 days.*

"Nine days," said Tadayoshi.

"That's what it says."

"It's nine days there. It doesn't say anything about coming back and it
says return required."

Ōtsuka put the hoop down, took the docket, licked the pencil, crossed out
the nine and wrote eighteen, and gave it back.

The whole of it took about six seconds and he did not look at the paper
while he did it.

---

Tadayoshi went up the ladder with that and sat with it for a while,
because he had got something and could not work out what.

*He did not check it.*

*He did not check it against anything and he did not ask me how I got
eighteen and he did not think about it for a second and a half, and I have
watched that man argue for four minutes about a sixteenth.*

*A number nobody checks is a number nobody is going to read.*

He turned it over.

*Which means either the number does not matter because the job does not
matter, or the number does not matter because somebody has already got
what they wanted out of the docket existing.*

He could not choose between them and he wrote both down, and then, because
it was the honest thing, he wrote the third one underneath.

*Or he is busy and it is a Tuesday.*

---

He did the sheets on the first of the twelfth month, because that was the
instruction and the instruction had a date on it and he had already
failed it once.

He unfolded them on the boards and read the second line, which was the
left-hander, 17/9, and then the white under it.

And there was nothing to add.

He sat with that for longer than the nothing warranted.

*Nothing at the ford since the twenty-first. Nothing at Shioya. Nothing at
Mikura, and Mikura was five days in a town.*

*So the whole of the eleventh month is empty and it is empty because it
was empty.*

He got the pencil out and ruled a line under the left-hander and dated it
the first of the twelfth month and wrote three words in the middle of the
white, and it was the first thing he had put on those sheets in
seventy-six days.

> *Nothing. Checked.*

---

He looked at it for a bit.

*And now I know.*

*I did not write the ford one because I did not write it. It was not that
there was nothing to write in the eleventh month and I have just found
that out by writing the nothing, which means the instrument works, which
means the twenty-first of the tenth is the only thing on these sheets that
is missing.*

He did not put that down. There was no column for it and he had four
columns.

He folded the sheets and put them back under the throwables.

---

The money took him three days and he did not solve it either.

He had six thousand in the tin and four thousand inside the frame and
about eighteen thousand against his ribs, which had gone up by the Shioya
and Mikura fees and by nothing going out except four hundred and eighty
for boots, and he was about to be nineteen days away from every one of
those places.

*The tin is under a board in a loft that four people can get into.*

*The frame is going with me, because the frame always goes with me, so the
four thousand is fine and I have made an object I need in a fight ring
wrong to keep it.*

*And eighteen thousand against my ribs for nineteen days on a mountain is
eighteen thousand that is on me when somebody takes the pack.*

He went round it for three days and there was no answer, because there is
no answer: there is nowhere in the world for a boy of ten with no house
and no name to put eighteen thousand ryō, and a bank is a person who
writes your name down, and the whole of his life was not being written
down.

In the end he split it four ways in four places and hated every one of
them, and the last thing he did on the sixth was walk past the cooperage
in the dark and look at the boards where the tin was and make himself
leave without checking it, because checking it in front of somebody was
worse than losing it.

---

He did the provisioning properly and it was the only part of the week he
enjoyed.

**The boots**, which had done four hundred miles since the twenty-eighth
of the tenth month and were exactly as good as the man with the bench had
said and about half again as good as the pair they replaced, and which he
had greased twice and would grease again at Ōgi.

**The old boots**, which he took, because a wet boot in the twelfth month
is a foot you lose and two pairs is the whole of the answer, and which
went in the bottom of the pack and weighed four pounds he did not want.

**Food for nine days and not eleven**, because there is food on a road
until about the fourth day out of Ōgi and then there is not, and he had
walked the first two thirds of that road in the sixth month and knew
exactly where it stopped.

**Fire.** *A twist of oiled rag, and the flint, and a bundle of split
kindling from the cooperage that was going on the fire anyway.* **Nine
nights above four thousand feet in the twelfth month is nine fires or it
is not nine nights**, and he costed the weight of the kindling against
the chance of finding wood dry above the pass and took the kindling, and
was right, twice.

**And the coat**, which was the one he had had since the sixth month and
which he had priced three replacements for on the twenty-ninth of the
tenth and had not bought, and which he now took up the highest road in the
country in the twelfth month.

He noticed that on the second evening out and put it in the ordinary
column with the date and no comment.

> *Coat. Own fault.*

---

Nobody said it was a punishment.

That is the thing about it and he had it in his hands the whole week and
could not do anything with it. Ōtsuka was exactly as he always was, which
was correct and not friendly and not unfriendly. Hōki had gone. Nobody in
that yard looked at him differently, and two of them asked him what he had
got and he told them and they said *cold* and went on with what they were
doing.

*If it were a punishment somebody would be pleased about it.*

*And nobody is. It is a docket and it is on the boards and it has got a
number on it that nobody read.*

He got to the bottom of that one on the fifth, sitting on the boards with
his pack half-loaded, and it was worse than the other answer.

*It is not that they have decided to be hard on me.*

*It is that this is what there is, and putting me on it did not cost
anybody a thought, and Hōki said the true thing in the back room and I
heard the wrong half of it. He did not say I was being punished. He said I
had got expensive.*

*An expensive thing is not a thing you are angry with. It is a thing you
put down carefully somewhere it cannot break anything.*

---

He left on the seventh at about four in the morning, in the dark, which
was not necessary and which he did anyway.

The pack was fifty-one pounds and he weighed it, because he weighed
everything, and fifty-one pounds is a comfortable load for a grown man
and it was about half what he weighed, and he had nine days of it in front
of him and the last three of them going up.

He went out of Ōtoma on the east road and had the light by seven and was
past Sakabe by the middle of the afternoon and did not stop in it.

And at some point on the second day, on a road he had walked in the sixth
month, going the other way, with a parcel and no fee and eleven towns he
had never seen, he had a thought about the eighteen days that he did not
like and did not put down anywhere.

*Eighteen days is the front half of a month.*

*And when I come back it will be the first month, and it will be a new
year, and I will be eleven.*

He put the strap higher on his shoulder and went on.
