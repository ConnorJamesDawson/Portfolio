# Chapter 12, Scene 10 — Six

*Act 1, month 12, the seventeenth. The hall of the Kadono-dera. Tadayoshi
Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,900 words.*

---

He counted them.

That was the first thing and it was not a decision, and it is the reason
there is any account of the seventeenth of the twelfth month at all: a boy
came back into himself standing in the middle of a room and the machine
that came back first was the one that counts.

*Six.*

He did it again, going round the room clockwise from the table, because
doing it once is not doing it.

*Six.*

---

The second thing was the time and he got that off the light on the floor,
which had moved about the width of a board and a half from where it had
been when the door opened.

*So it is not much past eleven.*

*And the door was half past ten.*

He did not have the four minutes. He had a number at the front of them and
a number at the back of them and nothing whatever in between, and he
worked out standing there that the gap was somewhere between three minutes
and eight, and that he could not close it, and that he was not going to be
able to close it later either.

---

He went at the reconstruction immediately, because that was the drill and
he had built the drill in the fifth month on a riverbed and had run it
four times since, and it had always given him something.

It gave him almost nothing.

*I have got the door.*

*I have got the shape of the room in the first half-second, and it is
accurate, and I can still see it: five men, the table on the left, the
shuttered window, two by the east door, one at the table, two in the
middle.*

*I have got the start of a sentence and I have got the end of a sentence
and I have not got the middle of it.*

*And then I have got the floor.*

He stood with that.

*That is not a gap in what I remember. That is the whole of it. There is
nothing on either side of the floor.*

---

He did the other half of the drill, which was the bodies, because the
bodies are evidence and evidence does not care whether you were there.

It took him about six minutes and he did it properly and he learned four
things from it and did not like any of them.

**The first one on the floor had gone at the back of the neck** and it was
the same thing he had done to a man in a doorway on the twentieth of the
tenth month above a weir, and he recognised the work, and there is no
other way to put that.

**The second had gone the same way and about a second and a half later.**
*Both of them were in the middle of the floor and neither of them had got
a hand up.*

**The one in the good coat had had the inside of his thigh opened above
the knee and had bled out where he lay**, which is a thing you do to a man
you are leaving, and which he had never done in his life, and which he
could not find anywhere in his own method.

**And the two by the east door had been done separately, with the table
moved.**

He looked at the table for a long time.

*Somebody moved that table.*

*Somebody put a table between himself and the far one so that he could
have them one at a time instead of two at once, and that is the correct
answer, and it is the answer I would give if you asked me the question on
a sheet.*

*And I have got no memory of it at all.*

---

The fifth was by the east door about a yard inside it and it was the man
with the hip.

He stood over him.

He had eaten a bowl of millet on a step twenty-four hours earlier because
this man had asked him whether he had eaten and had refused to accept
*I'm here for nine crates* as an answer. He had watched him do the beds
from a step at three in the afternoon and had worked out that the terrace
walls had been rebuilt twice and that the second man had been better at
it. He had been told, at a volume that had not been used in that building
in nine weeks, that she had been down there since the second of the
eleventh.

He stood over him and looked for something to do with it and there was
nothing, and there was no column, and the honest entry was one line and he
made it in his head and never wrote it down.

*He came in.*

---

The sixth was Enkai and it had taken some time and he did not look at it
for long.

He went to the table instead.

The book was on the floor where it had gone. The receipt was on the table
and had not moved, and it had his own mark on it in his own hand and the
date beside it in Enkai's, and it said nine crates and eight hundred and
seventeen pounds and nine hundred ryō, and it was the only object in the
room that was exactly as it had been at half past nine.

He picked it up and looked at it and put it in his coat, and could not
have said why he did that either.

---

Then he noticed the sword.

He had it in his right hand and he had had it in his right hand and he did
not know when he had drawn it, and that was not new, because he had not
known that on a riverbed either.

What was new was that it was warm.

He turned it over. He had had it since the tenth month of the year before
and had carried it every day and had mended two chips in it and had sat
with it on his knees on nine separate mornings doing the ritual, and it
had never in that time been anything but the temperature of the air.

It was warm along the whole of the length of it and it was cooling while
he held it, and he could feel it cooling, in about the way a stone comes
down after the sun goes off it.

*I have not done anything to it.*

He held it up and looked along it and there was nothing to see. He put his
thumb on the flat of it and there was nothing to feel except that it was
warmer than his thumb and getting less so.

And underneath the room, under everything, at the very bottom of what he
could hear, there was the note it always made, which he had stopped
hearing about a year ago in the way you stop hearing a stream.

*It is where it always is.*

*It is exactly where it always is and it has always been there and it is
there now, and this thing is warm, and I have got no line anywhere in
either book for a thing my own kit did that I did not do.*

He put it away.

He put it in the ordinary column that night, in four words, in the place
where he put facts, and it sat in that book unexplained for two years.

> *17/12. The blade was warm.*

---

She was still standing there.

That is the thing he could not get over afterwards, and it was not that
she had not run and it was not that she was not crying. It was where she
was standing. She had not moved. She was in the middle of the floor,
inside four feet of the first two, exactly where she had been when the
door opened, with her hands at her sides.

He looked at her properly for the first time.

Ten. His own size, or near it. Hair to about her shoulders and dark for
most of the length of it and about four inches of red at the top of it. A
coat somebody had given her and shoes that did not match, one much too
big.

*She has been in a cellar for forty-five days and she is standing up
straight in a room with six dead men in it and she is looking at me.*

*She has not looked at any of them since I turned round. I have been
watching her for eleven seconds and she has not looked at one of them
once.*

---

"They'll come," she said.

Her voice was all right. That was the other thing.

"Who?"

"The nine in the long building. Not now. They've been in there the whole
time and none of them came out, so they're not coming out for a while. But
they'll go down to Ōgi and they'll say it, and it's four days."

He looked at her.

*She has done the road and the number of them and how long it takes and
she has done it standing there.*

"There's eleven men on this ground," she said, "and you've done six, and
the other five are somewhere and they've heard it."

"Five is the beds and the kitchen and the store."

"Four," she said. "One of them went down to Ōgi on the fifteenth and
hasn't come back."

---

He did not say anything for a moment.

*She has got the building.*

*She has got the building off the inside of a root cellar and it is better
than mine and mine is a day old and hers is right.*

"Come on, then," he said.

---

And she did the other thing, and she did it fast, and she had it ready.

"I'll be worth having."

He stopped.

"I know the water and the ground and the wood and I can do a floor and I
can mend and I have got eight trips instead of eleven out of a step nobody
had looked at in nineteen years. I do not eat much. I am not going to
slow you down and if I do you can leave me, and I will not say anything
about it, and I will not follow you if you say now that I am not to."

She said all of it without stopping and without her voice going anywhere,
and she had plainly had it in her head for some time, and she got to the
end of it and stood there.

---

He thought about it for about four seconds and the four seconds were not
about any of that.

*None of that is why.*

*She is going to follow me. She said she would not, and she will, and she
said it because she has worked out that offering not to is what makes it
possible for me to say yes, and she is right, and she has done that in
about a minute in a room with six dead men in it.*

*So the actual question is what refusing costs.*

*Refusing costs a girl on a mountain road in the twelfth month with nine
people who are going to walk to Ōgi and say a thing, and it costs me
somebody who knows this building walking down that road four days behind
me talking to whoever asks her.*

*And allowing costs a bowl and a half a day and being two people on a road
instead of one.*

He got to the end of it and there was nothing after it.

*Two people on a road in winter are a father and a child, or a brother and
a sister, and nobody has ever asked a father anything.*

He did not notice where he had got that from. It had been said to him in
that room ninety minutes earlier by a man who was four feet to his left on
the floor.

"Get whatever you've got," he said.

"I haven't got anything."

"Then we go now."

---

They went out through the south door at about twenty past eleven and
crossed the ground and nobody came out of anything.

He went past the top of the four steps at the north end and did not look
down them.

She stopped for two seconds at a place on the ground about eight feet from
the top of them and picked up a piece of sacking with charcoal marks on it
and folded it twice and put it inside her coat, and did not explain it,
and he did not ask.

Then they went down the steps out of the Kadono-dera, eleven flights of
them, cut in different centuries, and the light was flat and white and it
was the seventeenth of the twelfth month.
