# Chapter 5, Scene 8 — Ninety Yards

*Act 1, month 5, the eighth, afternoon. The riverbed. Tadayoshi Ruri,
age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~2,420 words.*

---

He was kneeling.

That was the first thing and it took a while to be the first thing,
because for some length of time before it there had been nothing at all
and then there was a sound, which was the sea, coming up a beach beyond a
bank at about eleven a minute.

He was kneeling on stones and his right hand was closed.

---

There was something in it.

He was aware of that before he was aware of the rest of the bed, before
he was aware of the light having moved, before he was aware of his own
face being wet: something small and hard was in his right fist, and his
thumb was on it, and his thumb was going up and back along it, up and
back, the way his thumb went along everything, and it had evidently been
doing that for some time.

He opened the hand about an inch and did not look at it.

*Later.*

He put it in his coat pocket, the left one, the one with the grease tin
in it, and closed the flap, and did not look at it, because there were
four people in that riverbed and he had to know where they were before he
did anything else at all.

---

He got up.

His left shoulder did something on the way and he made a noise and had to
wait, and then went on getting up, and stood in the middle of the channel
with his hands hanging.

His hands were red to about the second knuckle and the backs of them were
cut in about eleven places in short straight lines, and he looked at that
and worked out where the lines had come from and did not put it into
words.

The tin was eight feet away on its side with nothing in it.

The light was wrong. It had been about eleven in the morning and it was
not about eleven in the morning; the shadow of the landward bank was out
across the channel and had two hours in it.

---

*Two hours.*

*Or eleven minutes and the sun does not care what I think.*

*Do not guess. Go and get it.*

He went and got it.

---

The riverbed was full of his work.

He saw that from the middle of the channel and it stopped him for about
four seconds, which was as long as he let anything stop him for the next
two hours: sixty yards of grit shoulder with points standing up out of
it, everywhere, in no order at all — not in fences; there were no fences
after the first four — just points, singly and in patches and in one
place a stand of about two hundred of them together like a thing growing.

*I did that.*

*There is no other explanation available and I am not going to spend any
time on the ones that are not available.*

He crouched at the edge of the nearest patch and ran his thumb up one.

*Two courses. Grain in it.*

*Of course there is grain in it. I have put grain in every piece I have
made for two years and I put grain in these and I was not there.*

He stood up.

*And that is going to be the only funny thing in the whole of this and I
am going to think about it at three in the morning for about forty
years.*

---

Then he did it properly, because the ground was the only witness and the
ground was made of the one substance on earth he could read.

He started at the channel and worked outward, and it took him about two
hours, and he did not hurry it, and twice he had to stop and go and sit
down on the bank for a while and then came back.

---

**THE FOURTH ONE. The one whose name he never got.**

He was four feet from where Tadayoshi had woken up.

He was on his back on the channel stones with his knees up, which is not
a position a person arranges, and his right hand was still open, and it
had been on Tadayoshi's wrist about — about however long ago it had been.

There was one point in him. One.

It had gone in under the jaw on the left side and it was about the length
of a little finger and it was tapered, and there was grain in it, and
Tadayoshi crouched beside a boy of thirteen for a long time and did not
touch it.

*He was on my back.*

*He had my right wrist above the joint from behind and there was a knee
across my shoulder blades and I could not get air and I could not get a
hand.*

*And then something in me got up.*

*And this is four feet from where I woke.*

---

**HOKUTO.** Thirty-one yards, up the shingle, towards the road.

He had gone thirty-one yards on a foot with a hole through it.

Tadayoshi measured it because measuring it was the only thing he had.
He paced it out from the place in the channel where the boot-print and
the mess of it said Hokuto had gone down — and there was a *track*, a
dragged, one-sided track through the grit going up the shoulder, thirty-
one yards of it, made by somebody hauling himself with his hands and one
knee.

At the end of it there were points.

Not one. He did not count them and then he made himself go back and count
them and there were nine.

*He was crawling.*

*He had been crawling for thirty-one yards and he was going towards the
road and there is nothing on that road, there is not a house within four
miles, he was not going anywhere, he was just going away from the middle
of the bed.*

*And he was face down and going away and I put nine of them into him.*

He stood at the top of the shoulder with his hands at his sides.

*Enter it.*

*He could not have hurt me. Not at thirty-one yards, not on that foot,
not with his back to me and both hands on the ground.*

*There is no version in which that was a fight.*

---

**THE THIRD ONE.** Fifty-four yards, at the landward bank.

Nothing in his hands. That was the thing Tadayoshi found and could not
get past.

He looked for it for eleven minutes. He walked the whole of the ground
between the channel and the bank in a grid the way you walk a beach for a
dropped thing, and he found it eventually about nine yards back the way
the boy had come, lying flat in the grit where somebody had dropped it
and gone on.

*He put it down.*

*He put it down and he ran, and he got another nine yards after he put it
down, and I did not stop when he put it down.*

---

**MIYO.** Ninety yards.

She was in the road.

She had got out of the riverbed and up the seaward bank and across the
verge and she was in the middle of the coast road, ninety yards from
where the fence had been, and her feet were what they were from coming
through eleven feet of standing points to get to somebody who was
screaming.

Tadayoshi stood on the road beside her for a long time.

*Ninety yards.*

*Say she was going as fast as a person goes on those feet. Say six or
seven a minute at the outside, and she had a start on me because she was
already running.*

*Ninety yards.*

*That is not four seconds. That is not a thing that happens because
somebody has hold of me and I cannot breathe.*

*That is a minute at least. That is a minute of me going up a bank and
across a verge after somebody who was not coming back.*

He knelt down and did what he had done for the other three, which was to
count.

There were three.

*Three, and one of them is at the back of the knee, and you do not put a
thing at the back of a knee to stop somebody hurting you.*

*You put it there to stop them going.*

---

He sat down on the verge.

He sat there for about twenty minutes and did not do anything at all, and
then he got up and went back down into the bed and finished it, because
there was one thing left and it was the only one that mattered and he had
been putting it off for two hours and had known he was putting it off.

---

*The fourth one.*

*The one at the channel. The one with his hand still open.*

He crouched by him again.

*Order.*

*One. Where did the others die? Thirty-one yards, fifty-four yards,
ninety yards. All of them going away. All of them with their backs to the
middle. All of them after.*

*Two. So there is a middle. There is a moment where the fight stops being
a fight and becomes the other thing, and every one of those three is on
the far side of it, and I can prove it, and I could stand in a room and
prove it to anybody with a rule and a pair of eyes.*

*Three. And this one is not on the far side of it.*

*This one is four feet from where I woke up and he had my wrist and there
was a knee on my back and I could not get air.*

*So: was he the last thing that happened before, or the first thing that
happened after?*

He sat back on his heels in the channel.

---

*Test it.*

*If he was before: then something in me got up because I was being
suffocated by somebody who was holding me down, and it killed the person
holding me down, and everything after that is a separate horror and this
one is a boy fighting for air.*

*If he was after: then I got out from under him some other way — he
shifted, or the shoulder went, or he heard Hokuto and looked up — and he
was off me and standing or turning or getting up, and I put a tapered
point under his jaw at four feet.*

*Which is it.*

He looked at the ground.

*The stones are turned over across about eight feet. That is consistent
with both.*

*His knees are up. That is a person falling backwards, which is
consistent with both.*

*There is one point in him. One. In every other case there are nine and
three and — I have not counted the third one, I counted the third one,
there were five.*

*One.*

He held onto that for about a minute.

*One is the fight. One is a thing that happens because it was the only
thing available and then it is over. Nine is the other thing.*

*So one says: before.*

Then he put his hand flat on the stones.

*No. One says nothing at all.*

*One could be before. One could also be the FIRST one — the first of the
other thing, before it got going, before it had built to nine — and there
is nothing on this ground that separates a last act of a fight from a
first act of what came after, because they are the same size and they
happen four feet apart and they happen inside about a second.*

*And I was not there.*

---

He sat in a dry riverbed with four dead children in it and worked at it
for another half an hour, properly, in order, the way he worked at
everything, and did not get anywhere at all, and knew after the first ten
minutes that he was not going to and went on for twenty more because
stopping was worse.

*I can prove three.*

*I can prove three of them past any argument, and there is not one person
alive who would need convincing anyway, because they are thirty-one and
fifty-four and ninety yards away with their backs to me.*

*And the one I cannot prove is the only one that would tell me anything.*

*Because if he was before, then there is a thing in me that comes up when
I am being killed, and it is horrifying and it is not — it is not — it
started as a person defending himself.*

*And if he was after, then it did not start as anything. It started
because it started, and the boy holding me down was simply the nearest
one, and the whole of it — Hokuto crawling, the one who put his weapon
down, ninety yards of road — the whole of it is what I am, without a
reason at the front of it.*

*And the difference between those two is the difference between the rest
of my life and the rest of my life.*

*And the evidence is my own hands.*

---

He got up eventually because the light was going.

He did not bury them. He thought about it and he costed it and the
arithmetic was that four graves in a riverbed is eleven hours he did not
have and a fresh dug place is a signpost, and he stood there with the
arithmetic in front of him, correct, complete, checked twice.

*Somebody will have to go down and shut the doors.*

He had thought that on a bed frame in a house on the sixth course at two
in the morning and he had gone down and done it, all thirty-one of them,
and had been three hours about it.

He looked at the four of them.

Then he picked up the tin, and put it inside his coat, and walked out of
the riverbed onto the coast road and north, past a girl of thirteen lying
in it, and did not stop.

He got about four hundred yards.

Then he had to go off the road and be sick behind a wall, for a long
time, on his knees, with his forehead against the stone; and after that
he sat with his back to it in the last of the light and put his hand in
his left pocket and took out the thing he had been holding when he woke
up.
