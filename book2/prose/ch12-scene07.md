# Chapter 12, Scene 7 — What Am I Carrying

*Act 1, month 12, the seventeenth, morning. The Kadono-dera. Tadayoshi
Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~2,400 words.*

---

He raised it at half past nine in the morning in the hall and he raised it
correctly, at the right volume, to the man who had signed the receipt.

"The crates are eight hundred and seventeen pounds."

"They are."

"It's four days to Ōgi and nine to Ōtoma and I'm one man."

"Yes," said Enkai.

He did not look up from the book while he said it, and he said it the way
you say *yes* to a person who has finally arrived at the thing you have
been waiting for them to arrive at.

---

"The crates go in the spring," he said. "There's a cart comes up from Ōgi
when the pass takes one. You'd not get them down this mountain in the
twelfth month with nine men."

"Then why have I signed for them."

"Because you're the courier, and the courier signs."

"For a consignment I'm not taking."

"For the consignment," said Enkai. "The crates are on the sheet because
the crates are what a sheet has on it."

---

Tadayoshi stood in the middle of a hall floor and did the arithmetic and
it took him about two seconds, and it was the same two seconds he had
spent on the step at four o'clock the previous afternoon, coming out the
other way round.

*The weights are not an error.*

*The weights are on the paper so that there is something on the paper.*

*And the thing I am actually carrying is a thing you send one boy on foot
into the twelfth month with, and there is no such thing that weighs
anything, and there is no such thing that goes in a crate.*

He said, "What am I carrying?"

---

Enkai closed the book.

What he did then was the thing Tadayoshi could not get past afterwards,
and it was not cruelty and there was none in it anywhere.

**He explained.**

He explained the way a man explains a routing decision to somebody who
will be executing it: unhurried, in order, with the reasons attached to
the parts they belonged to.

That a cart is watched and a man is not.

That the twelfth month is the best month for it, because the roads are
empty and nobody is counting, and he had come up an empty road himself and
would know.

That a boy is better than a man, because two people on a road in winter
are a father and a child, or a brother and a sister, and nobody has ever
asked a father anything.

That the buyer's man had walked four days on the fifteenth to confirm the
item was as described, and had confirmed it, and that there was a
correction to the description on his sheet which was in the buyer's
favour and would be settled at the far end.

That the item had been priced on the twenty-eighth of the tenth month and
held since, and that holding costs, and that the holding was why the
schedule was what it was.

That it was four days down, on the Ōgi road as far as the second ford and
then east, and that he would be met.

Every clause of it was a decision somebody had made about a vehicle and a
season and a cost.

---

*He is doing what I do.*

*That is the shape of it. That is exactly the shape of it — the road, the
cover, what the weather buys, what the waiting costs. I have got four
pages of that in my coat and I have been improving them since the sixth
month.*

*And he has got the same four pages and his are older.*

Tadayoshi stood on a hall floor at twenty to ten in the morning of the
seventeenth of the twelfth month and ran the questions, because the
questions were what he had, and they ran the way they always ran, which
was fast.

*One. Is it operational?*

*No.*

*Two. Does that matter?*

*No, and I settled that on the twelfth of the tenth month and took
fourteen thousand ryō for it.*

*Three. Can I do it?*

*Yes. It is four days down a road I came up yesterday, in the twelfth
month, with a child who has been in a cellar for six weeks and is not
going to run anywhere.*

---

The page was at the back of the ledger, two leaves past the one headed
**PEOPLE WHO WILL TELL SOMEBODY**, and it had five lines on it and a date
in the top corner, and it had been in the ledger since the twenty-second
of the eleventh month, and the ledger was in the coat, and the coat was on
him.

It had been on him for nine days and four hundred miles.

He did not think of it.

He was not going to think of it for twelve days.

---

"What's the fee?"

"It's on the sheet."

He looked at the sheet.

*Nine hundred.*

He stood with it and did the only thing he could do with it, which was to
put it in the series, and the series said what the series said.

*Anazawa was three thousand six hundred for one man in an hour.*

*Terasaka was fourteen thousand.*

*This is nine hundred for eighteen days.*

*So it is not priced off what it is. It is priced off what I am, and what
I am on this sheet is a courier, and a courier gets fifty a day.*

He turned that over once and it came out worse the second time.

*It is not an insult about her. Nobody has priced her at nine hundred.
She is priced somewhere else, on a different sheet, that I am not going to
see.*

*Nine hundred is what it costs to move a thing.*

---

"There's two ways this goes," said Enkai, "and I'd rather you did the
sensible one."

He said it without any pressure in it at all. He had put the book down on
the table beside him and he stood the way he always stood, which was
straight and not quite still, and he was about sixty and had ink on the
middle finger of his right hand.

"You take the consignment, which is what you came for and what you have
signed for, and you're paid what the sheet says and the whole of it is
finished by the twenty-first."

"Or?"

"Or you decline it and go down that road empty, and I write to the man who
sent you and say the courier declined. And he sends another one, and the
pass shuts in the first month and doesn't open till the third, and the
buyer's man came four days in the twelfth month to look at her and won't
come again till the road's fit."

He let that sit for about a second.

"So it's nine weeks. And she does the nine weeks where she is."

Then he said the thing that Tadayoshi did not have an answer for, and did
not say it unkindly, and did not appear to think it was a clever thing to
say.

"That's the whole of it. There's no third thing. You'd have done the same
arithmetic."

---

He had.

He did it again anyway, on the floor, standing up, because he could not
not, and he did it the way he did everything, which was to go looking for
the version where the man in front of him was wrong.

*Decline it and it costs her nine weeks.* — True. The pass shuts. I came up
it and I know what it is now and it will not take a horse in the first
month and it will not take a person in the second.

*Decline it and somebody else does it in the third month and it is worse
for her.* — Probably. A man they send in the third month is a man who does
this, and I do not.

*Take it and something happens on the road.* — Nothing happens on the road.
I have got four days on a road I walked yesterday and I am the most
capable thing on it.

*Take it and hand her over.* — Yes. That is the job.

He stopped there and looked for the next line and there was not one.

---

"All right," he said.

"Good," said Enkai, and picked the book back up, and that was the whole of
the transaction, and it had taken about eleven minutes.

"When?"

"Noon. There's things to do first and I'd rather they were done before you
had her."

"What things?"

"Nothing that concerns the road," said Enkai.

---

Tokuji was in the doorway.

Tadayoshi had not heard him come and did not know how long he had been
there, which was the second time in two days that a man of sixty with a
bad hip had got inside his attention, and he noticed it and it did not go
anywhere.

"She's been down there since the second of the eleventh," said Tokuji.

He said it loudly.

That was the only remarkable thing about it. He said it the way a man says
a thing he has been carrying up a hill for six weeks, at a volume that had
not been used in that building in nine weeks, and then he stood there in
the doorway having said it and did not seem to know what came next either.

Enkai looked at him for about a second and a half and did not answer him,
and went back to the book.

---

Tadayoshi did the arithmetic before he did anything else, because that is
the order things happened in him and he had never once been able to change
it.

*Second of the eleventh.*

*Today is the seventeenth of the twelfth.*

*That is forty-five days.*

Then, and only then, the other one arrived.

*And I came up that road at eleven yesterday morning and I read the ground
for ninety seconds and I counted the ways out of five buildings and I ate
a bowl on a step and I stood at the top of those steps in the last of the
light and worked out that somebody goes up and down them four times a
day.*

*I have been within thirty feet of it twice.*

He stood on the hall floor and had that, whole, at about ten to ten.

*And it did not tell me anything, because it was a root cellar with swept
steps and a temple that had just brought the beds up, and that is what it
looked like, and it was right.*

*It was all correct. Every part of it was correct.*

---

He looked at Tokuji, who had not moved.

*He has told me because there is nobody else in the world to tell.*

*He has fed her three times a day for forty-five days and he has not
looked at her, and I worked out on the steps that somebody goes down them
four times a day, and it is him, and he has been going down them four
times a day and not looking at her, and today a boy turned up with a
docket and he has said it out loud at the top of his voice to the first
person who was not from this building.*

*And it is not information. It does not change one line of it.*

He did not say any of that to anybody. He put it down where it went, and
then, because it wanted an answer and he had one, he wrote the answer
underneath it in his head and did not like the taste of it and did not
change it.

*Forty-five days is a fact about how long she has been in there.*

*It is not a fact about the road, or the pass, or the third month, or what
declining costs her.*

*Nothing he has said moves anything.*

---

"Noon," he said to Enkai.

"Noon," said Enkai, without looking up.

Tadayoshi went out past Tokuji and did not look at him either, which he
thought about afterwards for a very long time and could never work out
whether he had done on purpose.

He went back to the bed he had slept in and sat on the edge of it and got
the ledger out and did the sensible thing, which was to work.

He put down the road: four days, the second ford, then east, met at the
far end by somebody whose name he had not been given and had not asked
for. He put down what he had for four days and what he would need for
five. He put down the fee and the date it was due.

Then he came to the last line, which was the one operational thing he had
not settled, and he wrote it as a question because that was how he wrote
things he had not settled.

> *How is she secured on the road?*

He looked at it.

*A child who runs at night is four days of nothing and a fee I do not get.
A child who runs at a ford is worse.*

*And I have never moved a thing that could decide to be somewhere else.*

He did not have an answer and it was twenty past ten and the man who
would have one was in the hall.
