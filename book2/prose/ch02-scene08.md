# Chapter 2, Scene 8 — Nobody Sees This

*Act 1, month 3. Shioiri, the kitchen. Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV:
Tadayoshi. ~2,090 words.*

---

He got his afternoon on the twenty-third.

Meno was asleep. His father was at Ōura with the mule about a strap that
had been about a strap for three weeks. Hisui had taken Koishi up to the
fourth course to look for a cat that did not exist and was not going to
be back for an hour. Akagane was in the shed and would not come out of
the shed for anything short of the sea coming in.

His mother was at the table with the comb.

She had had it out every day since Iwao gave it to her, and she did not
use it at the table, she *looked* at it — turned it over, put it down,
picked it up an hour later — the way somebody handles a thing they have
not yet decided how to feel about. It was tortoiseshell and it had cost
two ryō of the extra and it was, by a distance, the nicest object in the
house.

He put the ledger on the table in front of her.

"Would you read that."

---

She looked at it, and then at him.

"All of it?"

"The last four pages. It is a proposal. It is written as a proposal
because I could not think of another way to make you sit through the
whole of it before you answered."

"Ah," said his mother.

"That is not — "

"No, it's fair." She put the comb down. "Go on then. Sit down, don't
stand over me, you're your great-aunt's boy."

He sat down.

---

She read it.

That was the first thing that went wrong, and he had not seen it coming
at all: she read it *properly*. He had prepared for her to look at a
page and hand it back. He had prepared for *not now*. He had prepared —
he had specifically prepared, at about two in the morning, four days ago
— for her to be angry that it was written down.

She turned the lamp round so it came over her shoulder and she read the
four passes in order, with a finger, and at the sums she stopped and did
them herself in her head, and he watched her do it because you could see
her do it, and at one of them she went back up a line and did it again.

It took her eleven minutes.

At the end she went back and read the narrow column.

Then she put her hand flat on the page, the way Meno did, and left it
there.

"That's good," she said.

"It is not — "

"It's *good*, Tadayoshi. Don't do that thing where you get told a true
thing and argue with it. The sums are right, the Kanaya line is worth
more than you've put on it, and the bit about Akagane and me is — " she
moved her hand and looked at it again. "I didn't know that. About the
frame. About what it's for."

"Nobody knows that. He does not know it, he has half of it, he thinks the
fingers run ahead — "

"They do run ahead."

"They run ahead *because the frame cannot do the job at all*," said
Tadayoshi, "and he has made a better version of a thing that should not
be necessary, and he is going to keep making them for the rest of his
life, and — "

He stopped, because his voice had gone somewhere he had not authorised.

His mother did not say anything about that. She let it go past, which he
was grateful for and did not say so.

"You've ruled a fourth column," she said.

"Yes."

"Narrow."

"That is how it is done."

"That's how *she* does it," said his mother, and something went over her
face and away again. "All right. Do you want the answer, or do you want
me to say I'll think about it?"

"The answer."

"You're sure."

"I did not spend eleven days carrying this about in my shirt to be told
you will think about it."

"No," she said. "No, you didn't."

---

"You're not declaring," said his mother. "You're confessing."

---

The fire went. Somebody's shutter, down on the second course.

"Say that again."

"Your case is: be worth something openly, on a day of our own choosing,
in a way that's boring. That's the whole of it and it's a good case." She
turned the ledger round so it faced him. "And there is no day of our own
choosing. There hasn't been one since you were eight."

"Why."

"Because a child of this house gets presented in the year they turn
eight." She said it perfectly evenly. "Assessed. Written down. Yes or no,
and it goes in the column. Hisui was. Akagane was. Iwao was — you don't
remember, you were three. Koishi goes in two years and Hisui has already
started dreading it and you'll see it on her when it comes.

"You weren't."

Tadayoshi sat with his hands flat on the table.

"So when you talk about declaring," said his mother, "what you are
describing is walking up to a door and saying *there is a producer in my
house and there has been for two years and we did not present him*. And
that is not a declaration, and it will not be boring, and it will not go
on an old list. It's an admission. And the first thing anybody who hears
it will want to know is not what you can do.

"It's what else we haven't said."

---

He looked for the hole in it.

He looked for it the way he had looked for the hole in his own case, and
he found it in about four seconds, and it was not a hole, it was the
place where his case had been standing.

*I built the whole of it on a day of our own choosing.*

*Every line after pass two assumes we get to pick the day.*

*And we do not, and we have not since the eighth month of the year I was
eight, and I did not know that, and I have had eleven days and four
passes and I did not know the thing the entire argument stands on.*

*And she has known it for two years.*

"You knew," he said.

"Yes."

"When you closed your hand over the drop and said *nobody sees this*.
You had already — "

"I'd already not presented you," said his mother. "It was the eighth
month. You'd have gone in the eighth month. I'd had three months of not
doing it before you ever came up those stairs with that thing in your
fist."

---

Tadayoshi got up and stood by the stove because he could not sit down any
more.

"Then it is worse," he said. "Do you understand that it is *worse*? Every
month it is worse. If it was three months in the eighth month it is
twenty-seven months now and it will be thirty-nine next year and there is
no version of this where the number goes down — "

"I know what the number does."

"Then why."

She did not answer.

"Why," said Tadayoshi. "Not the rule. I know the rule, I have had the
rule for two years, I have kept it every working day and I have never once
asked you for anything except this. Why."

---

His mother sat at the table with her hand on the ledger.

"They came for him in the daytime," she said.

"Kongō."

"They came in the daytime. That's the thing nobody says about it. I've
had — " she stopped and started again. "Everybody thinks a thing like that
happens at night. It didn't. It was the middle of the afternoon in the
eighth month and the whole compound was out, all forty of us, on the
paths, at the doors, on the second course and the third course, watching.

"It took about twenty minutes. There was no shouting. He put his tools
down and went and got his coat, and my mother's brother said something to
one of them and got an answer, and then they all went down the hill, and
there was a boat, and by the evening the fire was lit and we ate.

"And every single person on that hill was somebody who could have done
something. Forty of us."

She turned the comb over on the table.

"And I have never in my life been able to work out whether we could have.
Not once. Not in fifty years. I've had it at three in the morning about a
hundred times and it does not come out."

---

He waited.

He waited a long time, longer than he had ever waited with Meno, because
he understood that he was being given something and he did not yet know
how big it was.

"That is not a reason," he said at last.

"No."

"It is a thing that happened."

"Yes."

"You have given me a thing that happened and I asked you for a reason and
you know they are different, and — "

"I know they're different," said his mother.

"Then give me the reason."

"I haven't got one," she said. "I've got that."

---

She said it without any drama in it whatsoever, and that was what took
his legs out.

"I've got a hill with forty people on it and one man walking down it, and
I have had it for fifty years, and every time anybody in this family says
*we should* about anything at all, that is what I have got instead of an
argument. And it isn't good enough. I know it isn't good enough. Your
father's known it isn't good enough for eighteen years and he's too kind
to say it in front of Meno.

"And it is still what I've got, and you're ten, and the answer is no."

---

There was nothing left after that.

He knew there was nothing left because he went looking, properly, the way
he did, and every route he tried came back to a hill in the eighth month
with forty people standing on it.

He picked the ledger up off the table.

"May I ask one more thing."

"Yes."

"When Koishi is presented," said Tadayoshi. "In two years. Will you take
her?"

His mother looked at him for a moment.

"Yes," she said.

"Because if you do not, then Hisui goes on the same list I am on, and
Koishi goes on it, and — "

"Yes," said his mother. "I'll take her. Hisui's taking her. It's already
been said. That was said the week she was born."

"Then you and I are the only two people in this house who are not — "

"That's right," said his mother.

---

He stood there with the ledger.

"Your hands are freezing," she said.

"It is the second month."

"It's the third month, and they've been like that since the tenth, and
you sit out in that shed till the lamp goes because you think nobody
knows." She reached out and took both of them and turned them over,
briskly, without any ceremony at all, the way she would have looked at a
mount — and stopped, at the knuckles, where the cracks were.

"Ah," she said. "Tadayoshi."

"They close in the fourth month."

"They didn't close last year till the fifth."

"They closed in the fourth."

"They did *not*," said his mother, and got up and went to the shelf and
got down the flat tin with the grease in it that had been on that shelf
since before he was born, and came back, and took his right hand, and did
the knuckles one at a time with her thumb, hard, the way you would work
it into a strap.

It hurt. He let it hurt.

Neither of them said anything for about two minutes.

Then she did the left one, and put the lid back on the tin, and put the
tin on the table next to the comb, and said, "Take that out to the shed
and *use* it," and picked her comb back up and turned it over.

He took the tin and the ledger out to the shed.

He did nine drops that evening in the new frame, and every one of them
had grain in it, and the grease made the seals slip for the first four
and after that it did not.
