# Chapter 10, Scene 5 — It Was Adequate

*Act 1, month 9, the twenty-ninth. A house above Ōtoma. Tadayoshi Ruri,
age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,740 words.*

---

Ōtsuka told him to wash and told him twice, which was how he knew it was
not about washing.

"Both boots."

"I've done both boots."

"Do them again." Ōtsuka did not look up. "And the sleeve. And put the
other coat on, the one that doesn't fit you."

"Where am I going?"

"You're going to stand in a room."

---

It was an hour and a half up the hill east of Ōtoma and the house was not
grand and Tadayoshi noticed that immediately and put it away as
interesting.

*Six rooms. Good timber and no carving on any of it. Two men in the yard
who are not servants and are not hiding that they are not servants.*

*The gate is new and the wall is old.*

*So it is a man who has had money for about four years and had less than
this before that, and has spent it on the wall and not on the house.*

They put him in a side room for forty minutes with nothing to do, which he
spent going through the ways out, because that was now automatic and had
been since the tenth month, and there were three, and one of them was a
shuttered window he could not have got a grown man through and could have
got himself through easily.

Then Ōtsuka came and got him and said one sentence on the way down the
passage, which was the entire briefing he received.

"Answer what he asks you and nothing else."

---

The man was about fifty-five and he was called Kadoya and he had timber
on four rivers, which Tadayoshi did not learn in the room and worked out
afterwards from the hands and the smell of the house.

He was sitting down. There was another man standing behind him against the
wall who did not speak at any point.

"This is him?"

"This is him," said Ōtsuka.

"He's small."

"He is."

Kadoya looked at Tadayoshi for about eleven seconds, and Tadayoshi stood
and let him, because being looked at was a thing he had been doing since
the fourth month and he had no particular feeling about it.

---

"You did the Anazawa business."

"Yes."

"On the twenty-sixth."

"Yes."

"Nobody's come to me about it."

That was not a question and Tadayoshi did not answer it, which was correct
and which he could see Ōtsuka approve of out of the corner of his eye, and
which lasted about ninety more seconds.

Kadoya asked three more that were not really questions — how long he had
been with them, whether he had walked it, whether he had eaten — and
Tadayoshi answered all three in under four words each, and the room was
going perfectly well.

Then Kadoya said, in the tone of a man closing a pleasant subject:

"And it was clean."

---

"No," said Tadayoshi.

There was a silence of about two seconds, which he did not notice.

"It was not clean. It was adequate."

---

He went on, because he had been asked, and because the question had been
put to him in a form that had a fact in it and the fact was wrong, and
because nobody had ever once told him not to.

"There were four things wrong with it.

"The first is that I read the ground on the first evening and did the job
on the fourth, and the drain strip at the top of the lane had dried out in
between. I walked round it both ways for a condition that had stopped
being true. That cost forty-four seconds and it was for nothing, and if
there had been anybody in that lane on the way out, forty-four seconds is
the difference between being past it and being in it.

"The second is the sleeve. I took him from behind and above and I was
inside the arc, and there was no way to be inside the arc and not be in
it, so that one is not a mistake, it is a cost. But it is on the outside
of the right sleeve to the elbow and it does not come out and I have got
one coat.

"The third is the boots, which is the same thing and is worse, because a
sleeve is a sleeve and a boot has a welt.

"And the fourth is that there is a woman who came down that lane at twenty
past nine with a covered pot four nights running and I did not find out
who she was or where she was going. She did not see me. But I did not know
she was going to come, and she came four times, and I only worked out it
was four times afterwards. If she had been eleven minutes later she would
have been in the lane while the door was open."

He stopped.

"So it was adequate."

---

Ōtsuka said his name once, quietly, at some point in the second thing, and
Tadayoshi heard it and did not understand what it was for and kept going.

He worked out what it was for on the way down the hill and it took him
about two miles.

---

Kadoya sat forward.

He was not angry. That was the thing Tadayoshi got wrong for a long time
afterwards, and got wrong specifically because he was watching for anger
and there wasn't any, and he had no other category to put the expression
in.

"Say the bit about the woman again."

Tadayoshi said the bit about the woman again.

"Four nights."

"Yes."

"And you'd been in that lane four nights and you didn't know who she
was."

"No."

"And that bothers you."

"It's the fourth thing wrong with it. The other three cost time and cloth.
That one was luck."

Kadoya looked at the man standing behind him, and the man standing behind
him did not do anything, and Kadoya laughed — once, short, not unkindly —
and sat back.

"Right," he said. "Yes. Good."

---

Ōtsuka did not speak on the path down until they were past the gate and
about two hundred yards past it, and then he stopped in the road.

"What was that?"

"He asked me a question."

"He was not asking you a question."

"He said *and it was clean*."

"Yes."

"It wasn't clean."

Ōtsuka stood in the road in the dark and looked at him for a while, and
Tadayoshi looked back and waited, and was entirely willing to be told
something, and had no idea at all what he had done.

"You've cost us nothing," said Ōtsuka at last, "and I want you to
understand that that is luck."

"I understand."

"You don't. Say it back."

"That I've cost you nothing and it is luck."

"Say the other bit."

Tadayoshi thought about it.

"I don't know the other bit."

"No," said Ōtsuka. "You don't."

---

He worked at it for two days and got about two thirds of it, and wrote
down the two thirds, and left a gap for the rest.

*A man who says **and it was clean** is not asking whether it was clean.*

*He is saying **tell me it was clean**, and he is saying it in the shape
of a question so that he can be told it by somebody who appears to have
decided it themselves.*

*So the answer he wanted was one word and I gave him four hundred.*

He turned it over.

*And what is the four hundred for? It is not for him. He does not care
about the drain strip. He has never seen a drain strip.*

*It is for me, because it is true, and because being asked a question and
answering it accurately is the only transaction I actually understand.*

He read that back and did not like the taste of it and wrote it anyway.

*So the fault is not that I was rude. I was not rude.*

**The fault is that I answered the question that was asked and not the
question that was meant, and I cannot hear the difference, and I do not
know how you learn to.**

---

The third of it he never got, and it was the part that mattered.

Kadoya went on funding them. He went on funding them for two more years
and increased it twice, and the second increase came in the fourth month
of the following year with a note attached that Tadayoshi never saw, and
the note said, in effect, *the boy, if he's available.*

Because Kadoya had not been reassured in that room and had not wanted to
be. He had been shown a thing — a small, cold, precise thing that had come
into his house and given him a costed account of a man's death and then
told him it had been done badly — **and he had never in his life had
anything like it to talk about.**

He talked about it. He talked about it for years. He talked about it at
tables, in the way men talk about a horse they saw once, and the
description was affectionate and admiring and it was very, very accurate,
because he had been in the room for eleven minutes and had paid attention
to every second of it.

---

Tadayoshi put a new heading on the towns page on the first of the tenth
month.

He did not have a word for what it was and used four.

> *PEOPLE WHO WILL TELL SOMEBODY*

Under it he put Kadoya, and the date, and *house above Ōtoma*, and then he
sat with the pencil for a while and put a second line under that, because
he had realised something while he was writing the first one.

> *And the man behind him, who did not speak, and whom I did not look at
> properly.*

**It was the first entry on that page that was not a place.**

He looked at the heading for a long time and could not make it into a
number, and put the book away, and did not work out until much later that
what he had just started was a different file entirely — that the eleven
towns were people who *could* describe him, and this was a list of people
who *wanted to*, and that the second list was going to be the one that
killed him.
