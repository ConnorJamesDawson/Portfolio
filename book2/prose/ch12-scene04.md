# Chapter 12, Scene 4 — More

*Act 1. A room. A boy, age twelve. POV: the boy. ~1,300 words.*

---

He had broken the fourth one on the bowl after he got the number, because
there was no reason to wait and waiting was not a thing he did with
anything except answers.

Ninety-one bowls since.

*And it is coming.*

He felt along the back of his left hand every morning and it was coming
and it was coming at about the rate the third one had come, and the third
one had taken a hundred and twenty-nine, and ninety-one out of a hundred
and twenty-nine was most of the way, and the whole of it was going exactly
as he had worked out that it would.

He was pleased about that in a way he had no word for.

*I said a thing about what was going to happen and then it happened.*

*That has not been true before.*

---

The bowl changed on the ninth.

He did not know it was the ninth of anything. He knew it was the eight
hundred and something-th bowl of his life in that room and he had stopped
counting the total a long time ago because the total was not useful, and
what he had was the ninety-one, which was.

What he knew was that it was different, and he knew it before he had it in
his hands, and he knew it off the weight of the man's arm coming down.

*That is not the same coming down.*

Then it was on the floor in front of him and he sat and looked at it and
did not touch it.

*It is more.*

---

He was very careful about that, because it was the sort of thing you can
want.

*I have been hungry for two years and a hungry person looking at a bowl is
the worst instrument there is.*

So he did what he did with everything, which was to find a way to be
wrong about it.

*It might be that it is heaped differently and it is the same amount.*

*It might be that I am hungrier today and everything looks like more.*

*It might be that it is the light.*

There was no light. That one went in about a second and he let it go and
did not mind, because ruling out the stupid one first is how you find out
whether you are thinking.

He ate it. He was not going to not eat it.

And afterwards he sat with his back to the wall and had a thing happen to
him that had not happened in that room, which was that he was not hungry.

He sat with it for a long time.

*I do not like this.*

*And I cannot say why I do not like it and that is because I have not got
anything to say it with.*

---

He needed a way to measure a bowl of millet and he had four days to get
one, and he got it on the second day and it took him most of that day to
be willing to do it.

He had four pieces on the mat now.

Three of them were bent or short or both, and one of them — the third, the
one that had given him the number — was three and a quarter thumbs and
straight the whole way, and he had put it at the end of the row because
that was where it went.

He looked at it for most of an afternoon.

*It is a straight thing of a known length.*

*And I have not got another one.*

---

He did it on the third day.

He turned the bowl so that the crack in the glaze on the outside was under
his right hand, which meant the bowl was the same way round every time,
and then he put the piece in at the edge, upright, until it touched the
bottom, and put his thumbnail on it where the millet came to, and took it
out and looked at it.

Then he ate.

He did that four days running, at the same one of the three bowls, and on
the fourth day he had four marks on a piece of his own hand and he put
them in a row against the light from the gap and looked at them.

*Three the same and one under.*

*And the one under was the day the man came late and I think it was
knocked.*

*So it is three the same.*

---

He put the piece back at the end of the row and sat with his hands in his
lap and had a fact.

*It is more, and it is the same more every time, and it has been the same
more for four days.*

He knew what that meant on its own, because he had got there in the sixth
month about something else and had never had to get there again.

*A thing that is the same every time is a thing somebody decided.*

*So somebody decided it. On a day. Before the ninth.*

And then he came to the end of what he had, and he came to the end of it
fast, and the coming to the end of it was the whole of the scene and he
would not have called it anything.

*And I do not know anything about the person who decided it.*

---

He went at it anyway, because it was there.

*More food is a thing you do when you want a thing to be bigger.*

*Or when you have got more food.*

*Or when the person who does the bowls has changed and the new one does it
differently, except that it is the same man, and I know the arms.*

*Or when something is going to happen and the thing that is going to
happen wants me to be able to walk.*

He stopped on that one, not because it frightened him — nothing in the
room frightened him, and he did not have very much to be frightened with —
but because he had noticed something about the four of them.

*I have got four readings and I cannot get at any of them.*

*And the reason is not that they are hard. It is that every single one of
them is about somebody upstairs, and I have never once been told anything
about anybody upstairs, and I have been here for two years.*

He turned his hands over and looked at the backs of them.

*I have got a hundred and twenty-nine and a hundred and fifty-one and a
hundred and seventy-eight, and I got all three of those off my own hand,
and they were right.*

*And I cannot tell you one thing about the man who brings the bowl except
how his arm comes down.*

---

He did not conclude anything.

That should be said the way he would have said it if he had had the words,
which he did not: **he did not conclude anything, and it was not a failure
of nerve and it was not a failure of thinking. There was nothing to
conclude with.** A boy who has been told nothing for two years has a
perfect instrument for measuring a bowl and no instrument at all for
measuring a decision, and he had just found that out, at twelve, on a mat,
with a piece of his own finger in his hand.

So he put it where the true things went.

*It is more. It has been more for four days. Somebody decided it. I do not
know why and I am not going to.*

He lined the four pieces up with the bases in a row, the way he always
did, and put the straight one back at the end, and felt along the back of
his left hand at the place where the fifth one was coming.

*Ninety-five.*

*Thirty-four to go.*

He lay down on his side facing the wall and waited for the bowl.
