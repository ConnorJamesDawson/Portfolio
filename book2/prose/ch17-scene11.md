# Chapter 17, Scene 11 — Nobody Above You

*Act 2, year 1, month 4, the twenty-fourth to the thirtieth. The Kadono
road and a fire off it. Nozaki Uzuki, age eleven. POV: Uzuki. ~1,800
words.*

---

They did eleven miles in the afternoon and none of them said anything, and
she has never been able to decide whether that was three people being
careful or three people having nothing to say.

She watched him do the whole of it and she has the list.

*He counted the money at the table and again on the road.*

*He did the mule at the first stop and did the near hind twice.*

*He has not looked back at Kadono once and we came out of a gate you can
see for two miles.*

*And he has not got the book out, and it is four in the afternoon and he
does the book at four when we are walking, and he has done it at four every
day since the second month.*

She walked about a mile with that.

*That is the one.*

---

They put the fire in at about seven and he did the ground, which took him
forty minutes and which he did not need to do, because they were eleven
miles from Kadono on an open road with no reason for anybody to be
anywhere.

*He is doing five hours of work in a place that does not want it.*

*I have watched him do that twice. He did it on the eleventh of the first
month and he did it on the sixth of the second, and both times it was after
a thing he could not put in the book.*

She let him finish, because you let him finish.

Then he sat down and got the book out and did it, and it took nine minutes,
and she counted the pages he turned.

---

**What he wrote is on the page and she read it four days later when he left
the book open, which by then he did.**

> *24/4. Kadono. Not retained. Paid to 9th, 400. No prejudice.*
>
> *The sum is right. Checked what I could. 4 contacts is right, they are
> one under. The rest I would take off a man who was right about the
> others.*
>
> *It costs more to keep me than I make.*

And then a line, and under the line the last entry of the fourth month, and
it has no mark beside it because it is not a made number, it is a
description, and he had nowhere else to put it.

> *No boards.*

---

She did not say anything on the twenty-fourth.

She had it by about the second mile out of Kadono — she had had it since
the twenty-first of the third month, and it had gone live at about half
past ten that morning, and she knew that inside the hour — and she carried
it for six days.

Which is not like her at all.

She went at *why not* the way she goes at everything, on the road, over six
days, and got four answers and threw out three of them.

*Because he is not going to want it today.* — **True and not a reason. He
has never once wanted anything on the day.**

*Because it will look like I am pleased.* — **True and it is about me and
that is not a reason either.**

*Because I got it off his own working and he might mind.* — **He will not
mind. He has never minded a correct thing.**

And the fourth one, on the twenty-eighth, at about the ninth mile, which is
the real one and which she did not like.

*Because for a hundred and forty days there has been somebody above him,
and the whole of what I have got only works if there is nobody, and I have
been waiting for that.*

She walked with the mule for a bit.

*I have been waiting for the thing that happened this morning.*

*I did not want it. I would not have arranged it. But I have had a finding
in a drawer for a month that only works in a world where somebody takes his
boards off him, and this morning somebody did, and the first thing I felt
was not sorry.*

**She made herself put that down properly rather than walk away from it,
and it took her the whole of the twenty-ninth, and what she came out with
is the coldest true thing she had about herself at eleven.**

*It is not that I wanted this to happen to him.*

*It is that I had already worked out what to do with it, and having a use
for a thing before it happens is close enough to wanting it that I am not
going to argue the difference on a road.*

---

She said it on the thirtieth.

They were four days out and the money had gone from nine thousand two
hundred and eighty-nine to nine thousand five hundred and forty-one,
because four hundred had come in and a hundred and forty-eight had gone
out, and she knew both figures because he said them out loud now when he
did the book, which he had started doing at some point she could not date
and which she had never asked about.

He shut the book.

She waited until he shut the book. She had learned that in the first week.

"You said you don't set the fee."

He looked up.

"I did."

"You said it's on the docket."

"It is."

"There isn't a docket."

---

**He did not say anything for about four seconds and she let him have all
four.**

"No," he said. "There isn't."

"So the thing I found in the third month. About the buyer. You couldn't use
it because somebody upstairs put a number on a sheet and handed it to you."

"That's right."

"There's nobody upstairs."

---

He sat with the book shut on his knee and the fire between them and the boy
on the other side of it with his hands in front of him, and she watched
him do it, and it took him about eleven seconds, which is a very long time
for him.

And what he said at the end of the eleven seconds was not what she had
prepared for and was, she decided later, the most honest thing he had ever
said to her.

"I don't know how to ask for money."

---

"What?"

"I have never asked anybody for a number in my life." He turned the book
over in his hands. "Fifteen months. The number comes on a piece of paper.
I read the paper and I do the work and they hand me the number and I write
it down. I have never once said a figure out loud to a person who was going
to pay it."

He looked at the fire.

"I have watched you do it. In a yard, in the second month, with a mule. You
took twenty-three hundred off a man in about eleven minutes and I stood
four feet away and I could not have done one part of it."

---

She sat very still.

*He has been carrying that since the sixth of the second month.*

*He has had it for eighty-four days and he has never once said it and he
has said it now, on a road, four days after the worst thing that has ever
happened to him, because I asked him a question about a docket.*

She did not say any of that, which for her is extraordinary.

What she said was:

"Right."

And then, because she says everything, and because it was true, and because
it was the only thing in that entire month she was completely certain
about:

"Well. That's a thing I can do."

---

**He did not answer.**

He put the book inside his coat, and got up, and went and did the mule,
which did not need doing and which he had done at seven.

And she sat by the fire with the boy on the other side of it, and after a
while the boy said — flatly, to nobody, in the way he said the four or five
things a day he said —

"He does that."

"Does what?"

"Goes and does a thing that is finished."

She looked at him across the fire.

"How many times?"

"Nine," said Kimimaro. "Since the ninth."

---

She did not say anything else that night.

She lay down with her hand flat on her hip, which she did every night and
did not notice doing, and did the arithmetic she had been doing since she
was nine, and it came out at one thousand four hundred and six.

*And there is nobody above him.*

*And he cannot ask for money.*

*And I can.*

She lay in the dark on an open road four days from a town they had been
thrown out of, with eleven thousand ryō of nothing coming in and three
mouths and no employer and no boards and no docket and no name to work
under.

**It is the best night's sleep she has had since the seventeenth of the
twelfth month.**
