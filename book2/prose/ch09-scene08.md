# Chapter 9, Scene 8 — The Left-Hander

*Act 1, month 9, the eighteenth and nineteenth. Ōtoma. Tadayoshi Ruri,
age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,780 words.*

---

Hōki looked at the hand for about eleven seconds and did not touch it.

"Two," he said. "The one behind the middle finger and the one behind the
one next to it. And the second one's the bad one, it's gone across."

"How long?"

"Six weeks in a splint and then six weeks of it being no good to you and
about a year before it stops telling you when it's going to rain." He got
up. "Sit there."

He came back with two flat bits of lath and a strip of something and did
it in about four minutes without saying anything else, and he was better
at it than Anzai had been with Uzuki's — which Tadayoshi did not know and
would never know — and he tied it off and put the boy's arm across his
chest and stood up.

"Right," he said. "How long was she at it?"

"About four minutes. Four and a bit."

Hōki stood there.

"Then there's four minutes of you on a sheet in Kiri by the end of the
month," he said, "and it'll be right, because she was there."

---

*It'll be right.*

He sat on the bench outside with his arm across his chest and had that
for about an hour, and it was the worst thing anybody had said to him in
the whole of the ninth month, including the thing about the hand.

*Everything anybody has ever written down about me has been wrong.*

*Five of theirs. Or six. In a dry bed the far side of Sakabe. A grown man.
A big fellow. A Kiri man gone off. Four days old in a market at Nezu and
not one version of it had a boy in it.*

*And I stood in that market on the twelfth of the fifth month and I said
it was the best thing that could have happened to me and I could not
manage to be glad of it, and I said it was going to last exactly as long
as it takes somebody with a rule to finish measuring that ground.*

He looked at the splint.

*It was four months.*

*Not somebody with a rule. Somebody who stood on it.*

**There is not going to be a wrong description any more.**

---

He wrote it on the nineteenth because he could not do it on the eighteenth.

He sat with the ruled sheet on his knees and the pen in his left hand and
looked at the page for a long time before he put anything on it, because he
had never once in his life written a word with that hand and he knew,
before he started, that it was going to be bad and that he was going to
mind.

It was worse than bad.

The first character took him about eleven seconds and came out backhanded
and about a third too big, with the down-strokes going the wrong way at
the foot because a left hand pushes where a right hand pulls. The line
crawled up the page. He ruled it and the rule wandered. He got about four
words in and had to stop and shake the hand out because it had cramped
across the back.

He did the whole entry in about forty minutes and it took up two and a
half lines' worth of space in a five-column ruling that had been made for
one.

Then he looked at it.

---

And he had the strangest four seconds of his life to that point, which he
recognised at the time and could not do anything about.

*I don't know that hand.*

He sat with the sheet on his knees.

*I have read about nine hundred hands in my life and I can do most of them
in one look, and I am looking at a page I made forty minutes ago and it is
a stranger's.*

*It's a slow hand. It's a slow hand and it's been going carefully and it
has still come out wrong, so it isn't carelessness, it's that whoever did
it can't. There's no habit in it anywhere. There's no letter he's better
at than the others.*

*And there's a place at the end of the second line where he's given up
trying to make it good and just got it down, and it's the only bit that's
legible.*

*That's a man who has never done this before and is not going to get
better at it and knows it.*

He put his thumb on the edge of the sheet.

*That's me.*

*That is a true reading and it is completely accurate and I got it in
about a second and a half off my own page, and it does not tell me one
single thing I did not already know, and I have never been so unhappy
about being right.*

---

The entry said:

> *17/9. Aku bank, 2 days E of Ōtoma. About 4 pm.*
>
> *Came out of the scrub on the W side. Left-handed and taught that way,
> not swapped — the feet are wrong for a swap. About 28 to 30. No mask,
> and I do not know what that means.*
>
> *Went for my hands at 2:00, 2:40 and about 3:10. Had it right by the
> second one — moved where it came from after one go. Nobody has told her
> this. It is not on any sheet because there has not been a second time
> with anybody.*
>
> *Did not take the open end.*
>
> *Broke off at about 4:00 with the exchange going her way, on purpose,
> not hurt, and went N, which was neither the way she came in nor the
> short way off that bank.*
>
> *Stopped at 40 yds and looked at the ground I opened at 1:40. About 3
> seconds. Did not look at me.*

And then, in the last column, in the worst four words on the page:

> ***What it cost: the right hand, about six weeks.***

---

The first column would not take a blank twice.

He had left it blank on the third of the seventh month because there had
been nothing at all to put in it — a question through a floor and a man he
never saw — and that had been correct, and it had been correct because
there had been no way on earth to distinguish that man from any other man.

This was different and he knew it was different and he sat with the pen in
the wrong hand for about three minutes over an empty box.

*I have got a person.*

*I have got a person and I have got about eleven things about her and I
have got nowhere to put any of them, because a file is a thing you look
back at, and in a year I will have this line and the line about Hanase and
maybe four more, and I am going to have to be able to say which one is
which.*

*And I have not got a name.*

*I am not going to get a name. Nobody in this trade has a name. Kajiya has
not got a name and Hōki has not got a name and the man who wrote my
dockets for four months is called the inkstand, and the boy at the ford
had the top of his ear off and that is the whole of what I will ever have
of him.*

*So it has to be a thing off the sheet.*

He looked at what he had written.

*It has to be the first thing I had.*

Because it had been the first thing he had. It had been the first thing he
had before he had her age or her distance or her direction; it had gone in
about a second and a half, out of the scrub, at forty yards, because it was
the thing his eye did and always had.

He wrote it in the first column with his left hand and it took him about
nine seconds and it came out crooked and legible.

> ***The left-hander.***

---

He sat with the sheet on his knees for a while after that with the light
going.

*Two lines.*

*One of them has nobody in it and the other one has got a person, and now
there is a name in the book that I made up, and it is going to be in there
for as long as the book is.*

He read it again.

*And she is coming back.*

That one he had had on the bank on the seventeenth and had put down whole
because he could not lift it, and he lifted it now, on a bench at Ōtoma
with a splint on, and it came apart in the way things did when he finally
made himself do them.

*She broke off. Which means she chose to still be alive on the eighteenth.
And a person who chooses that, having spent four minutes on a job, is a
person who is going to be sent back to it, because she is the only one in
the world who has been.*

*So the next one is her.*

*And she will know four minutes' worth more than she did, and the one
after that she will know four minutes plus whatever the next four gets
her, and it will go on doing that, and it will go on doing that for as
long as I am any good.*

*And every single thing she knows, she is going to have got off me.*

He looked at the crooked line in the first column.

**I am the syllabus.**

---

He wrote that down too, because he had a rule about it, and it went on the
back of the sheet because the columns were full, and it was the last thing
he wrote left-handed for six weeks.

Then he put the ruled sheet inside his shirt on the left side, where it
went, and the ledger on the right, where it went, and sat on the bench
outside Hōki's with his arm across his chest and could not do a single
thing with either hand, and there was nothing to do until it was dark, and
so he sat there and did nothing, which was the first time in five months.

He thought, at some point, about the two seconds she had spent looking at
him before she went, and about what a person is deciding in two seconds.

He did not get anywhere with it.

He went in when it got cold.
