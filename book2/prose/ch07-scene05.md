# Chapter 7, Scene 5 — The Ticket

*Act 1, month 6, the twelfth. The back room of the Kiribata, Fukae.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,650 words.*

---

The step was not where he thought it was.

It was four inches lower and about a foot further along than the light
made it look, and he went down it wrong and put a hand on the frame and
did not fall, and behind him Ōtsuka said, "Everyone does that," in a voice
with nothing in it at all.

*He said it to warn me and then he said it again to make the stumble not
a thing.*

*Two goes at the same problem and neither of them cost him anything.*

---

The back room had a table in it and a stove that was in and three chairs
and, along the whole of one wall, shelves with paper on them.

Not books. Paper: sheets, in bundles, tied, and hung on nails in fans of
about a dozen, and some of it very old and brown at the edges. It was the
most paper Tadayoshi had seen in one place since the harbour office at
Sunaba and it was arranged by somebody who knew what they were doing,
because there was a system in it and he could see the shape of the system
from the door without being able to read one word of it.

*By month. No — by month and then by something else within the month, and
the something else is not alphabetical because that top one starts wrong.*

*By where they go.*

The parcel was on the table. Ōtsuka went past it without looking at it,
sat down, pulled a sheet towards him, and started writing.

---

Tadayoshi stood by the stove because he had been put there and watched
the man write, because there was nothing else to do and because watching
people work was, at that stage of his life, about the only entertainment
he had.

It took him about eleven seconds.

*Oh.*

He did not move.

*That's the hand.*

He watched a bit longer, to be sure, because being sure was the entire
business and he was not going to be pleased about a thing he had not
checked.

*The whole of it slopes. About a degree and a half, and it does not vary,
which is the part almost nobody manages, because people get tired at the
bottom of a page and go upright.*

*He rules off at the left before he writes anything, with the edge of the
sheet under his hand, no rule.*

*And — *

He waited for it. It came at the end of the second line.

*There.*

*The twelfth. And the foot comes back under itself.*

He got the docket out of his shirt and held it down by his side and
turned it over and looked at it without lifting it, and it was the same
sheet twice.

*He wrote my instructions.*

*Kajiya gave them to me in a yard at Sakabe and I have been carrying them
for ten days and I thought they were his, and they are not, and I asked
whose they were and he said "that's the ticket's" and I did not know that
that was a person.*

*So there is a man who writes and a man who hands over and a boy who
walks.*

*Three. Not two.*

He put the sheet away.

*Which means the man in the yard did not decide where I went either.*

---

"Sit down," said Ōtsuka, still writing. "You're making the room look
untidy."

He sat down.

"You want to ask me something."

"Yes."

"Ask it then."

Tadayoshi had had it ready since the step and he put it out plainly,
because plainly was what he had.

"How did you do that in the front room?"

Ōtsuka finished the line he was on, and put the pen in the tray, and
looked at the sheet for a second, and then reached over and got a cup
off the shelf without answering.

"She's all right, that one," he said. "Kiribata. It's the husband that's
the problem, but he's not in it much and he's not in it at all before
about four, so morning's fine and afternoon isn't. Bear it in mind."

*That is not the answer.*

*That is not the answer and it is an extremely good piece of
information.*

*And he has done it again. He has done it to me, in a room, with two
people in it, and I was looking straight at him and I still cannot see
where it happened.*

He got a cup of something hot put in front of him. It was mostly barley
and it had been on the stove too long and it was, at that particular
moment, one of the best things that had ever happened to him.

"Thank you," said Tadayoshi.

"Mm," said Ōtsuka, and went back to writing.

---

He drank the barley thing and did not say anything for a while and
neither did the man, and it was, he noticed, the first time in about
seven weeks that he had sat in a room with an adult in it and not been
working out what the adult wanted.

*He hasn't asked me a single question.*

*Not one. Not where I've come from, not whose I am, not what I can do,
not why a boy of ten is on that route. He asked me what the weather was
and that was a password and he has not asked me anything since.*

*Kajiya did the same. Kajiya said don't offer me your name.*

*So it is a rule and not a kindness.*

He turned that over.

*It is a rule.*

*And it is a kindness, and both of those are true, and I do not have to
pick.*

---

"Right," said Ōtsuka. He put the pen down properly this time and got a
tin out from under the table. "Ten days."

He counted it out on the table in front of him, out loud, which Tadayoshi
understood about a year later had also been for his benefit.

Two and a half.

*Two and a half.*

*That's ten days at a quarter, which is what he said in the yard, and
what he said in the yard was that a quarter is a man's day.*

*So I have been paid ten men's days for nine days of walking on a road I
would have walked anyway.*

*And that is either a great deal or it is nothing, and I still cannot
tell, and I have got no way of finding out.*

He put it in his shirt, and the flat weight of it against the ledger was
a completely new sensation and he did not like it as much as he had
expected to.

"You've not spent much," said Ōtsuka.

"No."

"Don't get clever about that. Sleeping in ditches is cheap until it costs
you a week." He said it entirely without emphasis, the way he had said
the thing about the step. "Buy a coat."

---

Then he pushed a sheet across the table.

Four lines, ruled off at the left, and the same degree and a half.

> *2nd of the 7th. Hanase. The yard behind the smithy, after dark.*
> *Ask for Torii.*
> *You will be offered a bed.*
> *No return required.*

Tadayoshi read it twice.

"There's a line in this that isn't an instruction."

Ōtsuka looked up.

He looked at him for about two seconds, and something in his face did a
thing that Tadayoshi could not read at all and remembered exactly.

"Read it out."

"*You will be offered a bed.*"

"That's right," said Ōtsuka. "You will be. Don't take it."

And he picked the pen back up.

---

Tadayoshi sat with the sheet in his hand.

*He is not going to say why.*

*He has put a thing on a docket that is not a place or a time or a name,
which means he thought about whether to put it on there, which means
there was a version of that sheet where it is not on there.*

*And he put it on.*

*In writing. Which he has just told me, without telling me, that he does
not normally do, because everything else on this sheet is where and when
and who and there is not one other word.*

He looked at the line.

*So it is important enough to break his own hand for and not important
enough to explain.*

*Or.*

He got the second one about four seconds later and it went through him
coldly and he did not have anywhere to put it.

*Or it is not that he will not explain it. It is that he does not want it
said out loud in a room, and a thing you will not say out loud in a room
with two people in it is not a thing about money.*

He folded the sheet.

"All right," he said.

"Say it back."

"I'll be offered a bed and I won't take it."

"Good."

---

He asked the other thing at the door, because it had been in his boot for
ten days and he was not going to walk out with it still in there.

"What does *no return required* mean?"

"It means I'm not expecting anything back off him," said Ōtsuka, without
turning round. "Half of them think they can send me a note with a boy.
They can't. It's on all of them."

"It's on all of them."

"Every one I've written for eleven years. It saves an argument." He
turned a sheet over. "Why?"

"It's the only line on there that's written like a form."

Ōtsuka laughed. It was a short laugh and it was genuine and it was
directed entirely at himself.

"That's because I was a clerk," he said. "Fourteen years, and you never
get it out of your hands. Go on, get out, you're four days from Hanase
and you look like something off a beach."
