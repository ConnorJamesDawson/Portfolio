# Chapter 14, Scene 1 — Two And A Load

*Act 2, year 1, month 2, the third to the fifth. Ōtoma. Tadayoshi Ruri,
age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,700 words.*

---

The docket came on the third of the second month and it was the first
thing on the boards in eighteen days, and it was freight.

> *Ōtoma to the Kanaya. 4 crates + 1 roll.*
>
> *18 days. 1,400.*

He read it in the yard with Ōtsuka four feet away and did not say anything
about it, and went up the ladder and sat down and did the thing he had
been trying not to do for a month, which was to put it in the line.

*Fourteen hundred.*

*Nine hundred and eleven hundred were the two contracts in the first
month. So this is more than either of them.*

He sat with that for a while.

*A courier run is more than a contract.*

*And I have got eleven words for that and none of them are the true one,
and the true one is a sentence I am not going to write down.*

He did not write it down.

---

Then he did the other sum, and the other sum did not close, and it took
him about four minutes to find out how badly.

*Four crates and a roll. Call it two hundred and thirty pounds, and I will
know on the day.*

*Eighteen days, nine each way, in the second month.*

*Me at fifty-one for nine days uphill in the twelfth was fine and I have
got the numbers for it.*

*Two hundred and thirty is not fine. Two hundred and thirty is three
loads, and three loads is twenty-seven days of walking and the docket says
eighteen.*

He put the pencil down.

*And there are two of us.*

---

That was the part that had been coming for a fortnight and had not
arrived until it was on a piece of paper.

He went at it honestly.

*She can carry. She carried four ranks of wood under an eave in the tenth
month and she has told me about it twice and both times she was being
careful not to sound pleased.*

*Call her forty pounds over nine days, which is generous, and she will do
it and will not say anything and I will find out about it on the sixth day
when something goes.*

*Fifty-one and forty is ninety-one. Two thirty over ninety-one is two and
a half trips.*

*So it is still no.*

He sat on the boards with the docket in his hand.

*It has stopped being one person's job because there are two of us, and
two of us makes it harder and not easier, and that is not a thing I
expected and I should have.*

---

He got to the answer on the fourth in about ninety seconds, which was
fast, and then spent the rest of the day not liking it.

*Buy an animal.*

He went round it four ways.

*A horse does eleven miles an hour on a road and thirty in a day loaded
and I would have the whole of this run done in six days each way.*

*And a horse eats about a fifth of its own value in a year, and it wants a
road, and it is useless the moment I leave one, and I leave roads.*

*And there is one boy of eleven and one girl of eleven and a horse in this
country, and everybody who sees it will describe it, and they will
describe it accurately, because a horse is the easiest thing in the world
to describe.*

He crossed it out and did not put a case under it because the case was
obvious.

*A mule does not do any of that. A mule does about what I do and carries
eight times what I carry and eats grass and goes up a track a horse turns
round on.*

*And nobody has ever described a mule to anybody.*

---

He costed it that evening and did it properly, in the ordinary column,
with a rule under it.

> *Mule, sound, young — ask 6,000-7,000. Kuriya's man got a fourteen-year-
> old at Nakadō in the 6th mo. and that was a fourteen-year-old.*
>
> *Feed on the road: negligible in the 5th mo., call it 25 a day in the
> 2nd. 18 days = 450.*
>
> *Headcollar, rope, a bag. 300.*
>
> *Against: 1,400 on this one and it does not close without one.*

He looked at the bottom line for a while.

*It does not pay for itself on this run and it does not pay for itself on
the next four.*

*And I am not buying it for this run.*

That was true and it was the first entirely true thing he had thought
about the mule, and he did not follow it any further, because following it
would have meant asking what he was buying it for, and the answer to that
was standing about six feet away going through a pack for the third time.

---

She asked on the fourth in the evening and he said yes in about two
seconds.

"Am I coming?"

"Yes."

She looked at him.

"That's it?"

"Yes."

*Because the other thing is a room in an inn at Kurihama with a girl in it
who sat by the door in the dark for six hours with a knife on her knees,
and I have written that down and there is no solution under it, and
eighteen days is nine times two days.*

He did not say any of that either.

---

He did say one thing, on the morning of the fifth, going out, and he said
it because it was operational and he said everything that was operational.

"You'll carry about forty. That's the top of it. If it's forty-five you'll
do it and you won't say, and then on about the sixth day something goes
and I've lost four days."

"All right."

"So when it's too much, say."

She said, "All right," in the flat way, and he had been travelling with
her for fifty days by then and had learned exactly nothing about what her
*all right* meant, which was not a thing he had noticed yet.

---

They went out of Ōtoma on the fifth at about six in the morning and did
eleven miles, and the pack he had made up for her was thirty-eight pounds
because he had weighed it twice and taken two things out.

He did not tell her that.

At about four in the afternoon she was walking about a foot behind him on
the left, which she had done since the twentieth of the first month
without either of them arranging it, and she said, "How much is a mule?"

"Six. Maybe seven."

"Thousand?"

"Yes."

She did not say anything for about a quarter of a mile.

Then she said, "That's a lot," and he said, "It is," and that was the
whole of that conversation, and he thought no more about it at all, and
she thought about it for eleven miles and most of the next day.
