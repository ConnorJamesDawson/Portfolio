# Chapter 13, Scene 8 — Nine Hundred And Three, And Nothing To Count With

*Act 2. A new room. A boy, age thirteen. POV: the boy. ~1,750 words.*

---

He held it as an anomaly for nine days and then had to give it up.

The bowls came. That was never the problem and he was careful about that,
because a person who is hungry will tell himself the food has stopped when
it has only moved.

*It has not stopped.*

*It has come every day since the road and it is the bigger amount every
time, which I checked with the third piece against the crack in the glaze
for the first four days until they took the bowl away between times and I
could not.*

*So the amount is holding.*

*And the times are gone and they have not come back and it is nine.*

---

He worked out what had happened to him at about the ninth day and it took
him under a minute once he stopped trying to make it be temporary.

*In the room the bowl came at the same three times because there was one
man and one room and one day, and the man had a round to do and I was on
it.*

*Here there are a great many people and I am not on a round. I am a thing
somebody remembers.*

He sat with that.

*And a thing somebody remembers is not a clock.*

---

Which meant the number was still there and could not be spent.

He got that out and looked at it, because it was the largest object he
owned.

> *Nine hundred and three.*

*Seven more times, at a half each time. A hundred and twenty-nine bowls
apiece, which is the wrong figure and is the only one I have got.*

*And ninety-five of the fifth one done, and thirty-four to go.*

*And I do not know how many days ninety-five bowls is any more, and I do
not know whether the thirty-four is thirty-four of anything.*

He put his hand flat on the floor.

*So I have got a number and it is in a unit that has stopped existing.*

**He did not despair about it, and that is not a virtue and should not be
read as one.** *Despair requires a picture of a future that is being taken
away from you, and he had one number and no calendar and had never in his
life been shown what a year was.* **What he had instead was the specific
flat unpleasantness of an instrument that has broken in the hand**, which
he had felt exactly once before, in the sixth month, and had no word for
then either.

---

He tried three things and he tried them properly and all three failed, and
he wrote none of it down because he had never had anything to write with.

**The light.**

In the room there had been a strip of it under the door on the side where
the steps were, and it had moved, and it had been good enough for roughly
where in the morning he was.

Here there was a gap high up on one wall and it did the same thing, and he
watched it for four days and then understood the problem, and the problem
was not the light.

*It comes and it goes and it moves the same way every time.*

*But it only tells me about the inside of one day. It does not tell me
where one day stops and the next one starts, because I do not always sleep
between them and sometimes I sleep twice.*

*In the room the bowls did that. Three bowls was a day. The light was
where in it.*

*The light was never the clock. The light was the hour hand and I have
lost the other one.*

**The noise.**

There were people over him and to the north of him and he had four days of
being interested in that before it went the way of the light.

*They are not regular.*

*They are a lot of people doing a lot of different things and some of them
are the same every day and some of them are not, and I cannot tell which
is which without a clock, and the reason I am counting them is to make a
clock.*

He sat with that one for a while because he liked the shape of it and had
never met one before.

*That is a thing that eats itself.*

**And himself.**

The third one was the worst and it took him the longest and he had known
it was going to fail before he started, and started anyway, because the
list had three things on it and he had done two.

*I could count sleeps.*

*And I cannot, because I have never once known how long I have been
asleep, and the whole of the reason a person knows that is that something
happened while they were out and they can see that it happened.*

*You cannot measure a thing from inside it.*

---

So on about the fourteenth day he did the only thing left, which was to
measure the room.

He did it because it was the one instrument he still had. He had thumbs
and he had paces and he had, since the yard, a thing he had made himself
which was a yard, and none of them were time, and a room is not time
either, and he did it anyway.

It took him two days and he did it four times over.

*Eleven paces by seven. The long wall is stone to about my chest and then
it is not stone, and I do not know what it is.*

*The floor is not level. It goes down about a thumb across the width of it
toward the north corner and there is a drain there, or a hole where a
drain was.*

*The door is one and a half paces from the east corner and it opens
inward and the hinge is on the side away from the drain.*

*There is a gap high on the west wall and it is about four thumbs by
eleven and I cannot reach it and I could if I stood on something and there
is nothing.*

*The ceiling is above my hand and about a foot above it, and I got that by
throwing a piece of the floor at it and listening, which took nine goes.*

He finished on the sixteenth day and had a room in his head, complete, to
about a thumb, and had absolutely no use for any of it.

*I have got the exact size of a room and I do not know what day it is.*

And he had, for about four minutes, the closest thing to being funny that
had ever happened inside his head, and did not recognise it, and it went.

---

The other thing was that somebody spoke to him.

It happened on the day he finished the room, in the passage, being taken
somewhere, and it was a man he had not seen before who was going the other
way with something under his arm, and the man slowed down as he passed and
said, without stopping:

"Cold enough for you?"

---

He worked on it for four bowls.

*That is a question.*

*It has got the shape of a question. It went up at the end.*

*So I should have answered it and I did not answer it, because by the time
I had it he was past me, and the next time somebody does that I am going
to be ready.*

He got ready.

He spent the next two days building the answer, because it was a question
about a thing he could measure and measuring was the one thing he was
good at, and by the end of the second day he had it and he was, in his own
flat way, pleased with it.

*It is colder than the room was. It is colder at the north end where the
drain is by about — I do not have a unit for cold. I have got: I can see
my breath in the morning and I could not in the room, and my hands are
worse in the morning than they were and better by the middle, and the
fifth piece has been coming at about the same rate and cold does not seem
to be in it.*

*That is the answer. That is a good answer, and it is true, and I have
checked it.*

Nobody asked him again.

He carried the answer for eleven days and then, at some point, stopped
carrying it, and it did not go anywhere and did not become anything, and
it was the first thing he had ever prepared to say to another human being.

---

He lay down on his side facing the wall on the night he finished the room,
in the dark, in a place eleven paces by seven with a drain in the north
corner, and put the four pieces in a row on the floor with the bases lined
up by feel, which he could do without seeing because he had done it about
two hundred times.

The straight one went at the end.

He felt along the back of his left hand for the fifth.

*It is coming.*

*And I do not know how much of it is done.*

He put his hand down.

*Nine hundred and three.*

*In bowls.*

He held it, because it was the only thing he had that anybody had ever
worked for, and he was not going to let go of it just because it had
stopped meaning anything.
