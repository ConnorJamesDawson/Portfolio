# Chapter 8, Scene 10 — Putting Him Back

*Act 1, month 8, the twenty-sixth. Nakadō. Nozaki Uzuki, age ten. POV:
Uzuki. ~1,720 words.*

---

It was nine and there was nothing behind her but a wall.

She came out of her own room at about half six in the morning with her
hair not done and her feet bare and her mother already at the range, and
he was at the table with the tally sticks and a cup, and he looked up.

And went on looking.

*One.*

*Two.*

She went to the shelf and got a bowl down and put it on the board, which
took about four seconds and which she did with her back three-quarters to
the room, and when she turned round again he was still doing it.

*Seven.*

*Eight.*

*Nine.*

Then her mother said something about the water and he said something back
and got up and went out to the shed, and that was the whole of it, and
Uzuki stood at the board with a bowl in front of her.

---

*Check it.*

*Do it properly. Do it exactly the way you did it on the sixteenth, and
do not put your thumb on it.*

*What's behind me.*

She did not turn round. She did not have to; she had lived in that kitchen
for eight weeks and she could have drawn it.

*The door to my room, and it's shut, because I shut it. The corner. And
the wall.*

*The wall is behind me.*

*The line off that table goes through where I was standing and it goes
into a wall about three feet the other side of me and it stops there.*

She stood at the board and made herself finish it.

*On the sixteenth of the seventh month I said: the door is behind me and
he had been staring at a stick with notches in it for an hour and people
go blank. And that was true and it was the whole of my case and I was
pleased with it.*

*And this morning there is a wall.*

*Same test. Same person. Same time of day.*

*And it fails.*

---

She got through the morning.

She did the water and she did the yard and she said the ordinary number of
words to both of them and she went up to Kuriya's in the afternoon and did
twenty-five minutes and it was a good one, which she was almost angry
about, and she came back down and did nine sacks and went to her room and
shut the door.

Then she sat on the floor with her back against the brick and reached for
the list.

*Put him on.*

And there was nothing there.

---

That was the first thing and she had not expected it and it took her a
while to understand what had happened.

*There isn't one.*

*I struck it. I struck it on the sixteenth of the seventh month lying
right here, deliberately, as an act, and I said "there" to myself, and I
went to sleep pleased about it.*

*So put him back on.*

She tried.

She actually tried. She sat on the floor and put the thing together — nine
seconds, no door, a wall, morning, hair not done — and went to file it, and
it would not go, and it took her about a minute of sitting there before
she found out why, and when she found out why she put both hands over her
face.

---

*Because if he goes back on, the sixteenth was wrong.*

*And if the sixteenth was wrong then I have been in this house for six
weeks with somebody I had already got right.*

She sat with her hands over her face.

*I had him. On the sixteenth of the seventh month I had him at six
seconds and I wrote him down and he was on it and it was done, and the
whole of the rest of that night was me taking him off, and I did that. He
didn't do it. Nobody made me. He was out in the shed asking about a
bucket.*

*I sat in a lane on the eleventh and decided I was allowed to like him.*

*I stood in a yard on the eighth while my mother laughed and I did not go
in.*

*I asked him about the fields.*

*I mended his sacks.*

*I have sat four feet from him every evening for six weeks being pleased
with myself for being fair about it, and the whole time —*

She took her hands down.

*No. Stop. That's not arithmetic, that's just being sick at yourself, and
you can do that any time.*

*Do the actual sum.*

---

So she did the list. All of it. From the beginning.

She had never once written any of it down and had never needed to, and it
came out of her in order, in about four minutes, the way it had gone in.

*The man at Ōgi market when I was seven, who was in the fish and who
looked at me for about six and who I did not have a number for then
because I did not have numbers then, and I have made that one six
afterwards, so it is a made number.*

*The one on the Ōgi road with the cart. Twice.*

*Tazu. Four. And Tazu is the worst there is and Tazu is four, and Tazu
came to this house eleven times when Dad was ill and I was never once on
my own with him and my mother made sure of that and never said one word
about it, and I did not work that out until the fourth month.*

*The one at the shrine at the harvest thing. Three.*

*The two men on the north road in the wood on the twenty-seventh of the
third month, and one of them looked at me and went on looking, and that
was two hours, and it was not the same thing, and it goes on anyway
because it goes on.*

*The one at Kayano with the dog.*

*Ine's brother. Two. Twice. And I have never done anything about it and I
never will, because two is two.*

*The tanner's man.*

*Him.*

Eleven. There were eleven on it in three years and she could produce every
one of them and where and how long and what she had been doing.

And then she found the thing that was actually wrong and it went into her
like cold water.

---

*I have never been wrong.*

She sat very still on the floor of her own room.

*Not once. Not in three years and eleven people.*

*Nothing ever happened. That's not the same as being right — nothing
happening isn't proof — but I have never once put somebody on and then
found out afterwards that I had it wrong about them, because there has
never been an afterwards, because I never go near any of them and my
mother never let me and my father never let me and that was the whole of
it.*

*The list has never been tested. Not once, in three years.*

*And the only time I ever took anybody off it —*

*— was the only one who lives in this house.*

---

She sat on the floor for a long time after that.

*Right.*

*Then put him back on.*

*Put him back on and be wrong about the sixteenth and take it. Sit here
and be a girl who got it right and then talked herself out of it, because
that is what happened, and it is better to be that than to be a girl who
is still doing it.*

She reached for it.

And she could not.

She sat there with her back on the brick and could not make it happen, and
she was completely clear-eyed about what was stopping her, and it was not
that she doubted the nine seconds.

*It's that the list is the one thing I've got that's never been wrong.*

*It's the only thing in me that has worked, every time, since I was seven,
and it has worked because I have never argued with it, and the one time I
argued with it I was wrong, and if I put him back on now then that is what
the list is: a thing I argue with.*

*And then it's not a list. It's an opinion.*

She put her head back.

*So I'm not putting him on it.*

*Not because he isn't. Because I have already proved I'll take him off
again if the barley turns out well, and I would rather have nothing than
have a thing I know I'll do that to.*

---

She got the bag out and looked at the fourth column and did not add
anything to it, because nothing had happened on the twenty-sixth.

*Six marks.*

*Nine, five, three, four, twelve.*

*And the twelve was me and I stopped.*

She turned it over and looked at the back, where the eleven questions were,
and the nine no's, and the *market?*, and the three gaps in a column in her
own hand.

*And there's nowhere on this to put a man looking at me for nine seconds
in a kitchen with a wall behind me.*

*There is no column for it.*

*The ninety's me and the money's me and the days is me and the marks are
her.*

*And there is still not one single thing on this bag about him, because on
the eighth I said I'd start and I didn't, I kept it in my head like the
list, and now I haven't got a list.*

She sat with the bag on her knees in the dark.

*So it has got to go somewhere.*

*It has to. A thing you know and can't write down and can't put on a list
is a thing that sits in you all night, and I have had two months of that
and I cannot do another one.*

*It has to go somewhere I can look at it.*

She folded the bag and put it behind the brick and did not go to bed.

She sat on the floor of her own room with her back against the wall for
about half an hour, doing nothing at all, and then she got up and went out
to the kitchen.
