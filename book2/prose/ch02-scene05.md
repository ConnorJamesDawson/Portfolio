# Chapter 2, Scene 5 — One Declared

*Act 1, month 3. Kirigakure, the returns office, fourth floor. Sakuma,
records. POV: Sakuma. ~1,620 words.*

---

The quarter closed on the twenty-eighth and Sakuma had four hundred and
nine files cleared out of four hundred and thirty-one, which was ahead,
and which he knew to the file because he kept the number on a slip in the
front of the ledger and moved it every evening at about six.

Four hundred and ten went at twenty past nine.

Four hundred and eleven did not.

---

It was a household return, which was the dullest category in the office
and the one he preferred, because household returns were arithmetic and
arithmetic either sat down or it did not.

The form had four columns and he had ruled a fifth on the right-hand side
himself, narrow, for findings, because the printed form did not have one
and a form without somewhere to put a finding was a form that made you
write on the back.

*RURI. Shioiri, inlet, second district.*
*Registered household. Bloodline: crystal.*
*Declared practitioners: one.*

He put his thumb on that and went down the column.

*One. One. One. One. One. One. One. One. One. One. One.*

Eleven years of it, in three different hands, going back to the year the
registers were re-ruled.

---

The output side came in from the buyers.

That was the part of the system Sakuma was quietly proud of, and he had
had the argument about it twice and won it both times: you did not ask a
household what it produced, because a household would tell you what it
thought you wanted to hear. You asked the *merchant*. Bloodline-sourced
stock was a controlled category, and every house that dealt in it made a
monthly return of what it had taken in and from whom, and those returns
came up the coast and landed on the fourth floor in bundles, and a man
with a ledger could put a household's declared heads against a merchant's
declared weight and find out whether the two of them agreed.

Kanaya, Sunaba, with a counter at Ōura.

Eleven years of Ruri, monthly, in a clerk's small even hand.

He ran a finger down it.

*Twenty-two. Twenty-six. Twenty-four. Twenty-one — that is a bad winter,
they all have a bad winter — twenty-five. Twenty-three.*

Then, about two years back:

*Thirty-one. Thirty-four. Thirty-eight. Thirty-six. Forty. Thirty-nine.*

And then it settled at about four dozen and stayed there, month on month,
for twenty-one months.

*Forty-nine last month. Fifty-one the month before.*

And this month's, on top of the bundle, still smelling of the road:

*Fifty-one, and the figure is fifteen where fifteen is two and a half
above the eleven-year average for that count.*

---

Sakuma sat back.

*Output up by two thirds against an unchanged declared head count.*

*Threshold on a household return is one third.*

He got the standing order down off the shelf and looked it up, because he
always looked it up, because the one time you did not look it up was the
time somebody senior asked you which paragraph and you had to say you had
been going from memory.

Paragraph nine. A third. He was correct.

*So it is over threshold and it has been over threshold for — *

He went back up the column with the edge of a card.

*Twenty-one months.*

*This has been over threshold for twenty-one months and nobody has pulled
it, because nobody looks at a household return unless the quarter is
short or somebody is being thorough.*

He wrote the date in the margin and underlined it once.

---

There were four honest explanations and he wrote all four in the narrow
column and did them in order, because doing them in order was the job.

*One. The merchant is over-declaring.* No. A merchant over-declares to
inflate what he can sell on and under-declares to reduce what he is taxed
on, and this was a controlled category and the tax ran on the buying
side. Kanaya had every reason to write down less. **Struck.**

*Two. A change in the material.* Possible. A better sand, a new bed, a
seam. He noted it and left it open, because he had no way of testing it
from a fourth-floor office and a man who closed a possibility he could
not test was a man who was going to be wrong eventually. **Open.**

*Three. The declared practitioner has improved.* He looked at the year of
birth on the register. Thirty-four. Twenty-two years in the trade.

He wrote: *A woman does not improve by two thirds in her twentieth
year.* **Struck.**

*Four.*

---

He did not write four for a moment.

He got up instead and went to the window, which stuck, and looked down
into the yard where somebody was arguing with a cart, and stood there
with his hands in his sleeves for about a minute and a half.

*Four. There are two hands in the work.*

Then he came back and sat down and wrote it, and then he did the thing he
was actually good at, which was to stop having an idea and start proving
it.

The register gave the household. Eight names. He went through them.

*Meno, 71. Practitioner, retired — declared out at fifty-one, hands.
Correct, and it is in the column, and it took a third of the output away
at the time and the figures show it.*

*Hisui, 29. Assessed at eight. Negative.*

*Akagane, 20. Assessed at eight. Negative.*

*Tadaharu, 38. Married in. Not of the line, not assessable.*

*Iwao, 13. Assessed at eight. Negative.*

*Koishi, 6. Under age. Presentation due in the eighth month, two years
from now.*

*And.*

He put the card under the line and read it twice.

*Tadayoshi, 10.*

*Assessed —*

The column was empty.

---

He looked at it for a while.

Then he took the standing order down again and looked up the presentation
requirement, and read it twice, and put the card under that too.

*Every child of a registered bloodline household shall be presented for
assessment in the year of their eighth birthday.*

*He was eight two years ago.*

*Output rises by two thirds beginning approximately two years ago.*

*And nobody has written anything in that column.*

---

Sakuma sat at his desk on the fourth floor with the window that stuck and
felt the particular thing he felt perhaps four or five times a year, which
he had never described to anybody, and which he would not have been able
to describe without sounding like a fool.

A number that would not sit down had sat down.

That was all it was. He had come in at seven with four hundred and ten
files cleared and a quarter closing on the twenty-eighth, and he had
picked up a household return out of the dullest category in the office,
and it had not reconciled, and he had taken it apart in a little under an
hour and it had come out clean.

It was, he thought, quite a good morning's work.

---

He wrote the recommendation on the proper form, which he did not have to
rule a column onto, because recommendations had one printed.

He was careful with it. He put in the twenty-one months and he put in
that nobody had pulled it, because if you did not put that in, somebody
upstairs would find it themselves and wonder why you had not; and he put
in *two* as a finding and not as a certainty, and wrote *undeclared
producer, probable* rather than *undeclared producer*, because he had
proved that there were two hands and he had not proved whose.

And he left explanation two open in the narrow column, in one line, where
it could be found: *material change not excluded, untestable from this
office.*

Then he initialled it and put it in the up-tray and cleared four hundred
and twelve, which was a fishing return and took nine minutes.

---

It came back on the fourth day.

He knew what it was from the colour of the docket before he had it in his
hand, and he opened it at his desk, standing up, with his coat still on,
because it was ten past seven and the stove had not been lit.

There was a signature on it and it was not his.

He did not know whose it was. It was two floors up and it was one of
about six that came down onto that floor, and the handwriting was not a
thing he had ever needed to learn.

There was a date under it.

And there was a schedule attached, and the schedule had a household on it
and a number beside the household, and the number was the one he had put
in the recommendation, because that was where they got the numbers from.

*Approved as recommended,* he read.

*Well,* thought Sakuma. *Good.*

He put it in the down-tray for the runner, and took his coat off, and hung
it on the back of the door, and sat down.

Four hundred and twelve had a query on it from Sunaba about a weight, and
that took the rest of the morning, and by about eleven he had stopped
thinking about it, and by the following week he could not have told
anybody the name of the family without going and looking.

---

He had rice and a piece of the dried fish that came in the office
allocation, at his desk, at half past one, with the ledger squared off to
his left where he could see the slip.

Four hundred and twenty-six.

Five days to the quarter and twenty-two files short and he was going to
finish it three days early for the second year running.

He wiped his hands, and moved the slip.
