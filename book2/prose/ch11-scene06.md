# Chapter 11, Scene 6 — Second Boots

*Act 1, month 10, the twenty-first to month 11, the seventh. Ōtoma,
Shioya and the Mikura road. Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi.
~1,900 words.*

---

The fourteen thousand came up the ladder on the twenty-second in a bag
with a knot in it, and he counted it on the boards, and it took him
eleven minutes, and at the end of the eleven minutes he had a problem he
had never had before in his life.

*Where does it go.*

He sat and looked at it.

*I have had money since the sixth month. I have had it in a tin and the
tin has been under the boards by the wall and the reason that has worked
is that the most that has ever been in it is about four thousand and four
thousand fits in a tin.*

*This does not fit in the tin. This is three times the tin.*

*And a bag of coin under a board makes a noise when you walk on the
board, and I know that, because I have walked on the board.*

It took him two days to solve, and he solved it badly, and he knew he had
solved it badly. He put six thousand in the tin. He put four thousand
inside the frame, in the hollow of the long member, wrapped, which cost
him an hour and made the frame ring wrong when he set it down. And he
carried the rest, which was eight thousand between what was left of the
fourteen and what had been in the tin already, and eight thousand in coin
against your ribs for a fortnight is a thing you feel every time you sit
down.

*This is worse than not having it.*

*That is not true and I am going to write it down anyway, because it is
what I think and the interesting part is why it is not true.*

---

He did Shioya on the twenty-fourth to the twenty-sixth.

Two thousand two hundred. A man with a boat and a share in a second boat
who had told somebody something in the eighth month, and the setting was
twenty minutes because the man went to the same place at the same time
every night of his life, and the work was under four, and Tadayoshi was
back on the road before the town knew it had happened.

The entry was two lines and there were no corrections at all.

He looked at the no corrections for a while, on the road, on the
twenty-seventh, because it was the second one in a row.

*Two in a row with nothing in the second column.*

*That is either that I have stopped making mistakes or that I have
stopped finding them.*

He put that in the second column, which meant the second column had one
thing in it, which meant it was not two in a row any more, and he was
aware of the shape of that and let it stand.

---

He bought the boots in Ōtoma on the twenty-eighth and he lost.

He had had it on the list since the twenty-seventh of the ninth month,
under *a coat that is not this coat*, written at a stream at half past
four in the morning with a ruined welt in his hand, and it had sat there
for a month being a thing he could not afford, and now he had eight
thousand ryō against his ribs and could have bought the man's whole
bench.

The man wanted four hundred and eighty.

Tadayoshi had done the work. He had asked three people in the yard what
boots went for and got three hundred, three hundred and fifty, and *for a
boy, less*, and he had a number before he walked in, and the number was
three fifty and he had decided he would go to four.

He came out having paid four hundred and eighty.

---

He went up the ladder with them under his arm and sat down and went at it
properly, because losing four days' wages to a man with a bench was the
kind of thing that had a reason in it.

*He did not out-argue me. He did not say anything I could not answer. He
said four eighty and I said three fifty and he picked one up and turned
it over and put it down again and said four eighty, and I said four
hundred, and he said four eighty, and I paid four eighty.*

*So what happened.*

He went round it three times and did not get it, and then got it sideways
at about the fourth go and did not like it.

*He knew I wanted them.*

*He knew inside four seconds, because I picked them up first and I picked
up the right one and I put my hand inside it to feel the welt, and
nobody does that to a boot they are thinking about.*

*I told him what I wanted before I told him what I would pay, and after
that there was no negotiation. There was a man with a thing and a boy who
had already said he wanted it.*

He sat with that.

*And I do this for a living. I stand in a lane for four nights and work
out what a man wants off the order he does things in. I did it to Sakuta
in two evenings. I did it to Kadoya in a room in forty minutes and he
never knew.*

*And a man with a bench in Ōtoma did it to me in four seconds because I
put my hand in a boot.*

He wrote it down. It went in the operational column, which was where he
put things he had learned, and it was the first entry in that column that
had nothing to do with the work.

> *Do not touch the thing first.*

---

He looked at coats on the twenty-ninth and did not buy one.

There were three. There was one at six hundred that was too big and would
have done for two years. There was one at nine fifty that was the right
weight and had a fault in the shoulder seam that he found in about
fifteen seconds and that would have gone in the wet inside a year. And
there was one at one thousand four hundred, which was the price of half a
man, and which was heavy and close-woven and had a collar that stood up
on its own, and which was — he checked it twice, properly, the way he
checked everything — the best-made object he had ever had his hands on.

He stood in the doorway with it for a while.

Then he put it back on the rail and said no thank you and went out.

He got about a hundred yards up the street before he made himself stop
and find out why, and he found out why, and what he found was that he had
known why in the shop and had walked out to avoid having to have it.

*It is a good coat.*

*I have got the money. I have got eleven times the money.*

*And it is not the coat.*

He stood in the street.

*The one on the list is on a nail in a lodging house at Hanase and it has
been there since the second of the seventh month and the entry in the
other book says **what it cost: one coat**, and I have not written in
that book since.*

*So I do not want a coat. I want that one back, and I cannot have that
one back, and buying a better one does not do it, and I have known that
since the doorway.*

He went back up the ladder and did not put anything in any column at all,
because there was no column, and because the only book that had a place
for it was one he had not opened since the eighteenth of the ninth month.

---

He slept, all through it.

That should be said plainly and then left alone. He slept the whole night on
the twentieth, on the road, in a byre. He slept in Ōtoma from the
twenty-first. He slept at Shioya and on the way back from Shioya. He woke at
the times he meant to wake and he did the six minutes on the roll before he
went down and the six minutes when he came up, and in the whole of the back
end of the tenth month he did not once lie on the boards in the dark thinking
about a house above a weir, and he did not once check whether he was avoiding
it, because it did not occur to him that there was anything to avoid.

---

On the first of the eleventh month he got the sheets out.

That was the instruction. He had written it himself on the eleventh of
the tenth, ruled under the left-hander and dated: *check these on the
first of every month; if there is nothing to add, that is the entry.*

He unfolded them on his knee and read the second line, which was 17/9 and
was still the best page he had ever made, and then he looked at the
white under it.

And there was something to add.

---

It had happened on the twenty-first, at a ford on the north road, at
about eleven in the morning.

There had been a man on the far bank with his boots off and his feet in
the water, and he had been on the upstream side of the crossing, which is
the side you sit on if you want to see what is coming down the water as
well as what is coming down the road, and Tadayoshi had noticed that at
about three hundred yards and had gone on, because there was nothing else
to do.

And the man had not looked up when he crossed.

*He did not look up because he had already looked. He did it at about
four hundred, while I was still coming down through the trees, and by the
time I was in the water he had finished and had gone back to his feet.*

*Which is what I would do.*

The whole of it had taken nine seconds and nothing had happened and he
had been six miles on before he stopped thinking about it, and it was, on
every measure the sheets had, an entry: a place, a date, a thing tried,
a length of time.

He sat with the sheets open and the pencil in his hand.

He did not write it.

---

He noticed that he was not writing it, which was the part that mattered
and which he could not do anything with.

*I have got the paper open and the pencil out and I have got the whole of
it in order and I am not putting it down.*

*And I am not deciding not to. If I were deciding not to I would have a
reason and I could look at the reason.*

He held the pencil a while longer.

Then he folded the sheets and put them back at the bottom of the pack
under the throwables, and ruled nothing and dated nothing, and went
down and did the water, and left for Mikura on the third.

Mikura was four thousand eight hundred and took him five days and there
were no corrections in it either.
