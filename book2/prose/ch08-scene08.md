# Chapter 8, Scene 8 — The Sixtieth

*Act 1, month 8, the twenty-third. Kuriya's kitchen, Nakadō. Nozaki
Uzuki, age ten. POV: Uzuki. ~1,740 words.*

---

He looked at her when she came in and did not say anything about it,
which she noticed and was grateful for and filed.

She got down on the floor beside the bench and put both hands on and did
not do anything for about a minute.

*Hand on. Nothing in. Take something out first.*

*What's different.*

It went round the way it always went round, over and under and the two
sides and behind, and she let it come to her instead of going after it,
and what came was the best thing that had happened to her in four days.

*It's better.*

*The front's cooler than it was on the sixteenth. It's still thick and
it's still going to be thick, but the heat's off the inside edge and
that's — that's three times a week doing that. That's the Thursdays.*

*It works.*

She had about four seconds of that and it was the only four seconds of
anything good she had had since the twenty-second, and she took them.

*And the hip's the same.*

*The hip's exactly the same as it was in the sixth month and it is going
to be the same in the ninth and the tenth, and I am putting hours into a
knee that is being broken every day by a hip.*

"You still walk on the outside of that foot going up," she said.

"I've walked on it forty years."

"I know."

"Is that a thing?"

*Yes.*

*Yes, it's the thing, it's the whole thing, and I have got about a thimble
and it takes twenty-five minutes to buy four and a half hours off a knee
and I have not got any idea at all what a hip would take.*

"It's a thing," she said. "It's not a thing I can do anything about."

"Right," said Kuriya, and put his head back, which was what he did when
they were starting.

---

*Little. Stop. Wait.*

*One.*

Nobody talked. That was the whole arrangement and it had been the
arrangement since the fourth of the seventh month: he put his head back
and she did twenty-five minutes and neither of them said one word, because
she had told him at the beginning that talking made her lose it, which was
true, and because he was a man who was extremely glad to be given a rule
about not talking.

*Two.*

*Three.*

Twenty-five minutes in the one room in Nakadō where nobody is going to
say anything.

*Four.*

*And I could say it now.*

---

She had known she was going to have this out before she got up the hill.
She had known it from about the time she put the mark on the bag, and she
had spent the walk telling herself she was not going to, and she had not
believed herself.

*Say it. Just say it. He's got his head back and his eyes shut and there's
nobody in this house and he doesn't talk to anybody and he told me so
himself at his own gate.*

*Say: my mother's husband hits her.*

*Nine words.*

*Eleven.*

She did not say it, and then she did the thing she did, which was to find
out why.

---

*One. I'd have to say how I know.*

*And how I know is: a chair went over and somebody picked it back up. And
she said she caught it with the bucket, and she gave me a reason nobody
asked her for. And I put my hands out for hers and she turned round and
put some kindling on a shelf.*

*Say that out loud in a kitchen.*

*Say it to a man of sixty with his eyes shut.*

She went at it honestly and it came apart in her hands exactly the way she
had known it would.

*It's a chair, a bucket, and a woman putting wood down.*

*I have tested it on myself about two hundred times and I am certain, and
I am certain because I have lived four feet from that woman for ten years
and I know the difference between her saying a true thing and her saying
a thing. And that is not evidence. That is me.*

*Fourteen.*

---

*Two. He'd have to do something.*

That one took longer and it was worse, and she got it at about the
twenty-fifth.

*He's a decent man. That's the entire reason I'm considering it. He's
decent and he's careful and he doesn't talk.*

*And a decent, careful man who is told a thing like that does not sit on
it. He goes and has a word.*

*He'd go and have a word with Shimoda. Quietly. On his own, in a lane, not
in front of anybody, being fair about it, because that's what he's like.*

She stopped, with her hands flat, in the gap.

*And then Shimoda knows.*

*He knows that somebody's told Kuriya, and there are two people in that
house who could have told Kuriya, and one of them is up at his kitchen
three times a week.*

*And it would be that night. It would be that same night.*

*Twenty-six.*

**So telling anybody makes it worse for her tonight, and I would be the
one who did it, and I would have done it by trying to help.**

---

*Three.*

She did not want to do the third one and she did it anyway, because
leaving out the ugly one is how you get a column of excuses.

*I'm being paid.*

*Five hundred ryō a month and three afternoons and one person in this village
who talks to me like I'm a person, and if I put this in his kitchen it
stops being that kitchen, and it stops being that whether he does anything
or not.*

*Forty.*

*I am not going to spend it.*

*I am ten and I have got exactly two things — my mother and this — and
one of them I cannot help and the other one I am not going to burn on
trying.*

*And that is a real reason and it is not a nice one and I am not going to
put a better one on top of it.*

*Forty-one.*

---

At about the sixtieth she nearly did.

It came the way it always came at the sixtieth — the flat certainty that
nothing was happening and had never happened — except that this time,
under it, there was a different thing entirely, which was that her mouth
had got as far as taking a breath.

*Sixty.*

*Say it.*

*Now. He's got his eyes shut. It's the middle. He won't move and he won't
look at me and I can do it into the top of his knee and it'll be over.*

*Don't push.*

She did not push.

She sat on a floor with her hands on a man's leg and held absolutely
still through the gap, the whole of the gap, and did not lean and did not
trail off, and stopped on purpose, as an action, the way she had been
taught in a kitchen in another village by a man with a cup of water.

*Sixty-one.*

And somewhere in there she caught the shape of what she was doing and did
not like it at all.

*That's the same.*

*That is the exact same motion. He taught me to want to push and not push,
and to stop on purpose and not trail off, and I have just used it on my
own mouth.*

*He taught me a thing for putting into people and I have turned it round
and used it to hold something in.*

*Sixty-two.*

She did not put that one in the list.

---

Ninety.

She took her hands off and counted the forty seconds and Kuriya moved his
foot and then bent the knee and then got up in one, and stood there for a
second with his hand not on the bench.

"That's better than Tuesday."

"It's the Thursdays."

"Then we'll keep the Thursdays."

---

He got the box down and counted it and put it on the table rather than in
her hand, which was a new thing he had started doing in the eighth month
and which she had worked out was so that she did not have to take it off
him.

Then he sat down and said, in a completely ordinary voice:

"Is it just the sacks?"

*Oh.*

She stood in his kitchen with her hands still going.

*There it is.*

*He's held the door open. He's held it open and he's made it as easy as
it is possible to make it, because if I say yes then that's the end of it
and neither of us ever mentions it again, and if I say no then he's asked
and I've been asked.*

*He has thought about how to ask me. He has sat in this kitchen and worked
out a way of asking that costs me nothing to refuse.*

"Yes," she said.

Kuriya nodded once.

"Right," he said, and that was the end of it, and he did not ask again
that year.

---

She stood there.

*Ask him something else. Quick. Ask him something else so that this isn't
the thing that happened.*

And what came out was not what she expected and she thought about it for
about four months afterwards.

"What would it take for you to let a house to somebody?"

Kuriya looked up.

"Let a house to who?"

"Anybody. What do you want off them."

"Money," said Kuriya. "Every month, on the day, and the day matters more
than the money." He rubbed his hands. "And a name on a paper. And the name
on the paper has to be somebody I can go and stand in front of, and if
they've got nobody they can't hold a house, and that's not me being hard,
that's every man with a house from here to Ōgi."

"So a woman on her own can't."

"A woman on her own can if she's got a trade and people know it," said
Kuriya. "Ine's sister holds one at Ōgi and she's been on her own eleven
years. It's not the woman. It's the trade."

*A trade.*

*Something people know you do.*

"How long does it take," said Uzuki, "for people to know?"

"Years," said Kuriya. "Why?"

"No reason," said Uzuki, and it came out perfectly smoothly, and she went
down the hill.

*1,646.*
