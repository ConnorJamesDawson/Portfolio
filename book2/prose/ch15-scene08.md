# Chapter 15, Scene 8 — I Don't Set the Fee

*Act 2, year 1, month 3, the nineteenth to the twenty-first. Ōtoma.
Nozaki Uzuki, age eleven. POV: Uzuki. ~1,700 words.*

---

She had nine days and she used all nine of them and she used them the way
he would have used them, which she did not notice she was doing and which
was the whole reason it worked.

She built the case against herself first.

She got three objections. She could answer two.

---

**The first was the one that nearly killed it.**

*He was not pleased about the money. He was pleased about the boy.*

She sat with that for most of a day and it was very good and she could not
get round it, because it was true that Kadoya had laughed at a child
telling him his arithmetic did not work, and any man might, and that is
not a thing about a fee at all.

She got it on the seventeenth and she got it with one question.

He was doing the frame and she came up beside him and said it the way she
said things, out of nowhere and with no run-up:

"When he laughed. Had he paid you?"

He did not look up from what he was doing.

"Four days before. On delivery."

"So the money was gone."

"Yes."

"Right," she said, and went away, and he did not ask her why, because he
did not ask her why about anything, and she was, for about four steps,
extremely glad of it.

*He was pleased with the money already gone.*

*That is not a man being amused by a child. A man being amused by a child
is amused at the time and it is over. This one paid, and it went, and four
days later he sat in a room being pleased about it, and then he went and
told somebody.*

*You do not do that about a thing you paid the right price for. Nobody has
ever in the history of the world gone and told somebody about a fair
price.*

---

**The second was easier and it took her an evening.**

*Maybe three six is just what it costs and Kadoya was a cheerful man.*

She put the whole series up in her head and looked at it.

*Three six, two nine, four one, two two, four eight, nine hundred. Take
the fourteen out.*

*If the fee is for who the man was, then those five ought to sort, and
they ought to sort the way the men sort.*

*And I cannot sort them, because I do not know who four of them were.*

She sat forward on the boards.

*And neither does he.*

*He told me. Nine times out of eleven. He said it the way you say the
price of bread.*

*So the rule he runs his whole life on is a rule about a thing he cannot
see, and he has never once noticed that, because he is not the one who
sets it, and a man who is handed a number does not ask what it is a
measurement of.*

---

**And the third she could not answer, and she did not try after the first
hour, and what she did instead is the reason the twenty-first went the way
it did.**

*I have got one buyer.*

*One. Kadoya. Out of eleven. And I have built the whole of this on him
being pleased, and I have got that off eleven words from somebody who was
not paying attention to that part.*

She turned it over four ways and it did not improve.

*So it is one case.*

*And one is not a line. Two is any line you like and one is not even
that.*

She looked at the fire for a while.

*Right. Then I am going to say that. First. Before he does.*

*Because he is going to say it in about ninety seconds, and if he says it
first then it is his objection and I am answering it, and if I say it
first then it is a thing we both already know and we are past it.*

She had never in her life planned an argument.

---

She gave it to him on the nineteenth after he shut the book, because she
had learned in the first week that you wait until he shuts the book.

She did it in his order.

"I've got something about the fees and there's a hole in it and I'm going
to tell you the hole first."

He looked up.

"The hole is that I've only got one buyer out of eleven and it's Kadoya
and I got him off about eleven words of yours. So it's one case and one
isn't a line and I know that."

"All right."

"Right." She sat back on her heels. "You said you get paid for who the man
was."

"That's right."

"Kadoya paid three thousand six hundred, and four days after the money had
gone he sat in a room and laughed and asked you three questions he didn't
need and told you he'd have work. And then he went and told somebody about
it, because that's what the laugh is for."

He did not say anything.

"A man who is pleased has paid less than he would have."

She let that sit for about two seconds, which she had decided on in
advance and which was the hardest part of the whole evening.

"So three six wasn't the price of Sakuta. It was what Kadoya was willing
to say out loud in a room with nobody in it who knew what he'd got. And
what he'd got was eleven years of names stopping and two more years of
funding, and he told you both of those himself."

She put her hands flat on her knees.

"The fee isn't for who the man was. It's for what the buyer got. And you
have never once looked at the buyer, and you told me you don't know who
they are nine times out of eleven, and you said it like it was nothing."

---

He did not say anything at all for about six seconds, and then he said,
"Say the last part again," and she said it again, and he said, "Right,"
and got the book back out and wrote for about a minute.

Then he said, "I'll come back to you," and that was the whole of the
nineteenth.

He did not speak to her for most of the twentieth.

She watched him do it. He did it walking, mostly, on the north ground, out
about half a mile and back, four times, with his hands behind him. Twice
he stopped and stood still for a long while. Once he came back and got the
frame out and had two sheets out of it and put them back.

She did not go near him and she did not eat until he did.

*He is taking it apart.*

*Good. That is what it is for.*

---

He came and sat down opposite her on the twenty-first, in the morning,
before anything, which he did not do.

He said: "I can't break it."

She waited.

"I've had a day at it. I went at the one-case thing and it's the best
objection and it isn't enough, because I don't need eleven, I need one
that goes the other way, and I haven't got one. I went at whether pleased
means anything and it does, because I've watched you take twenty-three
hundred off a man for being pleased and I was standing there."

He turned his hand over.

"And I went at the part where I'd have noticed. And I wouldn't have. It's
not in the sheet. It's never been in the sheet. There's nowhere on a
docket where it says what the man who's paying gets out of it, and I've
read seventy-two of them and it has never once occurred to me that there
was anything missing, because a thing that has never been on any of them
doesn't look missing. It looks like the shape of the paper."

He looked at her.

"You're right."

---

She had thought about what she would do with that and she had got it
wrong, because what she had planned was to be pleased and what happened
was that she could not say anything for about four seconds.

*He has never said that.*

*Not to me and not to anybody in front of me. He says **that's not wrong**
and he says **that holds** and he says **I'll come back to you**. He has
never once in a hundred days said the other thing.*

*And it is a hundred days. It is exactly a hundred days from the door of
that building, and I have known that since I woke up, and I was going to
say it to him at some point today and I am not going to now, because it
is not the thing that happened this morning.*

She got as far as, "Right," and then, because she wanted the rest of it:

"So what do we do."

And he said it flatly, without any weight on it at all, because it was
just a fact and he was giving her a fact:

"Nothing. I don't set the fee. It's on the docket."

---

She sat with that for a long time and he let her, and did not fill it in,
and got up after a while and went and did the mule.

*He is an employee.*

*He has never asked for a number in his life. The number comes on a piece
of paper and he does the work and they hand him the number and he writes
it in a book.*

*So I have spent nine days getting a true thing that he is not allowed to
use.*

She looked at her hands.

And then she got the rest of it, and she got the rest of it in about four
seconds, and she sat very still with it in exactly the way she had sat
still on the tenth.

*Unless there is nobody above him.*

She did not say it.

She went at *why not* properly, because she always did.

*Because I do not know what he does about things.*

*I have got a thing about my hair I did not give him for the same reason
and that was five weeks ago and I have not learned one more thing about it
since.*

*He would take that away and work on it. He would work on it for months.
And I do not know what comes out of him at the end of months, because I
have never seen the end of one.*

*So: not yet.*

---

He had already taken it.

He took it on the nineteenth, in the minute he spent with the book after
she said it the second time, and what he wrote was four lines with no
question under them, and he did not show her and did not mention it again.

She found out on the fourth of the eighth month.

It came back to her in a room with a door shut and two other people in it
and a number on a table, and by then it had a name and the name was hers
and she had not given it one.
