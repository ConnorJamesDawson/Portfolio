# Chapter 5, Scene 4 — In a Book

*Act 1, month 4, the twenty-sixth. The harbour office, Sunaba. Tadayoshi
Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,520 words.*

---

Nunome waited three days.

Tadayoshi worked that out afterwards and it was the part that stayed with
him: the old man had watched him stop dead on a page on the Tuesday, and
had said nothing on the Tuesday and nothing on the Wednesday and nothing
on the Thursday, and had spent three days deciding.

On the Friday he put his rule down at about four o'clock and said:

"You stopped on the fourth month."

---

"I did not stop."

"You stopped for a minute and eleven seconds," said Nunome, "which I know
because I was watching the clock, and the clock is wrong but it is
wrong *consistently*. And then you got up and went and looked at the
chart with your hands behind your back like a man waiting for a train,
and you came back and you did the rest of the month and about forty of
them were bad."

Tadayoshi put his pen down.

"Then you went back to the top," said Nunome, "and you counted."

---

The stove ticked. Ide was at the counter with somebody about a bill of
lading and they were both being patient at each other.

"You counted the exemptions," said Nunome.

"Yes."

"Nobody counts the exemptions. In eleven years I've had two clerks and a
harbour master and a man from the revenue in this room and not one of
them has ever counted the exemptions, because there's no money in the
column and money is the only thing anybody has ever wanted out of that
book."

He turned round on his stool, which took a while.

"Who was on it?"

---

Tadayoshi sat at the corner of the desk with his hands in his lap.

*He has gone straight through.*

*Three days. He has had three days and he has gone from "the boy stopped
on a page" to "there is somebody on a boat" without any of the middle,
and he has done it because he has copied out a coast for eleven years and
he knows exactly what an exempt entry is and exactly who looks at one.*

*And I cannot lie to him.*

*I could say nothing. Nothing is not lying.*

He said nothing.

Nunome nodded slowly, the way you nod at an answer.

"Aye," he said. "Well."

---

"I'll not say anything."

That was the sentence.

Tadayoshi heard it and his whole chest went loose for about a second and
a half with a relief so complete that he was ashamed of it for years,
and then the second and a half ended.

*He will not say anything.*

*Which means he has considered saying something.*

*Which means it has been a question in this room for three days, and he
has answered it one way, today, on a Friday, out of kindness — and a
question that has been answered once is a question that exists, and it
can be asked again on a day when he is frightened, or by somebody who is
not him.*

*He has just told me, as a gift, that I am a matter that has been
decided.*

"Thank you," said Tadayoshi.

"Don't thank me," said Nunome. "I'm seventy-four and I'm four years
behind and you do six hundred lines a day. It's not charity, it's
arithmetic," and he turned back round to his rule, which took a while,
and that would have been the whole of it.

---

Except that at about half past four Ide came through from the counter
with the day book under his arm and said, "Nunome. Month end's Tuesday."

"I know when month end is."

"I want the staffing on it by Monday because Sunaba's late twice running
and I've had a letter about it."

"You've had a letter about it because you *are* late twice running."

"Just do the staffing," said Ide, and went back through.

---

Tadayoshi sat perfectly still at the corner of the desk.

"Nunome-san."

"Mm."

"What is the staffing."

"Return," said Nunome, without looking up. "Everything goes up at month
end. Tonnage, dues, exemptions — " he snorted — "arrivals, and the
staffing, which is who's in the building and what we pay them, because
they'll not have anybody on a wage they haven't been told about."

"Who is it sent to?"

"Kiri."

"To whom in Kiri?"

"How should I know," said Nunome. "A room."

---

Tadayoshi looked at the pen in front of him.

*I have been in this building for fifteen days.*

*I am on nine a week and my dinner, which is a wage, which means I am a
line.*

*And the line has a name on it.*

He went back and got it exactly, because it mattered and because he could.

*Eleventh of the fourth month. He said "name" and I said "Ruri
Tadayoshi" and he wrote it in the day book at his elbow with his left
hand and asked me if I could count.*

*I gave him my name.*

*I did not think about it for one second. I did not consider it. A man
asked me my name in a room and I said it, because that is what happens
when somebody asks me something — I have never in my life been able to do
anything else and I did not even notice I was doing it.*

He put his hand flat on the desk.

*And it has been sitting in that day book for fifteen days waiting for
month end.*

*And month end is Tuesday.*

---

*Stop. Do it properly.*

*One. Is the name on a list anywhere that matters?*

*I do not know. I have assumed for two months that there is a description
and I have never once seen evidence of one. Nobody in Ōura looked at me.
Nobody in this town has looked at me.*

*Two. But my family was taken under an order, and the order came from a
room, and rooms are where returns go.*

*Three. And I am the one they did not get.*

He looked at the bound volume in front of him, four hundred and some
lines of a coast in his own small exact hand.

*Four. And the whole of my case for two months has been that nobody is
looking for me, and the whole of my evidence for that has been that
nobody has found me.*

*Which is the same thing said twice.*

---

*Five, and this is the one.*

*I came into this room to read a book.*

*And the thing this room actually does — the thing it has been doing the
entire time, every single day, while I sat at this desk copying it out
with my own hand — is take what happens on a coast and turn it into
lines and send the lines to a room in Kiri.*

*That is what a harbour office is. It is not a place with a book in it.
It is a place that makes books and sends them somewhere.*

*And I have been inside it for fifteen days being extremely pleased with
myself about a shelf.*

---

He finished the page.

He did that deliberately and he was afterwards glad of it: he finished
the page he was on, ruled the next one, squared the volume off, and put
the pen in the tray, because a desk left in the middle of a line is a
desk somebody looks at.

Then he went and did the stove for the evening, which was his job, and
banked it up properly for the night, which was also his job.

At about six he said goodnight to Ide, who said, "Night," without looking
up.

At about seven, when Nunome went, he said, "Goodnight, Nunome-san," and
the old man said, "Six hundred lines," in the tone of a man conferring a
title, and went off up the pier with his stick.

Tadayoshi went into the back room with the coal and rolled his blanket
and put the oilcloth round it and put the tin and the ledger and the
frame inside his coat, and sat on the coal in the dark for about forty
minutes.

*He is not going to say anything and it does not matter, because the
building is going to say it for him on Tuesday, and it has been going to
say it since the eleventh.*

*And I put myself here. Nobody found me. I walked in and asked for a job
in the one kind of room in this country whose entire purpose is to write
down who is in it.*

He got up at about eight, when the north arm was empty, and went out past
the tide board and along the pier and up the coast road with nine ryō and
some in four places on his body, going north.

He did not take the pen.

He thought about it for about four seconds at the door and worked out
that a missing pen is a thing a man notices in the morning, and left it in
the tray, squared up with the edge of the volume, the way it went.
