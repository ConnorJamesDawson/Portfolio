# Chapter 7, Scene 9 — How Many Are In The House

*Act 1, month 7, the second and third. Hanase. Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten.
POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,920 words.*

---

The Hanase job took eleven minutes and there is nothing to say about it.

The yard behind the smithy after dark; a man called Torii who was about
fifty and had a bad chest and did not ask him anything; the parcel out of
his shirt and into a sack of something; and Torii saying "right" and
"good lad" and then, at the gate, the thing the sheet had said he would
say.

"You'll want a bed. There's the loft."

"No, thank you."

"It's no trouble. There's nobody in it."

"No, thank you."

There was a pause, and Torii's face did something, and Tadayoshi stood in
a yard in the dark and could not do one single thing about it.

*He is offended.*

*He is offended and he is right to be, because a man has offered a child
a dry floor at nine at night for nothing and the child has said no twice
without one word of a reason, and there is no reason I can give him,
because the reason is that a man in Fukae wrote a line on a docket.*

"Suit yourself," said Torii.

*And now I am a boy who would not take a bed, and he will remember me
for that, and I have made myself memorable by doing the thing I was told
to do to stop being memorable.*

He said thank you again on the way out, which did not help.

---

He paid a sixteenth for a place on a floor at a lodging on the east side
of the town, which was the cheapest of the three he looked at and which
he resented.

*A sixteenth.*

*That is two and a half days of a boy's field work for a floor, and I
have a blanket, and there is a byre eleven minutes outside this town that
I saw on the way in.*

*And I am in here because the byre is outside and I do not know this
country and a man in a yard has just had a look at my face for four
seconds while being offended at me.*

It rained at about eleven.

He heard it start and lay there in the dark on a floor with four men on
it and thought, with entirely uncomplicated pleasure, about the byre he
was not in.

*Right. That was the sixteenth. That was worth it.*

His coat was on a nail on the back of the door with everybody else's,
because there was nowhere else, and it had been out in the first of it
and it was wet.

---

At about two in the morning somebody came into the yard.

He was awake before he knew why, which had been happening since the fifth
of the fourth month and which he had stopped remarking on. He lay
absolutely still on a board floor with four men breathing around him and
listened to a door open downstairs and two voices start.

It was not loud. That was the first thing and it was the thing that made
him stay still instead of sitting up: nobody was shouting and nobody was
being woken and there was no hurry anywhere in it.

The keeper's voice, and one other.

*Not drunk. Not a fight. Not a man wanting a bed at two, because a man
wanting a bed at two is a negotiation and this is not a negotiation, this
is one of them asking and the other one answering.*

The floor was a board floor over a beamed ceiling and he could not get
words, only the shape, the same as he had been getting shapes through
floors and walls his whole life.

Then the other voice went up very slightly at the end of something, the
way a voice does at the end of a question, and the keeper's voice did the
flat thing a voice does when it is giving a number.

*He's asked him something and the answer is a number.*

Tadayoshi lay on the floor in the dark.

*How many.*

*He has asked him how many are in the house.*

And then the second question came, and it went up at the end the same
way, and the keeper's answer was longer and had several numbers in it.

---

He was on his feet before the answer was finished.

He did it silently and he did it fast and he did not think about any of
it, because he had thought about all of it a great many times, lying in a
byre, working out what he would do, and the working out was the whole
point of having done it.

*Ages.*

*He has asked how many and then he has asked their ages.*

*Nobody asks ages.*

*A man after a thief asks for a name. A man after money asks for a name.
A man after a runaway asks for a name and he has got one.*

**You ask ages when you have got a description and you have not got a
name.**

*And there is one description of me anywhere in this country and it is
four days old and it is a boy of about ten, and this man is downstairs
asking a lodging keeper for a list of everybody in this building by age
at two o'clock in the morning, and he is being polite about it, and the
keeper is answering, because why would he not.*

---

The window was at the end and it was small and it was not a window
anybody had opened for some years.

He got it open in about four seconds, which was three seconds longer than
he wanted, and one of the men on the floor turned over and said something
and did not wake.

He went through it feet first with his blanket round his arm and got a
hand on the ledge and hung and dropped about seven feet into a
water-butt yard, and it went badly and he took it on the side of his foot
and the wall took some skin off his forearm, and none of that mattered.

The coat was on the nail on the back of the door.

---

He did the sum on the ledge. He had it before he was off it.

*Six seconds to cross that room. There are four men on that floor and
one of them is already half awake.*

*It is wet. Wet oiled canvas is four pounds and it is four pounds under
my arm, on a wall, in the dark, with eleven miles to do before light.*

*And.*

*And it is a coat that a woman on the middle street at Fukae sold to a
boy of about ten on the thirteenth of last month, in front of a street,
for double what it was worth, and she will remember that for a year.*

*It is the single most identifying object I own and I paid extra for it.*

*In that room it says a boy went out of a window.*

*On me it says which boy.*

He went over the yard wall and along the back of three houses and out
onto the east track and he did not stop for eleven miles.

The arithmetic was correct and complete and he had done it in under four
seconds and it was right, and he knew it was right at the time and he
knew it was right afterwards and he never once, in the whole of the rest
of his life, found a fault in it.

---

He minded far more than he could account for.

He noticed it at about the fourth mile, and it was not a small thing, and
he could not put it anywhere. It sat in him the whole night, on a track,
in the rain, in a shirt, with a blanket round his shoulders that did
nothing once it was wet.

*It is a coat.*

*It is fifteen hundred ryō, which I have now established was three-quarters
of a ryō, so what I have actually lost tonight is three-quarters of a
ryō and about two days of being warm.*

*I have got two and change. I will buy another one. I will buy a better
one, for less, because I know what three people paid now.*

*So why am I — *

He walked about a mile on that and got nowhere with it, and then, at some
point on the far side of the second stream, he stopped trying to make it
come out and simply put it down as it was, because it was information
about himself and information about yourself is the most expensive kind
there is.

*It is the first thing I have ever owned that I chose.*

*Everything else was given to me or made for me or was in a house. The
frame was made for me. The knife was Meno's. The tin came off a bench.
The clasp came off a body.*

*I stood in a street and looked at a rail and picked it and paid for it
out of money I was given for work, and it was mine, and it fit, and it
was the best thing I had.*

*And it is on a nail in Hanase.*

---

He wrote it in the morning, in a plantation, in the wet, with the light
coming.

And he did not write it in the book.

He sat with the ledger open at forty-eight and looked at the page for a
while, and then closed it, and got out the other thing he had been
carrying since the fourth month, which was eleven sheets of the harbour
office's spoiled ruling paper that Nunome had let him take because it was
going on the fire anyway.

He ruled it.

He ruled five columns and he made the fourth one narrow because that was
how it was done, and he put the headings across the top in the small
exact hand:

> *When. Who. What they tried. How long. *

And then he got to the end of the line and there was a column with
nothing over it, because he had ruled five and had only had four things
to say, and he sat there with the pen and looked at the empty space and
worked out what it was for.

He wrote:

> *What it cost.*

---

Then he filled the first line in, and it took him about a minute, and
most of it was blank.

> *2–3/7. Hanase. Lodging, east side.*
>
> *Who: —*
>
> *What they tried: —. He asked the keeper how many were in the house
> and their ages. He did not come up the stair. I do not know whether he
> was ever going to.*
>
> *How long: —*
>
> *What it cost: one coat.*

He looked at it for a long time.

Five columns, and three of them were a line, and the whole of the entry
was a question asked politely at two in the morning by a man he never
saw, in a building he had paid a sixteenth to sleep in, about a
description that was four days old and had never had his name on it at
all.

*That is the first one.*

*And there is going to be a second one.*

He blew on it, and folded it, and put it inside his shirt on the other
side from the book, and went on east in the rain without a coat.
