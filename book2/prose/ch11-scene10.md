# Chapter 11, Scene 10 — Nine Hundred And Three

*Act 1. A room. A boy, age twelve. POV: the boy. ~1,300 words.*

---

The third one finished on a Tuesday, except that he did not have Tuesdays,
so what it finished on was a bowl, and the bowl was the four hundred and
fifty-eighth since the sixth month.

He knew it was finished because he had been feeling the end of it every
morning for nine bowls and for nine bowls it had been the same.

*It has stopped.*

*It stopped three bowls ago and I did not believe it until now, and the
reason I did not believe it is that I did not want it to have stopped.*

He sat up and got all three off the mat and lined the bases up.

---

He did the lengths first because the lengths were the thing.

He measured with his thumb, laid flat, from the base, which was how he had
done it every time since the sixth month, and he had checked his thumb
against the same place on the wall four times over the five months to be
certain his thumb had not got bigger, and it had not, or had not enough to
see.

*Four.*

*Three and a half.*

*Three and a quarter.*

He put them down and put his hands flat on the mat on either side of them.

*It is going down.*

*I knew that. That is not the thing.*

---

The thing was the gaps, and he found the thing about the gaps in
somewhere under a minute and then sat with it for a very long time,
because the gaps did not do what he had expected them to do and he could
not make them.

*From the first to the second is a half.*

*From the second to the third is a quarter.*

He went back over the measuring in case he had done the measuring wrong,
and he had not done the measuring wrong; he had done it four times each
and had done the second one again in a different light on the day after.

*So it is a half and then a quarter.*

*And a half and then a quarter is not the same size of step, which means
it is not a straight line, which means I have got three points and I still
do not know what shape they are.*

He looked at the three of them lying on the mat with their bases in a row.

*I thought three would be enough.*

---

He did the bowls next, because the bowls were the other number and he had
all three of them and they were exact, which was the one thing in his life
that was exact.

*The first one came back in a hundred and seventy-eight.*

*The second one came back in a hundred and fifty-one.*

*The third one came back in a hundred and twenty-nine.*

He worked out the gaps in those, which took him longer than the lengths
because they were bigger numbers and he had to do it on his fingers
against the mat.

*Twenty-seven. And twenty-two.*

*So the coming back is getting faster and the thing that comes back is
getting smaller, and the getting-smaller is slowing down and the
getting-faster is slowing down, and both of them are slowing down by about
the same sort of amount.*

He held that for a while and it was the most complicated thing he had ever
held in his head at once, and it did not resolve into anything, and after
a while he understood that it was not going to and that this was because
he only had three.

*I would need five.*

*Five would be five hundred bowls.*

---

He lay down on his side facing the wall and did the part that came next,
which was to decide what to do with three.

That was new as well. He noticed it was new about halfway through and it
frightened him more than the numbers had.

*I have got two ways to read it and there is nothing in the room to tell
me which one is right and there is not going to be.*

*One. It goes down by less every time, and if it goes down by less every
time then it goes down for ever and never gets to nothing, and I have got
a hand that gets worse and never stops.*

*Two. It goes down by whatever it feels like, and the half was real
because I saw the half, and the quarter was a good one.*

He turned both of them over.

*And I do not know. And nobody is going to come and tell me. And I have to
have one, because a bad answer that you have got is not the same as no
answer, and I have been using that since the sixth month and it has held
up.*

*So I have to pick.*

---

He picked the half.

He was a long time about it and he did it in the end for a reason he could
say to himself in one sentence, which was the only kind of reason he
trusted.

*Because I saw the half happen and I did not see the quarter be the
pattern.*

*The half is a thing that happened to me. The quarter might be a thing
that happened once.*

*And if I am wrong about it being the half, then I am wrong the good way,
and if I am wrong about it being the quarter, then I am wrong the other
way, and I have got no reason at all to want to be wrong the other way.*

He did not know that there was a name for what he had just done, and there
was, and it was the only decision he had made in his life that was not
about which way to lie down.

---

Then he did the arithmetic, on the mat, in the dark, on his fingers.

*Three and a quarter left, and a half comes off each time.*

He counted it out.

*Three and a quarter. Two and three quarters. Two and a quarter. One and
three quarters. One and a quarter. Three quarters. A quarter. And then
there is not any.*

He counted it again to be sure.

*Seven.*

*Seven more times.*

---

The last part took him the longest and he knew while he was doing it that
he was doing it wrong, and did it anyway, because the right way was not
available to him and he had worked out that it was not available.

*Each one comes back faster than the one before. A hundred and
seventy-eight, then a hundred and fifty-one, then a hundred and
twenty-nine.*

*So the next one is not a hundred and twenty-nine. It is less. And the one
after that is less than that.*

*And I cannot do less-than-that seven times, because I do not know how
much less, and every one I guess makes the next one worse.*

He lay with his hand over the back of his other hand.

*So I will use the one I have got.*

*A hundred and twenty-nine, seven times.*

He did it in fives and then added the rest, which was how he did all of
them, and it took him a while and he got it wrong once and went back and
found where.

Nine hundred and three.

---

He said it in his head once and then did not do anything at all.

He did not do anything for a long time, and there was nothing in his chest
and nothing in his throat and nothing behind his eyes, and it was not
courage and it was not calm and there was no word for it in him.

He had a number. He had never had a number before. Everything he had ever
had was a bowl and then another bowl and no end to them, and now there was
an end to them and it was nine hundred and three away, and nine hundred
and three bowls was a thing you could count toward.

He thought that was better.

*Nine hundred and three.*

He put his hand out and put the three of them back in a row with the bases
lined up, in the dark, by feel, and left them there, and waited for the
bowl.
