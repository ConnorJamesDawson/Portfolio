# Chapter 16 — The Fetching

*Book two. Act two. Year 1, month 3 the 29th – month 4 the 9th.*
*11 scenes, 19,140 words. Tadayoshi ×5 · Kimimaro ×4 · Uzuki ×2.*



*Act 2, year 1, month 3, the twenty-ninth. The mill yard at Ochiai.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,700 words.*

---

They were at the ford below the mill by half past four and stood in the
dark for about forty minutes, because first light is a time and he had
been told a time.

She did not ask why they were early. She had stopped asking that in the
first month.

The wheel came up out of the dark before the yard did, which is the thing
he remembered about Ochiai afterwards and the only thing: a wheel taller
than a house, not turning, with water going under it and no sound off it
at all.

---

The man was in the yard when it got light enough to be in it.

"Ide," he said, and Tadayoshi said his own name, and that was the whole of
the introductions in either direction.

Ide was about forty and had a good coat on over working clothes and had
the look of a man who had been up for two hours and would be up for
twelve more. He did not look at Uzuki and he did not look at the mule. He
looked at the frame on the mule for about three seconds, which was the
only assessment anybody made of anybody in that yard, and it was correct
and it was about load-bearing.

"That'll do," he said.

"It'll do."

---

The case was in the middle of the yard on a pallet.

Tadayoshi went round it twice before he touched it.

*Four feet by two and a half by two and a bit. Bound at the corners with
iron, and the binding goes round the ends and not the sides, which is what
you do when a thing is going to be lifted by the ends.*

*Boarded, not crated. Close-boarded, so you cannot see in and you cannot
get a hand in.*

*Two seals, both ends, both intact, and they are the consignor's and I
have never seen that mark and there is no reason I would have.*

*And it does not smell of anything.*

He put his hands on the ends and rocked it about an inch each way, which
is what you do, and it did not shift inside and it did not sound hollow
and it did not sound full either.

*Ninety kan, and the case is fifteen of that at the outside.*

"You want to sign it before you lift it," Ide said, "or after."

"Before. If it's damaged I'm not signing it."

"It's not damaged."

"I know. I've looked."

---

The counterparts took about four minutes and were the most careful thing
either of them did.

Ide had his and Tadayoshi had his, and they read each other's, and the two
sheets said the same thing in two hands, and the two hands wrote the date
on each other's copy and that was the transfer.

*Ochiai to the crossing below Hane. 1 case. Ab. 90 kan. Received in order,
seals intact, 29/3.*

He read his own back twice before he put it away, because a counterpart is
the only thing standing between him and a man saying a case was full of
something else.

"There'll be one at the other end," Ide said.

"So I'm told."

"Right."

And then Ide did the thing that was not in the arrangement, which was to
put a hand flat on the top of the case for a second before he stood back.

*He has done that before.*

*That is a man touching a thing he has been standing next to for a while.*

Tadayoshi did not think anything else about it and did not write it down.

---

They got it onto the frame in about twenty minutes and it was four-handed
work and Ide did not offer and was not asked.

The lift is worth putting down because it is the only piece of physical
work in the whole of that day and because of what it cost.

He took the ends, because the binding said ends. He got it up onto the lip
of the pallet on his own — which Ide watched and said nothing about, and
which was a mistake, because a boy of eleven picking up ninety kan of
anything on his own is a thing a man remembers, and he had been told not
to be memorable and had not connected the instruction to himself — and
then Uzuki got under the far end with her shoulder and they walked it four
paces and got it onto the frame and she held the frame while he did the
lashings.

He did the lashings himself and did them twice.

Over the top and down both sides and through the frame and back over.
Then a second set, crossed, front to back.

*It is not going to shift, because it fills the frame. So the lashings are
not holding it still, they are holding it down. That is what you do with a
thing that is heavy and dead.*

He did not have a reason to notice that he had thought it.

---

The last thing at Ochiai took him about ninety seconds and he got it
completely right and did nothing with it.

He was doing the final walk round the mule with his hand on the girth and
he came past the pallet, which was now empty, and he looked at it because
it was there.

*That has been standing.*

He stopped.

*The north side of it has gone into the mud by about an inch. The south
side has not, because the yard falls that way and the water goes off it.*

*A pallet with three hundred-odd kan on it goes into wet ground at about
—* and he did not have a rate for that, and made one, and marked it in his
head the way he marked all of them.

*Call it a night for a finger. So an inch is four or five nights.*

He crouched down and put a thumb in the depression to be sure of it, and
he was sure of it.

*That case has been in this yard since about the twenty-fourth.*

He stood up.

*And the sheet says sealed at origin, and Ide said the seals are the
consignor's, and if the consignor sealed it on the twenty-fourth and it
has been standing in a mill yard in Ochiai for five nights then the origin
is not five days upriver, it is here, or it is somewhere and it came here
and waited.*

He turned that over for about forty seconds.

*So it waited for me.*

Which was true, and which was not sinister, and which he did not treat as
sinister, and he was right not to — because a consignment that has to leave
on the twenty-ninth and arrives at the depot on the twenty-fourth is a
consignment somebody organised properly, and he had spent fifteen months
wishing more people organised things properly.

*That is a well-run job.*

They were out of the yard by half past six and on the north track by
seven, and the mule went well, and the case rode the way a well-packed
case rides, which is that after about a mile you stop hearing it.

She said, at about eight: "What's in it?"

"Dye."

"How do you know?"

He walked about four paces.

"Because I was told."

Which was true, and which was the first entirely true and entirely useless
answer he had ever given her, and she heard both halves of it, and she did
not say anything else about it for eleven days.

She did think one thing and she did not say that either, and it was not
about the case.

*He said **because I was told**.*

*He has never once in a hundred and eight days answered a question that
way.*

---

He did the book that night and put two things in it and they are the only
two entries of the twenty-ninth.

The first went in the operational column, because facts about jobs went in
the operational column, and it was one line and there was nothing under
it.

> *29/3. Ochiai. Case had been standing ab. 5 nights. Seals intact.*

He did not put the mark beside it. It was a measurement, he had made it
himself off a depth in mud, and it was the first thing he had written in
three weeks that was actually a number.

The second is the only part of the day he enjoyed.

He had ruled the mule a page in the second month because it was a system,
and the page had four columns on it and three of them had nothing in them,
because a page about a load and a rate wants a load and there had not been
one.

He put the first real reading on it.

> *29/3. 90 kan + frame. 14 mi. No favouring. Fed at 4.*

He looked at it for a while with the pencil still in his hand.

*Fourteen at ninety, first day, on a road that is not flat, and it came in
sound and it ate.*

*So the animal is not a sixty animal. I had it at sixty comfortable off a
man in a yard who wanted five thousand two hundred for it, and a number
you get off a man who is selling is not a number.*

He drew the line under it and started the second column.

*Four more of those and I will know what I have got.*

And that is the twenty-ninth of the third month: a boy who wrote down two
true things, one about a pallet and one about an animal, and had them in
the right order for what they were worth and in exactly the wrong order
for what they were going to matter.

*Act 2. A box four feet by two. A boy, age thirteen. POV: the boy. ~1,650
words.*

---

There was light in it.

He had it inside the first minute and he did not do anything with it for
two days, and the not doing anything is the part he was proudest of
afterwards, and there was nobody to tell.

Four lines. One down each long side where the lid sat on the box, and one
across each end. He could not see through them and there was nothing to
see through them at, and that was not what they were for.

*They go grey.*

*And then they stop being grey.*

---

He had been given a clock three times.

The bowls, and they came at no time at all and he had used them anyway for
eleven months and they had lied to him the whole way. The light in the
first room, which had failed for three reasons and the third one killed
it. And the screaming, which had been the best of them and the only one he
had ever built properly, and which had stopped on a day he could not name.

**So he had been given three and had lost three, and he had a rule about
it and the rule was four days old.**

*I decided what somebody else was going to do.*

*I am not going to do that again.*

He put the seams next to that and looked at the two of them for a while.

*This is not the same thing. Nobody has given me this. A box has got a lid
on it and a lid does not fit, and the not-fitting is the whole of it, and
nobody decided that.*

Which was correct, and it did not settle him, because the thing that had
gone wrong three times was not who gave it to him. It was that he had used
one before he was sure of it.

*So I am not using it until it has done four.*

---

It did four.

He counted them off the only other thing he had, which was the lid coming
off, and the lid came off twice and both times it was the same three
things.

A face he did not see, because there was a lamp behind it and he was on
his side in a box.

Water, in a flask, held down to him, and he drank all of it both times
because he was not stupid and did not know when the next one was.

And something to eat that he did not identify and did not try to.

Then the lid.

Both times it was dark when it happened, which he could tell because the
seams were not grey, and both times the seams had been grey for a long
time before it and not grey for a long time before that.

*So they feed at night.*

*Which is a thing about them and not about me, and I am putting it in the
place where things about them go, and there are four things in there.*

By the second night he had it and it held and he checked it once more
anyway.

> *Grey, long. Not grey, long. Grey, long. Not grey.*

*That is a day and a night and a day and a night and it has done four and
it has not moved.*

He lay on his side in the dark with his hands in front of his chest.

*I have got a clock.*

---

It is worth putting down exactly what that was worth to him, because he did
not have a word for it and the book has to do the work instead.

He had not known what day it was since the ninth month of the year before
last.

Not roughly. Not within a fortnight. He had a rate and a count and a piece
of bone that had come through at a known number of intervals, and every
single one of those was a measurement of *him*, and none of them was a
measurement of the world.

*I have got about two hundred and eleven days of the sixth piece and I do
not know whether that is a season.*

*And now I have got days.*

He did not celebrate it because celebrating was not a thing he had ever
seen anybody do. What he did was start counting, and he started at one,
and he was very careful about the one, and he marked it in his head the
way he marked everything.

> *1. Made and not counted — I did not have it at the start.*

Then two. Then three.

---

The other thing he built in the box he built out of the road.

He could not see it and did not need to. A box on a cart is a box on a
cart and the road comes up through it, and he had a whole side of himself
against the floor for eleven days.

*Metalled for a long time. Then not.*

*Two river crossings on the second day and they were both fords, because a
bridge is a bang and a bang and then flat, and a ford is a slope and slow
and cold coming up through the boards.*

*Up on the third and the fourth, and it was a long up, and they walked the
animal at the top of it because the rhythm changed and did not change back
for about an hour.*

He built it as a line, in his head, in the place where things went, and
the line had about forty marks on it by the fifth day and he could go
along it in either direction.

*And it does not tell me where I am.*

*It tells me what has been under me. That is not the same thing and I am
not going to pretend it is.*

---

There is one more thing about the first days in the box and it is the only
one that was not work.

He could not lay the pieces out.

He had known that inside two minutes on the first day and had tested it
properly and had put it down, and it had gone, and he had been pleased
with himself for putting it down.

It came back on the fourth night.

He was awake in the dark with the seams not grey and nothing to do, which
was the ordinary condition, and his hand went inside his shirt and found
the fifth one and came out with it, and he lay there holding a piece of
his own hand about three thumbs long and could not do the thing you do
with it.

He tried the floor next to his chest again and the bases would not line up
because the floor was not flat where his shoulder was.

He put it back.

*I have got them all in my head. Three and a bit, two and a bit, two, one
and a half, one. I have had those since the tenth month and I could say
them in my sleep.*

*So there is nothing in the laying out. The laying out is not the
measurement. The measurement is done.*

He lay with his hand flat on his shirt over the place where they were.

*So why do I want to.*

He did not get an answer, and he went round it four ways, and the fourth
way was the closest he ever got and he did not recognise it when he had
it.

*Because when they are on the floor there is a row of them.*

*And a row of five is a thing somebody made, and I made it, and it is the
only thing I have ever made.*

He put that down too, and it went, and he did not go back to it for two
years.

---

On the sixth night the lid came off at the wrong time.

Not the feeding. The seams had gone grey and stayed grey and gone dark
again and there had been no water, and then a long while after that, the
lid.

He did not sit up, because sitting up is a thing and he did not do things.

There was a lamp, and behind it there were two of them instead of one, and
neither of them said anything to him. One of them put a hand in and took
hold of his shoulder and turned him a quarter over, and the other one
looked, and then the first one let go and put him back.

Then the lid.

He lay in the dark and did the whole of it in about nine seconds and got
it exactly right.

*That was not feeding. That was looking.*

*And the one who did the looking is not the one who does the water,
because the hands are different sizes and I have had the water hand twice.*

*So there is somebody with this cart who checks, and he does it at night,
and he did not do it on the first five and he did it on the sixth.*

He put it with the four things about them, and it made five, and he was
not frightened, and there was nothing in his head about what a person
checks a thing for.

*Day six,* he thought, because he had a clock now.

And then, because he had a clock and had never had one and the pleasure of
it had not worn off:

*Six.*

*Act 2, year 1, month 4, the fourth. The north track above Kurogi.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,600 words.*

---

He saw them at about four hundred yards and he had them at about three
hundred and fifty, and the whole of it took under a minute, and he has
never in his life done a better forty seconds of work.

They were coming down and he was going up and the track was a track, which
meant one line of it that was firm and about six feet of soft either side,
and it was a valley road with the ground going away on the left.

*Two. Together. Not in a hurry.*

He kept walking, because stopping is a decision and he had not made one
yet.

---

**Four hundred to three fifty. The large one.**

*He is very large. Not fat and not a big farmer, he is built the way a man
is built who has been made to be that size by somebody, and I have seen
about four of those in my life and three of them were in Kiri.*

*There is something across his back and it is long — six feet, six and a
half — and it is wrapped, and the wrapping goes the whole way and is not
tied at the ends, which means it is not a thing he expects to have to
unwrap in a hurry.*

*And the wrapping is worn through in two places.*

He got that at about three eighty and it was the best thing he got.

*Two places, and they are eleven inches apart, and eleven inches apart on
a six-foot object is where you put two hands.*

*So it comes off the back and it is used, and it has been used enough to
wear cloth through in exactly the two places a thing gets held, and
nowhere else.*

*So it is not a tool. Tools wear at the working end.*

---

**Three fifty. The other one.**

And here is where he stopped walking, and he did not decide to, and he
noticed afterwards that he had not.

*He is not doing anything.*

He went at that for about four seconds because it did not sound like a
finding.

*I am on a road with a girl and an animal and I have looked at the ground,
the bank, the drop on the left, the two of them, and the sky, in that
order, twice, since I came round the bend. That is what a person on a road
does. Everybody does it. I have watched about nine hundred people do it.*

*He has not done any of it.*

*He is walking down a road looking at a place about eleven feet in front
of his own feet, and he has not raised his eyes once in three hundred
yards, and that is not a man who is tired and it is not a man who is
thinking about something.*

*A man who is not looking at anything is a man who does not have to.*

He put his hand on the mule's headcollar.

---

They came on and he got the rest of it and none of the rest of it changed
anything.

Both in the same coat, and the coats were the same as each other, which is
a thing about an organisation and not a thing about two men. Dark, long,
patterned, and the pattern was not a family pattern he had ever been shown
and he had been shown a great many.

The large one had a colour to him that was not a colour people are, and
Tadayoshi looked at it for about a second and put it in the place where
things he had no account of went, which was a place with four items in it
and had had four items in it for a year.

*I do not know what that is.*

*Right.*

The quiet one was smaller than the large one and would have been ordinary
next to anybody else, and had his hands in his sleeves.

---

He went off the road.

He did it at about a hundred and forty yards, unhurried, with the mule, up
onto the soft on the bank side and back four paces, and he put the mule
between the road and Uzuki without saying anything about it, and stood
with his hand on the headcollar and looked at the middle of the track.

*Set it out.*

*One. Two. Arity.*

*Two is not two. Two where one of them is not looking at anything is one
and a problem, and I have got no idea what the problem is, and the whole
of what I know about it is that he has been walking down a road for three
hundred yards without checking it.*

*Two. I have not got a fee.*

*There is no docket. There is no name. Nobody has paid me anything about
these two men and nobody is going to and there is no version of today in
which they are work.*

*Three. So what is the argument for being on the road.*

He waited for one.

*There isn't one. The argument for being on the road is that I was already
on it.*

---

They came past.

The large one looked at them — at the mule first, then at the case on the
frame, then at Tadayoshi, and it took about a second and a half — and said,
in a perfectly friendly way, without slowing:

"Heavy."

"It is," said Tadayoshi.

And that was the whole of it, and the large one was past, and the quiet
one came past four feet behind him and did not turn his head and did not
change his pace and did not do anything at all.

They went on down and round the bend at about ninety yards and were gone
in about two minutes.

---

He stood on the bank for a while after that with his hand still on the
headcollar.

*I have never done that before.*

He was accurate about it, which he was about everything.

*I refused the Hanase road and I had a sheet and four reasons and I gave
them the reasons. I have turned two dockets down and both times I could
say why on a page.*

*I have just come off a road because of a man's eyes and I could not write
one line about it that would stand up.*

He turned it over.

*And it was right.*

*I do not know that. I am not going to write that down. What I have got is
that I came off the road and nothing happened, and nothing happening is
what happens on most roads, so it is not evidence of anything.*

He put nothing in the book about it that night and then, at about ten,
opened it again and put one line in the ordinary column and not the
operational one, because it was not about a job.

> *4/4. Two on the north track. Off the road at 140. No reason I can put
> down.*

And then, because he was not going to have a sentence in that book that
looked like a finding when it was not one, the mark.

*That is the fifth thing I have put that mark on in five weeks and four of
them are not numbers.*

He did not follow that.

---

There is a last part and it cost him money for two years.

*I want to know what that was.*

He lay on his back with the book shut on his chest and had it plainly.

*Not who. Who is a name and a name is a thing you can be told and being
told costs nothing. I want to know what the wrapping was and I want to know
what a man is when he does not look at a road, and neither of those is a
thing anybody in a yard is going to tell me, because the people who know
that are the people who are it.*

*So it is a teacher, or a scroll, or a corpse.*

*And a teacher is twelve to twenty thousand and a scroll is four to eleven
and I have got six thousand five hundred and seventy-seven and a page that
says WHAT IS LEFT and goes down.*

He lay there for a while.

*So I cannot afford to know what I nearly walked into.*

He did not write that down.

---

She had it differently and she had it right too, and she gave him none of
it, and the reason she gave him none of it was that he had already gone
off the road, and a thing you say after the decision is a thing you are
saying about yourself.

What she had was that the large one had liked it.

He had known they were looking and had come past close enough for it, and
**heavy** had been said to be heard, and a man who does that is a man who
is somebody and does not often get to be somebody on a road. She could
price him inside the second and a half he gave her, and what he cost was
being noticed, and he would have paid it.

And the other one she could not get anything off at all.

She walked about eleven paces with that.

**It was the third one in seven weeks she could not price, and the second
was walking next to her with his hand on a mule.**

*Act 2. A box four feet by two, and then four minutes outside it. A boy,

On the eighth day they stopped for a long time and then they took him out.

He knew it was the eighth day because he had a clock. He knew it was a
long stop because the road had gone out of the floor and stayed out of it
through a whole grey and most of a dark, and he had counted that the way
he counted everything, and got about eleven hours and marked it *made and
not counted* because he had been asleep in the middle of it.

The lid came off and there were three of them and one of them said,
"Out."

He got out.

---

It took him a while and nobody helped him and nobody hurried him.

That is worth setting down. He had been on his side in a box four feet by
two for eight days and he was thirteen and he had been in a room eleven
paces by seven for two years before that, and what happened when he tried
to stand up was that his legs did not.

He went down onto one knee on the boards of the cart and stayed there.

Nobody said anything. Nobody laughed and nobody kicked him and nobody
picked him up. Three men stood about four feet off in the dark and waited,
and one of them was holding a lamp down by his own leg with his hand over
most of it, and after about forty seconds Kimimaro got up.

*They are not in a hurry.*

*So they are not late and they are not worried, and this is a thing they
had decided to do and not a thing that has come up.*

---

He was outside for about four minutes and he did not look at the sky.

This has to be said plainly because it is the whole of him: he did not look
up, and he did not notice that he had not, and if you had asked him
afterwards what the weather had been he would have said *I did not take
it*, without any embarrassment at all, because he had been doing something
else.

What he did was count the men.

*Three here. One on the animal at the front, which I can hear and cannot
see. That is four.*

*And there is one about thirty feet off on the left who has not moved and
has not spoken and I can hear him because he shifted his feet twice in
four minutes and both times it was the same two feet in the same order.*

*That is five.*

*And there is one further off than that, and I have got him off nothing
except that the one on the left looked that way once.*

**Six.**

He was pleased with the sixth one. It was the best of them and it had come
off a man's head turning, and he had got it standing on a cart track in
the dark with his legs shaking.

---

Then he got the other thing, and it took him about a minute and a half, and
it is the only piece of work in the whole eleven days that was about
people and not about arithmetic.

*One of them is not one of them.*

He went at it properly because it did not sound like a finding.

*The three here talk to each other. Not much. They said about nine words
between them and every one of them was about the thing they were doing.*

*The one on the left has not said anything.*

*That is not it. A man on his own thirty feet off does not have anybody to
talk to.*

He stood on the boards and worked it.

*It is that they have not said anything to him.*

*Three men have been standing here for four minutes doing a job and there
is a man thirty feet away and they have not once said a word in his
direction — not an instruction, not a question, not the thing men say when
they want somebody to hold something.*

*You do that with a man who is above you or a man who is not with you.*

*And he is standing where he is standing, which is off the track on the
left with the ground behind him, and a man who is above you stands in the
middle.*

He put it in the place where things about them went.

> *Six. Five of them are one thing and the sixth is another thing, and the
> five know it.*

**It was the first true statement he had ever made about a relationship
between two people, and he was thirteen, and it had taken him four
minutes, and he had never once in his life been given the chance before.**

---

They gave him water standing up and he drank it, and then one of them said
something to another one and went away and came back with a bowl.

It was hot.

He held it in both hands and did not eat any of it for about ten seconds,
which is the closest thing to a reaction he had.

*That is hot.*

*The bowls in the room were not hot. Not one of them, in two years, was
hot when it came in.*

He ate it. It had barley in it and something else and he did not identify
the something else and did not try.

And then, holding the empty bowl, he had the thought, and he had it the
way he had everything, which was flat and in order and without any weight
on it at all.

*Warm water twice. A second blanket. This.*

*Somebody is keeping me in a condition.*

He turned it over once.

*Which is what you do with a thing you are going to use.*

**And that is as far as it went, because the next step is a question about
what people want, and he had never been given one thing to answer that
with, and he did not know that he had not.**

---

They put him back after about four minutes and the putting back is worse
than the taking out and neither of them meant anything by it.

He got in himself. Nobody made him.

He got his legs in and went down onto his side and put his arm where his

He had been in a box for twelve days and the box had done nothing but
travel, and travelling is a thing a box does with you inside it and does
not ask you about. This was different and he had it inside about half a
second, because a thing that rocks and comes back is a thing that somebody
has both hands on and is standing over.

*They are checking whether it is fixed down.*

He did not do anything. He did not push and he did not make a noise and it
did not occur to him to. Nobody had ever come to a place he was in and got
anything out of it for him, and he had no model in which a person on the
other side of a lid was a thing that could go either way.

Then the weight came off the tailboard.

And then somebody shouted, four feet away, very loudly, and it was a girl,
and he did not understand the words because they were shouted over cattle
and he was inside four inches of boarding.

*That was at us and it was not to anybody.*

He put it in the place where things about them went, and there was nothing
to attach it to.

---

The lid came off a long time after that — long enough that he had gone
through the whole of it twice and got nothing new — and it came off in
daylight, and the daylight was the first thing and it was not small.

He had seen the sun through a gap high on a west wall for two years and had
used it and lost it, and he had had four seams for twelve days.

This was the whole sky at once and it went into him like a hand.

He shut his eyes for about four seconds, which is the largest involuntary
thing his body did in the whole of that year, and then he opened them,
because there was something to look at.

---

There was a boy standing over him with a hand on the lid.

**He measured him.**

He did it immediately and he did it because it was the only thing he had
ever been able to do to a person, and it took about eleven seconds and he
got the following, and every single item of it is correct.

*Smaller than me. Eleven, twelve. Two years under.*

*He is standing square on to the box, not off to one side, so he is not
worried about what is in it.*

*Wet to the knee and dry above it. So he has been in the river and he came
out of it and he has been out of it long enough to be dry on top and not
long enough to be dry at the boot.*

*There is a great deal of blood on his right side and it is not his,
because a person with that much of their own does not stand like that.*

*And he has not said anything yet, and it has been about six seconds, and
he is looking at me the way I am looking at him.*

**He is counting too.**

That was the eleventh thing and it was the only one that surprised him.

*Nobody counts.*

*I have been looked at by about forty people in two years and not one of
them was doing this.*

---

Then the boy said:

"How long have you been in there?"

---

Here is what happened in Kimimaro's head in the second before he answered,
and it is the whole of him and it takes longer to write down than it took.

*That is a question.*

*That is a question and it is about a number.*

He had been asked one question in two years. A man in a passage had gone
past him with something under his arm and said *cold enough for you?* and
been gone before he had it, and he had built the answer over two days and
carried it eleven and nobody had asked again. And a man with a book had sat
on a stool in front of him for eleven minutes and opened his mouth and gone
over both his hands and had not asked him one single thing.

*That is twice.*

*And this is a question and it is about a number and I have got the
number.*

---

He gave him all of it.

He did not decide to. There was nothing to decide. Somebody had asked for a
measurement and he had the measurement and he had had nowhere to put it for
twelve days, and it came out the way water comes out of a thing that is
tipped over.

"Twelve days. Today is the twelfth. I have got it off the light in the
seams — there are four of them, one down each side and one at each end, and
they go grey and then they stop being grey, and I did not use it until it
had done four, because I have had three clocks and lost all three and I was
not going to use another one before it had held. It held. The first one is
a made day and not a counted one, because I did not have it at the start. So
it is eleven counted and one made and I have marked which is which."

He stopped.

Then, because it was true and it belonged with the rest of it:

"I do not know what day of anything it is. I have not known that since the
ninth month of the year I was eleven. I have got twelve days and I have not
got where they are."

---

It is the longest thing he had said out loud in two years and it was a
hundred and forty words about a light on a seam, and there was nobody in
the world who could have told you why he was proud of it, including him.

The boy standing over him said, "Right."

And then he took a book out of his coat, standing there, on a riverbank,
with six men behind him and a dead mule in the shafts, and opened it and
wrote it down.

Kimimaro watched him do it.

*He is writing it down.*

He had no framework for that at all and he did not try to build one. What
he had was the physical fact of it, and he took it whole and put it in the
place where things about people went, and it was the eleventh item and it
was the first one in two years that had gone in without a question attached
to it.

*He asked me a number and I gave him the number and he wrote it down.*

---

He got out of the box himself. Nobody made him and nobody helped him and
his legs went again and he knelt on the boards for about thirty seconds and
nobody said anything, and then he got up.

They went north at about half past ten.

He walked. Nobody offered him anything and he would not have known what to
do with an offer. He walked nine miles, which was further than he had
walked in two years by about nine miles, and he did it because they were
walking and because walking was what was happening, and at no point in nine
miles did anybody tell him to.

That is the part he could not get.

*Nobody has told me to do anything since the lid came off.*

*I have been walking for four hours on the strength of nobody stopping me.*

He worked at it and got nowhere, and put it down at about the seventh mile,
and picked it up again at the eighth, which he did not do with things.

---

And at some point in the afternoon he looked up.

He does not know why. He was not doing it on purpose and it was not the
sort of thing he did, and afterwards he could not put a mile on it.

It was very large.

That is the whole of what he had, and he stood in a road with it for a
moment and then went on walking, and the thing he had about it was not awe
and was not relief and was not any of the words that would be reached for
by somebody who had not been where he had been.

What he had was this:

*That is an instrument.*

*It moves and it comes back and it is the same one every day and it has
been going the whole time.*

*And I have not got a unit for it.*

He walked about eleven paces.

*I have got days now. I have got twelve.*

*And that has got all of them, and it has had them the whole time, and
nobody was keeping it and nobody could take it, and I have been counting
bowls.*

He did not feel robbed, because there was nobody to be robbed by.

He put it away in the place where the things went that he could not use,
which had never had anything in it before, and it was the first item, and
it was the sky.
