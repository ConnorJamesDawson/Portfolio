# Chapter 8, Scene 9 — The One With The Bad Ear

*Act 1, month 8, the twenty-fifth to the twenty-ninth. Ōtoma. Tadayoshi
Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,780 words.*

---

The sheet came on the twenty-fifth and he had it apart in about forty
seconds.

> *27th. Ide-no-shō. Two on the road from the east, one at the ford.*
> *You are at the ford.*
> *In on the 26th by the Hanase road.*

*The Hanase road.*

He read the rest of it and then went back to it, because the rest of it
was fine.

*In on the 26th by the Hanase road.*

*Which is the east road out of Hanase, which is the road I did eleven
miles of at three o'clock in the morning on the third of the seventh
month, in the rain, in a shirt.*

*And there is a man in that district who came into a lodging at two in
the morning and asked a keeper how many were in the house and their ages,
and he did it politely, and I never saw him, and I have got that on a
piece of ruled paper inside my shirt with four columns on it that are a
line.*

He got the paper out and read his own entry, which he had not looked at
since the third of the seventh month, and it said what he remembered it
saying.

---

He took it to Hōki at the bench outside.

"I'm not going in by the Hanase road."

Hōki did not look up from what he was doing.

"Say why."

He said why. It took about a minute and he did it in order: the date, the
window, the eleven miles, the question about ages, and the fact that the
description in that district was of a boy of about ten and that he was
still a boy of about ten.

Hōki listened to the whole of it without stopping him, which he always
did.

Then he said: "Right. Now the other side, and I'm going to give it you
properly, because you'll not get it off anybody else."

---

"It's five weeks and three days."

"I know."

"Nobody stands on a road for five weeks," said Hōki. "There isn't a man
alive who does that and there isn't an office that pays for it. He came
and he looked and he didn't find you and he went where he was sent next,
because he's got a list and you're on it and you're not the whole of it."

"That's probably true."

"It's almost certainly true. And the second thing is that the road is the
road. The thing that wants carrying is at Ide-no-shō and the man who
wants it is the other side, and there is one road down off that hill and
it is the Hanase road, and if you don't go in on it you go round, and
round is two days, and two days makes it the twenty-eighth, and on the
twenty-eighth there is nobody at Ide-no-shō to hand it to."

*So it is not a route. It is the job.*

"And the third thing," said Hōki, "is that you've got one line in that
book of yours."

Tadayoshi did not say anything.

"One. One night, one question, one coat. And you've built a road out of
it." He put the thing down. "That's not wrong. That's what a file is for
and you'll be right to keep it. But a file with one line in it will tell
you anything you want, because there's nothing in it to argue with."

---

Tadayoshi stood by the bench for a long time.

*He is right about all three.*

*Five weeks. The road is the job. And one line is not a file.*

*And I am right as well.*

*Because a man asked a keeper for ages in that district and the only thing
either of us knows is that he did it once, and he did not do it because
he was passing.*

He went through it four times looking for the version where both of them
were right and one of them still had to be acted on, and there was not one,
and that was the whole of it.

*There is not a right answer. There is a thing that is more likely and a
thing that is worse if it happens, and they are not the same thing, and
nobody can put them on the same page.*

"I'm not going in by that road," he said.

Hōki looked at him then. It was about the fourth time in seventeen days.

"That's your call and I'm not going to make it for you."

"I know."

"Then say it once and don't say it again," said Hōki, "because a man who
keeps giving you reasons is a man who wants somebody to argue him out of
it."

"I'm not going in by that road."

"Right," said Hōki, and picked the thing back up.

---

That was the whole of it.

Nobody said anything else about it. There was no argument and nobody was
cross with him and nothing at all happened, and by that evening the sheet
was gone off the bench and Tadayoshi did not know where it had gone and
did not ask.

He spent the twenty-sixth and the twenty-seventh on a bank at the top of
Ōtoma breaking about eleven square yards of it into grit, which was what
he had been told to do, and he got the time down to two and a quarter
seconds on the good ground and it stayed at three on the bad, and he
wrote both of those down.

He slept well on both nights, which he noticed afterwards and could not
do anything about.

---

Hōki said it on the twenty-ninth at the table, in the middle of eating,
without any lead-up at all.

"Ide-no-shō went."

*Went.*

"It went?"

"It went. The thing's where it wants to be."

Tadayoshi put his bowl down.

"Who was at the ford?"

Hōki ate for a bit.

"One of Yatate's," he said. "Sixteen, seventeen. You'll have seen him.
Bad ear on the left, the top of it's gone."

*Yes.*

*I have seen him twice.*

*Once at Ōtoma bottom end on the eleventh, going the other way, carrying
nothing, with his hands in his sleeves. And once outside the rope-walk on
the twenty-first when we came down, standing with two others, and he
laughed at something one of them said and it was the laugh of somebody who
has not got the joke and is joining in.*

*The top of the left ear is gone in a straight line, which is not an
accident, that is a knife or it is frost, and it was not frost because the
edge is clean.*

*And he had a way of standing with one foot turned out.*

*That is all I have got. That is the entire list.*

"And?" he said.

"He's not come back," said Hōki.

---

Tadayoshi sat at the table.

"Since when?"

"Since the twenty-seventh."

"That's two days. Two days isn't—"

"No," said Hōki. "Two days isn't. And it's the twenty-ninth and he was
four days from here and there's been a sheet through and it doesn't have
him on it."

*Right.*

He sat with it.

Then he asked the thing he should not have asked and he knew it was the
thing he should not have asked while it was on its way out.

"Was it the road?"

Hōki looked at him.

"I don't know," he said.

"But you must—"

"Nobody knows. Nobody's going to know. He was at a ford on his own on the
twenty-seventh and there is not one person on this earth who can tell you
what happened at it." He went back to his bowl. "That's what not coming
back is. It isn't a story with an end on it. It's a sheet with a name off
it."

---

He wrote it that night and it took him a very long time to work out which
book it went in.

He sat with both of them open on his knees in the dark — the ledger at
fifty-three, and the ruled sheet with the five columns and its one line —
and he could not do it.

*It is not a hunter entry.*

*The hunter book is for things that came for me and this did not come for
me. There is nothing in it that I can put in a column. I have got no when
and no who and nothing tried and no how long, and the only column I could
fill in is the last one and I have got no right to it.*

*Because I do not know that it was that. I do not know that it was
anything. He might have gone into a river.*

He put the ruled sheet away.

*And it is not a finding either.*

*A finding is a thing you know. This is a thing I do not know and am never
going to know, and I have got a rule about that, and the rule is that you
leave the half empty.*

He wrote it in the ledger, in the end, in four lines, and he ruled the
last one in two halves with a stroke between them and left the right side
of it blank, the way Meno used to rule a column when the total was not
known, the way he had done it on the nineteenth of the fifth month above
Nezu.

> *29/8. Ide-no-shō, the ford, the 27th. I refused the Hanase road on the
> 25th and I would refuse it again on the same evidence.*
>
> *They sent one of Yatate's. Sixteen or seventeen. The top of the left
> ear off in a straight line. He stood with one foot turned out. He has
> not come back and there is no report and there is not going to be one.*
>
> *I never found out what he was called and I am not going to, because
> nobody in this trade has a name. /*

He looked at the empty half of the last line for a while.

Then he wrote the finding underneath it, because he had a rule about that
too.

> **Being right did not remove the job. It only removed me from it.**

He read it back.

*And I would do it again.*

*And that is not the same as it being all right, and I have got nowhere to
put the difference.*

He put the book inside his shirt and lay down and did not sleep well, and
in the morning he went out and broke another eleven yards of the bank
into grit and got it down to two and an eighth.
