# Chapter 3, Scene 4 — Better

*Act 1. A room. A boy, age twelve. POV: the boy. ~1,370 words.*

---

The food came warm on the ninth day after the inspection.

It had never come warm. It came at the same times and it was the same
food and it had always been the temperature of the passage, which was the
temperature of everything, and on the ninth day the bowl was warm against
the sides of his hands and he sat with it for a while before he ate any
of it.

He counted the bowl. That did not tell him anything.

Then he ate it, and it was more than it had been.

---

*Eleven days since the inspection. Two days of warm.*

*More in the bowl on both of them. Not much more. The bread is the same
and the rest is more.*

He did not eat it faster. He worked that out on the first warm day and it
took him about as long as it took to eat: **if you eat more the same way
you ate less, nobody has to think about you.** If you eat it fast they
notice you ate it fast and then somebody wonders whether you were hungry
before, and then somebody has a question about a thing that had no
question in it.

So he ate it at the speed he had always eaten it, and it took longer,
which he liked.

---

The blanket came on the fourth day of warm.

The keeper brought it in under his arm and dropped it on the mat and said
nothing about it at all, and went out, and the bolt went over.

He did not touch it for a while.

He sat with his back against the wall and looked at it lying there on the
mat, which was not something he had ever had a use for, since a thing
lying on a mat was a thing you either had or you did not.

It was grey and it had been somebody else's and it was folded.

*Folded.*

Nobody had folded anything for him in two years. The bowl came, the
bucket went, the door shut. Folding is a thing you do to a thing that is
going to be somewhere.

He put his hand on it.

Then he unfolded it and put it round himself and sat against the wall
with it and was warm for the first time in a length of time he could not
give a number to, because the number was in the part of the count that
had broken.

---

The washing was the sixth day.

Two of them came in, and neither of them was the keeper, and they brought
a bucket and a cloth and something in a jar, and they did his hair and
his hands and the backs of his ears and under his nails, and they turned
him round twice, and one of them said to the other that the left one was
worse and the other one said aye, and they did the left one again.

They did not speak to him.

It took a long time and it was not gentle and it was not rough, and when
they had finished they took the bucket away and left the cloth, and he
folded the cloth, because it had been folded when it came in.

---

*Something has been decided.*

He knew that the way he knew the day was ordinary from the door. It was
not a thought he had to arrive at. Two years of a thing being exactly the
same, and then four changes in six days, and every one of them in the
same direction.

*Warm. More. A blanket. Washed.*

*Nobody does that to a thing they are going to stop keeping.*

He sat with his back to the wall with the blanket round him.

*So they are going to use me.*

---

He turned it over for a long time.

There was no other answer in it that he could find. He had gone through
the ones that were not it: they were not going to let him out, because
letting out is not a thing that happens and he had stopped putting it in
the list about a year ago. They were not moving him somewhere worse,
because you do not wash a thing for that.

*They have been keeping me for two years and keeping is expensive, and
they have decided what for.*

And then, because it was there, and because he had never once been able
to stop himself doing it:

*What for.*

*The wood, probably.*

*The man with the book comes and he lays the wood along it and he writes
what the wood says, and he has done that — five times? Six. Six times
that I have a count for and some before. He has never once told me what
the number is.*

*But he keeps coming back with the same wood. So there is a number he is
looking for. And people do not measure a thing six times unless they are
waiting for it to be a certain size.*

*So the number has got to where they wanted it.*

---

*So I am going to be used for something.*

He sat in the dark and let that go all the way through him, and it took a
while, and he had to be careful with it, because he had learned early
that a thing you let all the way through you is a thing that can take
something out on the way.

*Used is work.*

*Work is: being taken out of here, and being told to do something, and
doing it, and somebody looking at it afterwards and saying whether it was
right.*

He had never had that. He had had the wood and *he's fine*, which was
about a thing he was and not about a thing he had done.

*If I am used, then there is a thing I can be good at.*

*And if I am good at it they keep using me.*

---

He started the next morning.

He did it in the part of the day between the bowl going back and the
bucket, because that was the longest stretch when nobody came, and he did
it on the right forearm, where they always took the measurement.

Out, and hold.

He counted it. He had never counted it before, because there had never
been any reason to know; it was a thing you did when a man told you to
and put away when he told you to stop.

*Forty-one.*

He took it in and sat for a while and did it again.

*Thirty-eight.*

*Because the first one had a rest before it and the second one did not.
So the number is about what happened before it, and not only about the
arm.*

*Which means it can be practised.*

---

He did four a day for six days and he got the first one of the day from
forty-one to fifty-two.

He was careful about the aching. The deep ache came afterwards and lasted
about two hours and he did not want it there when anybody came in, so he
did the four in the long stretch and had it out of him by the bowl.

On the sixth evening he sat with the blanket round him and the ache
mostly gone and did the sums, which were the only sums he had.

*Fifty-two, and I have had six days.*

*If he comes at two hundred days again, that is a long time to get
better in.*

*He will lay the wood along it and it will be longer than it was, and he
will write it down, and he will be — *

He stopped there, because he did not have the word.

He had never in his life had the word for what he wanted the man with the
book to be, and he did not get it that evening either.

He sat against the wall in the dark with the blanket round him and the
ache going out of his arm and the count going forward from the bucket,
and he was, for the first time in about two years, looking forward to
something.
