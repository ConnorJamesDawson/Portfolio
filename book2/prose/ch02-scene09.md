# Chapter 2, Scene 9 — Six Things

*Act 1, month 3, the twenty-sixth. Nakadō, and the Ōgi road. Nozaki
Uzuki, age ten. POV: Uzuki. ~1,400 words.*

---

She had the bag packed for two days before she went, and unpacked it
twice, and it came out the same both times.

Bread and the hard cheese, wrapped. The waterskin. Her father's small
knife, which he had put on the table without saying anything about it,
and which she had taken without saying anything about it. The good coat.
Two pairs of the thick socks and no third pair, because a third pair was
weight and she had worked out that a wet pair dries against you at night
if you are sensible about it.

Her bokken, along the bottom of the cart, under the sacking.

And the mill's two parcels for Kayano, and the sixty in the small purse,
counted out in front of the miller's wife on the Thursday, which she had
asked for in exactly the way her father said and got without an argument.

She did not take the comb her mother had put out.

She looked at it for a while and then put it back on the shelf, because
a comb was for somebody who was going somewhere, and she was going to
work.

---

She got up at four.

The house at four was a different house. She had known that in a general
way and had never had a reason to find it out properly: the stairs made
noises in places they did not make noises in the daytime, and the room at
the bottom was full of the smell of a fire that had gone out, and
everything in it was a shape you had to identify.

She had her boots in her hand.

Her mother was sitting at the table in the dark.

---

Neither of them said anything for a moment.

There was no lamp lit. Her mother had her hands round a cup that had
nothing in it and had, by the look of her, been sitting there for some
time.

"Ma."

"Boots on outside," said her mother. "You'll wake him."

"I was going to."

"I know you were."

Uzuki stood in the middle of the room with her boots in her hand.

"I was going to leave a — I did leave a — it's on the — "

"I've read it."

"Oh."

"It's a good one," said her mother. "You spelled Kayano wrong."

"Did I?"

"There's no *u* in it."

"There is when I write it," said Uzuki.

Her mother made a small noise that in the daytime would have been a
laugh.

---

She got up and went to the stove.

Uzuki watched her do it and could not see what she was doing, because the
stove was the darkest corner of the room and her mother's back was in the
way, and there was the sound of the tin being moved and put down again.

Then her mother came back.

She took Uzuki's hand, and turned it over, and put something into the
palm of it and closed the fingers over.

It was a twist of cloth.

---

*It's the hundred.*

She knew it by the shape and the weight before she knew it by anything
else. She had carried it eleven miles in a cart in the second month with
her hand going to her pocket about every quarter of a mile to check it
was there.

*She's had it behind the tin since the Thursday and now she's giving it
to me.*

"Ma — "

"Don't."

"I said I wasn't going to need money, that's the whole — the whole thing
is that I'm not asking anybody for — "

"I know what the whole thing is."

"Then why are you — "

"Because you'll need boots," said her mother.

---

Uzuki stood in the dark with her fingers closed round it.

"I've got boots."

"You've got boots that'll do six days and you're doing nine, and there's
a wood on that road, and if you come out of that wood with a boot gone
you're a child in a town four days from home with no boot." Her mother's
voice was perfectly steady and did not go anywhere near the middle of
anything. "So that's not for the medic and it's not for food and I don't
want to hear about it again. That's for if something happens. And if
nothing happens you bring it back and put it where it was."

"All right."

"Say it back."

"It's for if something happens," said Uzuki, "and I bring it back."

"Good."

---

She did not ask where it had come from.

She thought about it — she thought about it for about a second and a half
at the door with her boots still in her hand — and she did not ask,
because she already knew where it had come from, and because her mother
had not said, and because there was something in the not-saying that she
did not want to open at four in the morning with a cart to get to Ōgi.

*She kept it since the second month.*

*She kept it and she wouldn't spend it on the roof and now she's given it
to me for boots.*

She got that far and stopped, and put the twist of cloth in the inside
pocket and buttoned it, and did not go any further, and it would be a
long time before she did.

---

Her mother did not come out.

She stood in the doorway with her arms folded while Uzuki put her boots
on on the step, and she said, "Nine days," and Uzuki said, "Nine days,"
and that was the whole of the goodbye, because her mother did not do the
other kind and never had and Uzuki would not have known what to do with
one.

At the gate she looked back once.

Her mother had gone in and shut the door.

There was a light on in the back room, which meant somebody had lit the
lamp, which meant her father was awake and had been awake and had let the
pair of them do it without him, because he had known perfectly well that
neither of them could have managed it with him in the room.

Uzuki looked at that for a second.

Then she put her shoulder into the cart handle and went.

---

It was about three quarters of an hour to the fork and it was still dark
when she got there, and she stopped at the milestone the way her father
stopped at the milestone, and did the thing he did, which was to walk
round the cart once and count the load out loud.

"Parcels, two."

"Purse."

"Bag."

"Skin."

"Sacking."

She got to the end and it came out six, and six was right, and she had
had six in her head since Tuesday.

Then she put her hand flat against her coat, over the inside pocket,
which was a thing she had done about four times since the gate without
noticing she was doing it.

*Seven.*

---

She stood at the fork in the dark with her hand on her coat.

The Ōgi road went off to the left, which she had done eleven times and
could have done asleep, and the north road went straight on past the
milestone into a country she had never been in, and there was a smell off
it of wet trees.

*Six things I'm carrying, and one I'm not supposed to need.*

*Four days that way. An afternoon in the middle. Four days back.*

*Nine, and she said if I'm eleven she'll stand in the road at Ōgi and
shout.*

She took her hand off her coat and put it back on the handle.

Somewhere behind her, a long way off, a cart was coming up out of Nakadō
with a lamp on it, and she thought about waiting for it, and worked out
that waiting for it would cost her half an hour and she would only pass
it again before the wood.

So she did not wait for it.

She went on past the milestone in the dark, with the cart making the
noise a cart makes, and she was over the first rise before it got light
and she did not look back from up there, because she had already looked
back once at the gate and you only get the one.
