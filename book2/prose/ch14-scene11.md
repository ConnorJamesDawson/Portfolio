# Chapter 14, Scene 11 — What Is Left

*Act 2, year 1, month 2, the twenty-fourth to the twenty-seventh. Ōtoma.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,550 words.*

---

They came into Ōtoma on the twenty-fourth in the afternoon and the whole
of the business took eleven minutes.

The receipt went in. The half he had been carrying since the fifth went
out. The fourteen hundred came across in a bag and he counted it in the
yard, which took four minutes, and it was fourteen hundred.

Ōtsuka said, "Right."

Tadayoshi waited about three seconds and then said, "Anything?"

"Not today."

That was the whole of it and it was the fourth time and he had stopped
expecting anything else, and he went up the ladder.

---

He did the ordinary column first because he did it first.

> *5–24/2. Ōtoma to the Kanaya and back. 4 crates + 1 roll. Signed off
> both ends.*
>
> *18 days. 1,400 paid.*
>
> *No corrections.*

He looked at *no corrections* for a moment.

*Eleven months and the second column is empty again, and it is empty
because it was a courier run and nothing happened, and a thing where
nothing happens does not produce corrections.*

*Which is not the same as being right.*

He left it.

---

Then he did the mule, and it took him about a quarter of an hour, and most
of that was working out where to put it.

*It is not a fee, it is not a cost of a job, and it is not kit, because
kit is a thing you carry and this thing carries.*

In the end he ruled it a page of its own in the operational column,
because it was a system and systems went there, and he put the four things
he had learned on it and the two he had not known he would have to learn,
and he put the feed at forty a day in the second month and put a note
under it saying that would be wrong in the fifth.

At the top of the page there was a space where the name of the thing went.

He wrote *the mule*.

He did not stop over it. There was nothing to stop over. It was an animal
and it had a load and a rate and a page, and a page has to be called
something, and *the mule* is what it was.

---

Then he did the money, and that was the one that took the evening.

He had never done it before.

That is the exact truth of it and it is worth setting down: he had kept a
ledger since the fourth month of the year before last and had entered
every fee and every purchase in it and could have told you any single one
of them, and he had never once totalled the thing, because there had never
been any point.

*It went up.*

*From the sixth month of last year to the twelfth month of last year it
went up every time I looked at it and I did not have to look at it, and
the only question I ever had about it was where to put it, and that was a
problem about boards and tins and a frame that rings wrong.*

He got the tin out and got the frame out and counted what was on him, and
it took forty minutes, and then he sat on the boards and did the
arithmetic of eleven months on a blank page.

---

It came out like this and he wrote it out like this, because he did not
know another way to look at anything.

> *End of the 12th month: 18,000, ab.*
>
> *Kit for her, 1/1: −1,140. Hat: −90.*
>
> *Kurihama, 9/1: +900.*
>
> *Sakabe, 11/1: +1,100.*
>
> *Hazaki, 17/1: −8,000.*
>
> *The mule, 6/2: −5,200.*
>
> *Feed and the road, 5–24/2: −830.*
>
> *The Kanaya, 24/2: +1,400.*
>
> **6,140.**

He looked at the bottom of the page.

*Six thousand one hundred and forty.*

*And eighteen thousand on the twenty-ninth of the twelfth month.*

*So it has come down by about twelve thousand in fifty-six days and it has
gone up by three thousand four hundred in the same fifty-six days, and the
three four is everything I have earned this year.*

---

He went round it four ways looking for the version where it was fine.

*Eight thousand of it is Hazaki and Hazaki was a one-off and is not going
to happen again.*

Which was true and did not help, because the eight thousand was spent
either way.

*Five two of it is the mule and the mule is an asset and it is worth about
five two and I could sell it tomorrow.*

Which was also true and did not help either, because he was not going to
sell it, and a thing you are not going to sell is not money.

*So the actual number is: I earned three thousand four hundred in fifty-
six days and I spent one thousand nine hundred and seventy on eating and
walking about, not counting the two large ones.*

He sat with that.

*Fourteen hundred over fifty-six days, and that is the whole of it, and
that is what a person makes.*

---

He turned back three pages to the eleventh of the first month.

The line was still there in the operational column with the date beside
it, in his own hand, and the small mark beside the line that meant *this
is a number I have made and not counted*.

> *Fees low + gaps long = 21 days away over the turn. Expect recovery in
> the 2nd month.*

He read it.

*The second month is over on Thursday.*

*And there has been one job in it and it was freight, and I have been in
this yard four times and been given nothing four times, and the man who
gives me things has said **not today** four times and has not once said
anything else.*

He sat with the pencil over the page for a while.

He did not take the mark off, because taking the mark off would have been
saying the sentence was right and it plainly was not.

He did not strike the sentence out either, because striking it out would
have been saying he had a better one, and he did not have a better one,
and he had spent an evening looking.

So he did the only honest thing available, which was to write the date
under it and one word, and the word was not an explanation and he knew it
was not.

> *27/2. No.*

---

Then he went to the front of the book and ruled a fresh page in the
ordinary column and put a heading on it.

The heading took him about four seconds.

> *WHAT IS LEFT.*

He put six thousand one hundred and forty under it and drew a line, and it
was the first page in that book that was going to go down.

---

He sat with the book open on his knee for a bit.

*I can head that one.*

He turned to the back, past the list, past the page with five lines on it,
to the page with a name at the top of it and one sentence under it and
nothing else, and looked at the white above the name where the heading
went.

Four minutes on the twenty-ninth of the twelfth month, and four more some
time in the first, and nothing.

*And I cannot head that one.*

*And it is the same book and the same hand and it took me four seconds to
call the other one what it is.*

He shut it.

---

Outside it was raining in the way it does at the end of the second month,
and the mule was under the lean-to at the end of the cooperage where he
had paid a man forty a night to have it, and she was asleep against the
wall by the boards with the hat over her face and the knife on the outside
of her belt.

He did the six minutes on the roll.

Then he sat for a while in the dark with his back against the wall and did
the thing he had done every night for eleven months, which was to work out
what the next thing was.

There was not one.

It was the twenty-seventh of the second month of the second year he had
been at this, and he had six thousand one hundred and forty ryō and a mule
and a girl and a page he could not head, and there was nothing on the
boards, and there had been nothing on the boards for thirty-six of the
last fifty-six days.

He went to sleep at about one and slept the whole night.
