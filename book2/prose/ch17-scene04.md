# Chapter 17, Scene 4 — Where I Have Put Things

*Act 2, year 1, month 4, the fifteenth to the eighteenth. North of Hane.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,900 words.*

---

He did the ground on the fifteenth because a fold with one way in is a
thing you either close or leave open, and leaving it open is a decision he
had never once made in his life.

It took him about five hours and he enjoyed all of it.

Nine articles, low, in the grass and the leaf, on the two lines a person
would actually walk if they came off the road and down. Four on the water
side. Two under the lip where the bank cuts back, because a man coming up
a bank puts a hand down and he had learned that at a ford six days
earlier and had written it in the book that night.

He set them the way he set them, which is that they do not stop anybody.
They take a foot, or a hand, or four inches of an ankle, and then the
person is on the ground and slow and he has four seconds he did not have
before.

He walked the whole of it twice at dusk and once in the dark and was
satisfied.

He did not tell anybody.

---

**It did not occur to him to tell anybody.**

That is the thing, and he could not have explained it on the fifteenth and
could explain it perfectly by about the nineteenth, and the explanation is
one line long.

*I have never had anybody on my ground.*

*I have laid that in about forty places and every single time the only
person who was going to be walking on it was me, and I have never needed a
map, because the map is that I put them there.*

---

She went for water at about half past nine on the eighteenth.

She had done it every night. She went down the water side because the water
side is where the water is, and she went in the dark because she could see
well enough and because she liked it.

He heard her stop.

Not a cry. A stop — the sound of somebody who was walking and is now not
walking — and then about four seconds of nothing, and then her getting up.

He was there in about eleven.

---

She was on one knee with her left hand held out from herself and her right
hand under the wrist of it, and she said, before he had said anything:

"That's yours."

"Yes."

"Right."

---

It was the outside of the left hand, below the little finger, and it was
about two inches, and it was deep enough to be a fortnight and not deep
enough to be a thing.

He got a light. She let him look at it and did not say anything while he
did, which she never did, and he noticed that she was not saying anything
and did not have anywhere to put it.

*She put a hand down.*

*Which is what you do on that bank, which is the whole reason there are two
under the lip.*

He sat back on his heels.

"How many did you go past."

"What?"

"Before that one. How many did you go past."

And she said, "I don't know," and then, because she is who she is and had
already worked out what he was asking, "and that's the thing, isn't it."

"Yes."

---

He did the arithmetic on the way back up and it took him about ninety
seconds and it is the coldest and most useful thing he did that month.

*She went down that side eleven nights and came back up eleven nights.*

*There are four on that side and two under the lip.*

*Twenty-two passages past six articles is a hundred and thirty-two
opportunities and she has had one.*

He got to the fire.

*One in a hundred and thirty-two.*

*So the ground is good. The ground is doing exactly what I built it to do,
which is that it does not catch a person who is going where they meant to
go, and it catches a person who puts a hand somewhere they had not
planned.*

He stood there for a second.

*And she was going where she meant to go, and she put a hand down, and she
is not a person who came off the road at night to see what was in a fold of
ground.*

> ## *The field has no judgement.*

*I have known that since I was nine. I wrote it down. I have never once
had a reason to mind it.*

---

He did the thing that fixed it on the nineteenth and it took him forty
minutes and it is the third page in that book.

He ruled it, and he put the heading on it in about one second, because it
was a thing and he can head anything that is a thing.

> *THE GROUND.*

Then he drew it.

The fold, the water, the lip, the way in, and the road at the top. Nine
marks with a number beside each. A note under each number saying what it
takes and from which direction. And a line down the middle of the sheet in
a different hand, because he did that when a page had two kinds of thing on
it.

He turned the book round and put it on the ground between the three of
them.

---

**And this is the part that cost him, and it is not the drawing.**

He had to say it out loud.

He got about nine words in and stopped, and started again, and got about
fifteen, and stopped again, and she waited, and the boy waited, and neither
of them said anything at all.

*What is wrong with me.*

He went at it while he was standing there, because he could not not.

*It is not that it is secret. I do not care who knows.*

*It is that I have never described it.*

*I have made it forty times and I have never once put it in words, because
words are for a thing somebody else has to do and this has never been a
thing somebody else has to do, and a thing you have never had to describe
is a thing you have not got the words for, and I have got about four
thousand words for other people's work.*

He looked at the page.

*And it is going to come out badly and I am going to have to do it anyway.*

He did it anyway. It took eleven minutes and it came out badly and every
word of it was accurate.

---

**The boy took about four seconds.**

Tadayoshi said, at the end of the eleven minutes, "So don't go past the two
stones on the water side unless I've said," and the boy said, "Yes," and
that was the whole of it.

And it held. Permanently, from that evening, with no further conversation
and no reminder and no near miss, for as long as they lived together.

Tadayoshi noticed on about the twenty-first and went at it for a mile and
got most of the way and stopped one step short.

*He has not been past the stones once.*

*Not once, and he goes down there for the water twice a day now because she
has started sending him.*

*And I did not give him the page and he did not look at the page.*

*He has got: **do not go past the two stones unless I have said.** That is
the whole of what he has and he has not wanted any more of it and he has
not tested the edge of it and he has not asked what happens if.*

He walked about four hundred yards with that.

*Because he does not do a thing that has not been said.*

*So a person who does not do a thing that has not been said is a person my
ground cannot catch, because the only thing my ground catches is somebody
moving in a way nobody specified.*

He stopped on the road.

*That is not a small thing.*

**And then he went the wrong way with it, and the wrong way was reasonable
and he could have defended it, and it cost him about two years.**

*That is a fact about him and it is going in the file with the other
fourteen and I have got nothing to do with it, because there is no version
of this where I am putting anybody on that ground on purpose.*

He went back to the camp.

---

She took four days and it was work and she did not say it was work.

She got the page off him on the twentieth and had it for about an hour and
gave it back, and then she walked the ground.

He watched her do it from the trees on the twenty-first without her
knowing, which he told himself was about the ground and was not.

*She is not walking it the way I walk it.*

*I walk it as a list — nine of them and where they are.*

*She is walking it as a route. She has done the water line four times and
she is doing it at different speeds, and now she is doing it carrying
something, and now she is doing it at a run.*

*She is not learning where they are.*

*She is learning where she can be.*

He stood in the trees.

*That is better than what I have got.*

*I have got a page. She is building a thing in her legs.*

**And he went back and did not tell her that either, and put nothing in the
book about it, and on the twenty-second of the fourth month a man came down
the road and there was no more time for any of it.**
