# Chapter 3, Scene 7 — Ask Three

*Act 1, month 3, the twenty-ninth — day four. Kayano. Nozaki Uzuki, age
ten. POV: Uzuki. ~1,590 words.*

---

She did the mill's two parcels first.

She had decided that on the road and she did not revisit it, because it
was the thing her father would have done and because a person who arrives
in a town four days from home with a job undone is a person who is going
to be thinking about the job. So she went in at the north side at about
two in the afternoon and found the cooperage the chit said and stood in
the yard until somebody signed it, and then she was carrying nothing but
her own bag and the empty cart, and Kayano could have the rest of the
day.

Kayano was very big.

She had thought Ōgi was a town. Ōgi had a mill and a market and about
sixty roofs and she had been walking through it since she was six and had
never once been lost in it.

Kayano had a *bridge with shops on it*.

She stood at the end of that for a while.

---

There was a man at the gate who told people where things were.

He had a stool and a hat and he had been there since before she came in,
and she had watched three separate people go up to him and get told
something, and she had noticed that two of the three had given him
something afterwards and that he had not asked them to.

*Don't ask at the gate.*

*Everyone at a gate makes their living telling strangers where things
are and half of them are wrong and the other half want paying.*

She went past him twice on purpose to see whether the second time cost
anything, and it did not, and she went and found a wall to sit on and
waited for six o'clock.

---

It was the longest three hours of the whole nine days.

She spent about an hour of it walking the streets on the near side of the
bridge in a rectangle, because a rectangle was a shape you could not get
lost inside, and she counted the turnings. She spent about half an hour
watching a man mend a barrel, which was interesting and made her think
about her father and then she stopped watching him. She spent the rest of
it sitting on the wall being extremely hungry and not eating, because the
bread had to do two more days.

At about ten to six a woman came down the lane with two buckets on a yoke
and went round the corner, and Uzuki got off the wall.

---

The queue was eleven women long and it was at a pump in a yard behind the
brewery, and it was exactly what Ine had said it would be, which was
people standing about in an order, waiting, with nothing to do.

She went to the back and stood in it as though she belonged there, which
worked for about four minutes, and then the woman in front of her turned
round.

"You've no bucket."

"No," said Uzuki.

"Well, you'll not get much water."

"I want to ask three people something," said Uzuki, "and take the answer
that comes twice."

The woman looked at her.

Then she laughed, and turned round to the woman in front of her and said,
"Here — this one's asking three of us and taking the one that comes
twice," and *that* woman turned round, and inside a minute Uzuki had the
back six of the queue looking at her with their arms folded and an
expression she could not read at all.

"Who told you that?" said the first one.

"A woman with a slate cart on the Sanzu road."

"Aye, well." The woman shifted the yoke on her shoulder. "Go on, then.
Ask."

---

"Where does the medic live? The proper one, the chakra one."

"Anzai."

"Anzai," said the second one, at the same time, more or less.

"Anzai," said a third, without being asked, from further up.

*That is three, and it is the same one three times, and it took nine seconds,
and the man at the gate would have had two thousand ryō off me and sent me to
a herbalist.*

"Where's his house?"

"Bottom of Kanmuri lane, blue door — "

"It's not blue, Setsu, it's *green*, it's been green four years — "

"It's blue underneath — "

"Bottom of Kanmuri lane," said the first woman, firmly, over the top of
it, "and there's a step down into the doorway and you'll go over it if
you don't look, everybody does."

"Bottom of Kanmuri lane, step down," said Uzuki.

"That's it."

"Thank you." She got her father's stub of pencil out and wrote it on the
back of the chit under Ine's line, standing up, and about four of them
watched her do it and one of them said something to another one that she
did not catch and that made them both look at her differently.

"You'll not get him tonight, though," said the third woman.

---

Uzuki stopped with the pencil on the paper.

"Why not?"

"He's not been home since Tuesday. He's up at the tannery."

"What's at the tannery?"

"A pit went in," said the woman, and did not elaborate, and the two in
front of her did not ask her to, and the whole queue moved up a place and
Uzuki moved with it without a bucket.

"Since Tuesday," said Uzuki.

"Since Tuesday. His girl's been up and down twice for things."

"Is he coming back?"

"He'll come back when he comes back, love," said the first woman, not
unkindly. "That's the trade. You've come a way, have you."

"Four days."

Something went round the back of that queue that Uzuki did not entirely
follow and did not enjoy.

"Well," said the first woman. "Bottom of Kanmuri lane. Mind the step."

---

She found it at about half past seven with the light nearly gone.

It was a green door and it had been blue and you could see where. There
was a step down into the doorway, deeper than a step has any business
being, and she went over it exactly the way she had been told she would,
and sat on the ground in the recess for a second saying a word her father
would have pretended not to hear.

Then she got up and knocked.

Nothing.

She knocked again, properly, four times, the way you knock at a house
where somebody might be at the back.

Nothing.

---

She stood in Kanmuri lane in the dark with her hand still up.

*He's at the tannery. Since Tuesday. That's four days.*

*I could go and find the tannery. It'll be on the water somewhere,
tanneries are always on the water because of the — because of whatever it
is, and I could ask, and everybody in this town seems to know where
everything is, and I could be there in twenty minutes.*

*And then I'd be a girl who turned up at a hole in the ground where a
thing had gone wrong, four days from home, at eight at night, and stood
about asking a man who has not slept since Tuesday to teach me
something.*

She stood there.

*He'd say no.*

*He wouldn't even say no properly, he'd say it the way you say it when
you're carrying something — he'd say "not now" and that's worse than no,
because "not now" is a thing you can't argue with and it's a thing you
can't come back from either, because the next time he sees you he's
already said it.*

*I get one go at it. That's the whole plan. The whole plan is that I walk
in and tell a busy person how to have a shorter week, and that only works
if he's got a week.*

She put her hand down.

*So I wait.*

*And I never find out if he'd have said yes tonight.*

She was, she noticed, getting extremely tired of that.

---

She slept in the doorway.

It was not as stupid as it sounds and she had thought about it: the
recess was a foot and a half deep and the step meant the wind came over
the top of her instead of into her, and there was a lane wall on one
side, and if he came home in the night he would fall over her, which she
counted as an advantage.

She put the sacking under her and her coat over her and the bag under her
head, and she put the little cart across the front of the recess on its
side, which made a wall about a foot high and made her feel enormously
better and would have stopped absolutely nothing.

The bokken went along her right side under the coat.

The last thing she did before she went to sleep was to put her hand flat
on the inside pocket, and the twist of cloth was there, and it had been
there for four days and she had not spent one coin of it, and she was
going to bring it back and put it where it was.

*Four days,* she thought. *And a step down into a doorway.*

*He'll come home.*

Somewhere on the other side of the bridge a bell went for something, and
Uzuki Nozaki went to sleep on the ground outside a green door that had
been blue, four days' walk from a house where her father was awake.
