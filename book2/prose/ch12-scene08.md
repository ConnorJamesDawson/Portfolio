# Chapter 12, Scene 8 — He Does Not Knock

*Act 1, month 12, the seventeenth, twenty-five past ten. The Kadono-dera.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~850 words.*

---

He went across at about twenty-five past ten with the ledger in his coat
and the question in his head and nothing else in it at all.

That is the whole of what he was carrying and it should be put down
exactly, because everything afterwards got attached to it by people who
were not there: **he was going to the hall to find out how a child is
secured on a road.**

Not to look at her. Not to decide anything. He had decided at ten o'clock
and the decision was four days down and the second ford and then east, and
he had written it in a book, and this was the last open line under it and
he did not leave lines open.

*Rope is no good in a town. Rope on a road is fine and rope through Ōgi is
a man being looked at.*

*So it is either something at night only, or it is that she does not want
to.*

*And I have not got anything for the second one.*

---

He noticed three things on the way across the ground and did nothing with
any of them, and he was correct not to, and he went over that for years.

**The bell did not go at half past.** It had gone at half past on the
sixteenth; he had heard it from the step with a bowl in his hands. *A
temple's bell is a temple's business and it is the seventeenth and not the
sixteenth.*

**Tokuji was not on the beds.** *He was in a doorway forty minutes ago
saying something out loud and a man of sixty with a bad hip who has said
something out loud is a man having a bad morning.*

**And there were no shoes at the south door of the long building**, where
there had been nine pairs the evening before. *So the winter people are
inside and somebody has told them to be, and it is the twelfth month and
it is cold and that is what you would do.*

Three observations, all true, all correctly filed, and not one of them
about the thing.

---

The hall had two doors, one at the east end and one on the south side, and
he had counted both on the evening of the sixteenth going round the
building the way Hōki had made him do it in the tenth month, which was
ways out and not ways in.

He went to the south one because it was nearer.

It was shut.

---

He did not knock.

There is nothing in that. That is the thing about it and it is worth
being flat about, because it became a sentence people said about him
later and they said it as though it meant something.

**He had not knocked on a door since the fourth month.**

Knocking is what you do when you are visiting, and he had not visited
anywhere. He had gone into a shutter-maker's at Anazawa and a lodging at
Sakabe and a house above a weir and a bank office at Aku and a room at
Fukae with two sheets in it, and in every single one of those the whole of
the work was in not being announced. Hōki had spent an afternoon on it in
the eighth month: *a door that opens is a door; a door that is knocked on
is a person*. He had written it down. He had it in the operational column
with a date on it.

And so at half past ten in the morning on the seventeenth of the twelfth
month, with a question about rope in his head and eighteen days on a
docket and nine hundred ryō due on the twenty-first, a boy of ten who had
been taught not to announce himself put his hand flat on a door and pushed
it open and walked into the hall of the Kadono-dera without making a
sound.

Nobody straightened up.

That is the other half of it and he never knew it, and neither did
anybody else in the building except one person: **if he had knocked, they
would have had four seconds, and four seconds is all it takes to stop
saying a thing.**

---

They did not stop saying it.

He came in on the end of it, two paces inside the door, with the light
behind him and the room still doing what it had been doing a second
earlier, and what he got in the first half-second was the shape of the
room, because the shape of the room is always the first thing.

Five men. The table on the left with the book on it. A window on the west
that was shuttered and was not a way out for anybody over about nine. Two
by the east door. One at the table. Two in the middle of the floor, and
between them, stood up, facing the wrong way, with her arms held —

And the one on the left was still talking, and finished what he was
saying, because he had started it before the door moved and a man finishes
a sentence.

Tadayoshi heard the end of it.
