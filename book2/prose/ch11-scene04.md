# Chapter 11, Scene 4 — Eleven Minutes

*Act 1, month 10, the twentieth. The top lane above the mill ground.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,500 words.*

---

The first thing that happened was the assessment, and it took about four
seconds and it came out clean, and that is worth saying because it is the
only part of the next eleven minutes that worked.

*Forty feet. He has seen me. He has stopped.*

*Light is coming but it is not come; at forty feet in this he has got a
shape and a size and he has got that I am not big. He has not got a face.*

*There is a wall on my right and a drop on my left and the lane is the
only thing either of us is on.*

*He is between me and nothing. I am between him and the house.*

That was all of it and all of it was true and none of it required
anything of him, and when it had finished running there was nothing else
queued behind it, and that was the first time in his life that had
happened.

---

The boy stood in the road with the bundle.

He did not shout and he did not run and he did not come on, and after
maybe half a minute he went four or five steps to the side of the lane
where the gate is and sat down on the stone by the gatepost with the
bundle on his knees, and stayed there.

*He has sat down.*

Tadayoshi did not move.

---

He ran the questions, because the questions were the thing he had, and
they went the way they always went, which was fast.

*One. Is it operational?*

*No.*

*Two. Does that matter?*

*No. Nothing about this is operational and nothing about the whole job
was operational and I settled that on the twelfth.*

*Three. Can I do it?*

He got to the end of the third one and it answered before he had finished
putting it, the way it always did, and the answer was yes and there was
nothing under the yes.

*Yes. He is seven and he is sitting down and he is forty feet away and
there is nobody on this road.*

*So all three are answered and I am still standing here.*

---

At about the third minute he started counting, and he did not decide to,
and he did not notice for another two.

When he did notice he stopped and looked at what he had, which was three
minutes he could not account for and a count running underneath them, and
he recognised it immediately, because he had written the shape down
eight days before in a loft with a book on his knee.

*It got decided once, by accident.*

*I have started a count. I did not put it there. It is there.*

He let it run, because stopping it would have been a decision too.

---

At four minutes he went at it properly, in the only order he had.

*What am I waiting for.*

He put that down and looked at it and it was a bad question, because a
person waits for something and there was nothing here to arrive.

*Not for him to run. He is not going to run; he has sat down. Not for
somebody to come up the lane, because if somebody comes up the lane it is
worse and not better. Not for light, because light is worse. Not for him
to say something, because —*

He stopped there.

*Because I would not answer it.*

---

At five minutes he made himself list what there was to do, and the list
came, and the list was the thing that went wrong, and he did not find out
that it was the thing that went wrong for another four weeks.

**Every item on it was an operation.**

*One. Go north. That is the route and it is walked and it costs nothing
and it is what I would do if he were not on the road.*

*Two. Go north past him. Same as one except it is eleven feet nearer and
he gets a face.*

*Three. Do him.*

He put the third one down flat with the others because it belonged with
the others, and he looked at it in the row with the rest and got the same
answer he had got in a loft on the eighteenth with a strip of paper in
his hand.

*He is not in the count.*

*Four in the count. He is not one of them. The fee is four at three and a
half and the paper had five names on it and one of them was not in the
money, and I do not do work that is not in the money, because doing work
that is not in the money is how you end up doing work you have not
costed, and I have never once done that.*

He looked at the third item for a while longer.

*And that is the whole of the reason.*

*That is the entire reason and there is not another one underneath it, and
I have gone and looked.*

---

At about seven minutes he thought of the money.

It arrived complete, the way things arrived when they were about
transferring something from one column to another, and for about fifteen
seconds it was a solution and he could feel it being a solution, which
was a thing he had felt perhaps four times in his life and always about
arithmetic.

*Fourteen thousand is in Ōtoma and it is mine on the twenty-second and it
is more than that house made in three years.*

Then it came apart, and it came apart the way everything he built came
apart, which was from underneath and all at once.

*He is seven.*

*A person of seven holding fourteen thousand ryō on a road is a person of
seven who has it for about a day, and then a grown man has it, and the
boy has had a thing happen to him twice instead of once.*

*And I would know that. I would know that on the twenty-second, sitting
in a loft, having done it.*

He put it down and it did not come back.

---

At nine minutes he understood that he was not going to produce anything.

He went through it once more to be certain, because being certain was
cheap and he had already spent nine minutes, and it came out the same
way: three questions answered, one list of three items, one solution that
was not one, and nothing else in the building at all.

*There is no fourth thing.*

*It is not that I cannot think of the fourth thing. There is no fourth
thing. I have got a road and a knife and fourteen thousand ryō and every
single one of those is a thing you do to a problem and he is not a
problem.*

*And I do not know what he is instead.*

---

At eleven minutes the boy moved the bundle from his knees to the ground
beside the stone and put both hands on the stone either side of him, the
way a child does when a wall is cold and he is going to be sitting on it
for a while.

Tadayoshi watched him do it.

Then he went north.

He went up the lane and past the gate on the far side of it, eleven feet
from the stone, and he did not slow down and he did not speed up and he
did not turn his head, and he got the boy in the edge of his eye for
about two paces, which was the whole of what he ever had: a bundle on the
ground, and a pair of hands flat on a stone, and a face turned to follow
him that he did not look at.

Nobody said anything.

He got to the top road and turned onto it and the count was still going
and he let it go for another minute and then it stopped on its own.

---

He walked until nine and stopped at a stream because his sleeve had gone
hard, and did the sleeve, and did what could be done about the boot,
which was less than at Anazawa because there was more of it and because
the welt had had it once already.

The light was up properly by then and it was a fine cold morning of the
kind you get in the back end of the tenth month, and there was frost in
the shadow on the north side of the bank and none on the other side, and
the water was very clear over the stones.

He sat on the bank with the boot in his hand and worked out that he was
about four hours ahead of where he had expected to be, because the
eleven minutes had cost him eleven minutes and the ninety seconds had
cost him ninety seconds and everything else had gone faster than he had
priced it.

*Nineteen days to the end of the month.*

*And I am going to be in Ōtoma tomorrow and there is nothing on the
boards until the twenty-fourth.*

He put the boot on wet, because there was no sense in carrying it, and
got up, and went on north.
