# Chapter 17, Scene 8 — Corrections

*Act 2, year 1, month 4, the twenty-fourth. A yard behind a rope walk at
Kadono. A boy, age thirteen. POV: the boy. ~1,900 words.*

---

He had four instructions and he had them in order and he checked them
twice, which took about nine seconds, and then he stood against the wall by
the gate and did nothing, which is what he does.

> *Stay here.*
>
> *If anybody wants you inside, you were told to wait.*
>
> *Don't answer anything.*

Three, and then the fourth, which was not said and which he had had since
the nineteenth of the fourth month and which sat above all of them.

> *Do not go past the two stones unless I have said.*

*That one is about a fold of ground four days south and does not apply
here, and I am keeping it anyway, because a rule you drop when it is not
convenient is not a rule.*

He stood in the sun.

*Day twenty-seven.*

---

The man came out at about twenty past nine.

He came out of the door at the far end, the one into the second building,
and he stopped in the doorway and did the thing a person does when they
come out of a dark room into a bright yard, which is to stand still for
about two seconds with their hand up.

Then he came across.

He was about forty-five. He had a bad left knee, which showed in the first
four paces and which he had plainly had for years and had stopped thinking
about. He had a pipe he was not smoking. And he came across a yard on a
diagonal towards a boy standing on his own against a wall.

*He has come out because of me.*

**He was right, and the reason was that a man of forty-five had come out
for four minutes' air and had seen a boy of thirteen standing against a
wall in the sun with nothing to do and had thought that was a shame.**

---

"You'll want to get out of that," the man said, and put his own back
against the wall about four feet along, in the strip of shade under the
eave. "It's not even ten."

Kimimaro did not move.

"Suit yourself."

He got the pipe going, which took a while, and did not say anything for
about a minute, and Kimimaro stood in the sun and waited, because there was
nothing else on.

---

**The first one was about the mule.**

"That your animal?"

*That is a question.*

He did not answer it.

He was aware of not answering it and it did not cost him anything, because
*don't answer anything* is an instruction and instructions are the easiest
thing in the world.

The man looked at him, and did not seem to mind, and after a second said:

"Fair enough."

And then, comfortably, to himself and the yard:

"Not much of an animal anyway. It's carrying too much for its shoulders."

---

**And Kimimaro said: "It is not. It is rated at about eighty and it was
carrying ninety on the ninth and it came in sound."**

---

He would like it noted — and there was nobody to note it to, and he did not
know it was worth noting, and this account is doing it for him — that he
did not answer a question.

*He did not ask me anything.*

*He said a thing and the thing is wrong.*

*A wrong thing is not a question and correcting one is not answering one,
and I have never let a wrong measurement stand in my life, and nobody has
ever told me to.*

---

The man took the pipe out of his mouth.

"Eighty," he said.

"About eighty. He has a page for it. There are four readings on it now."

"Has he."

"Yes."

The man put the pipe back in and looked out across the yard for a while and
Kimimaro stood in the sun.

**And then he did it again, and he did it because he had enjoyed the first
one.**

"You'll be his brother, I suppose."

---

"No."

"No?"

"We are not related. I have been with them since the ninth of this month.
Before that I was carried in a case for twelve days and before that I was
in a room for two years."

---

The man did not say anything for about four seconds.

Then he took his back off the wall, and came about two feet closer, and
came into the sun, and it did not occur to Kimimaro that any of those were
things.

"Right," he said. "Sorry. I'd got that wrong."

"Yes."

---

**The third one was the one that finished it and the man did not know he
was doing anything and neither did Kimimaro.**

He said — kindly, and this is exactly what he said and it went into the
sheet in these words —

"You're a bit thin for a lad your age."

---

*That is not a question.*

*And it is not wrong.*

*It is not wrong and it is not right either, because he has got the fact
and he has not got the cause, and a fact without a cause is a thing that is
going to be wrong the next time somebody uses it.*

**And he had had, since he was eleven, exactly one thing in the world he
could give anybody that was true and checked and complete, and nobody had
ever wanted it, and a man in a yard had just come within one step of it,
and there was no reason on earth not to finish the step for him.**

---

So he told him.

He told him for about three minutes and he did it the way he had done it
for her on the fifteenth, flat and in order and without one pause, because
he had done it once now and the second time is always faster.

He told him the name of his clan and what the clan is, and what his body
does, and the word for it, which the man did not know either.

He told him about the pieces. The first at eleven. Five in his shirt, in
thumbs, largest first. The sixth through on the fifth day of the box.

He told him the seventh has not started and that this matters, because the
seventh is the reading that finishes the instrument.

He told him about the halves, and the shape, and that seven more is the
shape and the size is dead because the size is in bowls.

And then he told him the last part, because it was the honest part and
because leaving it out would have made the rest of it read better than it
was.

"So I have got the shape and not the size," he said, "and the best I have
is that it is seven more, and they get shorter, and I do not know how much
shorter. It is not long. I have been wrong about it once and it was in the
direction of too much."

Then he stopped, because he had finished, and looked at the man, in case
there was another one.

---

The man was standing about four feet away in the sun holding a pipe that
had gone out.

He said, "Christ."

Kimimaro did not have anything to do with that. It was not a question, it
was not an instruction, it was not about his body and it was not a thing
that needed doing, and he went through all four in about two seconds and
came out with nothing, so he waited.

The man looked at him for what Kimimaro afterwards put at eleven seconds,
which is the longest anybody had looked at him since a man with a book.

Then he said, "Right," and, "Thank you," which Kimimaro also had nothing to
do with, and went back across the yard on the diagonal with his bad knee
and did not stop in the doorway on the way in.

---

**He was inside for six minutes.**

Kimimaro timed it, because timing is what he has, and he had no particular
reason to time it and did not need one.

He filed the whole exchange while he waited, because filing is also what he
has, and what he filed is this and every item of it is correct.

*A man of about forty-five, bad left knee, four minutes' air.*

*Came out for himself and came across because of me.*

*Asked one question, which I did not answer.*

*Said three wrong things and I corrected all three.*

*And the third one was not wrong, and I gave him the cause anyway, and I
have thought about that for six minutes and I would do it again, because a
fact without a cause is going to be wrong the next time somebody uses it.*

He put it in the place where things about them went.

*He is the first person who has ever wanted it.*

**That is the only sentence in the whole of the twenty-fourth of the fourth
month that anybody in that yard got completely wrong, and it was him, and
it was about kindness, and it was the closest he had ever come to being
pleased about a person.**

---

At about half past ten the door at the far end opened and people started
coming out of it.

He counted them, and he counted the order, and he counted the gaps.

And he knew, standing against a wall by a gate in the sun with his hands in
front of him, about eleven minutes before anybody came and told him, and
he did not say anything about it then or afterwards, because saying things
is not a thing he does.

*They are not keeping us.*
