# Chapter 9, Scene 4 — Ine's Sister

*Act 1, month 9, the fourteenth. Kuriya's kitchen, Nakadō. Nozaki Uzuki,
age ten. POV: Uzuki. ~1,590 words.*

---

It was the best one she had ever done and she knew it at about the
seventieth.

That did not happen. You cannot tell during; that is the first thing and
the fourth thing and the thing he said four times, and she had never once
in five months had any idea what was going on under her hands until forty
seconds after they came off.

But at about the seventieth on the fourteenth of the ninth month she went
into a gap and came out of it and something about the way the knee sat
under her palms was different, and she did not push, and she did not get
excited about it, and she finished the twenty at the end at exactly the
same size as the first twenty, and took her hands off, and counted the
forty.

Kuriya bent it.

Then he did the thing he had started doing in the eighth month, which was
to get up without putting a hand anywhere, and this time he did it and
then stood there for a second with his eyebrows up.

"Right," he said.

"What?"

"That's the best it's been since about the year before last." He walked to
the door and back. "That's not three times a week, that's — what have you
done different?"

"Nothing."

*Nothing. Nothing at all. It's just been three times a week since the
twenty-third of the eighth month and it's built up, and the thing about it
building up is that you can't see it while it's building up, you can only
see it after, and I have been putting hours into this knee since the
fourth of the seventh month and it has not once given me a single sign
that it was working until today.*

*Ten weeks.*

*Ten weeks before you can tell.*

She sat back on her heels on the floor of his kitchen and let herself have
it, because she did not get many.

---

"It'll be worse in the winter," said Kuriya.

"I know."

"Every year. Comes on about the eleventh month and it's the eleventh and
the twelfth and the first that are the bad ones, and I get about four
months of not being able to get up that hill without stopping at the
second turn."

"You stop at the second turn now."

"I stop at the second turn now for about the length of a sentence," said
Kuriya. "In the twelfth month I stop for about a minute and I do it where
there's a wall."

*Right.*

"Then it wants four," said Uzuki.

"Four a week?"

"Four a week from the beginning of the eleventh month, and I'd rather do
five. If ten weeks of three has done this then four through the winter
holds it, and holding it through the winter's worth more than getting it
better in the spring, because you can't get a thing better in the winter,
you can only stop it going."

Kuriya looked at her.

"Where'd you get that?"

*From a man in a kitchen in Kayano who told me eleven things in three and
a half hours and made me say all of them back.*

"I worked it out," she said.

"Four, then," said Kuriya. "From the first of the eleventh. And I'll pay
you for four and I'll not have an argument about it."

"All right."

"And that's month end same as always, and it's the fourteenth, so you've
got about a half and a bit owing and I'm not paying it before the
thirtieth, so don't ask."

"I wasn't going to ask."

"No," he said. "You never do. Ine's boy asks."

---

She got up off the floor and did the thing she had come up the hill to do,
and she had had it ready for three days.

"Can I ask about your Ine's sister?"

"What about her?"

"You said she holds a house at Ōgi and she's been on her own eleven years."

"Kiku," said Kuriya. He got the box down. "Older than Ine by about four.
Husband went in the second year of the — no, it was before that. It was
before that. Eleven years, anyway, and she's held that house the whole
of it and she's not been a day late in her life."

*Kiku.*

She put it away.

"What's the trade?"

"Laundry," said Kuriya. "Three inns."

*Laundry.*

"Three inns," said Uzuki.

"Three. She does the beds for the Kanaya and the one on the bridge and the
one at the top whose name I never remember, and she's got two girls in and
she's had the same three inns for about nine years." He counted the money
out onto the table. "That's a trade. Nobody in Ōgi is going to look at
Kiku and think about a woman on her own, they're going to think: that's
who does the beds."

---

*That's who does the beds.*

She stood in his kitchen with that.

*That's the whole of it. That's what he meant on the twenty-third. It's
not the woman, it's the trade — and the trade is not the thing you can do,
it's the thing everybody says about you when you're not there.*

*Kiku can put her name on a paper because when a man with a house reads
the name he thinks: beds.*

*And if he reads mine he thinks: that's Sae's girl.*

"How did she start?"

Kuriya put the lid on the box.

"She started doing it for one," he said. "The one at the top, I think, the
one I never remember. Just her, and about nine beds, and she'd have been
doing it for nothing much. And then that one told the other two, because
they all talk, and they'd rather have somebody who's already doing it than
find somebody."

*One.*

*One person, and then the one tells the others.*

She stood absolutely still in a kitchen with a hand on the back of a chair.

*That's it. That's the entire thing. You need one person to have you and
then the one person does the rest of it for you, and it's not the work,
it's not the hands, it's not the ten weeks — it's one person saying your
name to somebody else.*

*And I have got one person.*

*I have got exactly one person in the world who has ever paid me for
anything and who knows what I can do and who has watched me do it three
times a week for ten weeks and has just told me it is the best his knee
has been in two years.*

She did not say anything.

*And on the twenty-third of the eighth month he told me, in this kitchen,
without me asking, that he would like to send me to about six people and
that he is not going to, and it isn't because he doesn't rate me.*

*And he's right. He was right then and he's right now and I have not
thought of a single thing since that gets round it.*

**So the one thing I need is the one thing the one person I have got has
already told me he will not do.**

---

"You've gone quiet," said Kuriya.

"I'm thinking about something."

"Mm," he said, and did not ask what, which was one of about four things
that made him bearable.

She picked her way out of it, because she had got very good at picking her
way out of things in front of people that year, and asked the last one.

"How long before people know you?"

"I told you. Years."

"How many?"

Kuriya thought about it properly, which he always did when she put a number
on something, and which she had noticed and had never remarked on.

"Kiku had the one inn for about two years before the second one came," he
said. "And it was another eighteen months before the third. So call it
four before anybody in that town would have said she was the laundry.
Four, and then eleven, and she's forty-something now."

*Four.*

*And I'm ten and four months and I've got four years and seven months
before anybody will put a name on a paper for me, and Kiku had four before
anybody said she was the laundry.*

*So they run at the same time. They have to run at the same time. I have
not got four and then four.*

*Which means the first one has to start now.*

*And the first one is the thing that can't start, because the first one is
somebody saying my name to somebody else.*

She looked at the table.

*So it's Ōgi.*

*It's got to be Ōgi or somewhere like it. Somewhere where nobody knows me
and nobody's going to say that's Sae's girl, and I turn up and I do it for
one person for nothing much and I let the one person tell the others.*

*Four years and seven months.*

"Right," she said. "Thank you."

"For what?"

"For telling me about the beds."

Kuriya laughed at her, which he did about once a fortnight and which she
liked more than she was ever going to say.

"Get on home," he said. "And do the four in the winter and don't let me
argue you down to three when it's raining, because I will."

"I won't."

"Thursday, then."

"Thursday," said Uzuki, and went out, and down the hill, and it was about
five o'clock and the light was going early now and there were three days
left.
