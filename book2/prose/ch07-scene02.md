# Chapter 7, Scene 2 — Nine People

*Act 1, month 6, the twenty-ninth. Nakadō. Nozaki Uzuki, age ten. POV:
Uzuki. ~1,670 words.*

---

There were nine people, counting the priest and counting her.

She had it before the sake was out, because she always had it before the
sake was out, and she went round the yard once with her eyes and got
nine, and then went round again to see whether she wanted to make it
eight by leaving out the priest, and decided she did not, because he had
come and he had not been paid and both of those were true at once.

*Nine.*

*Iwasa and his wife. Ine. Kuriya. Fumi, which I did not expect and which
was kind. Tazu. The priest. Him. Her.*

*And me is ten. So nine and me.*

*Dad would have had forty.*

She put that one down and stood on it, because it had come out of
nowhere in the middle of counting and she was not going to have it in
her face in a yard.

---

It was in Shimoda's yard and not the shrine, because the shrine was for
the words and the words took about four minutes, and everything else
happened in a yard with a brick shed at the end of it and a mule looking
over the top half of the door.

Somebody had swept it. Somebody had put two planks on trestles and put
Iwasa's wife's cloth over them, which Uzuki knew was Iwasa's wife's cloth
because she had seen it on a line for four years.

The sake was in three cups and went round. There was rice. There was a
thing Ine had made that was mostly egg and was very good and there was
not much of it.

Her mother wore the grey and not anything new, and Uzuki had known she
would, and had known why, and had watched her put it on that morning
without saying one word about it in either direction, which was, she
thought afterwards, about the most grown-up thing she had ever done.

---

She counted the other thing as well.

She did not decide to. She had never once decided to; it ran on its own
and it had run on its own since she was about seven, and it ran in a yard
with nine people in it the same as it ran anywhere else.

*Tazu. Four seconds. When Mother turned round.*

*That's the third time this year, and the other two were both in the lane
and both when I was carrying.*

*Iwasa's wife looked at me for a long time and it was not that. That's
her working out whether I'm all right, which she's been doing since the
fourth month and which she has never once asked me about.*

*The priest, no.*

*Kuriya, no. Kuriya looks at the ground and at people's hands.*

*Ine, no.*

*Fumi, no, and Fumi has still not said anything to me, and she came, and
she walked forty minutes to come, and I do not know what to do with
that.*

*Him — *

She stopped that one before it finished, which she noticed at the time
and did not examine, and went and stood by the wall.

---

The thing she had come to do she did about halfway through, when the sake
had been round twice and the yard had got loud enough that a question
would be heard by everybody but would not be an event.

That was deliberate. She had thought about the timing for four days.

"Are we still going to Dad's?"

The yard did not go silent, because yards do not, but it did the thing
where about six conversations get quieter without stopping.

Shimoda had a cup in his hand and he put it down on the plank first,
which she noticed.

"Every month," he said. "Same as you've been doing. Sixth or thereabouts,
whenever the weather is."

"Right."

"And I'll come if you want me to and I'll not come if you don't." He said
it at ordinary volume, to her, in a yard with eight people listening.
"And you can change your mind about that as often as you like and you
don't have to tell me which way round it is this month. You can just say
at the gate."

*Oh.*

Ine said something to Iwasa's wife that Uzuki did not catch. Somebody's
eyes went, and she thought it was Iwasa's wife and she was not sure.

Her mother was looking at the cloth on the plank.

"Thank you," said Uzuki.

"Nothing to thank," said Shimoda, and picked his cup back up, and that
was the end of it, and the yard came back up to volume inside about four
seconds.

---

She stood by the wall afterwards and had it out with herself, properly,
because she had built the whole thing and it had come out and she was not
going to pretend she had not.

*I asked that in front of everybody on purpose.*

*I asked it in front of everybody because in front of everybody he has
to answer well, and if he answers badly then eight people have heard him
answer badly and it is on the record and I can use it.*

*And if he answers well then it is on the record that he answered well.*

*I knew both halves of that on Tuesday. I knew both halves of it and I
did it anyway, because I thought the first one was more likely.*

She looked at her feet.

*And he answered well. He answered better than well. He answered it the
way you would answer it if you had thought about it before today, which
means he has thought about it before today, which means he has been
sitting in that house working out how to be all right with a girl he does
not know.*

*And now I cannot un-ask it.*

*There are eight people in this yard who watched a man be decent to me
about my father's grave, and every single one of them is going to
remember it, and every single one of them is right.*

**You cannot put a thing on the record and then only keep the half you
wanted.**

---

The last hour was the part she was not ready for.

Ine got the priest onto something about a roof and the priest got
animated about it, and Iwasa said the thing he said about his brother
every single time there was sake anywhere near him, and Kuriya laughed,
which Uzuki had never heard and which was a bad noise and which she liked
enormously.

And at some point in the middle of it her mother said something.

Uzuki did not hear what it was. She was four feet away with a cup of
water and she was watching the mule and she heard the shape of it and not
the words, and Ine put her hand flat on the plank and laughed, and Iwasa
said "she's not wrong," and her mother put her hand up to her mouth for a
second the way she used to.

*She's not doing the thing.*

Uzuki stood very still with her cup.

*She's not doing the thing with her hands.*

For ten weeks her mother had sat at that table every night at the box and
done a thing with her hands that Uzuki had never given a name to and had
been watching without knowing she was watching: the left one flat, and
the right one on top of it, and then the right one going out to the pen
and the left one staying exactly where it was, over and over, for an
hour, every night.

*It's the counting hands.*

*She does it when she's counting and she does it when she's not counting
and she's been doing it since the fifth of the fourth month and she is
not doing it now.*

*She's been not doing it for about an hour.*

---

She stood by the wall with her cup and worked at it and it would not come
apart.

*Right. Say it.*

*My mother has not been frightened for about an hour, and I am watching
it happen, and I do not know what to do with it at all.*

*I could be glad.*

*I am not glad. I'm — *

She got as far as the word and stopped, and it took her about two more
years to be able to finish that sentence, and when she could finish it
the sentence was: *I am furious, and I am furious because it turns out
that a roof and a man with two fields buys it, and that means it was
always buyable, and my father could not buy it, and he worked until he
could not stand up.*

At ten, in a yard, with a cup of water, all she had was that something in
her chest had gone hot and that it was not a nice thing and that she was
not going to let it out in front of nine people.

So she drank the water and looked at the mule.

---

Later, when the planks were coming down, her mother came over to her by
the wall.

She did not say anything for a bit. Then she said:

"He's put the top of the door open for that animal every morning since
the fourteenth."

"I know."

"He gets up to do it. It's not on his way anywhere. He gets up and goes
out and does it and comes back in."

*She's telling me something.*

*She's telling me a thing she has decided is evidence.*

"He said he would," said Uzuki.

"Yes," said her mother. "He did."

And then she went to help Ine with the cloth, and Uzuki stood by the wall
in the dark with the empty cup, and the mule put his head over the top of
the door and looked at the yard, and there were eight people in it who
had all had a nice afternoon.

*Nine.*

*Nine and me.*
