# Chapter 14, Scene 5 — He Was Bred For More Than He's Ever Done

*Act 2, year 1, month 2, the sixth. A yard at Sakabe. Nozaki Uzuki, age
eleven. POV: Uzuki. ~2,150 words.*

---

She knew what it was in the first four words.

Not the sentence. The *shape* — a man beginning at the head end of an
animal with somebody standing beside him who is going to pay, and a
particular kind of unhurriedness in it, which is the unhurriedness of a
person who has decided to be fair and is enjoying being fair.

She had heard it once, in a yard at Nakadō, in the sixth month of the year
before last, with a halter in her hand.

She stood four feet behind Tadayoshi with her hat on and did not move.

*Right.*

*This one, then.*

---

She did not go anywhere.

That is worth being exact about, because there is a version of this in
which a girl who has been the mule stands in a yard and something happens
to her, and it is not what happened. What happened was that a thing she
had gone over four hundred times in the dark started happening again in
front of her, out loud, at the ordinary speed, and she found that she knew
every part of it.

She got the first lie before Deguchi had got to the feet.

*He said seven.*

*The groove has started. It comes down from the top of the corner tooth
and it starts at about ten and it is a third of the way down that one, and
Shimoda showed my father the groove on Genzō in the fifth month and told
him it was the only part of the mouth you cannot argue with.*

*So he is ten. Maybe eleven.*

She got the second one at the shoulder.

*He is saying that muscle is what he was bred with.*

*That is not what he was bred with. That is what you get from four or five
years of doing it and then being stood off. He has been worked hard and
then rested, and a man does not stand a good mule off at ten unless
something happened, and what happened is not in this speech.*

She had both of those inside three minutes and she stood in the yard with
them and did not do anything at all, because it was not her transaction.

---

Then Tadayoshi put his hand on the shoulder.

She saw what it cost him before Deguchi did.

*Oh.*

*He has just —*

She did not have a word for it and did not need one, because she had
watched him do the opposite of it in three shops in the first month: he
had never once picked a thing up first. He had stood, and looked, and said
a number, and the number had been the first thing about him that anybody
in the room learned.

And he had just put his hand flat on the most expensive object in the yard
and left it there for two seconds.

*He wants it.*

*And now the man knows, and it is going to be seven and a half, and he
knows that too, and he is standing there.*

She watched him take his hand off and step back and say nothing.

*He has stopped.*

*He has never stopped. He has not stopped once since I met him. He does
not have a stopped.*

---

"Can I?"

She said it before she had finished deciding, which was becoming a habit
and which she was going to have to look at some time, and afterwards she
could not reconstruct whether she had planned it in the two seconds or the
twenty minutes.

He said, "Yes."

And stepped back and put his hands behind him, and that was the whole of
it, and it was the first time in her life that anybody had handed her
something.

She had about four seconds to decide how to stand.

---

She did not go near the animal.

She worked out why afterwards and at the time it was not a decision, and
the reason was not what anybody would have guessed, and she went over it
that night and got it exactly.

*Because he has done it and it cost him two thousand.*

*And because I already know everything I need to know about it and I have
known since the second minute, and going over there would be telling him I
did not.*

*And —*

She stopped, behind the wall, later.

*And because I am not putting my hand on it while a man is saying what it
is worth.*

---

She let him finish.

That was the second thing and it was harder than the first, because he had
another ninety seconds to run and every one of them was a thing she could
have taken apart, and she stood in the middle of a yard with two lies in
her hand and let a man talk.

*Do not go in the middle of it.*

*Because he is building something, and if I hit it now he will just build
a different one, and if I let him finish he cannot.*

*Sixteen days at Kuriya's. She never once went in the middle. She waited
until my mother had said the whole of it and then said one thing.*

And then Deguchi got to the end of the shoulder and said it.

"He was bred for more than he's ever done."

---

She had it whole.

Not the sense of it. The *whole thing*, in order, in a voice, with the
pauses in the same places, and it came up out of her the way water comes
up a well when you have already got the handle turning:

> *He's sound. He's not lame and he's not going to go lame this year on
> those feet, they're good feet, your husband did them. He's fourteen,
> which is not old for a mule but it's not young, and I'd get five year
> out of him and the last two of those he'd be a pack animal and not a
> shaft animal. He'll not back. That's not a fault in him, it's that
> nobody ever made him, and I'm not going to make him at fourteen. He's
> quiet with a child on his head, which is worth something to me because
> I've got two. He eats a lot. That's the shoulder — big through there, he
> was bred for more than he's ever done.*

She stood in a yard at Sakabe with a dead man's speech in her mouth.

*He said the same sentence.*

*Word for word. The same eight words, in the same place, at the shoulder,
after the feet and before the price.*

*So it is not his. It is not Shimoda's either. It is the speech, and
everybody who does this has got it, and Shimoda did it honestly and this
one is doing it to me.*

She heard herself start talking.

---

She used it.

She did the whole thing in his own order and turned every item in it round
— the age off the groove, which stopped him for two seconds; the near fore
that had been done in a hurry and which he had told them about himself and
which she gave back to him as three months rather than a fault; the
shoulder, and what four years of work and a year of standing does to a
shoulder, and the question of what a man stands a good mule off for at ten.

She did not say *you are lying*. She never once said it.

She said the true version of his speech, with the pauses in the same
places, at the same speed, and let him hear it.

And at the end of it she said what she would give, and it was the only
number she said in the whole eleven minutes.

Asked seven thousand five hundred.

She paid five thousand two hundred.

---

The headcollar came afterwards.

That was not luck and it was not cheek and she had been holding it since
the fourth minute, and she waited until the money was out and Deguchi had
his hand halfway across, which is a moment in which a man has already
finished the transaction in his head and will pay a small amount not to
reopen it.

"And the headcollar and the rope."

He said, "Aye, go on," and went and got them, and was not annoyed, and
that was the point, and she had watched Kuriya do exactly that to her
mother over a set of pans in the fifth month of the year before last and
had not understood it at the time and had understood it about a week
later, on a road, with a cart.

---

She got behind the wall at the bottom of the yard afterwards and sat down.

It was about a quarter of an hour and she was not sick and she did not
cry, and she had half expected both and had made a small allowance for
them in the plan, and neither arrived.

What arrived was the list.

*I did it.*

*I did the whole of it and it worked and it took eleven minutes and I got
two thousand three hundred off a man who does this for a living, and I am
eleven.*

She put her hands flat on the ground.

*And I liked it.*

She made herself have that one properly and not go round it.

*I liked it while it was happening. Not afterwards — afterwards is this.
While it was happening I was — there is not a word. I was the best I have
ever been at anything and I knew it while I was doing it, and there was a
man in front of me who could not do anything about me, and I have never
had that, not once, not for a second, in my whole life.*

---

She sat with it and went at the next part, because there was a next part
and she had felt it coming since the shoulder.

*And it is the same speech.*

*It is the same speech that was done in front of me at Nakadō and the same
one that was done to me on a hall floor on the twenty-eighth of the tenth
month by a man with a book who wrote after my name and my age and not
after Nakadō.*

*And I have just done it.*

She turned that over for a while and it did not go anywhere and she made
herself find the difference, because there was a difference and she could
feel the shape of it and she wanted it in words.

She got it at about the twelfth minute.

*The mule did not mind.*

She sat with that.

*That is it. That is the whole of the difference and there is nothing
else in it. Nobody was standing in that yard being described. There was
an animal and it was eating, and it does not know what seven or ten is,
and when Deguchi said what it was worth in the spring it did not hear
him.*

*And that is not a small difference. That is the entire difference between
a trade and the other thing.*

*And it is not enough.*

---

She got up when her legs had stopped.

*It is not enough because I did not do it because it was allowed. I did it
because I could, and I was good at it, and the reason I was good at it is
that it was done to me twice and I paid attention both times.*

*So I have got a thing now, and I have got it out of that, and there is no
version where I give it back.*

She squared her hat and went up the yard.

He was standing where she had left him with his hands behind him and the
money away, and he said, "Right," and then, after about two seconds,
"Five two."

"Yes."

"That's under."

"Yes."

He looked at her for slightly longer than he normally looked at anything,
and she watched him not ask, for the fourth time, and this time she
noticed that she had started counting them.

Then he untied the mule and they went out of the yard, and nobody named
it, and she did not think about that at all until the eleventh month.
