# Chapter 14, Scene 3 — Regular

*Act 2. A room eleven paces by seven. A boy, age thirteen. POV: the boy.
~1,800 words.*

---

He had been without a clock for about a month and he knew it was about a
month only because he had counted the bowls, and the bowls came at no time
at all, so *about a month* was the most he had and he was not happy with
it.

He had tried the light and the noise and his own sleeping and all three
had failed and he had the reasons written in his head where things went.

So on a day he could not name he was lying on his side facing the wall
doing nothing, which was most of what he did now, and he heard it.

---

It was somebody screaming.

That is what it was and he identified it inside a second, because he was
not stupid and he had heard people make noises before, and the
identification did not go anywhere and did not connect to anything and he
did not return to it.

**What he noticed about it was that it was long.**

*That one went on.*

*I have heard six or seven since I came here and they were short and they
were at nothing in particular. That one was long and it went up and then
it stayed.*

He sat up.

---

He heard the next one some time after and he was ready for it and got
three things.

*It is the same one. It is the same voice, and I know that because of the
place it goes to at the top and where it stops.*

*It is north and above. Not directly. Through two walls at least, because
it comes in flat and there is no edge on it.*

*And it was long again.*

He put his hand on the floor.

*So there are two of them the same.*

*Two is not a line. Two is any line you like.*

He had said that to himself in the ninth month of the year before about a
piece of his own hand and it had been true then and it was true now, and
he had never been more pleased to have a rule ready.

---

He gave it eleven.

That took what he afterwards worked out was somewhere between three and
four days and he did not use one of them for anything, and he was careful
in a way he had not needed to be careful since the bowl.

He counted the gaps in the only unit he had, which was breathing, and he
knew breathing was bad and used it anyway because there was nothing else.

*One. Then about two hundred and ninety.*

*Two. Then about three hundred and ten.*

*Three. Then about two hundred and eighty.*

He sat with those three and did not like them and made himself go on.

*Four. Three hundred and forty. — That one is out.*

*Five. Three hundred.*

*Six. Two hundred and ninety.*

*Seven. Three hundred and five.*

He got to eleven and laid the ten gaps out in his head in the order they
had come, which was the way he laid out anything.

*Nine of them are between two eighty and three ten. One is three forty and
one is two forty.*

*And the two forty was the one where I lost count in the middle because
somebody walked past the door and I have already put that down as a bad
reading.*

*So: nine good, one out, one spoiled.*

---

He went at the one that was out, because you go at the one that is out.

*Three forty is sixty over. That is not noise. Noise is ten and twenty.*

He turned it over for a while and got it, and getting it was the largest
piece of work he had done since the sixth month.

*It is not sixty over. It is thirty short of two of them.*

*So that one is not a long gap. That is a missed one.*

He put his hand flat.

*The thing is happening at a regular interval and one of them did not
happen and the interval did not move.*

*A thing that goes on happening at the same interval whether or not it
happens is a thing somebody is doing on purpose, to a schedule, the way
the bowl was the same amount every time.*

He did not go any further than that and there was nowhere further for him
to go, because he had never been told one thing about one person in that
building or the last one, and a schedule is a fact about a building.

---

So he had a unit.

He tested it for another day before he would use it, which he did not
have to do and did anyway, and it held.

*Call it one. I have not got a word for it and I do not need one.*

*Nine bad readings in eleven would be no use. Nine good in eleven is a
clock.*

And then he did the thing he had been unable to do for a month, which was
to put something against it.

---

He got the third piece out.

Three and a quarter thumbs, straight the whole way, the one that had given
him the number, and he had carried it inside his shirt since the day they
took him out of the room and had not lost it.

Then he felt along the back of his left hand for the fifth.

*It is coming and I do not know how much of it is done, because I lost the
count when the bowls went and I have not had one since.*

*So the old number is gone. Nine hundred and three was in bowls and I have
not got bowls.*

He sat with that for a while and it did not upset him, and it did not
upset him because he had already had this exact thought about eleven times
in a month and had worn the edge off it.

*But I have not got to know how much is done. I have got to know how fast
it is going.*

*And I can have that from here.*

---

He measured it against his thumb on the day he was sure of the interval,
and he wrote the number in the place where numbers went, and then he did
nothing for four days except count.

He did not sleep well, because the counting had to go on through the
night, and he had never had a task that ran through the night before, and
he found — and this was the thing that surprised him — that he could do
it. He could half-sleep and hold it. He woke for every one of them.

At the end of the four days he measured again.

*It has come a thumb and a bit in about a hundred and ten of them.*

He did the arithmetic on his fingers against the floor, which took him
most of an afternoon and which he did three times because he had got it
wrong once and had found the place.

*And it wants about two more thumbs.*

*So it is about two hundred more.*

---

He put the piece back inside his shirt and lay down on his side facing the
wall and had it.

*I have got a rate.*

*And I have got a number, and the number is in a thing that is still
happening, and it will still be happening tomorrow.*

He went through the whole ladder from the beginning, because the ladder
was the thing he owned and he had not been able to look at it in a month,
and it came out clean.

*Four, three and a half, three and a quarter, and the fifth one will be
three when it is done, because it goes down by a half and I picked the
half and I am keeping it.*

*And after that there are six more.*

*And I know how long one of them is now. In this.*

---

He lay in the dark in a room eleven paces by seven with a drain in the
north corner and was more pleased than he had been since the sixth month
of the year before.

There is nothing else to say about it and it should not be dressed up. He
was thirteen, and he had lost the only instrument he had ever had, and he
had gone out and found another one and tested it eleven times and thrown
out the bad readings and built a rate on it in four days on his own with
nothing.

**It was good work and he knew it was good work and there was nobody to
tell.**

*Two hundred more.*

He put the four pieces in a row on the floor with the bases lined up, by
feel, the straight one at the end.

Then he waited, in the dark, with his hand on the back of his other hand,
until the next one came.

It came.

He counted it.
