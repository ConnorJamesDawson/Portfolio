# Chapter 17, Scene 6 — Somebody Comes

*Act 2, year 1, month 4, the twenty-second. North of Hane. Tadayoshi Ruri,
age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,800 words.*

---

The man came down the road at about eleven in the morning on the
twenty-second and he came down it openly, which is the first thing
Tadayoshi had off him and was worth more than the rest of the conversation.

*He is on the road and not off it.*

*He has come from the south and he has not slowed and he has not looked at
the trees.*

*So he knows where he is going and he knows what is at the end of it and he
is not worried about any part of it.*

He came about forty yards in off the road, stopped at a place that was
approximately where a person would stop if they had been told the ground
was set, and said:

"Ruri?"

"Yes."

"Right."

---

He was about thirty and he had a satchel and a good coat and he was
carrying nothing that mattered, and he did not introduce himself and
Tadayoshi did not ask, and neither of them thought that was strange
because neither of them had ever done it any other way.

"The twenty-fourth," he said. "Kadono. The rope walk on the north side,
there's a yard behind it. Ninth hour."

"Kadono."

"Two days if you go steady."

"I know how far Kadono is."

"Course you do."

---

Tadayoshi asked four and got four answers and every one of them was true
and none of them was any use, and it took him about a minute to work out
that the man was not being careful.

*He has not got it.*

"Who's asked for me?"

"Doesn't say."

"What's it about?"

The man thought about that for about a second, which was the only second in
the whole exchange in which anybody thought about anything.

"There'll be people there," he said. "That's what I know."

"Am I bringing the sheet?"

"Bring whatever you've got."

"Right."

---

**And then the last one, and it is the only line in that conversation that
mattered, and Tadayoshi did not query it.**

"Bring all three of you," the man said.

"Right."

He said it flatly and immediately and the man was already turning, and it
went into the book that evening in four words in the operational column
because facts about jobs went in the operational column.

> *22/4. Kadono, 24th, 9th hr. All three.*

---

He went at it for about two hours on the twenty-second afternoon and he got
almost nowhere and the almost is worth putting down.

*Why all three.*

*If it is about the cargo then it is about the cargo and the cargo goes.
That is not strange. That is a delivery.*

He walked out to the road and back.

*And if it is about the cargo then it is a hand-over, and a hand-over does
not need me and it does not need her, it needs a man and a receipt.*

*And I have not been told it is a hand-over.*

He stopped at the top of the fold.

*So it is not a hand-over, or it is one and somebody wants to see me while
it happens.*

*And **all three** is a phrase, and a phrase is a thing somebody said to
somebody who said it to him, and by the time it got to me it had been
through two mouths and it has still got the same shape, which means it was
said carefully at the top.*

He went back down.

*Right. So somebody up the line has asked for the three of us by name and
that is a fact and I have got nowhere else to take it.*

**And he never once, in two hours, arrived at the possibility that it was a
question about whether to keep him**, *because in fifteen months nobody had
ever asked him a question about whether to keep him, and a thing that has
not happened four hundred times does not present itself as a thing that
might.*

---

He did the money on the twenty-second night and it is short.

> *WHAT IS LEFT*
>
> *9,467.*
>
> *15–22/4, feed and food, three: −178.*
>
> **9,289.**

He looked at it.

*Nine thousand two hundred and eighty-nine and no line coming in since the
ninth.*

*Which is fifteen days.*

*And the longest I have gone with nothing coming in was thirty-six in the
second month, and I did not like it and I got through it, and this is
fifteen and I have a docket at the end of it on Thursday.*

He was steadied by that and he was right to be, and he wrote *Kadono 24th*
under the total with a line to it, which he had never done before on that
page, because that page had never had anything in the future on it.

---

He did the twenty-third on the road and he did it wrong and it is the only
preparation he has ever done in his life that was entirely wasted.

He built the defence.

He did it walking, over about four hours, the way he built everything, and
it came out in five parts and every part of it was airtight and he checked
each one against the counterparts in his coat as he went.

> *One. The brief said **get the cargo and go**. I have it in the book,
> dated, in the hand of the man who gave it.*
>
> *Two. Nobody told me it was a person. I asked one question — **whose is
> it** — and was told **ours now**, and that is an answer about ownership
> and not about contents.*
>
> *Three. I was told north and wait. I went north and waited fourteen days
> and nobody came.*
>
> *Four. The case was delivered at Hane on the ninth against a counterpart
> and I have the counterpart, and the seals were read out loud both ends
> and agreed.*
>
> *Five. And if the question is why I have still got him — nobody has ever
> told me to put him down anywhere.*

He went over the five twice more before the light went and then a third
time at about six in the morning on the twenty-fourth, walking, before
either of them was properly awake.

*It holds.*

*There is not a hole in it and I have looked for one for four hours and I
am good at looking for holes.*

**And that is the last thing he thinks before Kadono, and it is completely
true, and it was about a question nobody in that building had any intention
of asking.**

---

The last part of the twenty-second is about the boy and it is about ninety
seconds long.

Tadayoshi went over to the fire side, where the boy now sat, and said:

"We're going to Kadono. Two days. Thursday."

"Yes."

And he was about to walk away, and then he did not, and what stopped him
was a thing he could not have named at the time and could name perfectly
afterwards, which was that he had just given a person a fact and the person
had taken it and there had been no next part.

*He has not asked anything.*

*I have told a boy he is being taken to a town on Thursday and he has said
yes.*

He stood there.

*She would have asked six.*

*And he has not asked one, and it is not that he does not want to know, he
has not got a place where the wanting happens.*

He looked at him for about two seconds, and the boy looked back, waiting,
because there might be more.

"Do you want to know why?"

And the boy said, without any pause at all:

"Do you know why?"

"No."

"Then no."

---

He walked back to the other side of the fire and sat down.

*That is not what I asked.*

*I asked whether he wanted to know. He answered whether there was anything
to be told.*

*Those are not the same and he swapped one for the other in under a second
and he did not do it to be clever.*

He got the book out and did not open it.

*He has not got a want that is separate from a supply.*

*If a thing can be got, he will take it. If it cannot be got, there is
nothing there at all. There is no wanting it anyway.*

He sat with that for a while and it was the closest he ever came to
understanding the boy in one sitting, and he wrote none of it down, because
it was not a measurement and it did not have a column, and by the
twenty-fourth of the fourth month there was no time to find one.

**He never went back to that page. It stayed unheaded for four years and
then it was not a page about a boy any more and the heading it eventually
got is not in this book.**
