# Chapter 3, Scene 6 — Take's House

*Act 1, month 4, the third, night. Shioiri, the sixth course. Tadayoshi
Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,550 words.*

---

*Ruri Take. Born the second year of the rebuilding. Died here.*

He had that off a page in Meno's room three weeks ago and it was the only
thing he had, and he went along the top of the hill in the dark with it,
counting the roofs from the seaward end.

*Third from the end on the sixth.*

*"That's Take's. They took the shutters off it for the sheds and they
never took the bed, because it wouldn't go through the door. Somebody
built it in the room. That's a thing they used to do and it's a stupid
thing to do and there's four of them up there."*

She had said that to him about two years ago, on a path, apropos of
nothing, in the middle of complaining about somebody else entirely.

It was the whole of what he had about the sixth course.

---

The door was not locked because nothing on the fifth or sixth had been
locked since before he was born, and it had swollen and it took him three
goes with his shoulder, which made a noise that he stood still inside for
about a minute afterwards.

Nobody came.

It was black inside and it smelled of the thing that empty houses smell
of, which is not damp exactly; it is the smell of a place where nothing
has been warm.

He went round the wall with his hands.

Wall. Wall. A shelf with nothing on it and a broken thing on the end of
it that he did not identify and did not want to. Wall. A doorway.

The bed was in the back room and it was exactly where a bed goes and it
was enormous.

---

He stood and put both hands on it.

It was a frame with boards across and no mattress on it and no bedding of
any kind, and it had been made out of something heavy and it had been
made by somebody who could make things, and there was a moulding round
the top rail that had been run properly and not torn at the corners.

*She was right. It does not go through the door. It is longer than the
door is wide and the frame does not come apart, because the man who made
it pegged the head into the rails and pegged it from the inside.*

He got up onto it and lay down on the boards.

The roof was sound. That was the second thing Meno had been right about
without ever having said it: the sixth course had been shut before the
fifth, and shut earlier meant shut properly, and the eave that came down
two winters ago had come down on the fifth and not the sixth, and there
was not one drop of water anywhere in that room.

He was still soaked through and it was still about four degrees, and he
lay on the boards in his wet coat and shook for a long time.

---

At some point he took the coat off and turned it and put it over himself
inside out, which was warmer, and stopped shaking so hard.

At some point after that he stopped shaking.

He did not sleep.

---

*Eleven men and possibly more.*

*In at the top and the bottom at once, and the top party came over the
shoulder, and the churn on the seaward edge of the top road was going the
other way, which means they came from the Kiri side and not up the coast.*

*Four hundred and forty yards on the path from the second course to the
stone.*

*About twenty minutes.*

*One boat with a house on it, gone north past the bar at about twenty
past five, which is not Ōura and is not Sunaba.*

*Second boat, smaller, at about six, four men.*

*Nobody went above the fourth course except at the start.*

*Koishi was on the list. Koishi is six and unassessed and she was on the
list, therefore it is the household, therefore it is the blood, therefore
it is not about the bench.*

*And I do not know what the woman in the grey overcoat said to Aoyagi at
nine minutes past eleven this morning.*

He had all of it.

He had it in order, and it was correct, and he had checked the parts that
could be checked and had marked the parts that could not, and there was
not a line in it he would have to take back.

He got the ledger out.

---

It was inside his shirt and it had come through drier than he had any
right to expect, which was a thing he noted with the part of him that
noted things and could not do anything with.

He got the stub of pencil out of the spine.

He sat up on the boards of a dead man's bed in a house on the sixth
course with the ledger open on his knees in the dark, which meant he was
going to have to write blind, which he had done before and could do.

He put the pencil on the page.

---

He did not write anything.

He sat there with the point of it touching the paper for about, he
thought afterwards, four minutes.

*Write it.*

*You have it whole. That is the entire point of you. It is the only thing
you have ever been able to do that nobody had to teach you, and you have
just spent two hours doing it properly under conditions and it came out
clean, and if you do not write it down tonight then in six days it will
have twelve men in it and in a year it will have a fire in it, because
that is what happens, that is what happened to everything anybody in
this family ever knew.*

*Meno's book is a hundred and some years old and it is in a room on the
third course and it has nineteen names in it that nobody will say.*

*Write it down.*

The pencil sat on the page.

---

*I cannot.*

*Why.*

He went and looked for it properly, because there was nothing else to do
at eleven at night on a bed frame, and it took him a while, and when he
got there it was very simple and he did not like it at all.

*Because a finding is for somebody.*

*Everything in this book is written to be read. Not by anybody in
particular — I have never let anybody read it and I would not have — but
it is written the way a thing is written when there is going to be a
person at the other end of it. That is why it has columns. Columns are
for somebody looking. That is why I ruled the narrow one, because that is
how it is done, because Meno does it, because somebody after her would
have to be able to find the true things.*

*I have been writing a report for three weeks.*

*And the person a report is for is not there.*

He looked at the shape of the page in the dark, which he could not see
and knew by heart.

*Four columns. Name. What it is. What it cost.*

*And the narrow one.*

*And there is nobody to hand it to, and there is not going to be, and
there is not one entry I could write on this page tonight that is a
report to anybody about anything.*

*It is just what happened. It happened to me and I watched it and I am
the only person who has it and there is no other end.*

---

He sat for a while longer.

Then he wrote, because he had to write something or he was going to be a
person who had stopped, and he did not trust the version of himself that
stopped.

He wrote it in the first column, in the dark, in his small exact hand,
about a quarter of an inch out of true because he could not see the rule:

*The bed is six foot four and the door is five foot ten.*

He looked at where it would be.

*It is the only measurement I have taken today that I took with my hands.*

He put the pencil back in the spine and shut the book and put it inside
his shirt.

---

He lay down again on the boards.

The house was completely silent, which he had never in his life
experienced, because there had always been eight people on a hill and one
of them was always doing something — Meno's stick, or Akagane's shed, or
Koishi being told to go to bed for the fourth time, or the sea, and the
sea was still there but the sea did not count.

He listened to it for a long time.

Somewhere in the small hours he thought, quite clearly and without any
warning at all:

*Somebody will have to go down and shut the doors.*

And then he lay on a dead man's bed in a house that had been shut before
he was born, with his coat over him inside out, and did not sleep, and
waited for the light.
