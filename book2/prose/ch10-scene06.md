# Chapter 10, Scene 6 — Two On The Mat

*Act 1. A room. A boy, age twelve. POV: the boy. ~1,230 words.*

---

There were two of them now and he had been putting off looking at them
together for eleven bowls.

He knew he was putting it off. That was new. He had never in his life put
a thing off, because putting a thing off requires you to have some idea
what the thing will cost you and he had never had any idea what anything
would cost, and now he had one, and it was the first time.

*If I put them side by side I will know.*

*And if I do not put them side by side I will not know, and not knowing is
what I had before, and before was easier.*

He put them side by side.

---

The first one was from the sixth month and it had come off the back of his
left hand between the two middle knuckles, and it had taken a hundred and
seventy-eight bowls to come back, and he had measured it four times with
his thumb and had the number in his head where numbers went.

The second was the one he had broken off eleven bowls ago.

He lined the bases up, because if you do not line the bases up you are
measuring nothing, and he had worked that out on his own and was pleased
about it in a way he had no word for.

Then he ran his thumb along the first, and then along the second, and then
along the first again to check that his thumb had not changed.

*Shorter.*

*It is shorter and it is not much shorter and it is shorter.*

---

He did the other thing next, which he had not planned to do and which he
had known the answer to before he started.

*The first one goes out of the hand and it goes where the hand is
pointing.*

*The second one goes out and then it goes out to the side, about that
much, and there is a place near the top where it changes.*

He put both of them down on the mat and sat back on his heels.

There was a thing in his chest that had not been there before and that
he had no name for and would not have a name for at any point in his life,
and what it actually was, though nobody was ever going to tell him, was
the specific quality of disappointment that only arrives when a person has
been trying.

He sat with it until it stopped.

---

The right hand had never been broken.

That was the control and he did not know the word and he had built one
anyway, in the sixth month, for a reason he could still recite: *it is the
one that has not been broken, and if I put it away I will have nothing to
hold the other one against.*

It was still out. It had been out since the night he started. It was about
an inch and a half and it came up between the two middle knuckles and it
caught on the blanket while there was a blanket, and on the mat, and on
the inside of the bowl about twice a week, and it had gone through the
skin over the top of it three times and grown a hard shine on the end
where he had knocked it on the wall.

He felt along it now.

Straight. Long. Smooth the whole way, no place near the top where it
changed, no going out to the side.

*That one is what it is supposed to be and it is the one I have not
touched.*

---

He went round it three times looking for a way for it to be a different
answer.

*It might be that they are different because of when in the day.* — He had
done both at night. He did everything at night.

*It might be the place on the hand.* — Same place. On purpose. He had done
the first one in a different place and had got a different answer and had
not been able to tell whether it was the place or the doing, and had
fixed the place after that so that he would know.

*It might be that I am worse at it.* — He turned that one over for
longest, because it was the one that would have been his fault, and it did
not hold either, because there is no *at it*. There is no skill in it. It
comes off where it comes off.

*So it is not the day and it is not the place and it is not me.*

**It is that a thing which is broken comes back smaller, and comes back
bent, and I have got two and the second is worse than the first.**

---

He put his hands in his lap.

*I have got one more thing than I had eleven bowls ago and it is worse
than the thing I wanted.*

*And a bad answer that you have got is not the same as no answer.*

He said that in his head twice, because he had made it up in the sixth
month and it had held up so far, and he was aware — dimly, without the
words — that he was using it the way you use a stick on a bad leg.

Then he did the part that was actually work, which was to ask what the
next question was, and he got to it in about four minutes, sitting on a
mat, at twelve years old, with a bolt on the outside of the door.

*Two is a line with two points on it.*

*Two points is not a line. Two points is any line you like.*

*I need a third one before I know whether it is going down at the same
rate or whether the second one was a bad one.*

---

He felt along the back of his left hand for the place.

The skin over it was thicker than it had been in the sixth month and there
was a ridge under it now that had not been there, and he had noticed the
ridge about forty bowls ago and had put it down as a thing that was
happening and had not decided whether it was a good thing.

He put his right hand over the back of his left.

*Do not make a noise.*

It hurt more than the first time and more than the second, and he had
expected that, and it was not information.

He broke it off.

---

He put it on the mat beside the other two so that there would be three,
and lined the bases up, and did not look at them together, because it was
not finished growing and looking at it now would give him an answer that
was not an answer and then he would not be able to un-have it.

Then he lay down on his side facing the wall to wait for the bowl.

*Three.*

*And when that one comes back I will have a line.*

He did not have a word for the thing he had just done, and he had done it
correctly, and there was nobody in the building who knew he was doing it
at all.
