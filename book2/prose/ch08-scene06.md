# Chapter 8, Scene 6 — Correct

*Act 1, month 8, the twenty-first. A room above a rope-walk, Ōtoma.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,560 words.*

---

There were six of them in the room and he was the only one sitting down,
which he did not understand until afterwards.

Kajiya. Hōki, by the door, not leaning on anything. A woman of about
thirty whose name he was not given and who did not look at him once. Two
men he had not seen before. And a man of about forty-five with ink on the
side of his hand, who was called Yatate and who had written the sheets.

There were four sheets and they went round the table and Tadayoshi got
them last, which was correct, and he read them.

He read them the way he read everything, which is to say he read them
twice: once for what they said and once for what shape they were.

---

*The job is at Kamiya on the fourth and fifth of the ninth month.*

*Four of us. Two on the road, one at the house, one where the road comes
down, which is me, because a road that comes down through a cut is a road
you can shut.*

*Six names on the sheet, of which two are ours and four are theirs, and
one of the four is a man called Yasu who is expected at the house on the
fourth.*

He turned the sheet over and read the second one and then went back.

*And the second sheet has Yasu at Nagatsuka on the fifth.*

He sat with it.

*That is not a mistake in the sheet. The sheets are separate and they were
written for different people and neither of them is wrong on its own face.
Sheet one says a man is at a house on the fourth. Sheet two says a man of
the same name is forty miles away on the fifth.*

*Forty miles is two days.*

He did it again to be certain, because he was not going to say a thing
like that without being certain, and it took him about eleven seconds and
he had walked enough road in five months to know exactly what forty miles
was.

*Two days if you are grown and not being careful. Three if you are.*

*So there are three possibilities.*

*One. The date is wrong on one of the two sheets.*

*Two. There are two men called Yasu.*

*Three.*

---

He said it out loud.

He said it at the volume he said things, which was the volume of a boy
reading out a figure, and he said it into a room that had been talking
about the far end of the road for about a minute, and it went across the
whole of it like a dropped plate.

"Yasu can't be in both of those."

The room stopped.

"He's at the house on the fourth on this one and he's at Nagatsuka on the
fifth on that one, and it's forty miles. So it's either a date, or there's
two of him, or he's telling one of you something that isn't true."

---

Nobody said anything for about three seconds.

Then Yatate reached across and took the two sheets and put them next to
each other and looked at them, and Tadayoshi watched him find it, which
took him about four seconds, which was about right.

"Ah," said Yatate.

Kajiya said, "Which?"

"The fifth's off a Nagatsuka slip from the twenty-ninth of last month and
I've had the fourth off Kanō, and I've had them a fortnight apart and put
them on two different sheets." He did not look up. "It could be a date. It
could easily be a date. Kanō gives me the day he heard a thing and not the
day the thing happened, and I have told him about that twice."

"And if it isn't a date," said the woman.

Nobody answered her.

---

Tadayoshi sat at the table with his hands flat in front of him and
watched a room do something.

He could see it happening. That was the thing he could never afterwards
get past — he was not blind, he had eyes, and he could see the room
change in exactly the way he had seen the room at the Kiribata change on
the twelfth of the sixth month.

*Two of them have looked at each other.*

*The woman is looking at Kajiya and Kajiya is looking at the sheet and
Hōki has not moved and Hōki has not moved at all, which is the most
Hōki has ever done in a room.*

*And Yatate has gone red at the back of the neck and it is not shame,
because his hands are not doing anything, and a man who is ashamed does
something with his hands.*

*So what is it.*

He did not get it. He sat there with the whole of it in front of him and
he could not get from *the room has changed* to *why*, and it was, he
thought later, exactly like reading a hand you know and not being able to
say the word.

---

"Right," said Kajiya. "Yatate, go back to Kanō and get me the day the
thing happened. Not the day he heard it. If he can't give it me, then
we're not going to Kamiya on the fourth, we're going on the seventh, and
if the seventh doesn't work we're not going."

"That's a fortnight."

"Then it's a fortnight."

He got up.

"Right. That's it."

---

That was all of it, and Tadayoshi went down the stairs after them and out
into the rope-walk in the dark, and it took him until about the bottom of
the stairs to understand that nobody had said anything to him.

*Nobody said anything.*

*Not one of them. Not thank you, and not well done, and not shut up, and
not you were wrong, which would at least have been an answer.*

*And I was not wrong. Yatate found it in four seconds and Kajiya has just
moved a job by three days on the strength of it, and the whole of what
happened in that room happened because of a thing I said, and not one
person in it said one word to me about it.*

He walked about a hundred yards on that.

*So it was right and it was not welcome.*

*Those are not the same thing and I have been treating them as the same
thing since I was about six.*

He turned it over and could not get it any further open.

*Meno used to say: find it, and then find out who it is for. And I have
never once had a finding that was not for me.*

*Everything in that book is for me. Every single entry. I find a thing and
I write it down and I am the only person who is ever going to read it, so
the question of what a finding does to a room has literally never come up
in my life.*

*And I have just put one in a room.*

*And the room went quiet.*

*And I do not know what I did.*

---

Hōki was on the corner where the rope-walk came out, not waiting for him,
in the way that he was never waiting for anybody.

"You've got a face on you," he said.

"Was I wrong?"

"No."

"Then—"

"You weren't wrong," said Hōki. "You were right in a room."

He started walking. Tadayoshi went with him.

"There's four people in that room who have known Yasu for two and a half
years and one of them has stayed in his house. And a boy of ten who has
been here twelve days has just put it on the table that he might be
selling them, in about nine words, at supper."

"But he might be."

"Yes."

"So somebody had to—"

"Yes," said Hōki. "Somebody had to. That's why nobody told you to shut
up."

They walked about forty yards.

"I don't understand," said Tadayoshi, which was a thing he had said out
loud perhaps four times in his life.

"I know," said Hōki. "That's the other thing about you, and I've not
worked out yet whether it can be got at."

---

He wrote it that night and he wrote it as an anomaly, because that was the
only category he had for it.

*21/8. Corrected two sheets in front of the man who ruled them. It was
right — he found it himself in four seconds and the job has been moved
three days on it.*

*Nobody in the room said one word to me afterwards.*

*Anomaly. A finding can be correct and be a bad thing to have said, and I
have no way at all of telling in advance which kind I am holding.*

*Under review.*

He looked at that for a while.

Then he added a line, because it had been sitting underneath the whole
thing on the walk back and he had not let it up, and he was not going to
have a rule about writing the true one down and then not write it.

*And if I had it again tomorrow I would say it again.*

He did have it again, eleven days later, on the first of the ninth month,
about a different sheet, in a different room, and he said it again, and
that time somebody did tell him to shut up.
