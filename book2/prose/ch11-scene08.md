# Chapter 11, Scene 8 — A Reason I Can Take Upstairs

*Act 1, month 11, the ninth. A back room off the cooperage yard, Ōtoma.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,900 words.*

---

Hōki was in Ōtoma on the ninth, which had not happened since the eighth
month, and Tadayoshi knew what that meant before he got to the bottom of
the ladder and was wrong about which thing it meant.

There was a back room off the yard with a table in it and a window with
nothing outside it, and Hōki was on one side of the table and did not get
up, and Ōtsuka was standing by the wall with his arms folded and did not
say anything at all in the whole of the next twenty minutes, and that was
the part Tadayoshi thought about afterwards for a very long time.

"Sit down."

He sat down.

---

"Terasaka," said Hōki. "The twentieth."

"Yes."

"Tell me what you did."

Tadayoshi told him. It took about a minute and a half and it was accurate
and it was in order and he did not leave anything out except the eleven
minutes, and he did not leave the eleven minutes out to hide them; he
left them out because they were not part of the work and it did not occur
to him that anybody would want them.

Hōki listened to the whole of it without moving.

"And the boy."

"He wasn't in the count."

"I didn't ask you that. I asked you about the boy."

"He came down the top road at about twenty past five and I went north
past him and he was sitting on the stone by the gate."

"Did he see you."

"Yes."

"Right," said Hōki.

---

There was a bit of quiet, and Hōki turned his hand over on the table,
which was the only thing he did with his body in the entire conversation.

"Hazama's man walked that ground on the second," he said. "Eleven acres,
a wall, a gate, three sheep. And on the way back down he stopped in at
the mill because there's a man at the mill, and the man at the mill told
him there's a boy up at Ōgi who's Terasaka's grandson, and he's stopping
with an aunt, and everybody in that valley knows exactly whose he is."

Tadayoshi did not say anything.

"So Hazama has paid fourteen thousand ryō," said Hōki, "for a field with
a boy on it."

"There isn't a boy on it. He's at Ōgi."

"He's seven," said Hōki. "He'll not be at Ōgi in ten years."

---

Tadayoshi went at that the way he went at everything and got to the
bottom of it in about four seconds, and the bottom of it was a sentence
somebody had said to him at three o'clock in the morning on the twentieth
of the tenth month while sitting on the edge of a bed with both hands on
the frame.

*You'll not get it. It's in her name. It goes to the boy.*

He sat in a back room in Ōtoma and had that arrive whole.

*He told me.*

*He was seventy years old and he had about eight seconds and he did not
spend them on his son or his brother or asking me anything. He spent them
telling me the thing that was going to break the job, and I did not know
it was the thing, and I did not ask, and I could not have asked, because
asking would have been a disclosure and I had two more in the house.*

*So I heard it and I put it down and it was the answer and I had it in my
ears.*

He looked at the table.

*And the correct thing to do at the time was what I did. If I stop in a
front room at three in the morning to work out what a dying man means
about a deed, I lose the house.*

*So I did it right and it came out wrong, and that is the third time.*

---

"Do you understand what you've done?" said Hōki.

"Yes."

"Say it."

"He didn't buy four bodies. He bought the ground, and the ground has got a
claim on it that is still breathing, and in ten years somebody with a
name will come and stand on it, and that is worth more against him than
four people were."

"Right," said Hōki.

---

"Go back and finish it."

Tadayoshi said, "No."

He said it immediately. That is the fact of it and it should be put down
without decoration: there was no pause, and he did not weigh it, and it
was out of him before Hōki had finished the sentence, and he heard
himself say it at the same time as everybody else in the room did.

Hōki did not move.

"Why?"

And there was nothing there.

---

He looked for it. He looked for it with the whole of the machinery, in
front of two men, across a table, for what he later worked out was about
nine seconds, and it was the longest nine seconds of his life and he
would have said that at thirteen and at nineteen.

*Because he is not in the count. — He is in the count now. Hōki has just
put him in it and the arithmetic is Hōki's arithmetic and it is better
than mine.*

*Because it is not operational. — Nothing about this was operational and
I settled that on the twelfth and took the money anyway.*

*Because I have already been paid. — I have. That is an argument for
going.*

*Because —*

Nothing.

He got to the end of everything he had and there was nothing standing at
the end of it, and he was still not going.

"I haven't got one," he said.

---

Hōki looked at him for a while.

"Give me a reason I can take upstairs," he said. "Any reason. I don't
care whether it's a good one. Tell me the ground's watched, tell me
you've been seen at Ōgi, tell me the aunt's got four of her own in the
house and you can't get at one without the rest. Tell me it's twelve days
old and cold and you'd be walking into a valley that's had a shock. Any
of those and I write it down and it goes up and that is the end of it."

*Two of those are true.*

*The aunt has got four of her own. I counted them from a lean-to roof on
the fifteenth. And it is twelve days old and that valley has had a shock
and going back into it is genuinely worse than doing it on the night.*

*So I have got a reason. I have got two.*

"They're not why," he said.

---

Ōtsuka moved for the first time, which was to put his hand over his
mouth, and put it down again, and did not say anything.

Hōki sat back.

"In the sixth month," he said, "a man in Fukae told you that you'd be
offered a bed and not to take it. You didn't take it. Do you know why he
told you that?"

"No."

"Have you ever found out?"

"No."

"Did you ask?"

"Once. He didn't answer."

"And you did it anyway," said Hōki. "For four months you did the thing
you were told without the reason, and you did it four times that I know
about, and one of the four was carrying rags to Hanase and back for four
days, and Kajiya told you afterwards that it was rags and you worked out
in about ten seconds why it did not matter, and you were pleased with
yourself."

"Yes."

"And in the eighth month you turned down the Hanase road."

"Yes."

"And that was fine," said Hōki, "and I want you to be very clear about
why that was fine. That was fine because you gave us four lines on a
sheet and every one of them was checkable and two of them were right and
somebody read it. You were wrong about the outcome and you were not wrong
about anything on the sheet. That is a man refusing a job. What you are
doing now is not that."

---

"This isn't the same," said Tadayoshi.

"No," said Hōki. "It isn't. That's what I've just said."

"I mean the boy isn't the job. The job was the ground and I did the
ground. This is a second job."

"It is a second job," Hōki agreed. "It's a second job that costs us
nothing and takes a day, and the client has asked for it, and you've been
paid for the first one, and you have said no to it with nothing after the
no. I want you to hear that back. You have said no to a job with nothing
after the no. There is a word for that and it is not one either of us
wants to use in this room, and the reason I don't want to use it is that
it would make this about you and me and it is not about you and me."

---

"Is somebody else going?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because it costs more than it saves," said Hōki. "I'm not sending a
grown man four days to do a child in a house with four others in it for a
client who is already unhappy, and I'm not asking Hazama to do it
himself, because the day I ask Hazama to do a thing himself is the day
Hazama works out what we are. So we eat it. We eat the boy and we keep
the four thousand a quarter and in ten years somebody else's problem
stands on a wall in that valley."

He said all of that at exactly the same speed as everything else.

"So nothing happens," said Tadayoshi.

"Something happens," said Hōki. "You go on the boards."

---

Tadayoshi did not understand it for about two seconds and then did.

*Cargo.*

"For how long?"

"Until it stops costing," said Hōki, and got up, and that was the first
time he had moved from the waist. "And I want you to take the right thing
out of this room, because you'll take the wrong one if I let you. This
isn't a punishment. Nobody in this building thinks you did a bad thing;
half of them wouldn't have done it either and the other half wouldn't
have told you. It's arithmetic. You have got more expensive than you were
in the ninth month, and the fee is not the expensive part."

He picked his coat up off the back of the chair.

"Expensive things get put where they can't cost anything. That's all this
is. Do the boards, be cheap for a bit, and it comes back."

---

He went out. Ōtsuka went out after him and did not look over, and the
door was open about two inches and did not click, and Tadayoshi noticed
the two inches and hated himself for noticing it.

He sat in the back room for about twenty minutes.

Then he went up the ladder and got the ledger and turned to the page at
the back with the heading on it that he had ruled on the first of the
tenth month, which had two entries on it, and put a third one under them.

> ***PEOPLE WHO WILL TELL SOMEBODY***
>
> *3. Hazama, Ōgi. Timber and land. Never seen him. He has not told
> anybody anything and he is not going to, because what he has done
> instead is worse and it is quieter, which is that he complained, once,
> to the only people he could complain to, and they agreed with him.*

He looked at it for a while, and then wrote one more line under it, and
it was the only line on the page that was not about somebody else.

> *And he is right.*
