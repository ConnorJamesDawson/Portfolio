# Chapter 14, Scene 6 — What It's For

*Act 2, year 1, month 2, the seventh to the fourteenth. The Kanaya road.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,850 words.*

---

The mule was the best thing he had ever bought and he said so out loud on
the third day, which was rare enough that she stopped walking.

"What?"

"It's the best thing I've bought."

She looked at him for a second and then said, "Better than the boots," and
he thought about it and said, "Different," and that was the end of it, and
he went back to the numbers.

*Two hundred and thirty pounds and neither of us is carrying it.*

*I am doing eighteen miles a day with eleven pounds on me and I have done
eighteen miles a day exactly twice in my life and both times I was empty
and running from something.*

*And I am not tired. It is the fifth day and I am not tired and I do not
know what to do with the afternoon.*

---

It was not free and he wrote down what it cost, because he wrote down what
everything cost.

**Feed.** *Twenty-five a day was the estimate and it was wrong. It is
about forty in the second month, because there is nothing on the verges
and the man at Kubiki wanted a hundred and ten for a net of hay and got
it, and I did not argue because I have not got a way to argue about hay.*

**The feet.** *Twenty minutes a day, every day, and if you do not do it
you find out on the sixth day, and I know that because I did not do it on
the second day and did it twice on the third.*

**The headcollar**, which rubbed behind the ears on the second morning and
which he packed out with rag and then with rag and a piece of the old
blanket, and which he got right on the fourth day and was disproportionately
pleased about.

**And the water.** *A mule wants about five times what I want and it wants
it in one go and it wants it standing still, and there are three places on
this road where you can water and I know where two of them are and I found
the third by walking a mile and a half up a side valley on the fifth
day.*

He put all four in the operational column and drew a line under them and
wrote the total.

Then, under the total, he wrote the only line in it that was not a number.

> *Everything I have learned about this animal in eight days, somebody
> would have told me in four minutes.*

---

The four miles happened on the second day and he had costed the wrong
thing.

There is a cut above Sakabe where the road goes up a bank and over, and it
is about three feet wide at the top and has a step in it, and he had gone
up it about nine times in his life without thinking about it and had
never once thought about it, and there was no version in which he was
getting a mule up it.

They went round.

It took four miles and about two hours and he sat down at the top of the
long way and had it out with himself.

*I costed the feed and the feet and the collar and the water and I did not
cost the ground.*

*And the ground is the thing. The ground is always the thing. It is the
first thing I do in a town and the first thing I do on a job and I did not
do it about a mule.*

*Because I have been reading ground for a boy on foot for nine months and
a boy on foot goes everywhere.*

He got the book out on the bank and started a new page in the operational
column, and the heading on it was two words, and it was the first heading
he had written in about four months.

> *ROADS FOR AN ANIMAL.*

*Every route I have got is now wrong.*

He looked at that for a while and it did not upset him at all, and it was
the most cheerful he had been about anything since the eleventh month,
because it was nine months of work that needed doing again and he had
nothing else to do with the afternoons.

---

They came into the Kanaya on the eleventh and delivered on the twelfth,
and the freight was signed for by a man who did not look at it, and the
whole thing took nine minutes.

She was odd about the Kanaya and he noticed and did not ask.

She stopped at the top of the road down into it and looked at it for about
twenty seconds and then said, "It's smaller than I thought," and he said,
"Have you been?" and she said, "No," and went on down.

*That is not a no.*

*That is a no with a thing on the back of it and I have not got the thing.*

He put it away. It went in the same place as the hat and the not-asking,
and by the second month of that year that place had four items in it and
he had never once opened it and looked at the four together, which is
exactly what he would have done with any four items about anybody else.

---

The tone reading was on the morning of the fourteenth, on the way back,
about eight miles out of the Kanaya.

He did it the way he had done it since the fifth month, which was to get
about sixty feet off the road at first light and sit down with his back to
something and take the tin out.

It was a tin that had had grease in it and had had something else in it
before that, and inside it was a piece of card and inside the card was the
thing.

He held it in his left hand at the base and struck it with a nail.

It made the note.

He held his breath for the whole of it, which was about four seconds, and
listened to the end of it rather than the start, because the end is where
it is, and then let his breath out and put it back in the card.

Then he got the book out and turned to the page where the readings went
and wrote the date and one word.

> *Same.*

---

He was aware that she was awake and he had been aware since about the
third strike, which is to say since the fifth month, because she had been
awake for the last four of these and had never once come over.

She came over.

She sat down about six feet away, on his right, and did not say anything
for a while, and then said:

"What is it?"

---

He did not answer.

That is the fact of it and it should be set down without any softening,
because he had answered every single question she had asked him in fifty-
eight days, including the ones that were about killing people, and he
answered them completely and in order and without ever appearing to notice
that they were difficult.

He said, "It's a reading."

And then did not say anything else.

He put the tin inside his coat and put the book away and got up and went
back to the road, and she got up about four seconds later and came after
him, and neither of them said anything about it and they did nine miles.

---

He worked at it that afternoon while he walked, because he did not leave
things.

*Why did I not answer.*

*I have got no reason. She has asked me forty things and I have told her
all of them, including four I should probably not have.*

*It is not that it is dangerous. She could not do anything with it.*

*It is not that she would not understand it, because she would, she would
have the whole of it in about a minute and a half and she would probably
tell me something about it I have not got.*

He went at it for about three miles and got to the bottom of it and did
not like the bottom of it at all.

*It is that if I explain what it is I have to explain what it is for.*

*And what it is for is that I have got a way of finding out whether my
mother is alive, once every eleven days, and I have been doing it for nine
months, and it has been the same for nine months.*

*And I have never said that to anybody.*

*And I do not want to be a person who has said it, because the day I say
it out loud is the day it becomes a thing that is happening to me instead
of a thing I am measuring.*

He put that down and did not write it anywhere.

---

There is one other thing about the morning of the fourteenth and neither
of them noticed it and there was nobody there who could have.

He sat on a bank sixty feet off a road with a piece of crystal in his left
hand and struck it and listened to the note, which was the same note it
had been for nine months, and wrote *same* in a book.

And the sword was across his back the entire time, where it always was,
making the note it had always made, which was the one under everything and
which he had stopped hearing about a year ago in the way you stop hearing
a stream.

He had written four words about that object on the seventeenth of the
twelfth month and they were still the only four words in the book about
it.

> *17/12. The blade was warm.*

He had not gone back to them and he did not go back to them that morning,
and the two objects sat about eleven inches apart on a bank above the
Kanaya road for four minutes, and one of them told him what he wanted to
know and the other one had been telling him something else for fourteen
months.
