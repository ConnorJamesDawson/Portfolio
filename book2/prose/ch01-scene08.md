# Chapter 1, Scene 8 — Four Seals

*Act 1, month 2. The Academy, Kirigakure. Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV:
Tadayoshi. ~1,870 words.*

---

Aoyagi-sensei ran the wrist drill on the last hour of the day, which
Tadayoshi had worked out in the autumn was not laziness but policy: you
did the thing that made children look stupid at the end, so they went
home with it and came back having thought about it.

"Line," said Aoyagi.

Twenty-six of them made a line along the boards. Tadayoshi went where he
always went, which was the end, because the end had a wall on one side
and a wall meant one fewer person deciding whether to stand next to him.

"Substitution seal, five, on my count, and I'm walking."

There was a general noise. There was always a general noise; the wrist
drill was hated.

"You may complain," said Aoyagi, "on your own time."

---

The point of the wrist drill was the walk.

You did the sequence — Ram, Boar, Ox, Dog, Snake, five seals, roughly a
second and three quarters for the class average, rather less for the two
or three of them who had hands — and while you did it, Aoyagi went down
the line behind you and, whenever he felt like it, put two fingers on
the inside of somebody's wrist.

Nothing violent. You could ignore it. You could not, however, ignore it
and also complete a seal, because a seal is a shape you hold with both
hands for a moment and a person touching your wrist is a person with
whom you are now sharing a hand.

The technique died. That was all. There was no bang and no smoke and
nothing happened at all, which was the lesson, and which was why they
hated it: the drill did not hurt, it simply took your work away and put
nothing in its place.

"It is not a hard rule," Aoyagi had said, the first week Tadayoshi was in
that class, to a room that had heard it eleven times before and one boy
who had not. "There is nothing clever in it. *Everything we do, we do
with our hands, in the open, in order, in front of the person we are
doing it to.* So if you learn one thing in this room and nothing else,
learn what it costs to be reached."

---

"Count," said Aoyagi. "One."

Twenty-six pairs of hands came up.

The trouble with the wrist drill was that you could hear where he was.
Boards, in that building, and Aoyagi walked with his weight back, so
there was a click coming down the line behind you and the click got
nearer and there was no possible advantage in knowing that, and everyone
listened for it anyway.

"Two."

Somebody's technique died four along. A small sound of disgust.

"Three."

Two more. Someone said a word and Aoyagi said, "And that's a mark," and
went on walking.

*Ram. Boar.*

His hands were cold. His hands were always cold, and the Academy hall
was a stone room in the second month with the shutters open because
Aoyagi believed in air, and Ram to Boar was the transition that went
first when the fingers went — you had to open the whole hand and close
it in a different shape, and cold hands did that late.

*Ox.*

The click was about six along.

*Dog.*

Four along. Three.

And Tadayoshi, who had been laying two courses a day every day for two
years, and who had spent that particular afternoon getting a taper to be
convincingly tired, did the thing he had been extremely careful not to
do in that room since the day he arrived in it.

He stopped concentrating on being ordinary.

---

He dropped Dog.

Not fumbled it. Went past it — went from Ox to Snake with the hands
already moving, and closed the sequence, and put his palm on the boards.

The bench three feet behind him was suddenly where he was standing, and
where he had been standing there was a bench.

Aoyagi's two fingers arrived in the air about a hand's width from a
wrist that was no longer there.

---

The hall went quiet in the way a room goes quiet when everybody in it
has decided independently not to be the person who speaks.

Aoyagi looked at the bench. Then at Tadayoshi, on the other side of it.

"Come here."

He came.

"Again."

"Sensei — "

"*Again*, Ruri. Slowly, and out loud."

So he did it slowly and out loud, in front of twenty-five people, with
his ears going hot.

"Ram. Boar. Ox. Snake."

"That's four."

"Yes."

"There are five."

"There are five in the book," said Tadayoshi.

---

There was a noise from about the middle of the line. Not a laugh. Nearer
the noise people make when somebody says something rude to a shopkeeper.

Aoyagi did not look round.

"Explain it."

*Do not.*

*You are being asked in a room. Nothing said in a room stays in the
room. Say it is a mistake. Say you got cold and skipped one and it went
anyway and you do not know why.*

But he had been asked a direct question about the work, and he had been
that particular kind of stupid his whole life, and the answer was
already most of the way out before he had finished deciding not to give
it.

"Dog is a bracing seal," said Tadayoshi. "It steadies the shape between
Ox and Snake so the shape does not slump while you change grip. It is in
the sequence because Ox to Snake is an awkward change of grip and most
people need the moment.

"It does not do anything to the technique. It is not part of the
technique. It is part of the *hand*." He heard himself and could not
stop. "So if you can get from Ox to Snake without slumping, Dog is a
seal you are performing for no reason, and you are standing still for
about a third of a second in a room with somebody walking up behind
you."

Aoyagi said nothing for a moment.

"Do it again," he said. "With me watching your hands and not your
mouth."

Tadayoshi did it again.

The bench swapped with him and the hall was silent for a second and a
half and then twenty-six people started talking at once and Aoyagi said,
"Line," in a voice that got them back into it.

---

Nobody spoke to him for the rest of the drill.

He noticed the exact shape of it, because he noticed things and because
there was nothing else to do while the click came down the boards.
Ryūta, who was eleven, and whose left forearm had been in a splint for
six weeks the spring before last, and who had been extremely decent
about it in a way Tadayoshi had never quite been able to file — Ryūta
looked over twice and then did not look over again.

That was all it was. That was the whole thing. Two looks, and then a
decision.

*They are not frightened of the four seals.*

*They are frightened because the four seals means I have been sitting in
this room for a year taking it apart while they were learning it.*

*And they are right.*

---

Aoyagi kept him back.

He did it the way he did everything, which was pleasantly, without any
ceremony, by simply saying, "Ruri, a moment," while the others were
getting their things, so that it did not look like being kept back at
all. He was, Tadayoshi had decided long ago, a genuinely kind man, and
had also decided — separately, and without any contradiction — that
this was worth watching.

"Who taught you that?"

"Nobody."

"Ruri."

"Nobody, sensei. I did it in the eleventh month and I have been doing it
since. Not in here."

"No," said Aoyagi. "Not in here." He rubbed his eye with the heel of his
hand. He was about fifty and he looked about fifty at the start of the
day and rather more than that by the end of it. "Do you know how many
people have said that to me in nineteen years? About Dog?"

"No."

"Nor do I. Because it's none." He put the register under his arm. "I've
had three who could do it. I've never had one who could tell me why."

Tadayoshi said nothing.

"I'm going to put a note on your record," said Aoyagi.

---

The hall was cold and there was a shutter banging somewhere and
Tadayoshi stood very still.

"What sort of note."

"A good one," said Aoyagi, surprised. "What sort of note do you think?"

"There was a note last year."

"There was."

"It said *aptitude*."

"It did say aptitude," Aoyagi agreed.

"And I was moved."

"And you were moved. Up a year, into a room where the work would not
bore you into being a nuisance, which — " and here the tiredness came
into his voice for the first time all afternoon, and it was not unkind
and that was the thing about it — "which was the correct decision, and
I made it, and I'd make it again."

*Ask him.*

*Ask him whether the word on the form was the reason. Ask him whether
there was a second reason and whether the second reason was Ryūta's
arm. He is standing here being honest with you and he will answer.*

*And then you will know, and he will know you know, and you will have
to be in this room with him for another year.*

"Thank you, sensei," said Tadayoshi.

Aoyagi looked at him for a second longer than the sentence needed.

"Get off home," he said. "It's a long walk and it's going to rain."

---

It rained.

He went out through the yard with his collar up and his hands in his
sleeves, and about halfway to the gate somebody came up on his left
shoulder at a trot, and it was Ryūta, and Ryūta said, "Hey — the Dog
thing — does it work on the clone one, or is that — "

And then Ryūta's friends came out of the door behind them, three of
them, laughing about something else entirely and not about him at all;
and Ryūta slowed down for about one step, and it was one step, and it
was not even a decision, it was the sort of thing a body does; and then
he said, "Never mind, see you Monday," perfectly pleasantly, and dropped
back.

Tadayoshi went on out through the gate.

*Item. He asked. That is the first question anybody in that room has
asked me since the eleventh month and it was a good one and I would have
liked to answer it.*

*Item. He did not stop asking because of me.*

*Item, and this is the one: I am going to remember his name for a very
long time and he is not going to remember that this happened at all.*

The rain got heavier on the road down and he put his hands under his
arms to keep them warm, because he had nine drops to do when he got in,
and cold hands made bad work, and bad work was what he was for.
