# Chapter 17, Scene 2 — Nothing To Do

*Act 2. A fold of ground under larch, north of Hane. A boy, age thirteen.
POV: the boy. ~1,600 words.*

---

He had twenty-two days and nobody had told him anything since the lid came
off.

That is not a complaint and it did not arrive as one. It is the two facts
he had about his position and he held them the way he held everything,
which was flat and next to each other, and he checked them both about once
a day because a fact you do not check is a bowl.

*Twenty-two. Twelve in the box and ten out of it.*

*And I have been told: **out**, **in**, **north**, and **are you all right
for that shirt**.*

He had counted the words. There had been fewer of them in ten days outside
than there had been in the last four days of the box.

---

Before any of that there was his body, and it took four days and nobody
asked him about it and he did not mention it.

He had walked nine miles on the ninth having not walked more than eleven
paces in a straight line for two years, and he had done it because they
were walking, and he had done it without stopping.

He paid for it from the tenth to the thirteenth.

*The backs of both legs, above the knee, and it is not the muscle, it is
the long thing behind it.*

*Both feet. The left is worse and the left is worse because I have been
putting weight on the outside of it, which I have been doing because the
floor in the room went down toward the drain and I have been walking on a
floor that is not there any more.*

He found that one on the twelfth and was more pleased with it than anything
else that week.

*Two years of a floor that falls a thumb across the width of it, and I have
got it in my legs, and I did not know it was in my legs until I put them on
flat ground.*

*So a room gets into you. That is a thing about rooms and I have never
heard it anywhere.*

He fixed it in three days by making himself walk the fold end to end
twenty times a morning with his weight moved over, which nobody told him to
do and which is not a thing he did unbidden, because he had been told
*north* and the north was not finished, and a man who is going to be told
*north* again had better have feet.

*That is not doing a thing nobody said. That is being ready for the thing
they said.*

**Nobody in that camp noticed him doing it and he did twenty a morning for
nine days.**

---

The days were the problem and he knew it was the days by about the third
one.

In the room there had been nothing to do and that had been correct, because
there had been nothing to do *with*. No floor to lay anything on that
mattered, no light to test, and for the last four months no clock. You
cannot be given nothing to do if there is nothing available.

*Here there is everything and I have not been given any of it.*

He worked at it on the fourth and fifth days and got it clean, and it is
the best thing he did in that fold of ground and it went into the place
where things about them went and stayed there.

*They do things. The two of them do things all day.*

*He does the animal and the ground and the fire and a book. She does the
food and the water and about eleven other things and she talks the whole
time.*

*And neither of them has said one word to me about any of it.*

He sat with that.

*It is not that they are stopping me. Nobody has said stop.*

*It is that a thing nobody has said do is a thing I do not do.*

**And he had known that about himself since he was eleven and had never
once thought of it as a thing about himself.** *It was simply the shape of
a day, the way a wall is the shape of a room.*

---

On the sixth day he did the count and it is the count he keeps for the rest
of his life.

*There are three of us.*

*And there are two of them.*

He went at it properly because it did not sound like a finding and he
wanted it to be one.

*She says things and he does not do them.*

*She says things and I do them.*

*He says things and I do them.*

*He says things and she does about half of them and argues about the other
half and he lets her.*

He put his hand flat on the ground.

*So there is a thing between the two of them that instructions do not go
through, and there is a thing between her and me that is nothing but
instructions, and I am in the second one.*

> ***It is not three. It is two of us and him, and I am in the wrong one.***

**And he was not unhappy about it, and that is important and the prose is
not going to soften it.** *He had a correct finding about a structure and
he had got it in six days off nothing but who did what somebody said, and
it was the first piece of work he had done since the box and he was pleased
with it in the small flat way he was pleased with things.*

*The wrong one is the one I am in. That is a fact and not a complaint.*

*And I would rather know.*

---

**The seventh piece had not started.**

He checked it every day and had done since the eleventh day in the box, and
he was going to go on checking it every day for a long time.

*The sixth came through on the fifth day of the box, which is day five, and
this is day twenty-two, so it is seventeen days and nothing.*

*The gap between the fifth and the sixth was about sixty days in the unit I
have not got, and if the seventh is a half of that then it should have
started by about now.*

He sat against the bank with his hand over the back of his other hand.

*It has not.*

*So either it starts late and the half is wrong, or it starts on time and I
am wrong about when the sixth started, and I am wrong about when the sixth
started, because I have never had the start of it.*

He did that four ways and every way came out the same, which was that he
could not get anywhere without the seventh, and the seventh was not
starting, and he could not make it start.

*Right.*

*Then I wait for it.*

**And it did not occur to him, at any point in twenty-two days, that
waiting for it was the same thing everybody else in that fold of ground was
doing.** *Nobody had told him what they were waiting for, and he had not
asked, because asking is a thing you do when somebody has invited a
question and nobody had.*

---

On about the eighth day he did the thing that is the closest he came to
misery, and it was four minutes long and it looked like nothing and he
would not have used the word.

He got the five pieces out.

There was a floor. There had not been one in the box and there was one now,
flat ground under the larch with the needles brushed off it, and he had
been aware of that for eight days and had not done it.

He laid them out in a row with the bases lined up.

Three and a bit. Two and a bit. Two. One and a half. One.

He went along them with a finger, which is the reading, and the reading
told him what it had told him every time for four months, which was
nothing he did not already know.

*Right.*

He sat and looked at them.

*That is the whole of it.*

*That is everything I have made in thirteen years and it is five pieces of
my own hand and it took two years and it is on the ground under a tree.*

He did not put them away for about four minutes.

Then he did, one at a time, largest first, the way he did, and got up and
went back to the bank and sat down, and that is the eighth day.

---

There is a last thing and it happened on the ninth or tenth and he could
not date it, which annoyed him.

She looked at him.

Not a glance. She was doing something with the fire and she stopped and
looked at him for a period he did not measure because he was not ready and
by the time he was ready to measure it, it had gone.

He got about four seconds and marked it *made and not counted*.

*She has done that eleven times in ten days and I have got two of them
timed.*

*He has done it four times and all four were about the shirt or the food or
whether I could walk, and they were about a second and a half.*

*Hers are longer and they are not about anything.*

He put it in with the things about them.

*I do not know what that is.*

**And there was nothing in the world to attach it to, and nobody to ask,
and it went into the same place as everything else about her and sat there
for two years** — *and it is, in the whole of his life, the single closest
he ever came, unassisted, to working out that somebody was worried about
him.*
