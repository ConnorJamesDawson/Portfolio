# Chapter 4, Scene 9 — The Book at the Customs Post

*Act 1, month 4, the seventh, afternoon. Ōura. Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten.
POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,250 words.*

---

He tried asking people first and he was extremely bad at it.

He worked out how bad at about the fourth attempt, standing on the wharf below
the bridge with fourteen thousand ryō on him and a question that had seemed
straightforward for four days.

*A boat with a house on it went north past the bar at Shioiri on the
third at about twenty past five. Where does a boat like that go.*

That was the question. It was a good question. It had a shape and a date
and a direction in it and it was answerable.

Nobody answered it.

---

The first man said, "North," and laughed, and went back to what he was
doing.

The second man said, "Who's asking?" and Tadayoshi said, "I am," and the
man looked at him for a while and said, "Aye," and went away.

The third was a woman mending a net and she was perfectly pleasant and
she said there were dozens of boats and she did not know one from
another, and then, when he had got about halfway through describing the
house on the deck, she stopped mending the net and said, "Whose boat is
it, love?" in a completely different voice, and he said, "I do not know,"
and she said, "Then why are you looking for it," and he said nothing at
all, and she went back to the net and did not look at him again.

The fourth was a boy of about twelve who wanted paying and then did not
know.

---

He went and sat on the steps under the bridge and worked it out.

*Four. Four attempts and I have got the word north, which I had.*

*And the third one is the one to look at, because she was helping until I
said the house on the deck, and then she stopped, and the stopping was
not about the boat.*

*She asked me whose it was. And I said I did not know. And then she asked
me why I was looking for it, and I did not answer, because I could not
answer it, and she heard that.*

He put his chin on his fists.

*A question is not a thing you hand over. It has a person at both ends of
it.*

*She was not answering my question. She was answering a boy, and what she
had in front of her was a boy of ten on a wharf on his own who wants to
know about a boat and will not say why, and everything she knows about me
came out of that, and none of it was good.*

*And I cannot fix that. I cannot make her think a different thing. I do
not know how that is done — I have never known how that is done; I
watched Iwao do it to a man in a shop for ninety seconds and got nothing
out of it at all.*

He sat under the bridge for about eleven minutes.

*So do not ask people.*

---

The customs post at the Ōura end of the bridge had nobody in it and had
had nobody in it every single time he had ever walked over that bridge in
his life, which he had assumed for ten years meant it was disused.

It was not disused. It was unmanned, which is a different thing, and the
difference turned out to be a book.

He went in at about four when the bridge was busy, because a boy walking
into an empty hut when the street is empty is a boy walking into an empty
hut, and a boy walking into an empty hut when forty people are going past
is nobody.

There was a shelf, and a stool, and a cold brazier, and a ledger.

---

He stood in the doorway of the customs post at Ōura with a ledger open in
his hands and felt something go through him that he did not have a name
for and did not much care for.

It was ruled in five columns.

*Day. Craft. Master. Cargo. Dues.*

It went back four years. It was not complete — there were runs of a
fortnight where nobody had written anything, and one place where somebody
had written *see other book* and there was no other book — and about a
third of the master column was blank or said *ditto* or said something
that was obviously a nickname.

And it did not have his boat in it, because his boat had gone north past
a bar forty miles up the coast and had never come near Ōura or the river
or the bridge.

None of that mattered.

*This exists.*

*A hut with nobody in it has a book in it with four years of every craft
that tied up at this bridge, and it is ruled, and it has a day column,
and a man I have never met has been writing in it since before I could
read.*

*I have spent four days on a wharf trying to make people tell me things.*

*And there is a book.*

---

He read it for about forty minutes standing up, and put it back exactly
square with the edge of the shelf, and went out.

Then he walked to the end of the bridge and stood and looked at the
water going down towards the coast.

*Sunaba is the port.*

*Kanaya's house is at Sunaba and everything from this counter goes to
Sunaba and everything that leaves this coast leaves out of Sunaba, and a
port that size does not run on an unmanned hut. A port that size has a
harbour master and dues and a proper book with somebody in a room whose
job it is.*

*Two days' walk.*

*And I have fourteen thousand ryō, which is about a month if I am careful and
sleep badly.*

*I do not know what the boat was called. I do not know who was on it
except who was on it. I do not know where it went and I have got one
direction and one time and one date and I am not going to get anything
else out of a person as long as I live.*

*But something went north past that bar on the third of the fourth month
at about twenty past five, and boats stop somewhere, and when they stop
somebody writes it down.*

He stood on the bridge at Ōura for a while.

Then he went and bought a knife and a blanket and a piece of oilcloth for
one and a half of the fourteen, and got out of the town before dark on
the Sunaba road, which he had never been on, and which was busy, and on
which nobody looked at him at all.

---

He wrote it that night under a hedge, in the second column, in the
dark, about a quarter of an inch out of true.

*Do not ask people. Find where it is written down.*

He looked at where it would be.

Then he put the pencil back in the spine, and put his hand flat against
his coat over the place where the drop was, and lay down.

He was ten years, three months and eleven days old, and he had fourteen
thousand ryō in four places on his body, and he had just invented the method
he was going to use for the rest of his life.
