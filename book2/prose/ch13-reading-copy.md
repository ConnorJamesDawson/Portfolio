# Chapter 13 — A Place To Put Her

*Book two. **Act two opens.** Year 1, month 1.*
*10 scenes, 16,530 words. Tadayoshi ×5 · Uzuki ×3 · Kimimaro ×2.*



*Act 2, year 1, month 1, the first to the third. Ōtoma. Tadayoshi Ruri,
age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,700 words.*

---

He noticed her feet on the second day of the year, going up the lane out
of Ōtoma toward the cooperage, and he noticed them the way he noticed
everything, which was as a fault in a system.

*She is walking wrong.*

*She has been walking wrong for four days and I put it down as tired and
it is not tired, it is the left one, and the left one is a shoe that is
not her shoe.*

He got in front of her and stopped and said, "Take them off."

She took them off. She did it immediately and without asking why, which
he registered and did not think about for another three weeks.

---

The right one was hers and was worn through at the ball. The left one was
about a size and a half too big and had been somebody else's and had had
a rag in it and the rag had come out in the rain on the twenty-fifth of
the tenth month and had never gone back in.

The foot inside it had a place on the outside edge where the shoe had been
taking her weight for eleven weeks in a place a shoe is not supposed to.

He looked at that for a while.

*Fifty ryō for the pair off a rag heap on the ninth of the tenth month.*

*And she has done four hundred miles in them, and eleven flights of stone
steps, and four days down a mountain, and she has not said anything about
it, and I have not looked.*

He handed them back.

"We'll do it this afternoon," he said.

---

It took an afternoon and it cost eleven hundred and forty ryō and he did
not hesitate over one item of it.

**The boots.** *Three hundred and twenty, from a man with a bench who was
not the man with the bench, and Tadayoshi did the entire thing in about
six minutes and got sixty off the asking price without appearing to try.*
He noticed that afterwards and had no time for it.

**A pack**, a proper one, small, at two hundred, because she had been
carrying a rolled blanket with a cord round it since the seventeenth of
the twelfth month and a cord across a shoulder for four days is a shoulder
you cannot use.

**A blanket that was not the blanket**, at three hundred, and he checked
the weave twice and made her hold it against herself in the shop.

**And a knife.**

---

That one he thought about for about ninety seconds, which was longer than
he had thought about any of the others.

*She has got the one from the eighth month and she has had it since Nakadō
and she keeps it inside her coat, and a knife inside a coat is a knife you
cannot get to and a knife nobody knows you have got.*

*And on a road, the second one of those is worth more than the first.*

He worked it out standing in a yard with a knife in his hand.

*No. It is not.*

*It is worth more if she is a girl on a road on her own. It is worth less
if she is a girl on a road with me, because a girl with a visible knife is
a girl somebody has to make a decision about, and most people will decide
not to.*

He bought her one she could wear where people could see it. Two hundred
and twenty, plain, a working knife and not a good one, and he put it on
her belt himself because she had never worn one on the outside and did not
know where it went.

---

He entered the whole of it that night in the ordinary column, item by
item, with the prices.

Then he sat and looked at the entry, because there was a thing wrong with
it and he could not find the thing.

*Eleven forty. Four items. All four necessary and all four correct and I
would make the same purchases again tomorrow.*

*So what is wrong with it.*

He went round it twice and did not get it and left it, and what was wrong
with it was that he had done the whole thing in an afternoon, at speed,
without one moment of the thing that had happened to him in a shop in
Ōtoma on the twenty-ninth of the tenth month with a coat in his hands, and
he had not noticed the difference, and he was still wearing the coat.

---

She asked for the hat on the third.

That was the only thing she asked for and it was the first thing she had
asked him for in five weeks, and she asked for it in the flat way she said
most things, standing by the door with her pack already on.

"Can I have a hat."

"What sort?"

"One with a brim on it that goes all the way round."

He said, "Right," and went and got one, and it was ninety, and it was the
sort a man wears on a road in the rain, and it was too big for her and she
put it on and did not take it off.

He did not ask what it was for.

That is exact and it is worth putting down: **he did not ask, and it did
not occur to him to ask, and he was not being tactful.** *A person asks
for a thing they need. She had asked for a thing. It was ninety.*

He put it in the ordinary column with the other four and drew a line under
the lot.

---

The docket came up the ladder on the third and it was nine hundred ryō.

He read it twice, because that was the shape of the year now, and then put
it down and did the thing he did with any number, which was to put it in
the line.

> *3,600. 2,900. 4,100. 14,000. 2,200. 4,800.*
>
> *900.*

He looked at it for about four seconds.

*It is a small one. There are small ones.*

*Sakabe was two two and Sakabe was fine.*

He picked it up and read the rest of it and it was four days out and back
and one man in a lodging house, and it was, by every measure he had, an
ordinary job that would take him five days and would come out clean.

He said yes and did not think about it again for eleven days.

---

The last thing he did on the third was the thing he had been not doing
since the twenty-ninth of the twelfth month, which was to open the back of
the ledger and look at the fourth column.

There were two things in it.

The first was a name at the top of a page with nothing else on it and no
heading, and he had put it there on the twenty-ninth and had not added
anything to it since, and he did not add anything to it now.

The second was on the facing page and was one line, and he had written it
in Ōgi on the twenty-first of the twelfth month while she was asleep four
feet away in a stable with a coat over her.

> *Open: get her to a door.*

He looked at it.

*It has been thirteen days.*

*I have not done one thing about it in thirteen days and I have done four
other things and bought a knife.*

He got the pencil out and put a date beside it, because dating a thing is
the first half of doing it, and then sat with the pencil over the page for
a while, because the second half wanted a place in it and he did not have
one.

*There is no list.*

*There is no list of doors and there was no list in the eleventh month
either, and the difference is that in the eleventh month I had fourteen
thousand ryō and no time and now I have got time.*

He wrote three words under it and shut the book.

> *Find one. Properly.*

---

She was asleep by the wall with the hat on the floor beside her and the
new pack under her head, and the knife was on the outside of her belt
where he had put it, and she had not taken that off either.

He looked at that for a second longer than he needed to and then did not
know what to do with having looked, so he did the six minutes on the roll
and went to sleep.

*Act 2, year 1, month 1, the second and third. Ōtoma. Nozaki Uzuki, age
eleven. POV: Uzuki. ~1,600 words.*

---

She took her shoes off in the lane in under two seconds and she was
thinking about it before he had turned round.

*I did not ask why.*

*He said take them off and I took them off and I was down on one knee with
the strap before the end of the sentence, and I did not ask why, and I did
not think about not asking, and it took me about a second and a half to be
holding them.*

She stood in a lane in the first week of the year with a shoe in each hand
and had it arrive whole, the way things arrived now, which was fast and
sideways and about herself.

*That is the fifteenth of the twelfth month.*

---

She had done this before. That was the whole of it and there was no way
round it.

A man had stood four feet from her in a root cellar with a book and said
*open, please*, and she had opened, and he had said *hands, both*, and she
had put them out at the right height **without being shown the height**,
and when he had wanted the hair he had made a small motion with two
fingers and she had turned round and stood and waited and turned back.

Eleven minutes, and nobody had had to tell her anything twice.

*And I worked out afterwards why I was good at it and the answer was
because I had had practice.*

She looked at the shoes in her hands.

*And I have just done it again in a lane. Faster.*

---

She worked at it for most of the afternoon, in the gaps, while he was
doing things.

The first go was the obvious one and she threw it out inside a minute.

*It is not the same, because he was looking at my feet and my feet are
broken.*

*True, and it does not help, because I did not know that when I did it. I
did it before I knew why. The reason arrived afterwards and I was already
on one knee.*

The second go was worse.

*So what I have got is a thing that happens to me before I decide
anything. Somebody who is above me says a short instruction and my hands
do it, and it does not matter what the instruction is or who is saying it,
and I have not got a way to tell the difference from inside.*

She sat on a wall while he was in a shop and put her chin on her knees.

*And it was worth a bowl three times a day for forty-five days and it is
the only reason I am alive, so I am not going to pretend I wish I did not
have it.*

*I just would like to know where the edge of it is.*

---

The other thing happened over the course of the same afternoon and it took
her until the evening to see it, because it was an absence and absences
are slow.

He bought four things.

He bought boots and he stood in the shop and did the whole transaction in
about six minutes, and she watched him take sixty ryō off a man who had
not intended to give it, and she watched *how*, which was that he did not
appear to want them.

He bought a pack, and a blanket he made her hold against herself, and a
knife he put on her belt himself because she had never worn one where
people could see it.

And at no point in the entire afternoon did he say a number in front of
her.

---

She got it on the wall outside the fourth shop and it went into her like
cold water.

*He has not told me what any of it cost.*

She went back over it to be certain, because being certain was the only
thing she had that was hers, and she was certain.

*Not once. Not the boots and not the pack and not the blanket and not the
knife. He said prices to the men. He did not say one of them to me.*

*And he is not hiding it. He wrote every single one of them down in a book
four feet from me and did not put his hand over it.*

She sat with that.

*So it is not that he does not want me to know.*

*It is that it did not come up.*

---

She had a list in her head of everything she had ever been given and she
had never had to make it, because it made itself, and it was not long.

*The coat at the Kadono-dera. Enkai gave me that, and on the twenty-eighth
of the tenth month a man in a hall said a number about me while I was
wearing it, and then said something else about the second column, and both
of those were what the coat was for.*

*The bowl. Three times a day, the same amount, and I proved it eleven
times off a chip in the rim, and what it was for was the thing on the
paper still being worth what it says.*

*The mat and two blankets and a lamp for an hour, and it was warmer than
upstairs, and that was so I would keep.*

*My mother gave me a house and Kuriya put a figure on the pans in it.*

*Shimoda gave me a halter to hold.*

She stopped there because she had got to the end and there was nothing
after it.

*Everything I have ever been given had what it cost written on the back of
it, and I could always read it, and I have been reading it since the sixth
month, and I am good at it.*

*And I have just been given four things and I have turned all four of them
over and there is nothing on the back.*

---

She did not enjoy that and she made herself notice that she did not.

*I do not like it.*

*And that is not sense. That is a person who has been handed a bowl for
forty-five days wanting to know what the bowl is for, and being told
nothing, and finding out that being told nothing is worse.*

She turned it once more and could not get anywhere with it, and put it
where things went, and got down off the wall when he came out.

---

She asked for the hat on the third.

She had had it planned for two days and had gone over the wording about
nine times, because she needed a brim that went all the way round and not
one that only went at the front, and a brim that goes all the way round is
a specific thing to want and specific things get asked about.

She had four answers ready.

*It is for the rain.*

*It is for the sun on the water.*

*I had one at home.*

*And the true one, which is that it is the fourth of the first month and
it has been sixty-eight days since the rain took it out and it is about
five inches now and in the second month it will be seven, and there is a
man somewhere with a piece of paper about me on it that has a measurement
on it he made with his own hand.*

She said, "Can I have a hat."

"What sort?"

"One with a brim on it that goes all the way round."

He said, "Right."

---

He went and got one and it was too big and it was the sort a man wears on
a road, and she put it on and did not take it off for the rest of the day.

And he did not ask.

She waited about four hours for the question, in the way you wait for a
door to bang in a house where the doors bang, and it did not come at any
point, and by the evening she understood that it was not coming and had to
sit down with what that meant.

*He does not want to know.*

*No. That is wrong and it is the sort of wrong I do when I am tired.*

*He has not thought about it. It is a hat and it was ninety and I asked
for it and now I have got one. That is the whole of the transaction in his
head and there is no second half to it.*

She lay down on the boards with the hat beside her and the pack under her
head and the knife still on the outside of her belt, because she had
decided at about noon that she was going to sleep in it until it stopped
feeling like somebody else's.

*Second time in five weeks that a question has not arrived.*

*And I have not worked out whether that is a good thing.*

Then she did the count, because she did it last and always had, and it
lived in her head now and had done since the ninth of the twelfth month
when the charcoal ran out.

*One thousand five hundred and eighteen.*

She held it for a second to be sure she had it, and had it, and went to
sleep.

*Act 2. A room, and then not a room. A boy, age thirteen. POV: the boy.
~1,900 words.*

---

The door opened between bowls.

He knew it was wrong before it was all the way open, and he knew it off
the light under it, which had come and gone across that floor about two
thousand times and had never once done what it did that morning, which was
to break in the middle.

*Somebody is standing in it.*

*And it is not a bowl, because the bowl comes with the door shut behind
the arm and this one is open.*

He sat up.

---

There were two of them and one was the arm.

He knew the arm the way you know a thing you have measured, which is
exactly and without any feeling attached to it: how it came down, how far
it came down, how long it stayed. He had never seen the whole of the man
attached to it. He saw him now and did not learn anything, because a face
is not a measurement and he had never been given a way to read one.

The other one said, "Up."

He got up.

He did not think about not getting up. That is not courage or its absence,
and it is not obedience either, and there is no word in general use for
what it actually was, which is that **a person who has never been offered
a second option does not have a mechanism for producing one.**

---

He put his hand down on the mat and picked the four pieces up before he
went.

That was the only thing he did that morning that was his, and he did it
fast and without deciding to, and he had them closed in his left hand
before either of the men had finished turning round.

Nobody said anything about it.

*They did not see. Or they saw and it did not matter.*

*And I do not know which and it is the same question as the bowl and I am
not going to get an answer to that one either.*

He put them inside his shirt at the waist where the cloth was doubled, and
walked out of the room he had been in for two years, and did not look back
at it, because there was nothing in it and he knew exactly what was in it.

---

There were eleven steps up and he counted them.

Then there was a passage with a door at the end and the door was open, and
the light in the passage was not lamp light and he had not seen light that
was not lamp light since he was eleven years old.

He stopped.

Neither of them made him move. That is worth putting down: he stopped in a
passage for about four seconds and nobody said anything and nobody put a
hand on him, and afterwards he could not work out whether that was because
they were being decent or because it did not cost them anything.

Then he went out.

---

The first thing he did outside was look for the wall.

He did it before anything else and he did it without knowing he was doing
it, and it took him about a second and a half to understand what he was
looking for and about four more to understand that there was not one.

He turned right round on the spot, all the way, which the second man
watched him do and did not comment on.

*There is no wall.*

*There is ground and then there is more ground and then there is a thing
that is a long way off and is a shape and I do not know what it is, and
then above all of it there is —*

He stopped there because the sentence had nowhere to go.

He stood in a yard on the first week of the first month of a year he did
not know the number of, thirteen years old, having been in a room since he
was eleven, and looked up, and there was nothing to hit.

---

What he did about it was count.

That was not a failure of feeling and it should not be read as one. **It
was the only instrument he owned and he put it on the largest thing he had
ever seen, immediately, because that is what an instrument is for.**

*How far can I see.*

He tried to do it and could not, and found out why in about ten seconds
and was interested rather than upset.

*I have not got a thing to say it in.*

*I have got bowls, and bowls are for how long. I have got thumbs, and
thumbs are for how long a thing is when it is in my hand. And that shape
is not in my hand and it is not a length of time.*

*So I have not got a unit.*

---

He made one.

He did it standing in the yard while they were doing something with a
cart, and it took him about two minutes, and it was crude and he knew it
was crude and it worked.

*The yard is a hundred and something paces across and I can pace it.*

*So the yard is one.*

*And that shape is — *

He held his thumb up at arm's length, which he had done ten thousand times
with a piece of bone and had never once done with anything else, and put
it against the shape, and the shape was about four thumbs, and the wall of
the yard at the far end was about a hundred.

He did the division on his fingers against his leg.

*So it is about twenty-five yards away.*

*Twenty-five yards. Where a yard is the yard.*

He had no way at all of knowing whether that was right, and it was not
right, and it was wrong by a factor he would not find out for two years.
It did not matter in the slightest. **He had turned the largest thing he
had ever seen into a number in under three minutes with a thumb, and he
was pleased about it, and he had no word for that either.**

---

They walked.

He had not walked more than eleven steps in a straight line in two years
and he found out about it in the first hour, in the backs of his legs, and
he went on walking because that was the instruction, and by the middle of
the afternoon the backs of his legs had stopped being information and had
become weather.

He counted the day into the only unit he had.

*This is longer than four bowls.*

*This is longer than eight.*

At about that point he understood that he was doing something he could not
do, and stopped, and sat with it while walking, which is a thing you can
do.

*Bowls are not a length. Bowls are a thing that happens and I count the
things.*

*And nothing has happened since this morning except walking, so I have not
got a number for any of today.*

He put it down as an anomaly.

He was wrong about that, and it was the largest thing he was wrong about
in his life, and he was wrong about it for about nine days.

---

The other thing about that day was people.

He saw fourteen of them and he counted them, and eleven were on a road
going the other way and three were in a field doing something to it, and
one of the eleven was small.

*That one is smaller than me.*

*That one is smaller than me and it is outside.*

He did not have anywhere to put that so he did not put it anywhere, and he
looked at the small one for as long as the road allowed, which was about
nine seconds, and the small one looked back at him for about two of them
and then looked at the man walking behind him and then looked at the
ground and kept walking.

He thought about that for some time afterwards and never got it to come
out.

*It stopped looking at me when it looked at him.*

---

The bowl came at the end of the day, in a yard he had not seen before,
handed down by a different arm.

He looked at it for a moment before he took it.

*This is one.*

*And the last one was this morning and there was not one in the middle.*

He ate it. It was the bigger amount, the one that had started on the ninth
of the twelfth month and had been the same eleven times and had told him
that somebody had decided something.

*So the amount is holding.*

*And the times have gone.*

He sat with his back against a wall that was not his wall, with four
pieces of himself inside his shirt and a fifth about ninety-five bowls
grown on the back of his left hand, and put it where the true things went.

*The amount is a thing about the person. The times were a thing about the
building.*

*I have got a new building.*

That was as far as he got and it was correct as far as it went, and what
was underneath it was that the number he had spent five months getting —
nine hundred and three, seven more times, a half each time, done in fives
on his fingers in the dark — was a number in bowls, **and bowls had just
stopped being a clock.**

He did not know that yet.

He lay down on his side facing the wall, because that was how you lay
down, and felt along the back of his left hand for the place, and it was
where it had been that morning.

*Ninety-five.*

*Thirty-four to go.*

Then, because it was the first thing he had made in a very long time and
he wanted to be sure he still had it, he held his thumb up in the dark
against nothing at all.

*Twenty-five yards.*

*Where a yard is the yard.*

*Act 2, year 1, month 1, the fourth to the eleventh. Ōtoma, Kurihama and
back. Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,700 words.*

---

The first problem was where to put her while he did it, and he solved it
badly and knew he had.

*I cannot take her onto it and I cannot leave her in Ōtoma, because Ōtoma
is a yard with eleven men in it who know exactly who I am and what I am
for, and a girl in that yard for five days is a girl being asked
questions.*

He took her with him to the edge of it, which meant four days on a road
and then an inn at Kurihama with a room paid for two nights and a girl in
it who had been told to stay in it.

She stayed in it.

He came back at about four in the morning on the ninth with a job behind
him and found her sitting on the floor by the door with the knife across
her knees and the lamp out, and she had been there, by her own account
afterwards, for about six hours.

He did not ask why she was by the door. He worked it out on the road the
next day and put it in the operational column, and the entry had no
solution in it.

> *A room she cannot leave is a room she has been in before.*

---

The job itself was nine hundred ryō and it was the easiest thing he had
ever been paid for.

A man called Sagawa, in a lodging on the east side of Kurihama, who had
been taking money from two people at once for about four years and had
finally taken it from the wrong second person. First floor, one door,
one window, a stair that a man of sixty had been going up at about the
same time every night for eleven years.

Setting: twenty-five minutes.

He went in through the window at ten past one and did it with the knife at
the base of the skull while the man was on his side, and the man's right
hand came up and got hold of the bedding and closed on it and did not let
go, and Tadayoshi held the back of his head down with the other hand until
the hand on the bedding stopped meaning anything.

It took under a minute and the man never made a word.

He did the leaving in three, went out over the same sill, put the shutter
back at the angle he had found it, and was two miles east before anybody
in that building was awake.

The entry was two lines and the second column was empty.

---

He did the arithmetic on the road back, on the tenth, because it had been
sitting in him since the third and he had not let it out.

*Nine hundred for a man who had been doing that for four years to two
people, one of whom had money and one of whom had enough money to buy
this.*

*Sakuta was three six for eleven years of passing names.*

He put the line up in his head, which he could do now without the book.

> *3,600 — 2,900 — 4,100 — 14,000 — 2,200 — 4,800 — 900.*

*And the fourteen is off the line and always was and I know why.*

*And the nine hundred is off it the other way and I do not.*

---

He did the second one, which was eleven hundred, on the eleventh, and it
was at Sakabe, which was four days the wrong way, and there was nothing
wrong with it either.

Two lines. No corrections.

And then he sat on a wall above the Sakabe wharf in the middle of the
afternoon and did the thing properly, with the book open, because two is a
line and he had said that to himself about a boy's hand once and it was
still true.

**He did the fees first.**

> *Ninth month: 3,600.*
> *Tenth month: 2,900, 4,100, 14,000.*
> *Eleventh month: 2,200, 4,800.*
> *First month: 900, 1,100.*

*Take the fourteen out, because the fourteen was the ground and the ground
was a different kind of job.*

*Then the mean of the first five is three thousand five hundred and twenty.*

*And the mean of the last two is one thousand.*

**Then the gaps**, which was the part he had not done before and which
took him about twenty minutes.

> *26/9 to 2/10 — six days.*
> *2/10 to 9/10 — seven.*
> *9/10 to 20/10 — eleven.*
> *20/10 to 24/10 — four.*
> *24/10 to 3/11 — ten.*
> *3/11 to 5/1 — **sixty-three.***

He looked at the sixty-three for a while.

*Twelve of those are the mountain. Nine are the road down. So call it
forty-two days in Ōtoma with nothing on the boards.*

*And I did not notice, because I had a girl and a debt and four things to
buy and a page at the back of a book I could not close.*

---

He went at it the way he went at everything, which was to build the case
against himself first and see whether it stood up.

*One. It is the twelfth and first months, and the twelfth and first months
are the worst two months in the year for everything.*

Which was true. He had walked an empty road for nine days and counted six
people where he had counted forty-one in the sixth month and had written
it down himself.

*Two. There is a war on and it has been going badly since the eighth month
and people who are frightened do not commission things, they wait.*

Which was also true and he had heard three separate men say some version
of it in a yard.

*Three. I was away for twenty-one days and a docket that comes for a man
who is four hundred miles up a mountain gets given to somebody else.*

Which was true and was the one he liked best, because it had a mechanism
in it.

He put all three down and looked at them.

*All three of those are true.*

*And all three of them are things about the world, and every one of them
explains the gaps and not one of them explains the money.*

He sat with that.

*A thin market gives me fewer jobs at the same price. It does not give me
the same number of jobs at a third of the price, and it does not do it in
one month, and it does not do it in the same month that the gaps went from
eleven to forty-two.*

*Two things moved at once and in the same direction and they are not the
same thing and they have got one cause.*

He got that far. He got to precisely that line and no further, and the
line is correct and is the whole of the chapter, and what he did next was
the mistake.

*Act 2. A new room. A boy, age thirteen. POV: the boy. ~1,750 words.*

---

He held it as an anomaly for nine days and then had to give it up.

The bowls came. That was never the problem and he was careful about that,
because a person who is hungry will tell himself the food has stopped when
it has only moved.

*It has not stopped.*

*It has come every day since the road and it is the bigger amount every
time, which I checked with the third piece against the crack in the glaze
for the first four days until they took the bowl away between times and I
could not.*

*So the amount is holding.*

*And the times are gone and they have not come back and it is nine.*

---

He worked out what had happened to him at about the ninth day and it took
him under a minute once he stopped trying to make it be temporary.

*In the room the bowl came at the same three times because there was one
man and one room and one day, and the man had a round to do and I was on
it.*

*Here there are a great many people and I am not on a round. I am a thing
somebody remembers.*

He sat with that.

*And a thing somebody remembers is not a clock.*

---

Which meant the number was still there and could not be spent.

He got that out and looked at it, because it was the largest object he
owned.

> *Nine hundred and three.*

*Seven more times, at a half each time. A hundred and twenty-nine bowls
apiece, which is the wrong figure and is the only one I have got.*

*And ninety-five of the fifth one done, and thirty-four to go.*

*And I do not know how many days ninety-five bowls is any more, and I do
not know whether the thirty-four is thirty-four of anything.*

He put his hand flat on the floor.

*So I have got a number and it is in a unit that has stopped existing.*

**He did not despair about it, and that is not a virtue and should not be
read as one.** *Despair requires a picture of a future that is being taken
away from you, and he had one number and no calendar and had never in his
life been shown what a year was.* **What he had instead was the specific
flat unpleasantness of an instrument that has broken in the hand**, which
he had felt exactly once before, in the sixth month, and had no word for
then either.

---

He tried three things and he tried them properly and all three failed, and
he wrote none of it down because he had never had anything to write with.

**The light.**

In the room there had been a strip of it under the door on the side where
the steps were, and it had moved, and it had been good enough for roughly
where in the morning he was.

Here there was a gap high up on one wall and it did the same thing, and he
watched it for four days and then understood the problem, and the problem
was not the light.

*It comes and it goes and it moves the same way every time.*

*But it only tells me about the inside of one day. It does not tell me
where one day stops and the next one starts, because I do not always sleep
between them and sometimes I sleep twice.*

*In the room the bowls did that. Three bowls was a day. The light was
where in it.*

*The light was never the clock. The light was the hour hand and I have
lost the other one.*

**The noise.**

There were people over him and to the north of him and he had four days of
being interested in that before it went the way of the light.

*They are not regular.*

*They are a lot of people doing a lot of different things and some of them
are the same every day and some of them are not, and I cannot tell which
is which without a clock, and the reason I am counting them is to make a
clock.*

He sat with that one for a while because he liked the shape of it and had
never met one before.

*That is a thing that eats itself.*

**And himself.**

The third one was the worst and it took him the longest and he had known
it was going to fail before he started, and started anyway, because the
list had three things on it and he had done two.

*I could count sleeps.*

*And I cannot, because I have never once known how long I have been
asleep, and the whole of the reason a person knows that is that something
happened while they were out and they can see that it happened.*

*You cannot measure a thing from inside it.*

---

So on about the fourteenth day he did the only thing left, which was to
measure the room.

He did it because it was the one instrument he still had. He had thumbs
and he had paces and he had, since the yard, a thing he had made himself
which was a yard, and none of them were time, and a room is not time
either, and he did it anyway.

It took him two days and he did it four times over.

*Eleven paces by seven. The long wall is stone to about my chest and then
it is not stone, and I do not know what it is.*

*The floor is not level. It goes down about a thumb across the width of it
toward the north corner and there is a drain there, or a hole where a
drain was.*

*The door is one and a half paces from the east corner and it opens
inward and the hinge is on the side away from the drain.*

*There is a gap high on the west wall and it is about four thumbs by
eleven and I cannot reach it and I could if I stood on something and there
is nothing.*

*The ceiling is above my hand and about a foot above it, and I got that by
throwing a piece of the floor at it and listening, which took nine goes.*

He finished on the sixteenth day and had a room in his head, complete, to
about a thumb, and had absolutely no use for any of it.

*I have got the exact size of a room and I do not know what day it is.*

And he had, for about four minutes, the closest thing to being funny that
had ever happened inside his head, and did not recognise it, and it went.

---

The other thing was that somebody spoke to him.

It happened on the day he finished the room, in the passage, being taken
somewhere, and it was a man he had not seen before who was going the other
way with something under his arm, and the man slowed down as he passed and
said, without stopping:

"Cold enough for you?"

---

He worked on it for four bowls.

*That is a question.*

*It has got the shape of a question. It went up at the end.*

*So I should have answered it and I did not answer it, because by the time
I had it he was past me, and the next time somebody does that I am going
to be ready.*

He got ready.

He spent the next two days building the answer, because it was a question
about a thing he could measure and measuring was the one thing he was
good at, and by the end of the second day he had it and he was, in his own
flat way, pleased with it.

*It is colder than the room was. It is colder at the north end where the
drain is by about — I do not have a unit for cold. I have got: I can see
my breath in the morning and I could not in the room, and my hands are
worse in the morning than they were and better by the middle, and the
fifth piece has been coming at about the same rate and cold does not seem
to be in it.*

*That is the answer. That is a good answer, and it is true, and I have
checked it.*

Nobody asked him again.

He carried the answer for eleven days and then, at some point, stopped
carrying it, and it did not go anywhere and did not become anything, and
it was the first thing he had ever prepared to say to another human being.

---

He lay down on his side facing the wall on the night he finished the room,
in the dark, in a place eleven paces by seven with a drain in the north
corner, and put the four pieces in a row on the floor with the bases lined
up by feel, which he could do without seeing because he had done it about
two hundred times.

The straight one went at the end.

He felt along the back of his left hand for the fifth.

*It is coming.*

*And I do not know how much of it is done.*

He put his hand down.

*Nine hundred and three.*

*In bowls.*

He held it, because it was the only thing he had that anybody had ever
worked for, and he was not going to let go of it just because it had
stopped meaning anything.

*Act 2, year 1, month 1, the eighteenth to the twenty-fourth. The Hazaki
road and west. Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,800 words.*

---

He came out of Hazaki on the eighteenth and got about four miles and then
found out something about himself that he had not known, which was that he
did not know where he was going.

He stopped in the road to work it out.

*Ōtoma is four days west and there is nothing on the boards at Ōtoma,
because I was in the yard on the twelfth and there was nothing then and
there is no reason for there to be anything now.*

*So I am walking four days to stand in a yard.*

He stood there.

*And that is the first time in nine months that I have not had a thing at
the end of a road.*

---

He spent the nineteenth and the twentieth and most of the twenty-first
doing things that were not nothing but were not anything either, and he
was aware of every hour of it.

He did the kit properly, which took a morning: both pairs of boots
greased, the frame gone over, the throwables counted and four of them
replaced, the grit checked and it was fine.

He did the sheets on the twentieth, which was ten days early, and there
was nothing to add and he wrote nothing this time because he had written
the nothing on the first and a second nothing is not an entry.

He walked six miles up a valley he had no business in and looked at a mill
for about an hour, and worked out how it was built and what was wrong with
the race, and came back.

And on the evening of the twenty-first he sat on a wall above a road with
eighteen thousand ryō split four ways about his person and had it arrive
properly.

*I have got money and no work and nowhere to be.*

*And I have been in that state for four days and I have filled it with
kit.*

---

She caught him on the twenty-second at about eleven in the morning and he
knew she was there for eleven minutes before he did anything about it.

He knew the way he knew anything, which was that there had been somebody
on that road behind him at about the same distance for long enough to stop
being a coincidence, and he had gone through the four things he did about
that, in order, and the fourth thing was to look.

He looked.

*Forty yards.*

*Hat. Pack. Boots.*

He turned round and kept walking.

---

He did the arithmetic while he walked, because that was what he had, and
it took about a mile and a half and it did not go anywhere good.

*She left Hazaki. She has been walking since about the twenty-first, so
she came out on the fifth morning.*

*Sen has eight thousand ryō and a year and no girl in it, and that is not
Sen's fault and it is not recoverable and I am not going back to ask for
it, and if I did she would be right not to give it to me.*

*So the eight thousand is spent. Call that the cost of the operation.*

*And the operation was correct. I have gone over it and I would do the
same again with the same information, and the information was good — two
days at four hundred yards and three people asked and the fourth question
was in the right place.*

He walked on.

*The door was not the problem.*

---

He went at what to do about it and got four options and they took him
another mile.

*One. Send her back.* — *Four days there and four days back, and she came
out on the fifth morning of it and would come out again on the fifth
morning of the next one, and I would have spent eight days and learned
what I already know.*

*Two. Find a different one.* — *Same answer. It is not the door.*

*Three. Ask her why.*

He stopped on that one for about four hundred yards.

*And do what with the answer.*

*If she says a thing I can fix, I fix it and take her back, and she comes
out again on the fifth morning, because whatever it is is not a thing that
lives in Hazaki.*

*And if she says a thing I cannot fix, then I have asked a person a
question I cannot do anything about, and I have done that once, on a road
above a weir, to somebody who did not answer it, and I have not got a
column for it yet.*

*Four —*

He did not have a fourth. He noticed that he did not have a fourth and
noticed that he had been here before, at forty feet, on the twentieth of
the tenth month, with a count running that he had not started.

*So there is no fourth thing.*

*And the last time there was no fourth thing I went north.*

---

He stopped at noon.

He did it at a place where a road comes over a shoulder and there is a
stone that people have obviously sat on for about two hundred years, and
he sat on it and got the food out and put half of it on the stone beside
him and did not look back down the road.

It took her about four minutes.

She came up the last of it and stopped about six feet away and stood
there, and he did not look at her and she did not say anything, and after
a bit she came and sat down on the other end of the stone and picked up
the half that was on it and ate it.

Neither of them said one word.

---

They did six miles that afternoon like that.

He did not ask and she did not offer, and he thought about it for the
first two of the six and then stopped thinking about it, and by the fourth
mile he was doing what he always did on a road, which was reading the
ground and pricing the walls and working out which of the two hills he
could see had been coppiced and which had not.

At about four o'clock she said, "That one's been cut," about the near
hill.

He said, "Twice. There's a second line under the first one, about a third
of the way up."

She looked at it for a while and then said, "Yes."

That was the whole of the conversation and it was the only one, and it was
the first time either of them had said anything to the other that was not
about the work or the road or an instruction, and neither of them noticed
it.

---

He did the book that night.

He did the ordinary column first, because he did it first, and it took him
four lines and had nothing in it.

Then he went to the back and turned to the facing page and looked at the
line he had written in Ōgi on the twenty-first of the twelfth month while
she was asleep four feet away in a stable with a coat over her.

> *Open: get her to a door.*

He looked at it for a long time.

*I could strike it out. It would be a lie and it would be a small one and
nobody would ever read this book but me.*

*And the whole reason I keep it is that nobody reads it but me.*

He did not strike it out.

He put the date beside it, and under it, in the same hand, he wrote three
lines, and they took him about eleven minutes and he changed two words.

> *24/1. Door found at Hazaki. Verified 2 days at 400 yds + 3 in the town.
> Paid 8,000 for the year, against 6,000 asked.*
>
> *Correct on every column I have got. Would do it again with the same
> information.*
>
> *Entry not closed. The door was not the problem.*

---

Then he turned two leaves on to the page with her name at the top of it
and nothing else, which had had nothing else on it since the twenty-ninth
of the twelfth month.

He sat with the pencil over it for a while.

He still could not think of a heading and he was not going to be able to,
and he had stopped expecting to, and what he wanted was not a heading, it
was somewhere to put a fact about an object that had not behaved the way
its specification said it would.

He wrote one line under the name.

> *Will not stay where she is put.*

He read it back and it was accurate and it was the only accurate thing he
had, and he shut the book.

---

The last thing he did that day was the thing he had been not doing since
the eighteenth, which was to open the ordinary column at the front and
count backwards.

*Nine days since the eleventh.*

*Nine days and nothing on the boards, and I have been in three towns and
have not been asked for anything by anybody.*

*And I said in the second month.*

He looked at the mark he had put beside that sentence on the eleventh,
the one that meant *this is a number I have made and not counted*, and did
not take it off, and did not add anything to it either.

Then he put the book away and did the six minutes on the roll, and the
girl was asleep on the other side of the fire with the hat over her face,
and there were seven days left in the first month.

*Act 2, year 1, month 1, the twenty-sixth to the thirtieth. The Kanō ford
and west. Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,750 words.*

---

He saw him at four hundred yards, coming down through the trees on the
east side, and he stopped walking before he had decided to.

*Somebody at the crossing.*

*And he is not crossing.*

He put his hand out backwards without looking round and she stopped, and
he registered afterwards that she had stopped instantly and without a
sound, and did not have time for it.

---

The Kanō is not much of a river in the first month and the ford is about
thirty yards of shingle and slack water with a bar down the middle of it,
and there is a road on each side and nothing else for two miles either
way.

There was a man standing in it.

Not at it. In it. About eight yards out from the near bank, on the
upstream side of the bar, with the water at about his knees, doing nothing
at all.

Tadayoshi went down on one knee behind a stump and looked at him for
eleven minutes.

---

*He is not fishing. There is nothing in his hands and nothing on the bank.*

*He is not washing anything.*

*He is standing where you stand.*

That was the first thing and it arrived whole and he did not have to build
it.

*Eight yards out puts him where he can see both roads and the whole of the
bar, and it puts twenty yards of open water between him and anybody coming
down either side, and there is nothing behind him but the far bank and
that is a slope he could take at a run.*

*If I wanted to hold this ford on my own that is where I would be
standing, and it took me four seconds to find it and he was already
in it.*

He went on looking, because the first reading is the cheap one.

*He has not moved his head.*

*I have been here eleven minutes and he has looked left once and right
once and both of those were at the start and neither of them was for me.*

---

And then the other thing, and he was never able to say afterwards how he
got it, and he tried.

*There is something wrong about the right one.*

He put everything he had on it for about two minutes and could not close
it.

*It does not match. That is all I have got. The left one is doing
something and the right one is doing something and they are not the same
something, and at four hundred yards I have got no business seeing that at
all and I am seeing it.*

*And I cannot say what it is.*

He wrote that down afterwards exactly as it stood, and it stayed in his
sheets in that form for six years, and the first person who could have
told him what it meant was four hundred miles away and eleven years old
and had not been born when it happened.

---

He went round.

It cost him three hours and about five miles and he had to do the last of
it in daylight because the detour came out on an open shoulder with no
cover on it, and daylight was the part he minded.

He did not consider crossing. That should be said plainly, because it was
not a near thing and there was no debate in it: he had a girl of eleven
with him, and a man standing in a river in the first month is a man who
does not feel the first month, and there is nothing at the end of that
sum.

*He has not come for me.*

*He is standing in a ford on a road that goes to four places and he is
looking at all of them, and if he had come for me he would be somewhere
else, because a man who has come for you does not stand in the one place
on this road that everybody can see.*

*So he is looking for anybody.*

*And that is worse, because a man looking for one person can be avoided
once.*

---

She said it at about four in the afternoon, on the shoulder, in the open,
walking.

"He wasn't cold."

Tadayoshi stopped.

"What?"

"He wasn't cold." She was looking at the road and not at him and her voice
was doing the thing it did, which was to go flat when the content went up.
"He'd been in it a while. His trousers were wet above the water line, so
he'd been in deeper at some point, and it had dried on him at the top and
not at the bottom. That's an hour. And he was stood straight."

"So?"

"So I've been in a river in the first month," she said. "Twice. You don't
stand straight. You stand like this."

She did it, in the road, for about a second and a half — shoulders up,
elbows in, chin down — and then stopped doing it, and it was so exactly
the shape of a person in cold water that he had a moment of not being able
to get the picture of the man back.

"He was stood like a man in a doorway," she said. "For an hour. In the
first month."

---

He walked about two hundred yards without saying anything and then said,
"How long have you had that?"

"Since about the second minute."

"Why didn't you say?"

"You were working."

He turned that over and could not do anything with it, and put it down,
and it stayed down for a fortnight and then came back up in a different
shape entirely and cost him a great deal.

---

He did the sheets that night in a stand of trees with no fire, because the
shoulder had been open and he did not want one.

He unfolded them on his knee. There were eleven leaves of the harbour
office's spoiled paper, ruled in five columns in the sixth month of the
year before, and there were two lines on them and a rule under the second
one with *Nothing. Checked.* in the middle of the white.

He wrote the third.

> *26/1. The Kanō ford. About noon.*
>
> *Who: —*
>
> *What they tried: nothing. Stood 8 yds out on the upstream side of the
> bar for at least an hour before I saw him and 11 min after. Did not move
> his head. Did not cross.*
>
> *How long: 11 min observed, 1 hr+ actual.*
>
> *What it cost: 3 hrs and 5 miles, and the last two of them in daylight
> on an open shoulder.*

Then he sat with it and wrote two more lines under it because they were
the whole of it and there was nowhere else for them to go.

> *Something wrong about the right one. Cannot say what.*
>
> *And he was not cold. That is hers and I did not have it.*

---

He looked at the first column for a while, which had a dash in it, the way
the first line had a dash in it.

*Three lines and two of them have got nothing in the name column.*

*And the one that has got something in it has not got a name in it either,
it has got left-handed and about twenty-eight to thirty, and I have been
carrying that for four months and calling it a person.*

*So I have got a file with three entries and no names.*

He put it away.

---

There is one more thing about the twenty-sixth of the first month and he
did not think it that night or that year.

**He had been seen.**

Not by the man in the ford — the man in the ford never turned his head and
never crossed and never, as far as anybody was ever able to establish,
made any report about anything. But four hundred yards is four hundred
yards in both directions, and the road on the east side of the Kanō was
not empty at noon on the twenty-sixth, and a boy of eleven going round a
ford in daylight with a girl in a wide hat behind him is not a thing you
forget.

He had written the rule himself, in the eighth month, in the operational
column, after an afternoon with Hōki in a yard.

> *A boy walking is nobody. Walk.*

It had been true for nine months and he had built four hundred miles on
it, and it had stopped being true at about twenty past eleven in the
morning on the seventeenth of the twelfth month, when a girl came out of a
hall behind him with nothing on her and got on a road.

**Two children on a road are not nobody. Two children on a road are two
children on a road**, and there is exactly one of that in this country,
and he was walking about in it in daylight with eighteen thousand ryō and
a file with three entries and no names in it, and he had four days left in
the first month and nothing whatsoever on the boards.
