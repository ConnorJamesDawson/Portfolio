# Chapter 15, Scene 6 — Eleven Minutes

*Act 2. A room eleven paces by seven. A boy, age thirteen. POV: the boy.
~1,800 words.*

---

They brought a stool in, which had not happened.

Two of them brought it and one of them put it down about four feet from
the wall he sat against and a bit to the left, and then they both went out
and the door stayed open, which also had not happened.

He looked at the stool.

*That is for somebody to sit on.*

*Nobody sits down in here.*

---

The man came in about a minute later and he had a book.

He was not one of the two and Kimimaro had not seen him before. He was
somewhere past forty and he was clean and he had a book under his arm with
a cord round it, and he sat down on the stool and undid the cord and put
the book on his knee and opened it and looked at the page for about four
seconds before he looked up.

Then he said, "Stand up," and Kimimaro stood up.

---

What happened after that took eleven minutes and he can give you the whole
of it in order, because he counted it, because counting was what he had.

The man said, "Open your mouth."

He opened his mouth.

The man got up off the stool and came over with the book shut on his
finger and put two fingers on Kimimaro's chin and turned his head to the
left and then to the right, into the light from the door, and looked, and
took a thumb and pulled his lower lip down and looked at that, and then
did the top one, and then said, "Wider," and he made it wider.

It took about ninety seconds.

The man went back to the stool and sat down and did not write anything.

*He did not write.*

Kimimaro had that at the time, at the moment it happened, and put it away
whole.

---

The man said, "Hands out. Both."

He put both hands out.

The man came over again and took the left one and turned it palm up and
went over it, and then turned it over and went over the back of it, and
then did the right one the same way, and it took longer than the mouth.

He did the fingers one at a time. He put a thumb into the web between the
first and second on the left and pressed, and Kimimaro did not do anything
about that because there was nothing to do about it. He turned the left
hand over again and put the flat of two fingers on the back of it, about
an inch below the knuckle of the middle finger, and stopped.

He stayed there for about eight seconds.

Then he moved the two fingers about a quarter of an inch and pressed
again, and found it, and stayed on it.

*That is the sixth one.*

*That is exactly where the sixth one is and he has gone to it in about
eleven seconds, and he did not go over the rest of the hand looking for
it, and he did not do the same on the right one, and there is nothing on
the right one.*

The man let go and went back to the stool and sat down and wrote.

He wrote for about forty seconds. It was more than one line.

---

Then he said, "Shirt off," and Kimimaro took his shirt off, and the five
pieces went with it because they lived inside it, and he heard them go
down onto the floor beside him and did not look at them and did not pick
them up.

The man looked at his back and his front and his ribs and did not touch
any of it, and that took about a minute and a half, and then said, "All
right," and Kimimaro put the shirt back on and got the five pieces back
into it off the floor, one at a time, without hurrying, and nobody said
anything about that at all.

Then the man wrote again, and it was shorter, and then he did the cord up
round the book and stood up and went out, and the two came back and took
the stool.

The door shut.

---

He sat down against the wall and did it.

He did it immediately and he did it properly, and he got four things, and
they are the only four things he ever got out of it and he had them within
about ten minutes of the door shutting.

**One.** *Eleven minutes and a bit. Ninety seconds on the mouth, about
three on the hands, about a minute and a half on the rest, and the writing
and the sitting is the difference.*

**Two.** *He wrote twice. He did not write after the mouth. He wrote after
the hands and he wrote again at the end and the one after the hands was
the long one.*

He sat with that for a while, because it was the best of the four and he
knew it was.

*So the hands are the part.*

*He looked at my mouth for ninety seconds and did not write one word, and
he had my hands for three minutes and wrote for forty seconds, and if you
are writing about a thing then the thing is worth writing about.*

**Three.** *He knew where the sixth one was.*

*He went to it. He did not find it, he went to it — an inch below the
knuckle on the back of the left, first time, twice, and then he stayed on
it. And he did not look on the right, and there is nothing on the right,
and there has never been anything on the right, and he did not check.*

He turned that over four ways and every one of them came out the same and
he did not like any of them, and he did not have a word for not liking
them.

*Somebody told him.*

*He came in here knowing a thing about the back of my left hand, and I
have never told anybody about the back of my left hand, and there is
nobody to tell.*

**Four.** *He spoke to me seven times.*

*Stand up. Open your mouth. Wider. Hands out. Both. Shirt off. All right.*

He went through them again on his fingers, which he did not usually need
to do, and got thirteen words.

*Thirteen, in seven goes, in eleven minutes.*

*And six of the seven were a thing to do, and the seventh was **all
right**, and **all right** is what you say when you have finished, and it
was not said to me.*

---

And then he had the fifth one, and it is not a finding, and he did not
file it with the others, and it is the only part of the eleven minutes
that was about him.

*He did not ask me anything.*

He sat with his back on the stone and his hand on the back of his other
hand.

*Not one. He was in here eleven minutes and he opened my mouth and went
over both my hands and looked at my back and he did not ask me one
question.*

*He asked the book things. He looked at the page before he started, and he
looked at it again after the hands, and whatever he wanted he got off me
without saying anything to me at all.*

He had had the answer ready for a long time.

He had built it in two days out of true things and checked all of it, and
it had a unit problem in it which he had been honest about, and it was the
best piece of work he had ever done that was for somebody else. He could
see his breath in the morning and could not in the other room. His hands
were worse in the morning and better by the middle. And the cold was not
in the growth rate, and he had checked that over four pieces and it held.

He had carried it eleven days and then stopped carrying it, and it had
come back the moment the stool went down, all of it, in order, ready.

Nobody asked him.

*That is twice.*

He put it away again.

---

He did not do anything for a while after that, and then he did what he
always did, which was to go and get the useful part.

*He is going to come back.*

*You do not do a thing like that once. You do it and then you do it again
and you see what has changed, because a thing on its own is not anything.
I know that. That is the whole of what I know.*

He sat forward.

*So he has got a first reading and he will want a second one, and when he
comes back I will have the gap between them, and a gap is a rate, and a
rate is the only thing I have ever had that was worth anything.*

He was, for about a minute, more pleased than he had been in some weeks.

*And I have got a thing to measure it against, because the sixth one is
at seventy-and-something out of about two hundred and it is going at a
thumb and a bit in a hundred and ten, and if he comes back when the sixth
one is through then I will know how long that was in my own units and not
in his.*

He put his hand over the back of his other hand and found the place, which
was easier to find than it had been, and pressed on it the way the man
had, with two fingers, at the same angle, because that was the closest
thing to a second opinion he was ever going to get.

*Not sore yet.*

---

He was thirteen, and he had just been examined by a man who had been given
his description in advance and had written a long note about his hands,
and he had got the timing of it and the order of it and the length of the
two entries and the fact of the advance description, which is four things,
and every one of them correct.

He never got the fifth.

The fifth was that people who write things down about a body are writing
them down for somebody, and he had no idea that anybody in the world ever
wanted anything, because nobody had ever wanted anything from him except
that he stand still, and he had always stood still, and it had always been
enough.
