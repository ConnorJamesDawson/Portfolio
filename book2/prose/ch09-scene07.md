# Chapter 9, Scene 7 — No Shoes

*Act 1, month 9, the seventeenth night and the eighteenth. Above Nakadō.
Nozaki Uzuki, age ten. POV: Uzuki. ~2,040 words.*

---

She went the Kayano way because it was the only way out of that valley she
had ever walked on her own.

She worked that out later and it was correct and it was also not a
decision. What happened was that she came out of the gate and turned left,
and left is the shrine and the lane and the fork above Iwasa's, and at the
fork she went right, and right is the north road, and the north road is
the road she had walked on the twenty-sixth of the third month with a cart
and a hundred thousand ryō in a fold of cloth and four days in front of her.

She had been eleven days short of ten years old.

*Six months.*

*Six months and eight days.*

She did not know why she was doing that sum and she did it anyway, going up
the north road in the dark on bare feet with a bag in one hand.

---

She did not go on the road for long.

She got about a mile and a half up it and then she stopped, because a
thing had arrived that she had not had in the lane, and she stood in the
dark and had it out with herself properly, standing still, which she had
not done since the light woke her.

*Somebody is going to come up this road.*

She turned that over.

*Are they?*

*Who is?*

*He can't. He is on a floor and he is not getting up and the only reason
he would be able to is if she — *

She stopped that one.

*Right. Say it.*

*Somebody is going to come up this road, and the only person who can come
up it is my mother, and she was sitting at a table when I went out of that
window, and she has been sitting at a table for the whole of the ninth
month.*

She stood in the middle of the north road at about midnight.

*So nobody's coming.*

*Nobody in the world is coming up this road, and that is not me being
frightened, that is the arithmetic, and I have just done it standing in a
road at night and it came out at nobody.*

She went off the road anyway, onto the field side, in the wet, because Ine
had told her on the twenty-eighth of the third month that you do the
careful thing and nothing happens and you feel daft and you do it again
tomorrow, and because doing a careful thing was, at that moment, the only
thing left that she knew how to do.

---

She stopped at about two in the morning under a thorn on the bank above
the Ōgi fork, because her feet had stopped being something she could go on
using.

The lane out of Nakadō is stone at the top end and the north road is
metalled for the first mile because of the carts, and she had done about
two and a half miles on both of them with nothing on her feet, and she sat
down on wet ground under a thorn and put them out in front of her and could
not see them properly and felt them instead.

*The left one's worse. There's a place at the outside edge behind the
little toe that's off.*

*And there's about four cuts across the ball of both and they're not deep
and they're going to be filthy.*

*And I can't do anything about any of it because there is no water up here
and I have got nothing to put on them and nothing to put round them.*

She sat under the thorn with her feet out in front of her.

*Four days to Ōgi.*

*It's four days for a person with shoes.*

---

She did the inventory at about first light because she could not sleep and
because it was the only useful thing available.

She laid it all out on her lap in the grey.

*The bag.*

Four columns and eleven questions and the gaps on the back and *17th* with
nothing after it. Folded four times, damp at one corner where she had had
it in her fist.

*The knife.*

Seven inches, and the leather still on the point, and she had oiled it on
the ninth and it had been under a mat for eight days since and it was —

She looked at it in the grey for a while.

Then she wiped it on the grass, properly, both sides, and did the flat and
the edge and the ricasso where it collects, and it took about four minutes,
and she did not look at what came off onto the grass and she did not think
one single thing about it, and it was two years before she thought
anything about it at all.

*The leather.*

Which she had carried up two and a half miles of road in her left hand
without once being aware of it, and which she now put back on the point,
and which was the reason the knife had not gone through the bag.

*The money.*

Behind the brick, with the bag, because everything was behind the brick,
because there had never been anywhere else. Five hundred ryō and a bit in a
twist of cloth.

*Five hundred ryō, a bit, a bag, a knife.*

*That's it.*

---

She counted it three times and then found she was doing something else, and
stopped, and looked at what she was doing, and it went into her like cold
water.

*I'm counting what I haven't got.*

She sat under the thorn with her hands still.

*The shoes. The shoes are by the kitchen door on the inside.*

*The bokken. That's in the corner of my room and I have had it since I was
six and my father made it and I have never once thought of it as a thing I
own.*

*The mill chit with Ine's method on it, which is in the bag, so that's all
right.*

*The blanket.*

*The —*

And then she got it, and she put her head back against the thorn.

*The sword.*

---

*It's above the door in the back room.*

*He put it up there himself on two nails on the second of the seventh
month and I watched him do it and I did not go in and look at it, and I
have gone past that door about four hundred times in eleven weeks and I
have never once been in that room.*

*And the back room is through the kitchen.*

She sat with that.

*So there was never a version where I got it.*

*Not one. Not even if I'd had an hour. It is through the kitchen and the
kitchen is where she was sitting and the doorway is where he was, and to
get to it I would have had to walk past both of them with a knife in my
hand and take a sword off a wall and walk back.*

*So it isn't that I forgot it.*

*I didn't forget it. I never had it.*

She did the honest one afterwards, because she had a rule about that and
because there was nothing else to do under a thorn at first light.

*And I did not think about it once.*

*I was in that room for — I don't know how long, and then I did the brick
and the window in seven, and at no point in any of it did the sword come
into my head, and it did not come into my head in the lane and it did not
come into my head on the road and it has taken me until first light on a
bank above the Ōgi fork.*

*Grandad carried that out of a country.*

*It's above a door in a house with a man on the floor of my room.*

---

She would have gone back for it.

She knew that, sitting under the thorn, and it was the only warm thing she
had that morning and she held onto it for about four days: that if there
had been a way, at any point in that night, she would have gone back
through that kitchen for it, and it would have been the stupidest thing
anybody in her family had ever done, and she would have done it.

*Not for the sword.*

*Because it's the only thing left that says we were anything. There's
nobody in this world who knows what we were called before. Grandad never
said and Dad didn't know and Mum wouldn't know if you asked her, and the
whole of it is a red thing above a door with a whirl on the guard, and I
have left it in his house.*

She put her head back on the thorn and did not cry, which she noticed at
the time and which bothered her for about a year.

---

The other one came at about eight in the morning and it came sideways,
while she was doing her feet with wet grass, and it stopped her hands.

*I don't know if he's dead.*

She sat very still.

*I don't know.*

*I looked at it. I looked at it and I made myself look and I know what the
side of his face is and I know the eye is gone, and I do not know whether
that kills a man.*

*I have never seen anybody die. I have seen one dead person and it was my
father and he did it in a bed over four months.*

*He made a noise.*

She held onto that one and turned it over and could not get anything out
of it at all.

*He made a noise after. So he was alive after. And he was alive when I
went out of the window, because he was making it then as well, I think, I
think he was still — *

*I don't know.*

*I do not know whether I killed him and there is not one person I can ask
and there is not going to be.*

*If he's dead they'll come for me, and they haven't, and it's been ten
hours, and ten hours is nothing.*

*And if he's alive — *

She stopped there and could not go on with it and did not go on with it for
about a year and a half.

---

She got up at about nine.

She had been going to go on north, because north was the way she was
facing and the Ōgi fork was a mile up, and she stood on the bank with the
bag in her hand and found out that she had made a mistake on the road at
midnight and had been sitting on it for seven hours.

*The Ōgi fork goes left.*

*And I came up the north road, which is the Kayano road, because it is the
one I know, and Kayano is four days north and Ōgi is four days
north-west, and the fork's above me, so that's fine, I've not lost
anything, I go left at the fork.*

*Except.*

She stood on the bank with her feet in the wet grass.

*Except everybody in Nakadō who has ever asked me anything knows I went to
Kayano in the third month. Ine knows. Iwasa's wife knows. Kuriya knows,
because I told him, because he asked me where I'd been and I told him.*

*And there is exactly one road out of that valley that a girl of ten has
ever been up on her own, and I am on it.*

She looked up the road at the fork.

*So if anybody does come, this is where they come.*

*And nobody is coming.*

*And I have got to go left anyway.*

She went left at the fork at about half past nine on the morning of the
eighteenth of the ninth month, on the Ōgi road, on bare feet, with a bag
and a knife and five hundred ryō, and she had four days of walking in front of
her and a name and a street and a woman who did the beds for three inns.
