# Chapter 3, Scene 8 — Twenty Minutes

*Act 1, month 4, the fourth, night. Shioiri, the second and third
courses. Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~2,170 words.*

---

He spent the whole of the fourth on the shoulder.

Nobody came. He established that by lying in the same gorse from before
first light until an hour after dark, and eating nothing, because there
was nothing, and drinking out of a runnel because there was that.

Then he went down.

He went down the shoulder path in the dark on the seaward side, slowly,
stopping at the turn of every course, and it took him thirty-one minutes
to do six hundred yards, which was the most careful he had ever been
about anything in his life.

*Somebody will have to go down and shut the doors.*

That had been in his head since about two in the morning and it had not
gone away, and it turned out to be the only thing he was capable of
wanting.

---

The first door was the end house on the third and it was not their door,
it had been shut for eleven years and it was standing open eighteen
inches, and he stood in front of it for a while before he understood what
that meant.

*They opened it.*

*It has no shutters and nothing in it and everybody in this compound
knows it has nothing in it, and they opened it anyway, because they did
not know, because they had a hill with thirty-one roofs on it and eight
people and no way of telling which was which.*

He put his hand on it and pushed it to.

Then he went along the third course opening the rest of them, one at a
time, and looking, and shutting them.

He did nine. Every single one had been opened and every single one had
nothing in it and none of them had been searched.

*That is a finding and I do not know what it means yet.*

---

The workshop was open and it was untouched.

That stopped him longer than anything else on the hill.

The tray of grit was where he had left it on the Tuesday, levelled off,
with the scoop laid across it the way he laid the scoop across it. The
tin had eleven drops in it and the eleven were the eleven. Akagane's
frame was on the bench, dished side up, exactly where his own hands had
put it.

The old frame was in the drawer at the back under the rag.

Nothing had been moved. Nothing had been taken. A room with a bench in
it, in a bloodline household under order, with the finished work of two
producers sitting in an open tin on the bench, and they had come in and
looked at it and gone out again.

*They did not want the trade.*

*They did not want the trade at all. It was never the trade.*

He picked up the frame and put it down again in exactly the same place.

---

The low house on the second course had its door open and the lamp had
been lit in it and had burned out, which he could tell from the reservoir
being dry and the wick burned right down into the collar.

The stove had gone out about a day and a half ago.

Meno's kettle was on it and had boiled dry and cracked across the base
and the crack had a white bloom round it, and he stood and looked at that
and did the sum.

*A full kettle on a made-up stove is about forty minutes to boiling and
about two hours to dry, and a cracked base means it sat dry on a hot
plate for a good while after, and the stove was made up at noon because
the stove is always made up at noon.*

*So there was nobody in this room from about half past four.*

*Which I already knew.*

He put his hand on the plate and it was cold.

---

The table was laid.

Not a meal — the afternoon thing, which happened every day at four and
had happened every day at four for as long as he had been alive: the pot,
and Meno's flat cakes on the board, and six cups because Iwao and his
father were between them at Ōura and Kiri and there were never eight
except at night.

Four of the cups had been drunk from. Two had not.

The cakes had been cut and there were three gone off the board out of
eight, and one of the three was on a plate at the end where Koishi sat,
in pieces, the way she did it, because she took a whole one and broke it
up and ate the bits and left the middle.

The middle was on the plate.

Tadayoshi stood in the doorway of the low house on the second course for
what turned out to be a long time.

---

He did the rest of it because he had to do something with his hands.

He picked the cups up and put them in the bowl. He put the lid on the pot
and moved it off the plate. He put the board with the five cakes on it
into the crock and put the stone on top of the crock because otherwise it
would go and that was the rule.

He took the plate with the middle of a flat cake on it and stood holding
it and did not put it anywhere at all for about a minute, and then he
tipped it into the bin under the sink the way you tip anything into the
bin under the sink, and washed the plate in the cold water in the pail,
and put it on the rack.

The comb was on the table.

---

It was tortoiseshell and it had cost two thousand ryō of the extra and it had
been on that table every single day for two and a half weeks, and she had not
used it once — she had looked at it, and turned it over, and put it down,
about four times a day, for eighteen days.

It was face down beside her cup, which was one of the four that had been
drunk from.

*Do not take it.*

*It is not for taking. It is a thing she was in the middle of deciding
about.*

He picked it up and carried it through to the back room and put it on the
shelf above the bed where her things went, on the left, next to the box
with the pins in it, and squared it up with the edge.

Then he came out and shut the bedroom door.

---

He did the rest of the reading on the way out, because it was the last of
it and he was not going to be able to come back in.

*Boots. Eleven sets in the passage in the wet off the path, and I can
separate about seven of them, and two of those went in twice.*

*No drawer open in this house. No box out. The press is shut. Meno's room
—*

He had done Meno's room first and had come out of it fast, and he made
himself go and stand in the doorway of it again now.

*The shelf is empty and the box is gone from under it.*

*The bed has been pulled out about a foot from the wall and pushed back
about eight inches, so somebody looked behind it, and the rug is turned
up at the corner nearest the door, so somebody kicked it and did not put
it back.*

*That is the only room in this house anybody searched.*

*They searched the room with the papers in it.*

---

He stood in the passage of the low house with his hand on the frame.

*They came for the household. They opened every door on the hill because they
did not know which houses were which. They did not touch the workshop, they
did not touch the tin, they did not touch forty-eight thousand ryō of finished
work sitting in the open.*

*They searched one room and they took what was in it.*

*So it is a list and a set of papers, and everything else on this hill is
somebody else's problem now.*

He went out and down the path.

---

The last thing he did was the walk.

He did not plan it. He got to the turn of the second course, where the
path goes round the rock, and stopped there because she had stopped
there, and then he thought: *I can get this.*

So he went back up to the door of the low house and stood in it, and
counted himself down, and walked.

He walked it at the pace they had walked it, which he had watched for
four hundred and forty yards from six hundred yards away for twenty
minutes and could reproduce inside about a step, and he did the whole
thing: the second course, the turn at the rock, the long slope, the
flagged bit at the bottom that goes greasy in the wet, and out onto the
stone.

Four minutes and ten seconds.

*Now the house time.*

He went back up and did that too, because it could be done: eleven men
into a house with five people in it, and finding two more on the third
course, and getting an old woman down off the third who cannot walk it,
which is the two of them with her arms over their shoulders and which he
had watched, and which is slower.

*Call the house eight minutes and be generous.*

*Call the getting-onto-the-boat six, because there was business with the
plank and I heard it.*

*Four and a bit. Eight. Six.*

*Eighteen. Call it twenty.*

---

He was standing on the stone at the bottom of his own hill in the dark
with his arms at his sides when it arrived.

*About twenty minutes, and there was no shouting.*

He had heard three sounds in the whole of it. A door on the third course.
A man laughing at something and stopping. And a child's voice asking a
question twice at the pitch children use when nobody is answering.

*It took about twenty minutes. There was no shouting.*

*And by the evening the fire was lit and we ate.*

---

He sat down on the stone.

*That is her sentence.*

*That is the sentence she said to me at that table on the twenty-third,
about the eighth month, about a man walking down this hill fifty years
ago, and she said "it took about twenty minutes" and she said "there was
no shouting" and I have just gone and got the same two things off a
flagstone with my own feet.*

*Which means one of two things.*

*Either it is the same because they do it the same way — they have done
it a hundred times, they have a method, and the method takes twenty
minutes and does not involve shouting, and I have measured a procedure.*

*Or I have measured nothing at all. I have stood on a hill for twenty
minutes with her voice in my head from three weeks ago and I have gone
and found exactly the number she gave me, because that is what a person
does, because I knew the answer before I started and everything I did
afterwards was in the shape of it.*

He put his hands over his face.

*And I cannot tell.*

*That is the finding. That is the actual finding of the whole of tonight
and it is not eleven men or the boot count or the fact that they searched
one room. It is that I have just done the only thing I know how to do,
carefully, twice, checking as I went — and I do not know whether what
came out of it is the compound or my mother.*

*And there is no way to find out.*

*The person who could tell me is on a boat that went north past the bar
at twenty past five yesterday afternoon.*

---

He sat on the stone for a long time.

The tide was on its way in and it came up the flags below him in the
dark, and after a while it was over the place where he had come out of
the path, and he had to get up and go round.

He went up to the second course and shut the door of the low house.

Then he went and shut the two on the third, and the one on the fourth
that he had not done, and he went up the hill shutting doors in the dark
until every door on Shioiri was shut, all thirty-one of them, which took
him until about three in the morning.

The last one was the end house on the sixth, which was Take's, and he did
not shut that one, because he was going to sleep in it.
