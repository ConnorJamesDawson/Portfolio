# Chapter 6, Scene 8 — How Long It Takes

*Act 1. A room. A boy, age twelve. POV: the boy. ~1,030 words.*

---

Four days, and nothing.

Then nine, and nothing.

The door opened three times a day the way it had always opened three
times a day, and the bowl came and the bucket went and the bowl went
back, and there was nothing wrong with any of it.

*It is fine.*

*It takes time. Things take time. The last one took twelve days before
anything happened and this is nine.*

---

On the tenth the keeper came in and picked up the blanket and went out
with it.

He did not say anything about it. He did not look at the boy. He came in
after the bowl had gone back, which was not one of the three times, and
he crossed the room and picked it up off the mat and folded it over his
arm as he went, and the bolt went over.

The boy sat where he was.

*Folded.*

*He folded it going out.*

*It is going to be somewhere.*

---

On the eleventh the food came cold.

He had it in his hands before he understood what was different about it,
because he had got used to it in twelve days and a thing you have got
used to in twelve days does not announce itself.

It was the temperature of the passage.

He sat with it.

Then he counted the bowl, which did not tell him anything, and then he
looked at the bowl properly, which did.

*Less.*

*Not the bread. The bread is the same. The rest is less and it is the
amount it was before.*

He ate it at the speed he always ate it, and it took less time than it
had for twelve days, and he did not like that at all.

---

He went through it that night in order and he went through it four times.

*One. How old are you. I don't know.*

*Two. Do you know what month it is. No.*

*Three. Can you put one out for me.*

He did all eleven and then he did all eleven again, and both times he got
to the end of them without finding it, and both times he started at the
beginning without stopping, because there was nothing else to do and
because it had to be in there somewhere.

The third time he found it.

*Ten.*

*How long does it take to come back.*

*I don't know.*

---

He sat with his back on the wall and had it and could not put it down.

*Every single one of the others I answered.*

*I answered eleven of them and ten of them I had the thing for, and one
of them I said I don't know.*

*And he stopped writing.*

*That is the only time in the whole of it that he stopped writing. He
wrote after everything and he did not write after that one, and he looked
up, and then he said nobody has taken one, and then he said ah, and then
he did not write.*

*He wanted that one.*

*That was the one he came for and I did not have it.*

---

He thought about that for a long time, and about the bowl, and about the
blanket going out folded over a man's arm, and he arrived at the end of
it, which was very simple and which he did not question at any point:

*I have to have the answer for next time.*

---

He did it that night, on the mat, in the dark.

He put one out of the back of the left hand, in the same place, between
the two middle knuckles, where the skin had gone over about six days ago
and was still a different colour.

It came up smaller than the first one had and it hurt in the same way and
he had expected both of those and neither of them was interesting.

Then he put one out of the back of the right hand, which he had never
done, and which came up straighter, and which he left alone.

*That one is for comparing.*

*That one is not broken and this one is going to be, and in a while I
will have two of them and I will know whether they are the same.*

He did not have the word for what he had just built. He had never heard
the word and would not have understood it if somebody had said it to him.
He had worked it out from nothing, sitting on a mat with the bolt over,
in about the length of time it takes to eat a cold bowl.

He put his right hand over the back of his left.

He broke it off.

---

It was worse than the first time because he knew what it was going to be
and did it anyway.

He made no noise. He sat with his eyes shut and his hand in his lap and
the blood coming up in the two knuckles and made no noise at all, and
after a while it went from being the whole of everything to being a thing
he had, and then he opened his eyes.

*Now.*

*Start now. Start at the bowl in the morning, because the bowl is the
only thing there is, and count bowls, and three bowls is a day.*

*One.*

He put the piece down on the mat beside him where he would not roll onto
it, because he was going to need to look at it against the next one.

---

He lay down.

*I do not know how many it is going to be.*

*It might be four. It might be a hundred. It might be the number where I
stop being able to count it and then I will have got it wrong again.*

*But I will have it.*

*And when he asks me, I will say it, and he will write it down.*

Somewhere along the passage a door opened, and it was not his, and it was
not the one with the nine-count gap after it, because that one had
stopped a long time ago.

He counted the bowl in the morning.

*One.*
