# Chapter 14, Scene 8 — Nineteen Seconds

*Act 2, year 1, month 2, the nineteenth to the twenty-first. West of the
Kanaya. Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,700 words.*

---

They had done it every night since the fifth and by the nineteenth he had
sixteen sessions and a page.

He had ruled the page on about the eighth, because three readings is a
series and a series wants a page, and it had two columns on it: the date,
and the longest she had stayed on her feet in that session.

> *5/2 — 9 sec.*
>
> *6/2 — 9.*
>
> *7/2 — 11.*
>
> *8/2 — 10.*
>
> *9/2 — 13.*

And so on down, in his hand, and the last one on it was:

> *19/2 — 19 sec.*

---

He was pleased with the page and he was right to be pleased with it, and
that is the part that has to be said before anything else, because it is
true and it is the reason the rest of it happened.

*Two to nineteen in fifteen days.*

*That is not nothing. That is not a child getting used to being knocked
down, because getting used to it is worth about four seconds and then it
stops. That is the curve of somebody who is learning where her feet go.*

*And she has not once said it hurts and she has not once not got up, and I
have counted the not getting up because it is the number that would matter
and it is nought out of sixteen.*

He put a line under the nineteen and wrote a note in the margin, and the
note was the first thing about her he had written that was not
operational, and it took him a while to phrase it and he ended up with
four words.

> *She does not stop.*

---

He told her the number every night because it was the only thing he had.

He was aware that it was the only thing he had. He had gone at that on
about the fourth night, on a road, and had got to the bottom of it and had
not liked the bottom of it and had done nothing about it, because there
was nothing to do about it.

*I cannot tell her what she did wrong.*

*I can tell her what she did wrong. That is not the problem. I could give
her four faults out of every one of those and they would all be correct
and she would fix three of them by the sixth month.*

*The problem is that I cannot tell her what to do instead, because what I
do instead is a thing I have never said out loud and could not put in an
order, and the day I try to put it in an order I will invent one and she
will build on the invented one and I will have made her worse in a way
neither of us can see for two years.*

*So: a number.*

*Nine, and then nineteen. That is a true thing and it is the only true
thing I have got that is any use to her.*

He gave it to her every night, flat, without any decoration on it, and she
took it the same way, and he had no idea whether it was any good.

---

On the nineteenth he did notice something and he noticed it correctly and
then did nothing with it, and it was there in front of him for about four
seconds.

She got up off the ground after the fifth one and said: "Do it again."

Which she had said perhaps forty times.

And then she said: "The same one."

He stopped.

"What?"

"The same one. Do the same one again."

He did it again and she went down, and she got up and said, "Again," and
he did it again and she went down, and she got up and said "Again" a
fourth time and he did it a fourth time.

On the fourth one she got a hand on his wrist for about a quarter of a
second before she went over.

She did not say anything about that and neither did he, and he wrote the
session up as nineteen seconds because nineteen seconds was the longest
she had stayed up, and the four in a row had all been short.

---

He went at it for about a mile the next morning and got the wrong answer,
and the wrong answer was reasonable and he could have defended it and it
was still wrong.

*She wanted the same one four times.*

*So she is drilling. She has found one she cannot get out of and she is
going at it until she can, which is what I would do and it is the correct
instinct and it is why the number is going up.*

He was pleased about that too.

*She is doing it herself. Nobody told her to do that.*

He put it in the margin under the four words.

> *20/2. Asked for the same one 4x. Drilling on her own. Good.*

---

What had actually happened was this, and he did not have it for four
months and did not have the whole of it for two years.

She had not been drilling.

She had been *checking*. She had wanted to know whether the fourth one
would be the same as the first one, because if it was the same then it was
still one thing, and if it changed then it was two, and the whole of her
attention that night had been on his right hand and not on her own feet at
all.

It had been the same all four times.

And so the number she was keeping — which had been one since the fifth of
the second month, and which was not on any page and was not in any book
and was in the head of a girl of eleven walking about four feet behind him
on the left — had stayed at one for sixteen sessions, and she had gone to
sleep that night knowing something about him that he did not know about
himself.

**He had used one thing on her sixteen times and had never once needed a
second.**

---

He did not find out about the number for four months.

But there is one thing about the twenty-first that is worth putting down,
because it is the only time in that fortnight he got anywhere near it, and
he got near it and then went past it and never came back.

They were doing the sixth one on the twenty-first and he put her down and
she went over and stayed down for a second longer than usual, and he said,
"All right?"

And she said, from the grass, not moving:

"How many have you got?"

"How many what?"

"Ways of doing that."

He said, "I don't know," which was true, and then, because it was a
question about a number and he did not leave a question about a number
alone, he went and sat down and actually tried to count them, and got to
about eleven that he could name and stopped because the rest of them did
not have names.

"Eleven," he said. "More. I can't get at the rest."

She said, "Right," and got up, and asked for another one, and he gave her
another one, and it was the same one.

---

That night he wrote the session up and it was twenty-one seconds and he
put it on the page and drew the line, and the page had seventeen readings
on it and it went two, nine, nine, eleven, ten, thirteen, and on down to
twenty-one, and it was a good page and it was accurate and every number on
it was true.

He looked at it for a bit before he shut the book.

*Two to twenty-one in seventeen days.*

*That is a person who is going to be able to look after herself in about
four years.*

He was wrong about that and he was wrong about it in the good direction,
and it was the second time that fortnight he had watched her do something
in front of him and come away with a number that was true and useless.

The first had been in a yard at Sakabe on the sixth.

He had not connected them and he did not connect them for a very long
time, and the reason he did not is that in both cases he had been
measuring the thing that could be measured, which is what he did, and had
never once asked himself whether it was the thing that was moving.
