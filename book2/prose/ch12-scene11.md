# Chapter 12, Scene 11 — A Page With Nothing Else On It

*Act 1, month 12, the seventeenth to the twenty-ninth. The mountain road,
Ōgi, and Ōtoma. Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,900 words.*

---

They did four miles on the seventeenth and eleven on the eighteenth and
she did not complain once, and he had not expected her to and had not
expected anything else either, and it turned out that having no
expectations about a person is a poor way to travel with one.

She was slower than him going up and faster going down, which he worked
out on the first afternoon and did not understand until the third, when he
watched her come down a set of steps and saw that she was going down the
outside of them where the tread was not worn.

*She is not being careful. She has found the good line and she is on it.*

*And she found it in about a day and a half on a mountain she has never
been down.*

He put that in the operational column that night and did not put a name on
it because he did not have one.

---

The other thing about the first three days was that she talked, and he had
no procedure for that either.

She did not talk at him. That was the part he could not get hold of. She
talked at about a fifth of the time, in short pieces, mostly about what
was in front of them — where the water was going, why the wall on the
uphill side was built the way it was, that the frost had come out of the
south side of a rock face and not the north — and when he did not answer,
which was most of the time, she stopped and went on walking, and started
again forty minutes later about something else.

On the second evening he worked out what it was and it took him a while
because he had never seen it before.

*She is not asking me anything.*

*Not once in two days. She has said about two hundred things and not one of
them was a question, and she is not doing that by accident.*

He thought about it for a while and did not arrive anywhere, and put it
down, and the answer would have cost her a great deal to give and he never
asked for it.

---

They came into Ōgi on the twenty-first and he bought her a coat.

It took four minutes.

The man wanted six hundred and Tadayoshi got it at four hundred and
fifty, and he got it by doing nothing at all except stand there and say
four hundred and look at the door once, and the man came down in two
steps, and the whole thing was over so fast that he was outside on the
street with a coat in his hands before he understood what had happened.

He stood in the street and had it.

*I did not want it.*

*That is the whole of it. That is the entire thing and it took a boot and
four hundred and eighty ryō and eight weeks to get to and it is one
sentence: I could not buy boots because I wanted them, and I can buy a
coat because I do not.*

He looked at the coat.

*And the correction I wrote on the twenty-eighth of the tenth month was
**do not touch the thing first**, and that is not the correction. That is
a way of hiding it. The correction is that I am the leak, and there is no
procedure for not being the person who wants a thing.*

He gave her the coat and she put it on and it was too big, deliberately,
because he had bought it too big on purpose, and she looked at the sleeves
and then at him and did not say thank you.

She said, "How much did you get off him?"

"A hundred and fifty."

"Right," she said, and did the cuffs over twice, and that was the whole of
the conversation about it.

---

They were in Ōtoma on the twenty-eighth of the twelfth month.

He gave Ōtsuka his half of the receipt and told him, standing in the yard,
in about ninety seconds, and it was accurate and in order and he did not
leave anything out.

That the crates could not be moved before the spring and had never been
intended to be. That the consignment on the sheet was not the consignment.
That the client was dead, along with five other people, and that he had
done it, and that he could not give an account of the middle of it.

That there was no fee.

That there were nine or ten people who had walked out of that valley by
now and had almost certainly been to Ōgi.

And that there was a girl.

---

Ōtsuka listened to the whole of it with the half-receipt in his hand and
did not interrupt, and at the end of it he looked at the girl for about a
second and a half and then back at Tadayoshi.

"Right," he said.

"That's it?"

"What do you want me to say?"

"Something," said Tadayoshi, and it came out of him without any decision
in it, which was the second time in two months and he noticed it while it
was happening and could not stop it.

Ōtsuka put the receipt in his coat.

"I'll write it up," he said. "You'll want to be somewhere for a bit."

Then he went and did something to a barrel, and that was the whole of it,
and nobody in that building said one word to him about the seventeenth of
the twelfth month for the rest of his life.

---

He got to it on the twenty-ninth.

He was on the boards in the loft in the afternoon with the ledger open
doing the ordinary thing, which was to close the month, and he came to
the back of the book to check a date on the list headed **PEOPLE WHO WILL
TELL SOMEBODY**, and he turned two leaves too many.

The page had five lines on it and a date in the top corner and no heading.

He read it.

> *Not the ones who did not choose.*
>
> *I do not know what did not choose means. That is the stroke the whole
> of it hangs off and I cannot say what it is for. Whoever comes to widen
> this will come at that word and they will be right that it is loose.*
>
> *No exceptions. If there is an exception I will find it, because I am
> good at finding them, and it will arrive with the paperwork correct.*
>
> *This is not backwards from today. It does not undo the twentieth. I
> have written that down here so that it is on the page and not in my
> head, because it is the first exception and it is on the same sheet as
> the words no exceptions and it is mine and it is for me.*
>
> *I am writing this after. I know I am writing it after.*

He sat and looked at it for a long time.

---

*I did not look at this.*

*I stood on a hall floor on the seventeenth of the twelfth month and did
the three questions and got to the end of them and said all right, and
this was in the coat I was wearing, two leaves past a list I had written
myself, and it did not come into my head.*

*It was thirty-seven days old.*

He went at it the only way he had.

*Does it cover her?*

*She is ten. She was in a cellar for forty-five days. She was priced on
the twenty-eighth of the tenth month by a man she had never met and she
was not asked about any part of it.*

*Yes. Obviously yes. It is the clearest case there will ever be and I would
have got it in about a second if anybody had put it to me on a sheet.*

*So why did it not come.*

---

He found the answer in about four minutes and it was worse than not
finding one.

*Because it says not the ones who did not choose, and I wrote it about a
boy I might have killed, and I have read it ever since as a rule about
killing.*

*And I was not going to kill her.*

He put his hand flat on the page.

*That is a technicality.*

*That is a technicality and it is the exact shape of the technicalities I
take apart for money, and if a man in a room said that sentence to me
about somebody else's rule I would have the load-bearing stroke out of it
in four seconds and tell him what he had done.*

*I wrote **no exceptions** on this page thirty-seven days ago and the first
one came thirty-seven days later and it did not even feel like one. It
felt like the rule not being about it.*

---

He tried to write the correction and he could not make it be the right
shape.

He got as far as the obvious one and put it down and looked at it and knew
immediately that it was not it.

> *Read this page before every job. Not after.*

*That is not the correction.*

*Reading it would not have done it. I would have read it on the sixteenth
and gone in on the seventeenth and it would still have been a rule about
killing, because that is what I think it says, and I would have read it
and not seen it.*

*So the correction is not about when I read it. It is about what I think
it says. And I cannot correct what I think a thing says by writing another
thing.*

He left the line where it was and put a small mark beside it, the mark he
used for a number he had made and not counted, which he had never once
used on a sentence, and which meant, when he used it: *this is what I have
got and it is not the answer.*

Then he shut the book on it, because there was nothing else in him.

---

She was on the boards by the wall with her back against it and had been
for about two hours, doing nothing, in a coat that was too big, and had
not said anything for the whole of it.

He got the book out again and turned two leaves on from the page with no
heading and ruled a fresh one, because a person you are carrying goes in a
column and he did not have one.

Then he stopped, because he did not have the first thing you need.

"What's your name?"

She looked up.

It took her about a second and a half, and he did not know what was in the
second and a half and never found out, and what was in it was that in two
hundred and seventy-three days — on a road, and in a house with a brick
wall in it, and on a hall floor, and in a cellar — the last person who had
asked her that had written it down and then put a number under it.

"Uzuki," she said.

"The rest of it."

"Nozaki."

He wrote it.

He wrote it at the top of a page in the small exact hand, the way he wrote
everything, and then he sat with the pen over the paper for a while
because there was supposed to be a heading above it and there was not one,
and he tried for about four minutes the way he had tried in the eleventh
month and got nothing that was not either a lie or a description of a
feeling.

So he left the top of the page white, and the name under it, and nothing
else on it at all.

> *Nozaki Uzuki.*
