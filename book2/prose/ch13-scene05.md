# Chapter 13, Scene 5 — Who Paid For It

*Act 2, year 1, month 1, the fourth to the eleventh. The Kurihama road and
an inn. Nozaki Uzuki, age eleven. POV: Uzuki. ~1,600 words.*

---

The four days on the road were the best four days she had had since the
seventeenth of the tenth month and she did not say so.

She had boots that were a pair. That was most of it and there is no point
dressing it up: she had two shoes that were the same size and did the same
thing and neither of them had a place in it where a rag had been, and by
the second day the outside edge of her left foot had stopped being the
first thing she thought about in the morning.

She got the good line down a set of steps on the second day and he saw her
do it.

She knew he had seen because he did not say anything and then did the same
thing at the next set, on the outside, where the tread was not worn, and
she had four hours of being extremely pleased about that and did not tell
her face.

---

The inn at Kurihama had a room at the back on the ground floor with one
window and one door and he paid for two nights.

"Stay in it."

"For how long?"

"Two days. There's food downstairs at the front, and you can go and get it
and come back."

She said all right.

Then he said the other thing, and he said it in exactly the same tone as
the first thing, which she thought about for a long time afterwards.

"If I'm not back by the morning of the tenth, don't wait. Go west and
don't stop at Ōtoma."

"All right."

He went out at about six in the evening and she did not see him again for
fifty-eight hours.

---

She did the first day well.

She went and got the food twice and came back and sat on the mat and did
what she had done for forty-five days in a cellar, which was to build the
building out of sound, except that this building was an inn on a main
road with about nineteen people in it and the sound was useless, and she
got about four hours into it before she understood that she was doing a
thing that had had a purpose in a different place and had none here.

She stopped.

*So what do I do.*

She sat with that and did not have an answer, and the not having an answer
went on for the rest of the second day and most of the third, and by the
end of it she had arrived somewhere she had not been since the twelfth
month.

*I am bored.*

*I have been in this room for two days and nothing has happened and
nothing is going to happen and I am not frightened and I am not hungry and
I am not doing anything, and I have not been bored since the fourth month
and I had forgotten it existed.*

She turned it over and got the second half, which she liked less.

*And a person who is bored is a person who is not being used for anything.*

*And I told him in a hall with six dead men in it that I would be worth
having.*

---

She was by the door from about ten on the second night.

She did not decide to be. She noticed at about midnight that she was
sitting with her back against the wall beside the door with the knife
across her knees and the lamp out, and worked backwards to find where she
had put herself there, and could not find it.

Then she worked out why, which took under a minute and was worse.

*Because the door is the thing.*

*Because in the eleventh month the door was the thing and I went up four
steps and put my hand on it every day for twenty-eight days, and on the
seventeenth of the twelfth month it was locked and that was how I knew.*

*And this one is not locked. It is not locked and I have got a knife and
I can walk out of it and I am sitting next to it in the dark.*

She stayed there anyway.

She was still there at four in the morning on the tenth when it opened,
and he came in with the cold on him and stopped for about half a second
when he saw where she was, and did not say anything about it, and went and
sat down on the other side of the room and started taking his boots off.

**And she watched him not ask.**

*Third time.*

---

The road back was three days and she got the thing on the second of them.

He had the book out at a milestone at about noon, doing what he always did
at milestones, and she came and sat where she could see it, which she had
been doing for a week and which he had not objected to, and she read the
entry over his arm.

It was two lines.

> *9/1. Kurihama. Sagawa, lodging, east side.*
>
> *Done. 900 due.*

She read it twice.

"Nine hundred," she said.

"Yes."

"Is that a lot?"

"No."

---

She sat with that for about a mile, working, because it had a hole in it
and she could feel the edge of the hole and could not get her hand in.

*Nine hundred is four hundred and fifty a day for the two days he was
gone, and the boots were three twenty, so it is three pairs of boots.*

*And he went into a building at one in the morning and did a thing to a man
that cannot be undone by anybody ever, and came out, and it is three pairs
of boots.*

*So the price is not for the doing. He has told me that already, in a
different way.*

She got it at the top of a hill and turned round and said it before she
had decided to say it.

"Who paid for it?"

He looked at her.

"I don't know."

She stopped walking.

---

"You don't know."

"No."

"Somebody paid nine hundred ryō to have a man killed in Kurihama and you
did it and you don't know who they are."

"No."

"Do you ever know?"

He thought about it, properly, which she was learning was a thing he did
with any question at all no matter who asked it, and which she had never
once had from an adult in her life.

"Twice," he said. "Kadoya on the twenty-ninth of the ninth month, because
I was in the room. And the ground above the second weir, because I was
told."

"Out of how many?"

"Nine."

---

She went at it for the rest of the afternoon and could not make it come
out, and what she could not make come out was not the number.

*He does the work and he does not know who wants it.*

*And every single thing I know about anything I got from watching who
wanted it.*

*Shimoda wanted the mule at a price, and the price was the whole of it.
Kuriya wanted the pans. Enkai wanted the eleventh month and not the tenth,
because of the road trade, and I got that off him saying it in front of me
because I was the thing. The man with the book wanted my grandfather's
name and it took him longer to write and that is how I know it was worth
the most.*

*I have never once been able to do anything about any of them. But I knew.
I always knew.*

*And he has been doing this for four months and has not asked.*

She got it into words at about four in the afternoon and got it wrong, and
being wrong is the point and she was wrong in the direction she was going
to spend the rest of her life being right in.

"You should have asked for more."

"For Sagawa?"

"Yes."

"How much more?"

She said, "Four thousand," because it was the biggest number she could
justify and she had no way at all of justifying it, and she knew while she
was saying it that she had made it and not counted it.

"Why four?"

"Because —"

She stopped, because the reason was not there.

---

He did not tell her she was wrong.

That was the other thing about the afternoon and she went over it more
than once. He did not say *no* and he did not laugh and he did not
explain, and he did not do the thing every adult she had ever met did,
which was to close the subject by being the one who knew.

He said, "The fee is for who the man was."

"Is that a rule?"

"It's the only one I've got."

She walked for a bit.

"It's wrong," she said.

He did not say anything to that at all, and she looked at the side of his
face and could not tell whether he had not heard her or whether he had
heard her and was doing the thing where he took it away to work on, and
she found out five months later that it was the second one.
