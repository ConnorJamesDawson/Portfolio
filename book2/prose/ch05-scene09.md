# Chapter 5, Scene 9 — Her Hand

*Act 1, month 5, the eighth, dusk. Behind a wall on the coast road.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~2,120 words.*

---

It was a clasp.

He knew that before he had it properly out of the pocket, because his
thumb had been on it for however long he had been kneeling in the channel
and his hand already had the shape of it: a bar, and a tongue, and a
bevelled face about an inch across, with two lugs on the back for the
webbing to go through.

It had a piece of somebody's webbing still on it.

He got that off with the knife and did not look at what was on the strap,
and put the strap under a stone.

Then he held the clasp up in the last of the light with his back against
a wall four hundred yards from four dead children, and looked at it
properly.

---

*Crystal.*

*Which is not remarkable. The whole coast wears crystal. It has been sold
as gemstone for four hundred years and it is in rings and it is on
scabbards and there are eleven pieces of it in a corps kit and everybody
knows that and nobody thinks about it.*

*It is grey-blue and it is a plain bevel and there is no ornament on it
anywhere.*

*Two courses.*

He ran his thumb up the bevel, up and back, from the bar to the tongue.

*The note is at the wide end where the courses finish. It is exactly where
it should be. It is a commercial-grade fitting made by somebody
competent, and there are probably nine thousand of them in the Land of
Water, and I am sitting behind a wall being sick about a bead.*

*Put it down.*

He did not put it down.

He ran his thumb up it again.

---

*The taper is fuller a third of the way down.*

He stopped.

He sat behind the wall with his back against it and the light going and
did not move at all.

*The taper is fuller a third of the way down and it should not be,
because it is a bevel and a bevel is a straight run, and a bevel that is
fuller a third of the way down is a bevel that got MORE for a moment near
the start and then went back to what it was getting.*

*Which is the heel of a hand.*

*She pushes with the heel of the hand at the start and gets tired of
pushing.*

*It is on every drop she has ever made and it is the thing I spend four
minutes putting into mine, on purpose, every day, because I do not get
tired and she does and if I do not put it in then hers and mine do not
match.*

He got the thing about two inches from his face in the last of the light
and went up it with his thumb a fourth time and a fifth.

*It is her.*

*It is her, it is her hand, I know that hand better than I know my own
because I have been counterfeiting it since I was eight years old.*

---

He put it down on his knee and put both hands over his face and sat like
that for a while.

Then he took his hands down, because there was a very large amount of
work to do and no time at all in which to want to be doing it.

---

*Do it in order and do not skip anything because you are frightened of
where it goes.*

*ONE. Is it hers, or is it somebody with the same fault?*

*Anybody who pushes with the heel gets tired of pushing. That is not a
signature, that is an ordinary bad habit and there will be four hundred
makers in this country who have it.*

*So: is there anything else.*

He went over the whole of it again, slowly, with the light nearly gone
and mostly by feel.

*The note is a fraction low. Not wrong — low. It sits about a
sixteenth further down the bevel than it ought to for two courses on a
piece that shape, which is what happens when the second course is laid a
touch short at the finish, which is what happens when somebody is working
to a size and stops at the size instead of stopping at the piece.*

*She does that. She has always done that. Meno used to say it to her at
the table and she used to say Meno had not laid a course in twenty years
and Meno used to say that was not the point.*

*Two faults. Both hers. Both in the same object.*

*I would take that to a board.*

---

*TWO. And this is the one.*

He turned the clasp over on his palm.

*She has never made a fitting.*

*Not one. Not in twenty-two years. She makes drops and she makes beads
and she has made about eleven mounts in her life and she was not good at
them, and the whole of that household's output for as long as I have been
alive is small jewellery going in a tin to a counter at Ōura by weight
and grade.*

*Because that is what we are. We are a gemstone family. We sell to a shop
and the shop sells it on and what happens to it after that is nobody's
business and my father has never once asked.*

*This is not gemstone. This is a bar and a tongue and two lugs and a
bevelled face, and it is not decoration, it is a FASTENER — it is a piece
of equipment made to a pattern to hold a strap on a man, and the pattern
is the same one that is on the other three of them back there, and it
came out of a store with a number on it.*

*She has never in her life made one of these.*

*So somebody has put her in front of a bench with a different order on
it.*

---

He sat behind the wall with the clasp in his hand and got the whole of
it, all at once, and it went through him so hard that he had to put a
hand out on the ground.

*She is alive.*

*She is alive and she is at a bench and she is making military fittings
to a pattern, and she has been doing it long enough to have made this
one.*

*THREE. When.*

*The kit is new.*

*All four of them were new. The webbing was pale where it had not gone
grey, and the plate on the girl at the top had the packing crease still
in it, and one of them had a pouch on the wrong side and had been told
about it and had not moved it, and I stood in a riverbed at eleven
o'clock this morning and read every bit of that off them in about four
seconds and thought: they graduated in the spring.*

*Spring graduation. New issue. Eight weeks, ten at the outside.*

*This clasp came out of a store and onto a boy's chest inside the last
ten weeks.*

*And she was taken on the third of the fourth month, which is five weeks
ago.*

He sat very still.

*It is inside the window either way. It could have been made before —
sixth week of the issue cycle, and she was at Shioiri, except she has
never made a fitting in her life and there is no bench at Shioiri that
has ever had a pattern on it.*

*So: after.*

*She has been at a bench for five weeks.*

---

*FOUR.*

*And this is the one that is going to be the rest of it, so get it right
and do not decorate it.*

He turned the clasp over.

*I can read her.*

*I have just held a piece of her work that she made about a fortnight
ago, in a building I have never seen, in a country the size of this one,
and I have got two faults off it and a grade and a course count, and if
I had another one from the month before I could put them side by side and
tell you which was which.*

*The heel of the hand is fuller than it is on the ones in the tin. Not
much. I would have to have them both to swear to it and I do not have
them both, and I am putting that down as a thing I believe and not a
thing I know.*

*But I believe it.*

*She is more tired than she was.*

He sat behind a wall on the coast road with a fastener in his hand.

*I cannot find her. I have four days of walking and a station called Nezu
and an entry nine characters long, and a book that records what came in
and never where anything went.*

*But she is putting a thing into the world every day that has her in it,
and the things go into a store and out of a store and onto people, and
people walk about.*

*And I am the only person alive who can read it.*

---

He got the ledger out.

It was very nearly dark and he wrote it by feel, the way he had written
on a bed frame on the sixth course, about a quarter of an inch out of
true — and it came out in four lines and it took him about a minute, and
nothing fought him at all.

*8/5. Coast road south of Nezu. Clasp, corps issue, taken and kept.*

*Two courses. Heel-fault at a third. Note a sixteenth low. Her hand,
and I will say that in front of anybody.*

*She has never made a fitting. New issue, ten weeks at the outside.*

*She is alive and she is working and she is more tired than she was.*

He did not write anything about the riverbed.

He noticed that he had not, and sat with the pencil in his hand for about
eleven seconds deciding whether that was a failure, and decided it was
not, and that it would go in tomorrow when he could do it in order.

*(It went in eleven days later, on the road above Nezu, in nine lines,
and it took him two hours, and he did not get up afterwards for the rest
of that day.)*

---

Then he did the other thing.

He went back to the front of the ledger — to the first page, to the
second column, to the entry he had written at a bench at Shioiri on the
twenty-third of the second month with a lamp going down, which said
*nineteen went. Nobody says the names.*

And beside it, in the margin, he wrote a small *1*.

Then he turned the page and wrote a *2*, and a *3*, and went forward
through the whole of it in the dark: the four days of relearning where his
hand was; the frame; the case with its four passes; *she will say no*; the
bed being six foot four and the door five foot ten; *do not ask people,
find where it is written down*; Nezu; *find out what the book was for
before you look in it.*

Forty-one of them.

It took him about twenty minutes and he could not see any of it and he
did not need to, because he knew where every one of them was on the page.

He put the new one at the bottom and gave it a *42*.

He did not, at any point in the twenty minutes, say to himself why he was
doing it.

He would not have been able to. He was ten years and four months old and
he had killed four people that morning, three of them after it was over,
and he had no word at all for the thing he was making — and he went on
numbering the pages in the dark behind a wall on a coast road, carefully,
in order, the way you number a thing that is going to be handed to
somebody.

---

He put the ledger away.

Then he put the clasp in his right-hand pocket, on its own, with nothing
else in it.

The drop with the fault in it was in the left, with the grease tin, where
it had been for thirty-three days.

He sat there for a while with his hands flat on the outside of the coat,
one on each side, which is a thing he would go on doing for a very long
time without once noticing that he did it.

Then he got up and went north, in the dark, towards Nezu.
