# Chapter 1, Scene 9 — Three Days

*Act 1, month 2. Nakadō, the back room. Nozaki Uzuki, age ten. POV:
Uzuki. ~1,460 words.*

---

Old Tazu came on the Thursday, which meant her mother had asked him,
which meant her mother had decided something in the eleven days since
the box came out early.

He was not a doctor. Nakadō did not have a doctor. He was a man of about
sixty who had a garden of things and a shelf of jars and forty years of
having been the person you sent for, and he had set Uzuki's wrist when
she was seven and had done it well.

"Sit forward. Shirt up. Breathe in — no. Breathe in like you're not
being asked to."

Her father laughed and then could not stop laughing and then it turned
into the other thing, and Tazu waited it out with his hand flat on his
back and his head on one side, listening, not to the cough — Uzuki
watched him do it — but to the space at the end of it.

"Again."

"You've heard it."

"I'll hear it again."

---

Uzuki was in the room because nobody had told her not to be, and she had
established years ago that in a house that small the trick was simply to
be there before anybody had thought about it.

She sat on the floor by the door with her back against the wall.

Tazu did a lot of listening. He put his ear on a folded cloth against
her father's back and moved it, six or seven places, and stayed a long
time at one of them and came back to it twice. He looked at the ends of
his fingers. He looked in his mouth, and at the whites of his eyes, and
asked how long, and got told *since the harvest*, and looked at Sae for
the real answer and got *since the harvest before*.

Uzuki did not move at all when that happened.

*The harvest before.*

That was a year and a bit. That was — she went back through it — that
was the summer she was nine, the summer of the ford, and he had been
coughing on the ford job and she remembered because he had made a joke
about it.

Then Tazu washed his hands, and sat down on the stool by the door, and
said, "Right."

---

"It's in the deep part of the left one and it's been there a while and
it's got a shape to it," he said. "I can hear where it stops. That's not
a chest cold that's gone on. A chest cold that goes on sounds like the
whole of it. This sounds like a bit of it."

"Meaning what," said her mother.

"Meaning I don't know," said Tazu. "I want to be plain with you about
that, because I've watched men in my trade be unplain about it for
forty years and it never once helped anybody. I know about six things
and this isn't one of the six. I can give you something for the nights
and I can tell you to keep him warm and off the road, and both of those
are worth having and neither of them is treatment."

"And what is."

"A medic," said Tazu.

---

He meant a proper one. He said so, in case anybody was in doubt: not a
man with a garden, a *medic*, one of the chakra ones, who could put a
hand on a chest and see the inside of it the way you or I see the
outside.

"There isn't one at Ōgi," said her mother.

"There isn't one at Ōgi and there isn't one at Fusano and there hasn't
been one at Ōgi since the old woman went. There's one at Kayano."

"Kayano."

"Kayano," said Tazu. "Three days if the road's dry."

Uzuki, on the floor by the door, took that and put it somewhere she was
not going to lose it.

*Three days. Kayano.*

---

"And what does a medic at Kayano cost," said her mother, in the voice
she used for the box.

Tazu told her.

He told her the visiting figure, which was for a medic who came to you,
and then he told her the other figure, which was for going to them, and
he said the second one like a man offering a smaller loaf.

Uzuki did the sum.

She was ten and she was not good at sums, but she had spent a certain
amount of the last fortnight watching her mother's hand move coins from
one side of a table to the other, and she knew what was in the tin on
the shelf because everybody in a house that size knows, and she knew
that there was a hundred behind the tin that was not going to be
mentioned again.

The second figure was more than the tin.

It was not a great deal more. That was the thing she could not stop
looking at afterwards: it was not five times the tin or ten times the
tin. It was about the tin and a bit. It was the tin, and then a stretch
you could see the far side of, and the far side of it had her father on
it.

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"Right," said her mother.

"I'm not telling you to do it," said Tazu. "I'm telling you it exists.
Those are different, and the second one's my job."

---

Then he said the other thing, and he said it standing up, with his bag
half done and one arm in his coat, in the tone of a man tidying up a
loose end on his way out of a house.

"Mind, if you did get to a medic," said Tazu, "ask about the teaching."

"The what?"

"They can teach it. Bits of it." He shrugged into the coat. "It's not
all of it a bloodline thing, whatever they let you think — the palm work,
the basic of it, that's control and that's practice, and a medic worth
the name will sit somebody down for an afternoon and give them the
bones of it. I've seen it. Fellow at Fusano got taught by an army one
during the trouble and he kept a whole village on his own for six
years."

"And that would — "

"That would do the nights," said Tazu. "That would do the nights, and
the pain, and the bad weeks. It wouldn't do the thing." He picked up his
bag. "But you'd have it in the house. That's what I'd want, in your
place. Something in the house."

He said goodnight and went out, and her mother went out after him to see
him to the road, and their voices went off down the path and got quieter
and stopped being words.

---

Her father lay back against the cushion with his eyes shut.

"Well," he said, "that was cheerful."

Uzuki did not answer.

"Uzuki."

"I'm here."

"I know you're there, you've been there for an hour and a half like a
cat." He opened one eye. "Off the floor. It's cold."

She got up and sat on the end of the bed instead and pulled the blanket
straight over his feet, which did not need doing.

"Three days," she said.

"Mm."

"Three days is nothing. I did Ōgi in under three hours with a cart."

"Ōgi is eleven miles and Kayano is not."

"I know it's not, I'm saying — "

"I know what you're saying," said her father, "and I want you to hear
me, because your mother's going to say it badly later and then you'll
both be cross. There's no *money*, love. It isn't three days. It's three
days and then a sum, and the sum is the bit that doesn't work, and it's
not going to start working because somebody in this house can run."

He shut his eye again.

"Go and see if she wants a hand with the fire."

---

Uzuki went and saw about the fire.

Her mother did not want a hand with the fire and said so and then let
her do it anyway, and they knelt in front of it together and neither of
them said one word about Kayano, and at some point her mother put a hand
briefly on the back of Uzuki's neck and took it away again.

Later she lay in the dark and did the sum four more times, and it came
out the same way four more times, and then she stopped doing the sum.

*Ask about the teaching.*

*They can teach it. The palm work. The basic of it.*

*An afternoon.*

She turned over.

*Three days there,* she thought. *Three days back. And an afternoon in
the middle.*

*That's seven days.*

*And seven days is not a sum. Seven days is a walk.*
