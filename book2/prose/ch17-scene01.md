# Chapter 17, Scene 1 — A Page He Could Not Head

*Act 2, year 1, month 4, the tenth to the fourteenth. North of Hane.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,700 words.*

---

They went nine miles on the ninth and eleven more on the tenth and then he
stopped, because going north is a direction and not an instruction, and he
had gone as far north as a person can go on a direction.

He found a place off the road on the eleventh: a fold of ground under a
stand of larch with water at the bottom of it and one way in, four miles
from a village called Onouchi and about a mile from anything anybody would
walk past.

He put the mule in the trees and did the ground the way he did ground, and
that took him most of the eleventh, and on the twelfth he sat down and did
the thing he had been not doing for three days.

---

*Set it out.*

*One. I have been told north and I have gone north.*

*Two. I have been told to wait and I am waiting.*

*Three. I have not been told for how long, and when I asked, the man said
**till somebody comes**, and when I asked who, he said **I don't know,
that's not mine**, and he was not being difficult, he was telling me the
truth about the shape of what he had.*

He turned it over.

*So somebody up the line knows and nobody down here does, and the down here
is me.*

*Which is the ordinary condition and has been for fifteen months and I have
never once minded it, because there has always been a date on the sheet.*

He sat with that for a while.

*And there is no sheet.*

---

He went at the waiting itself on the thirteenth, because a thing you are
doing for an unknown number of days is a thing with a rate on it, and a
rate is the only handle he had ever found on anything.

*What does a day here cost.*

He did it properly and it took him about an hour and it is the only piece
of work he did in eleven days that came out clean.

> *Feed, the mule: 4.*
>
> *Food, three: 14 to 18 depending.*
>
> *Nothing else. No lodging, no road, no tolls, no boards.*
>
> **Call it 22.**

*Twenty-two a day and nothing coming in.*

He sat back on his heels.

*And I have got nine thousand five hundred and seventy-seven.*

*So the waiting costs me one part in four hundred and thirty a day, and I
could wait for a year and be beaten by the winter and not by the sum.*

He was pleased with that for about four seconds, in the specific way he was
pleased with a number that had come out better than he expected, and then
he was not, and this time it took him under a minute to find out why.

*That is the arithmetic of a man with a house.*

*I have not got a house. I have got a fold of ground and three people and
the sum only holds while nothing happens.*

He wrote it down anyway, because it was true, and put the mark beside it,
because he had made the eighteen.

---

**And then he did the money, and there is one new line on it.**

> *WHAT IS LEFT*
>
> *6,577.*
>
> *Hane, 9/4: +3,000.*
>
> **9,577.**
>
> *10–14/4, feed and food, three: −110.*
>
> **9,467.**

He looked at the *three*.

He had written it without stopping over it, which is what he did, and it
was correct, and there were three people eating out of that number and it
had been two for a hundred and eleven days and one for the eleven months
before that.

*That is the first time that page has had a three on it.*

He did not do anything about that. There was nothing to do about it. He put
the pencil in the fold of the book and shut it and went to see to the mule,
and it was an entirely ordinary evening, and he did not sleep well and did
not connect the two.

---

The other thing he did in those five days took him four minutes and he did
it on the fourteenth and it is the reason this scene is here.

He ruled a page.

He does it for anything that is a system. The mule has one, because an
animal with a load and a rate is a system. The hunter file has eleven
sheets and five columns. There is a sparring page with seventeen readings
on it and a margin note in his own hand that says *she does not stop*.

So he turned to the operational column and ruled a page and put the pencil
at the top of it where the heading goes.

And then he sat there.

---

*What is it a page about.*

*It is about a boy.*

*That is not a heading. A page called **a boy** is not a page, it is a
place I have put a piece of paper.*

He turned the pencil over.

*What does it hold. What goes in the columns.*

*The mule's page has got a date, a load, a distance and a condition, and
the reason those four are the columns is that they are the four things I
need in order to decide whether to use it tomorrow.*

He stopped there for a bit.

*So what do I need in order to decide about him.*

And there is no answer to that, and he went round it four ways looking for
one, and the fourth way is the one that got closest and he did not follow
it.

*I do not need to decide anything about him. Nobody has asked me to. He was
put in my hands with an instruction and the instruction has run out and he
is here.*

*So it is not a page about a decision.*

He looked at the white at the top of the sheet.

*Then what is it a page about.*

---

**He did not head it.**

Four minutes, and then he shut the book, and it is the second page in that
book with a name at the top and nothing above it, and the other one has had
nothing above it since the twenty-ninth of the twelfth month.

He noticed that. It took him about two seconds and he did not enlarge on
it.

*Two of them now.*

*Same book. Same hand. And I headed **WHAT IS LEFT** in four seconds and a
page about a mule in about one.*

He put the book inside his coat.

*I can head anything that is a thing.*

---

There is one more part of the fourteenth and it is about forty seconds long
and it is the only time in eleven days that anybody in that fold of ground
asked anybody else a question.

He was doing the fire and the boy was sitting against the bank about eight
feet off, where he had been sitting since about four, doing nothing at all,
which he had done every day since the ninth and which Tadayoshi had
registered on the first day and had not known what to do with.

*He has not moved in two hours.*

*He is not asleep. He is not hurt. He is not watching anything.*

*Nobody in my life has ever done that and I have watched a great many
people do nothing.*

So he said, because it was the only question available and because it was
operational:

"Are you all right for that shirt?"

And the boy looked up and said, "Yes," and then, after about a second, as
if he had checked:

"It is a good shirt."

"Right."

And that was the whole of it, and Tadayoshi went back to the fire, and what
he had of it afterwards was the second and not the first — *he checked
before he answered, about a shirt* — and he put it in the operational
column two hours later in five words and no mark:

> *14/4. Answers everything. Checks first.*

**And it is the most accurate thing anybody wrote about that boy in the
whole of the fourth month, and it is a hundred per cent correct, and he
took it as a fact about honesty.**
