# Chapter 14, Scene 10 — Don't Answer That

*Act 2, year 1, month 2, the twenty-second. A fire off the Kanaya road.
Nozaki Uzuki, age eleven. POV: Uzuki. ~2,050 words.*

---

He was there before they were, which is the part she went back to
afterwards.

They came up on a fire at about six in the evening on the twenty-second,
in a place that was not a place — a stand of trees off the road with a
bank on the north side and a fall of ground behind it — and there was a
young man sitting at it, and Tadayoshi stopped about forty yards out and
looked at it for a long time.

She stopped where he stopped.

Then he went on in, and she went after him, and she worked out about a
week later that he had gone in because going round would have taken them
past the front of it in the open and he had decided that sitting at a
stranger's fire was cheaper than being looked at from one.

---

The man said, "There's room."

He said it without getting up and without looking round, and he was about
twenty and he had a coat that had been good and a pipe and nothing else
that she could see, and his boots were off and by the fire and drying.

Tadayoshi said, "Thanks," and sat down on the near side.

She sat where she always sat, which was on his left, about four feet, and
put the hat on her knee, and the mule went where the mule went, and the
whole of the first ten minutes nobody said anything.

Which is when she started.

---

*He is on the side the smoke isn't.*

That was the first one and she got it inside about a minute and she got it
because Tadayoshi did it too, every single night, without ever mentioning
it, and she had worked out in the first week that it was not about the
smoke, it was that the smoke tells you which way the wind is going and the
wind tells you which way a sound goes.

*He has got the road at about forty degrees behind his right shoulder.*

*Which is wrong for warmth and wrong for talking and right for one thing
only, and it is the same thing.*

She put both of those down.

*He has not put anything on the ground.*

*His boots are off, and that is the only thing off him. There is no pack
by the fire. There is no roll. Either he has got nothing at all, or it is
somewhere in the dark and he has been sat here long enough to have brought
it in and has not.*

---

Then he offered them the food, and that was the thing that made it
difficult.

He had a cloth with about four things in it and he opened it out and put
it between them and said, "Go on," and it was not performed and there was
no second half to it, and he went back to the fire.

There was a hard black thing in it that she knew, because she had bought
one on a bridge at Ōgi on the twelfth of the twelfth month and had not had
a word for it then either.

She looked at Tadayoshi. He had already taken something and was eating it,
which she found out later was the whole of his reasoning: *a man who has
poisoned the food does not eat first and this one has been eating for an
hour.*

She ate.

---

He talked to her.

Not much and not at her. He asked whether the mule was any good and she
said yes and he said they were better than people said, and then he said
something about a mule he had known that would not go past a particular
tree, any tree, if it had a certain shape, and she said that was not true
and he said it absolutely was and she said prove it and he said he could
not because the mule was four hundred miles away and probably dead.

And she laughed.

It came out before she had anything to do with it, and it was the first
time since the third month of the year before last, and she heard it
happen and had to sit with the fact of it for about a minute afterwards
while he went on talking about the tree.

---

He did not ask her one thing about herself.

She noticed at about the sixth minute and started keeping it, and by the
tenth she had it whole and it was the third one.

*He asked where we were going.*

*He did not ask where we have come from.*

She sat with that.

*Those are the same question to anybody who is not either of us.*

*And he asked the one that is about tomorrow and not the one that is about
before, and that is a decision, and he made it in the first thirty seconds
and has held it for ten minutes.*

---

She had it at about the eleventh minute.

Not the name of it. She would not have a name for any part of it for
another two years. What she had was the shape, complete, and she got it in
the order she got everything.

*Nothing on the ground. On the side the smoke isn't. The road behind his
right shoulder. Asked the one about tomorrow. Boots off, which is the only
thing that is off, and which he could have back on in about four seconds.*

*He is doing what we are doing.*

*And he is on his own, and he is about twenty, and he has been doing it
long enough that he does not have to think about the side of the fire, and
he is doing it better than us and he has got nothing.*

She looked at him properly for the first time.

*That is us in nine years.*

She did not say anything about it. She did not say anything about it for
eleven years.

---

The other thing was the pipe, and it happened three times.

He did not smoke it. He held it and put the end of it in his mouth and
blew, gently, the way you blow on a splinter, and a bubble came off the
end of it, one, about the size of an apple, and went up over the fire and
went about eight feet and burst.

The first time she thought she had seen it wrong.

The second time he did it while he was thinking about something and she
watched the whole of it, and it was a bubble and it came off a pipe and
there was nothing in his hands but the pipe.

The third time was much later, nearly midnight, when he had stopped
talking, and it went up higher because the fire had gone down, and it hung
for a long time above the three of them, turning, and there was firelight
on it.

Nobody said anything about it at all.

She looked at Tadayoshi. He had seen all three. His face had done what his
face did, which was nothing, and she found out afterwards what he had
written down, and it was two words, and neither of them was about the
bubble.

---

The last part happened at about that point, and she had the whole of it
and Tadayoshi had none of it, and that was not his fault.

The man looked across the fire at him and said, in the same voice he had
used about the mule and the tree:

"How long have you been out here?"

---

Tadayoshi did not answer.

She was four feet from him and she watched it happen and there was nothing
in it: he did not tense and he did not look away and he did not do the
thing people do when they are deciding whether to lie. He looked at the
fire and he did not open his mouth, and about two seconds went past.

And the man's face changed.

Not much. It was a small thing and it was entirely internal and she got it
because she had been reading faces since the sixth month of the year
before last and this one was easier than most.

*He has just found something out.*

*And it was not the answer. It is what happened instead of the answer.*

The man said:

"No. Don't answer that. I'm sorry."

---

And that was all of it.

He did not explain and he did not soften it and he did not do the thing
adults did, which was to fill the next thirty seconds with something else
so that nobody had to sit in it. He said it, and he meant it, and he
turned the pipe over in his fingers once and looked at the fire, and after
about a minute he said something about the road going north and whether
the water at the second crossing was still bad.

Tadayoshi said it was fine in the second month.

The man said, "Right," and that was the last thing either of them said to
the other.

---

He was gone before light.

She woke at about the time she always woke and the fire was out and the
cloth was gone and the boots were gone and there was nothing at all where
he had been sitting, and she went and looked at the ground for a while,
because she had watched Tadayoshi do that eleven times and had worked out
what it was for.

*He has not left anything.*

*And he has not taken anything either.*

---

She read the entry over Tadayoshi's arm on the road that morning and it
was one line.

> *22/2. Man at a fire, ab. 20, alone. No threat, going N.*

She looked at that for about four miles.

*No threat, going north.*

*That is not wrong.*

*He has got the whole of the outside of it and none of the inside, and I
have got the inside and I could not have told you which way the road went
behind his shoulder if I had not learned it off watching him.*

---

The thing she did not get to until the second evening was the one that
mattered, and it came out of nowhere while she was doing the fire, and it
undid a finding she had had since the sixth month of the year before last.

*I could not price him either.*

She sat back on her heels.

*Everybody has a number. That is the finding and it has never been wrong
and I have had it two years.*

*And it has been wrong twice in seven weeks.*

She went at it hard, because a finding that fails twice is not a finding
any more and she was not going to keep one out of habit.

And she got it, and it was better than the old one, and she was eleven and
sitting on the ground with a handful of sticks.

*He had nothing.*

*A man with nothing has got nothing he wants from you, and that is why
there was no number on him, and it is not a mystery, it is arithmetic.*

She put a stick on the fire.

*And the other one has got eleven thousand ryō and a mule and a sword and
he has not got a number on me either.*

*So they are not the same and I have been treating them as the same for
two days.*

*The man had nothing to want with.*

*He has got everything to want with, and does not want anything, and I do
not know what that is, and I have never once heard of it.*

She looked across the fire at him, doing the throwables, in the dark, four
feet away.

*And I have got one thousand four hundred and sixty-seven days to find
out.*
