# Chapter 14, Scene 9 — It Was Going Well

*Act 2. A room eleven paces by seven. A boy, age thirteen. POV: the boy.
~1,650 words.*

---

He got two hundred and eleven of them and then it stopped.

Two hundred and eleven is not a guess. He had every one of them and he had
them in order and he had the four that were out and the reasons the four
were out, and by about the eightieth he had stopped counting his own
breathing between them because he did not need to any more.

**He could tell when the next one was coming.**

That was the thing he had got out of it that was not the count, and it was
new, and it was the first time in his life he had ever known what was
about to happen.

*It is coming.*

*It is coming in about the length of the wall.*

*It is coming — now.*

And it came, and it came at the place he had put it, and he had that
about a hundred and sixty times, and there is no sensible word for what
that was like for somebody who had spent two years in a room where
everything arrived without warning and left without explanation.

---

He also got the piece.

The fifth one came off the back of his left hand at about the hundred and
ninetieth, and it was three thumbs on the nose, and he had said it would
be three and it was three, and he sat with it in his hand for a long time.

*I said three.*

*Two months ago I said the fifth one would be three, because it goes down
by a half and I picked the half, and it is three.*

He put it at the end of the row.

Five pieces now, bases lined up: four, three and a half, three and a
quarter, three — and the straight one, which was the control, and which
had never been broken and never would be.

*So the half was right and the quarter was the bad one.*

*And six more.*

He broke the sixth off that night, in the dark, with his right hand over
his left, and did not make a noise, and started the count again with a
clock he trusted.

---

Then, on a day he could not name, it did not happen.

He noticed at the place where it should have been and did not do anything
about it, because one missing is not information — he had four of those in
two hundred and eleven and he had them written down where things went, and
a schedule that holds through a gap is still a schedule.

*One. That is a missed one and the interval does not move.*

He counted on.

*Two.*

He sat up.

*Four.*

*Six.*

*Eleven.*

He stopped counting at eleven, which was about a day and a half by the old
figures, and got up and stood in the middle of the room, which he did not
do.

---

He did the work.

That is the part that should be said properly, because he was thirteen and
he was in the dark and the only thing he had had for a month had just gone,
and what he did was sit down against the wall and do it in order.

**One. It is happening and I have stopped hearing it.**

*Because I have gone deaf, or because I have got used to it the way I got
used to the stream.*

He tested it. He listened to everything else for a long time and could
hear the feet above and to the north and the thing that goes in the
passage and the water somewhere under the floor, and he had all of those.

*No. I am hearing everything else.*

**Two. It has moved.**

*They have taken it somewhere further off and it is going on and I cannot
hear it from here.*

He sat with that one for a while, because it was the one that would let
him keep the number if he could ever get back in range of it.

*I cannot check that. There is nothing in this room that would tell me,
and I have not got a way of getting to a different room.*

*So it goes down as possible and not usable, which is the same as no.*

**Three. It has stopped.**

*And that is the one I can check, because it is the one that looks like
this.*

---

He checked it the only way available, which was to wait.

He waited a very long time. He did not sleep properly and he was not doing
it out of hope, because hope was not one of the things he had; he was
doing it because a negative result wants a length of time attached to it
and he was not going to write one down that he had not earned.

He got to about sixty of the old intervals and stopped.

*Right.*

*It has stopped.*

---

He did not wonder why.

**That should be set down without any commentary at all, because it is the
whole of him and there is nothing else in it.** *He had been in a room for
two years and a room for a month and had never once been told a thing
about a person, and wondering why requires you to have some idea what
people do and what happens to them, and he had never been given one.*

A thing had been happening at a regular interval.

It had stopped.

That was the entire content of it, and everything a reader might put
underneath it was not available to him, and he was not being brave about
that and he was not being cold about it, and he would not have understood
either word if it had been used to him.

---

What he did about it was arithmetic and he did it badly, once, and then
did it properly.

The bad go was: *how much have I lost.*

The proper go, which took him the rest of the night, was:

*I broke the sixth one at about the hundred and ninetieth and I had about
another sixty after that before it went, and then eleven, and then sixty
of waiting, and the last sixty are not real because I was not measuring
anything, I was sitting still.*

*So I have got the sixth one at about seventy, out of about two hundred.*

*And I have got no way of knowing how long the waiting was.*

He put his hand flat on the floor.

*Call it four days. It might be three and it might be six. Four is the
middle and I have marked it as a number I have made and not counted.*

*So the sixth one is at seventy and something, and the something is
between nothing and a great deal, and every measurement I take from here
carries it.*

---

He put the five pieces in a row and felt along the back of his left hand,
where the sixth was, and it was there, and it was coming, and it was
coming at a rate he could no longer measure.

He lay down on his side facing the wall.

*I had it.*

*I had two hundred and eleven of them and four out and I knew which four
and why, and I could say when the next one was and I was right about a
hundred and sixty times, and I said the fifth piece would be three thumbs
and it was three thumbs.*

He did not go on with that, because there was nowhere for it to go, and he
had never in his life had a place to put a thing that was over.

He lay in the dark in a room eleven paces by seven with a drain in the
north corner and had one more thought about it, and it was the last one he
had about it, and he never had another.

*It was going well.*
