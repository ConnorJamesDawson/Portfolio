# Chapter 15, Scene 9 — A Thing With a Lid

*Act 2. A room eleven paces by seven, and then not. A boy, age thirteen.
POV: the boy. ~1,600 words.*

---

The man did not come back.

He gave it a long time, in the units he had, which were the sixth piece
and the bowls, and the sixth piece went from seventy-and-something to
about a hundred and ten and the man did not come back, and the second
reading he had been counting on did not happen and was never going to.

*So there is no gap and there is no rate.*

*I had that in about ten minutes and I was pleased with it and it was
wrong, and the reason it was wrong is that I decided what somebody else
was going to do, and I have never done that before, and I am not going to
do it again.*

He wrote that where things went and he meant it and he kept it for the
rest of his life.

---

They came for him on a day he could not name and there were four of them.

Four had never happened. Two happened. Two was the number.

They opened the door and there were four and they came in and one of them
said, "Up," and he got up.

---

He does not resist. It has to be said plainly and once, because it is the
whole of him at thirteen and there is nothing underneath it: he did not
resist, and he did not consider resisting, and if you had asked him
afterwards whether he had thought about it he would not have understood
the question.

*They said up.*

He went out of the door between two of them and along the passage the way
he went when he went anywhere, and then a different way, and he counted
the turns because counting was what he had, and got four, and then a
short flight up, which was seven, and then a room he had not been in.

There was a thing in it on the floor.

---

He looked at it for the two seconds they gave him and he got its outside.

*About four feet by two and a bit. Wood. Bound at the corners, and the
binding is on the outside, and it goes round the ends and not the sides.*

*And it has got a lid.*

One of them said, "In," and he got in.

He had to fold to do it. He got his legs in first and then went down onto
his side, which is what it was for, and the last thing he saw was one of
them reaching over him with both hands, and then the lid.

It did not go dark all at once. It went dark and then there was a line of
grey along one edge for about four seconds while something was being done,
and then that went too.

He heard four things put on and turned.

---

He had it inside two minutes.

He did it by feel, in the dark, with the sides of his hands and the flat
of his feet and his forehead, and he did it in thumbs, because thumbs was
the unit he had and it had never once let him down.

*Length: forty-nine of mine and a bit, and the bit is about a third, so
call it forty-nine and a third and mark it.*

*Width at the shoulder: nineteen.*

*Width at the foot: nineteen. It does not taper. That is the same all the
way and I have done it three times.*

*Height: fourteen at the middle and thirteen and a half at both ends,
which is not the box, that is the lid bowing, and it will do that with
weight on it.*

He lay on his side with his knees up and his hands in front of his chest.

*It is the smallest space I have ever been in.*

*It is smaller than the room by an enormous amount and I have got the room
to a thumb so I can say how much, and I am not going to do that now
because it is not useful.*

He put that down and went on.

---

There was a problem and he found it about a minute after the measuring and
it was the only thing in the whole of it that got anywhere near him.

*I cannot lay them out.*

The five pieces were inside his shirt where they had been since the ninth
month, and the fifth one was three thumbs on the nose and the fourth was
two and a bit and the third and second and first went down from there, and
the way he read them was to put them on the floor in a row with the bases
lined up and go along them with a finger, and he had done that perhaps
four hundred times and could do it in the dark and had.

*There is no floor.*

*There is a floor. There is a floor under my shoulder and my hip and it is
about nineteen thumbs wide and my shoulder is on it, and I would have to
be off it to lay them on it, and I cannot be off it.*

He worked at it for a while.

He tried it against his own chest, holding them up against his shirt with
one hand and going along them with the other, and it did not work, because
the bases would not line up and it was the bases that carried the whole of
the reading.

He tried it in the fold of his knees and it was worse.

Then he stopped, because there is a point at which you have tested a thing
enough, and he had.

*Right.*

*I have got a rate and I have got the sixth one on the back of my hand and
I can reach that with the other hand and I can feel how far through it is,
and that is one reading and one is not a line.*

*And the five are in my shirt and they are still there and they are still
three and two and a bit and the rest, and I know all five off by heart and
have done since the tenth month.*

*So I do not need to lay them out. I want to lay them out.*

He held very still with that one, because it was new.

*I have not had that before. I have not had a thing I wanted that was not
a thing I needed.*

He did not do anything with it. He did not have anywhere to put it. It sat
in him for about a minute and then it went, and he did not go looking for
it again for two years.

---

Then he was lifted.

It went up at the foot end first and then levelled and then moved, and he
counted it — thirty-one paces, a turn, nine, a stop, and a change in the
sound that meant outside — and then it went down onto something that gave
slightly and then took the weight, and then that thing moved.

*A cart.*

He lay in the dark with his hands in front of his chest and did the last
piece of work and it took him about six seconds and it is the whole of the
scene and he did not know that.

*A thing with a lid is a thing that is going somewhere.*

He turned it over once, the way he turned everything over once.

*You do not put a lid on a thing that is staying where it is. A lid is
for carrying. The bowls did not have lids. The room did not have a lid.*

*This has got a lid and four things put on and turned, and it has been
picked up and put on a cart.*

*So it is going somewhere.*

---

That is the most hopeful thought he had had in two years and it was not
hope.

It has to be said exactly, because the difference is the whole of him: it
was a deduction about containers. He did not feel anything about it. He
did not want it to be true and did not want it not to be. He had a fact
about the world, which was that objects with lids get carried, and he had
applied it correctly to his own case, and the answer had come out the way
it came out.

He did not think *where*.

He did not think *why*.

He did not think about the water that somebody had heated, or the second
blanket, or the boards that had been cut to a gap by a person with the
measurement, or the man with the book who had known where the sixth one
was — although he had all four of those, filed, in order, and would have
given them to you in about nine seconds if there had been anybody to give
them to.

He put his hand over the back of his other hand and found the place.

*Through by about the middle of it now.*

*A hundred and ten, or a bit more, and the four days are still in it.*

The cart went about forty paces and stopped and something was said above
him that he could not make out, and then it went on, and he lay on his
side in a box forty-nine and a third thumbs long with five pieces of
himself inside his shirt and nowhere to put them down, and after a while
he slept, which he had learned to do in about nine seconds anywhere, and
which was the single most useful thing anybody had ever accidentally
taught him.
