There was one on the first and one on the fourth.

She put both of them on the bag in the dark with the door shut and then

*Twenty-second to the twenty-seventh is five.*

*Twenty-seventh to the first is five.*

*First to the fourth is three.*

*And the twelve is sitting in the middle of it like a stone in a boot.*

---

It took her most of the fourth to decide and she was not honest with
herself about the deciding, and she knew that afterwards: she told herself
she was going back to the sacks because there were eleven left and she had

*Six a night. That's two nights.*

*So do four. Four a night and it's three nights and nobody's going to say

*Right.*

*One.*

---

She heard it go.

the door shut, and she knew, in about a second, in the way she now knew

*They've gone in.*

*Both of them. Together. At a quarter past ten with me sitting here.*

*And the door's shut.*

*Get up.*

*You are up. You are already up. You are sitting on a stool with your

She sat there with the needle in her hand for the whole of it.

too big, and had to take them out.

going to sleep.

*Right.*

*Say the thing.*

*It happened with me sitting in the kitchen. With the lamp on. Four feet

*And I did nothing different.*

She lay in the dark.

*I did nothing different. That's the whole of the finding and it took me

*In the eighth month I sat on that stool eleven nights and it did not
happen once and the gap went to twelve, and I worked out that I was a
thing that slowed it down, and I have carried that around for a fortnight

*Not me. I have not done one thing differently.*

*Not the day. It's a Tuesday and the twenty-ninth of the seventh was a

*So it isn't a variable I've got.*

---

*Or it was never that.*

She lay on the mat with her eyes open.

*Say it properly.*

*In the eighth month I sat in that kitchen for eleven nights and I decided

*And the other thing it could have been, the whole time, is that a man who
wants to do a thing and has a girl sitting in his kitchen with a lamp on

bothered, and on the sixth of the ninth month he could.*

**I was not a reason. I was a nuisance.**

*And a nuisance is a thing you go round.*

She put both hands over her face.

*So the twelve wasn't me stopping it. The twelve was me making it

I do not know why it stopped being inconvenient, and that is the same
answer I have had since the eighth of the eighth month.*

*It isn't in the house.*

---

She did the seventh and the eighth anyway.

She could not have said why and she did not try. She got the stool out at
seven on both nights and did four sacks and sat there until half ten with
the lamp going, and nothing happened on either of them, and she got no
comfort out of that at all, because she now knew exactly what nothing
meant, which was nothing.

*That's done, then.*

*Forty sacks. Nobody's going to have to do those for four years.*

*And I did it for a reason that wasn't a reason and it turns out I have
just mended forty sacks.*

She went back in and did not tell anybody they were finished, and nobody
noticed for eleven days.

She got the knife out on the ninth, in the afternoon, in the light.

She had not looked at it since the twenty-sixth of the eighth month. She
had put her hand on it perhaps forty times, lying on her right side in the
dark, through the matting, and had known the shape of it through two
layers of straw the way you know the shape of a thing you are not looking
at. But she had not had it out, and she had not had it out on purpose, and
she knew that too.

She sat on the floor of her own room with the door shut and took the
leather off the point.

*It's just a knife.*

That was the first thing and it was not what she had been expecting.

*It's seven inches and it's got a wooden handle and there's a nick out of
the back near the guard and there's a place about a third of the way down
where the edge is thicker because somebody's taken it back on a stone at
the wrong angle, probably twice, probably him.*

*It's an ordinary thing off a drawer.*

She turned it over in the light.

*And it's dry.*

---

That was the thing that got her.

She had taken an oiled knife out of a drawer where somebody looked after
it, and she had put it under a mat and lain on it for a fortnight, and it
had been against straw and against her and it had not been touched, and
there was a bloom on it now down the flat where the oil had gone.

*I've had it a fortnight and I haven't looked after it.*

*He oils that twice a year and he does it because it's a thing that gets
used once and has to work when it does, and I took it off him and I've let
it go in a fortnight.*

She sat there with it.

*And if I oil it —*

She stopped, and made herself finish it, because she had a rule about
that.

*If I oil it, then it's mine, and I've got a thing I look after.*

*And a thing you look after is a thing you're going to have for a while.*

*And a fortnight ago I could still tell myself I was carrying it.*

---

She oiled it.

She went out to the shed and got the little pot down off the shelf where
it had been since the second of the seventh month, and she did the whole
of it properly, the way she had watched her father do a scythe about nine
hundred times: the flat, both sides, thin, with a rag, and then the edge,
and then the leather back on the point, and then the pot back on the shelf
exactly where it had been with the lid turned the way it had been turned.

It took about four minutes.

Then she went back to her own room and put it under the mat at the top,
where her hand went, and sat on the floor and looked at the mat.

*Right.*

*It's mine.*

*And I still can't say what it's for and I've stopped trying, and I am not
going to sit here and be frightened about not being able to say, because
being frightened about it hasn't got me anywhere in a fortnight.*

She got the bag out.

*Nine, five, three, four, twelve, five, five, three.*

*And the next one's about the eighth or the ninth, and it's the ninth
today.*

She put the bag back behind the brick and put the brick back and went out
to do the water.

Nothing happened on the ninth.

Ine doesn't and Kiku knows there's a girl but she doesn't know a name, I
never gave her a name, she never asked and I never gave it.*

*If I stopped, nobody would come.*

*If I went in the river tonight there is not one single person who would
find out, and there is nowhere I am written down, and nobody in the whole
of this country is going to say my name to anybody for any reason ever.*

She sat with that.

*And that's the worst thing that's ever happened to me.*

*And it's the safest I've ever been.*

*And they're the same fact.*

She put the bag inside her shift and put her arms round her knees.
