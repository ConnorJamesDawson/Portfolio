# Chapter 9, Scene 2 — Right

*Act 1, month 9, the fourth to the tenth. Ōtoma to Fukae and back.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,520 words.*

---

The sheet came on the fourth and it had Fukae on it.

> *6th. Fukae. The house on the corner of the middle street with the two
> steps. After dark. Ask for Mori.*
> *In from the west on the 6th and out the same night.*
> *No return required.*

He did it exactly the way he had done it on the twenty-fifth of the eighth
month, because it was exactly the same problem and there was no reason to
do it differently.

*The middle street.*

*The rail outside the place with the shutters is on the middle street and
the woman on it sold a boy a coat for one and a half on the thirteenth of
the sixth month, in front of a street, for double what it was worth, and
she gave him a pair of mittens afterwards because she felt bad about it.*

*She will remember that for a year. Ōtsuka said she'd remember it for a
year and Ōtsuka has been in that town eleven years.*

*And the Kiribata is ninety yards up from the corner and the innkeeper's
wife asked me whose I was on the twelfth of the sixth month in front of
four people, and it went on long enough that two carters turned round.*

He got the ruled sheet out and read his own file, which had one line on
it, and put it away.

*And a man was asking for ages in a lodging at Hanase eight weeks after a
riverbed, and Hanase is four days east of Fukae, and a district is not a
town.*

*Which is the same objection. It is word for word the same objection and I
have got it in ten days less time than I had it last month.*

---

"Right," he said.

---

He went and got his things.

That took about four minutes, because there were not many of them and he
knew where all of them were, and he went through the whole of it the way
he went through it every time: the tin, filled, and the second tin, filled
and stoppered, and the grease, and the knife, and the boots which had
about six weeks left in them and which he had known that about since the
twenty-second of the eighth month.

The blanket. The oil. The ruled sheet on one side inside his shirt and the
book on the other.

He took a coat off a peg, which was not the coat, because the coat was on
a nail in Hanase, and this one was Hōki's second one and was too big and
had been given to him with about eleven words attached to it.

And he went at about two in the afternoon and did the four days in three
and a half, off the road, parallel, three crossings, one town.

---

He was on a bank above Fukae on the evening of the sixth waiting for the
light to go, and he got the book out to do the day, which he did every day,
and he sat with the pen over it and there was nothing to write.

*The road was the road. The weather was the weather.*

*Nothing happened.*

He looked at the page.

*So there's no entry.*

*Which is right. An entry for a day that had nothing in it is not an
entry, it is a diary, and I have not got a diary, I have got a book of
findings, and there was no finding.*

He put the pen back.

Then he took it out again.

*Except.*

He sat on a bank above Fukae with a book open on his knees and did not
write anything for about a minute and a half.

*On the twenty-fifth of the eighth month I had this and I put it in front
of a man and I said it out loud and I said it twice and I said I would say
it again on the same evidence, and it went in the book at forty-nine and I
ruled the last line in two halves.*

*And on the fourth of the ninth month I had exactly the same thing and I
said right.*

*And there is no entry.*

He looked at the page for a while longer.

*Because nothing happened. That is not me being dishonest. Nothing
happened. I read a sheet and I said right and I put my boots on.*

*So what would I put.*

He tried it. He got as far as putting the pen on the page and building the
first four words, and could not get any further, and it was not that he did
not have the sentence.

*I have got the sentence.*

*The sentence is: I did not object this time.*

*And that is a finding about me and I have got about nine of those in this
book and I have never once had trouble writing one down.*

He shut the book.

---

The job took eleven minutes.

A house on the corner with two steps; a man of about thirty called Mori
who was frightened of something that was not Tadayoshi and did not stop
looking past him at the street; the thing out of the shirt and into a
cupboard; and out of the town by the west end at about nine, on the road,
in the open, at the speed of a boy going somewhere he is expected.

He was four miles out before he let himself think about any of it.

*Nothing.*

*Nobody looked at me. I was in that town for about fifty minutes and I did
not go up the middle street past the rail and I did not go within ninety
yards of the Kiribata and I planned the route in on the bank and it worked
exactly.*

*So the objection was answered by the route.*

He turned that over for about a mile.

*No it wasn't.*

*I could have done that route on the twenty-fifth of the eighth month as
well. There was a way round the Hanase district that was two days and I
said the two days made it the twenty-eighth and there was nobody at
Ide-no-shō on the twenty-eighth, and that was true.*

*And this one was one street. This one was: do not walk up the middle
street. And I did not walk up the middle street and it cost me eleven
minutes.*

*So they are not the same job at all and I have been treating them as the
same job since the fourth, and that is a mistake, and I am glad it is a
mistake.*

He walked about half a mile on being glad.

*And I did not work any of that out on the fourth.*

*I worked it out four miles outside Fukae on the sixth, after it had gone
right.*

*On the fourth I read a sheet with Fukae on it and I said right, and if
there had been no way round the middle street I would still have said
right, because I did not look for one until I was on the bank.*

---

He got back to Ōtoma on the tenth.

Hōki was at the bench and did not look up, which was the ordinary state of
things, and Tadayoshi gave him the two words he was owed and sat down and
started on his boots because they had about six weeks in them and it was
the ninth month.

"Did you go round?" said Hōki.

"No."

Hōki went on with what he was doing.

He did not say anything else. He did not say *good* and he did not say
*you want to be careful* and he did not ask which way Tadayoshi had gone
in or out or whether he had gone up the middle street, and after about
four seconds he asked whether the grit at Fukae was any good, and
Tadayoshi said it was the second bar and about a thirtieth and worse than
Ōtoma, and that was the whole of it.

---

He sat with his boots for a while after Hōki had gone in.

*He knew.*

*He had that sheet before I did. He knew Fukae was on it and he knew what
I said about Hanase on the twenty-fifth and he knew I had said I would say
it again on the same evidence, and he handed it to me and said nothing at
all and waited.*

*And then he asked one question and did not ask the second one.*

He worked at that for a while and could not get it open, which was the
usual state of things now, and he had begun to be able to sit with it,
which was new.

*He was finding something out.*

*He was finding it out for eleven days and he found it out on the fourth
in about a second and a half, and it is a thing about me, and I have not
got it.*

He looked at the boot.

*And I would like to know what it is.*

He did not write that down either.
