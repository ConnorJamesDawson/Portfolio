# Chapter 5, Scene 6 — Six Minutes

*Act 1, month 5, the eighth. The riverbed. Tadayoshi Ruri, age ten. POV:
Tadayoshi. ~1,920 words.*

---

They let him put his hand inside his coat because they had asked him to.

That was the whole of the first move and it took a second and a quarter,
and it worked entirely because four people who have been sent to collect
a boy have already decided what a boy's hand in a coat is.

He got the tin out with his left hand and held it up.

And with his right, low, against his ribs, in the shadow of his own arm,
he did Ram, Boar, Ox, Snake.

---

The strip went in across the bed behind him.

Eleven feet of it, in a line, from the channel stones out to the landward
bank: points about the length of his little finger, tapered, tips up,
laid in through the grit at about a hand's width apart, and every single
one of them was a drop.

That was the whole of it. He had made four hundred of that object and he
made forty of them in a second and a quarter and put them where he wanted
them, which was the only thing he had ever been able to do.

They did not go through anybody. They were not for that.

They were a *fence*.

---

"What was — "

The one behind him on the left had come forward two steps and had stopped
because the ground in front of him had changed colour, and he was looking
down at it with the expression of somebody who has walked into a room and
found the furniture moved.

"What is that?"

"He's making seals," said Miyo.

She said it in a completely different voice from the one she had used for
*nobody's going to hurt you*, and Tadayoshi heard the paper come off her
in it.

"He's not on file for — "

"He's *making seals*, Hokuto — "

"There's nothing on the — "

"*I know what's on the tasking.*"

---

He went sideways to the channel.

The channel was fist-sized stones down the middle of the bed and it was
the worst footing in the riverbed, and it was the only cover on sixty
yards of open ground, and he got into it and put it between himself and
the two at the top.

And he laid another fence, out from the channel on the seaward side.

*Now.*

*Now there is a line behind me and a line to seaward and the channel down
the middle, and there are four of them and they are on three sides, and
the only clean approach to me is up the channel.*

*Which is stones the size of a fist, and you cannot come up that at
speed.*

*So they can come at me one at a time at a walk, or they can come round
the fences, which is thirty yards, or they can come through the fences.*

*And they cannot come through the fences, because they are wearing boots
and because they will not be able to make themselves.*

---

It worked for six minutes.

Six minutes and about ten seconds, and he had it afterwards to the second
because he had been counting off the eave — there was no eave; he had
been counting off the sea, which came up the beach beyond the bank at
about eleven a minute, which was near enough.

Six minutes, and in six minutes he did not hurt anybody.

He put a fence in front of the fourth one when he tried the landward
bank. He put a fence across the top of the channel when Miyo came down
to it and she stopped, and he laid another one behind her while she was
deciding, so that she had to go back the way she had come. He put eleven
of them across a flat place in a second and a quarter when the one on
the left tried to come round wide, and the man — the boy — swore and went
back.

He did not throw anything. He did not know how to throw anything. It did
not occur to him then or later that throwing was a thing that was
available to him, because he had spent two years making objects that
stayed exactly where he put them and his whole body understood the trade
as *placement*.

*They are being careful.*

*Every single time, they stop. Every time the ground changes they stop
and they look at it and they go round, and the reason they go round is
that they are wearing new boots and they have been told to bring in a
boy who is not to be hurt, and a person who is not to be hurt is a person
you cannot rush past a thing you do not understand.*

*The instruction is doing the work. Not me.*

*They have got a rule on and I have not, and that is the entire
difference and it is going to last exactly as long as the rule does.*

---

He was down to about a third of the tin.

He noticed that at the fifth minute and it went through him like cold
water, because he had been putting his hand in it without counting, which
he had never in his life done with material.

*Three fences left. Perhaps four.*

*And I am in a channel in the middle of sixty yards and the road is at
both ends and there are four of them and I am not going anywhere.*

*This is not a fight I am winning. This is a fight I am not losing yet,
and those are different, and they are about to stop being different.*

---

Hokuto came up the channel at six minutes and ten seconds.

He came fast. That was the thing. He came up fist-sized stones at a run,
which you cannot do, and he did it because he had been standing on a bank
for six minutes being made to look stupid by a ten-year-old in front of a
girl he had graduated with, and Tadayoshi understood every part of that
and understood none of it, and it did not matter.

He had a second and a quarter.

He used it on the ground under the leading foot.

---

It went in through the sole.

He had not intended that. He had intended what he had intended forty
times in six minutes, which was a fence — a line of points in front of a
foot, so that the foot stopped — and a foot that is walking stops, and a
foot that is coming down at a run out of a stride does not stop, it
arrives.

It arrived.

The point went up through the boot and through the foot and out and stood
a hand's width proud, and the whole of Hokuto's weight and the whole of
Hokuto's speed went onto it and past it, and he went down forward onto
the stones with his own leg behind him.

And he screamed.

---

Everything ended.

That was how it registered — not as a change in the fight; as an
*ending*, complete and instant, of the thing that had been happening for
six minutes. There had been a procedure, with four people carrying it
out at a walk and stopping when the ground changed, and a boy screaming
in a riverbed took the whole of it away in about a second.

Miyo was already coming.

Not down the channel. Straight across the seaward fence, through it,
without slowing down, and Tadayoshi watched a girl of thirteen put both
feet through eleven feet of standing points to get to somebody who was
screaming, and did not see her face while she did it and was extremely
glad of that for the rest of his life.

*The rule is off.*

---

He got about forty seconds.

He was never able to reconstruct them properly, which he minded about
more than he minded most things: they were the last forty seconds he was
awake for and he ought to have had them, and what he had instead was
four or five pieces with no order to them.

He had the fourth one coming up the channel behind him and getting a hand
in his collar.

He had the tin going out of his left hand and hitting the stones and the
grit going everywhere at once, which he had not costed, because he had
been holding it and had never once considered that a person might simply
take it off him.

He had going down onto the stones on his side, and the noise his own
shoulder made, and knowing what the noise meant.

He had a hand on his right wrist.

---

That one he had entire.

That one he had for the rest of his life, in perfect order, at four
times the length it lasted, and it was the only part of the whole
afternoon he could have given evidence about.

Somebody had his right wrist, above the joint, one hand, from behind.

*Ram.*

He got the left one round and his fingers went into the shape and the
right hand did not come with it, because the right hand was not his any
more; it was a thing at the end of somebody else's arm, and it went where
it was taken, and the shape did not close.

Nothing happened.

That was all. There was no bang and no smoke and nothing at all
happened, in a riverbed, with somebody kneeling on him.

*Everything we do, we do with our hands, in the open, in order, in front
of the person we are doing it to.*

*So if you learn one thing in this room and nothing else, learn what it
costs to be reached.*

He tried it twice more.

The second time he got as far as Boar. The third time the hand on his
wrist tightened, and somebody said, from a long way off, "He's *doing* it
again, hold him — " and there were two of them on him.

---

Somebody put a knee across his back.

It was not cruel. He was clear about that afterwards and clearer every
year, and it was one of the things he never once let himself soften: it
was a boy of about thirteen putting a knee in the middle of a smaller
boy's back on a stone riverbed to stop him getting his hands together,
which is what you do, which is the whole of what you do, and it was
correctly placed and it was not spite.

It went across the shoulder blades on the left side, where he had gone
down on the stones.

His chest could not do anything.

That was the entire content of the next second and a half: his chest was
against stone and there was a weight on it and it could not go out and it
could not come in, and his mouth was open on the shingle with the grit
against his teeth, and somewhere above him Hokuto was still going.

*I cannot.*

*I cannot get a hand and I cannot get air and I have got about four
seconds of being awake and then this is finished, and there is a woman in
a room with a bench and I am the only person alive who knows she is in
it, and nobody is going to*

*nobody is going to look for her*

*nobody*

---

Something in him got up.
