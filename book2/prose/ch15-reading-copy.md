# Chapter 15 — The Part Doing No Work

*Book two. Act two. Year 1, month 3.*
*10 scenes, 17,490 words. Tadayoshi ×5 · Uzuki ×2 · Kimimaro ×3.*



*Act 2, year 1, month 3, the second to the fourth. Ōtoma. Tadayoshi Ruri,
age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,700 words.*

---

It was on the boards when he came into the yard on the second and he saw
the figure before he saw anything else, because the figure was in the
bottom right where figures went and he had been reading that corner first
for fifteen months.

> *4,000.*

He stood in front of it and did not do anything for about four seconds.

*That is the biggest number on that board since the twentieth of the
eleventh month.*

*And the one before that was fourteen thousand and that was Terasaka and
Terasaka is not a comparison, because Terasaka was a house and a man of
about sixty and two nights of work, and this has got a weight on it.*

He read the rest of it properly, in order, the way he read everything.

> *Ōtoma to Sarugaya, thence to the crossing below Hane.*
>
> *1 case. Ab. 90 kan gross. Sealed at origin.*
>
> *11 days. 4,000, of which 1,000 on acceptance.*

---

He went through it twice more standing there and then a third time with
his thumb going down it, which he did when a thing was going to be his.

*Ninety kan is the mule and not me.*

*Eleven days is Sarugaya in four if the river road is up and five if it is
not, and then six across, and six across is generous by about a day and a
half, and generous is a thing a good clerk does and a bad one does not.*

*Four thousand for eleven days is three hundred and sixty a day.*

He did that one again because it had come out high.

*Three hundred and sixty a day against fourteen hundred over fifty-six,
which is twenty-five.*

He stood in the yard with his thumb on the board.

*It is fourteen times.*

---

Ōtsuka was at the far end doing the thing he did with the ledger and the
hooks, and Tadayoshi went down the yard and stood the correct distance
away and waited until the man's hand came off the page, which he had
learned in the ninth month off being told once.

"The Sarugaya one."

"Mm."

"I'll take it."

Ōtsuka looked up, and did the two seconds he did, and then reached under
the counter and brought out a tin and counted a thousand ryō onto the wood
in front of him without saying anything about it, which was itself an
answer to a question Tadayoshi had not asked.

*He had it counted.*

*So it was going to be mine and he knew that on the first and I did not
know it until eleven minutes ago.*

He put the money away.

"Sealed at origin," he said. "Who seals it."

"Says on the sheet."

"It doesn't."

Ōtsuka turned the ledger round and looked at his own copy of it for
about four seconds and then said, "The consignor," in the tone of a man
reading a thing off a page, which is what he was doing.

"Right," said Tadayoshi.

And that was the whole of it, and there was nothing wrong with any of it,
and he went out of the yard with a thousand ryō on him and the eleventh
of the first month sitting in his coat where it had been sitting for
fifty-one days.

---

He did the book that night with the fire between them and she went to
sleep before he finished, which she did not usually do.

He did the money first because the money was the reason.

> *WHAT IS LEFT*
>
> *6,140.*
>
> *27/2 – 2/3, feed and food: −152.*
>
> *Sarugaya, on acceptance: +1,000.*
>
> **6,988.**

He looked at it.

*It went up.*

*That page has existed for four days and it has gone down once and up
once and the up is bigger.*

He sat with that for a bit and did not enjoy it as much as he had expected
to, and went and found out why, and it took about a minute.

*Because a thousand of that is not earned. It is against. If I do not
deliver the case I give it back or I am a thief, and I have not got a
column for money that is on me and is not mine.*

He ruled one, three inches in, and wrote *AGAINST* at the head of it and
put the thousand in it, and then took the thousand out of the main figure
and wrote **5,988** underneath and left both numbers on the page, because
both of them were true and he was not going to pick.

*And it still went up on the day it arrives.*

*I am not taking that off the page either.*

---

Then he turned back three pages.

He had known he was going to do it since the yard. He had known it while
he was doing the feed arithmetic, and he had done the feed arithmetic
first anyway, in order, because the order was the order.

The line was where he had left it.

> *Fees low + gaps long = 21 days away over the turn. Expect recovery in
> the 2nd month.*
>
> *27/2. No.*

He read it four times.

*Four thousand.*

*Not fourteen hundred and not nine hundred. Four thousand, and eleven
days, and a thousand of it in a tin under a counter before I asked.*

*That is a recovery. That is not a good week, that is the boards going
back to what they were, and it is what I said would happen.*

He put the pencil on the page and did not write yet.

*It is four days late.*

He went at that properly, because it was the only thing standing in the
way and he did not step over things.

*The second month ended on Thursday and this is Saturday and the docket
went up on the first. So the sentence said the second month and the thing
happened in the third, and the gap between those two is thirty-six hours
and one wall of a calendar.*

*I have never in my life marked a thing wrong for thirty-six hours.*

*If a man tells me a cart will be at a place on the fourth and it is there
on the fifth I do not write him off. I write down that it was a day and I
carry the day.*

He turned the pencil over.

*So the sentence is right and it is a day and a half out.*

---

He took the mark off.

He did it the way he did everything, which was completely and without any
decoration on it: he put a small square bracket round the mark and wrote
the date beside it, because you do not rub a thing out, you show that it
stopped applying and when.

> *2/3. Recovery. 4,000, 11 d. Called it 50 days out, 36 hrs late.*

And then, because it was true and because he wrote true things down, he
put one more line under it.

> *Sentence stands.*

He looked at the page with the bracket on it for a while.

*I have had one of those wrong all year and now I have not.*

He did not know what to do with that, so he did the thing he did with
everything he did not know what to do with, which was to put it in the
book and shut the book.

---

The third and the fourth were preparation and there is one thing in them.

He did the load on the third. Ninety kan gross on an animal rated at
about sixty of it comfortable and eighty if you are prepared to argue
with it meant the case went on a frame and the frame went on the mule and
the rest of what they owned went on the two of them, and he did that
arithmetic twice and wrote it out and was satisfied with it.

He did the road on the fourth. River road if it was up, four days; the
long way if it was not, five; and eleven days total either way, which
meant the clerk who wrote the sheet had allowed for the river road being
down and had not said so.

*That is a good sheet.*

He wrote that down and did not look at it again.

---

And on the fourth evening she asked him for the dockets.

She did it the way she did things, which was all at once and out of
nowhere, over the fire, with no run-up:

"Can I see the old ones?"

"Old what?"

"Dockets. The ones you've done."

He thought about it for about two seconds, and the two seconds were not
about whether she could have them.

*They are in the frame and the frame is under the boards and getting the
frame out is forty minutes.*

*And there is nothing in them. They are jobs. She has seen four of them
and she has been in the yard when I read three.*

"Why?"

"I want to look at them."

Which was not an answer and she plainly thought it was one, and he
recognised the shape of that, because it was the shape of every answer he
had ever given anybody about anything.

"Sixth," he said, which meant the sixth, because the frame was under the
boards and getting it out was forty minutes and he had the load and the
road to do first.

"Right."

She said it in the way she said *right*, which was the end of a thing and
not the middle of one, and went back to the fire.

And it was not the sixth. It was the tenth, and the four days in between
were not hers and were not his either, and he noticed on the tenth that
he had put a thing off for four days and could not immediately say what
had been in the way, and it bothered him for about eight seconds and then
it did not.

It cost him forty minutes.

It was the most expensive forty minutes he ever spent and he never found
that out either.

---

There is one part of the evening left and it is nine words long, and it
is the reason the rest of it is written down.

He got up to put the book away and stopped, standing, with it in his hand.

*Fourteen times.*

*Fourteen times what I made in fifty-six days, for carrying a box.*

He waited to have a thought about that.

The thought that came was that ninety kan is a load and a load is a rate
and the mule had a page now with a rate on it and this was the first job
since the page existed that would put a real reading on it, and he was
pleased about that, and he went to sleep pleased.

And what did not arrive — not that night and not on the third and not on
the fourth, and not for eleven days, and when it did arrive it arrived too
late to be any use to anybody — was the other question, which is the one
he had asked in a room above Ōtoma in the ninth month of the year before
last and had been right about, and which had gone into a file, and which
was the whole of what he was for.

*Who pays four thousand ryō, and what for.*

He did not ask it, because it was freight.

*You ask that about a man. You do not ask it about a box.*

*Act 2. A room eleven paces by seven. A boy, age thirteen. POV: the boy.
~1,700 words.*

---

Three things happened in nine days and he had all three of them and could
do nothing with any of them, and he knew that he could do nothing with
them, which is a different thing from not trying.

He put them in the order they came, because that was the only order he
had.

---

The first was the water.

It came on a day he could not name, in the ordinary way, which was that
the door opened and a thing happened and the door shut, and he did not
resist it because resisting had never once been an option that was
offered.

Two of them came in. One had a bucket and one had a cloth and they did it
standing up in the middle of the floor with him out of his shirt, and it
took about four minutes, and they did his arms and his chest and his neck
and his hair, and one of them did behind his ears with a thumb inside the
cloth, and neither of them said anything to him at any point.

The water was warm.

*That is warm water.*

He stood in the middle of the floor with his arms out where they had put
them and he had that thought and then he had the next one, and the next
one took longer.

*I have not had warm water since the sixth month of the year I was
eleven. That was a tub in a yard and there were four of us and it was
warm because it was summer and it had been standing.*

*This is warm because somebody has heated it.*

He put the shirt back on when they gave it to him and sat down against the
wall in the place he sat and worked at it for a long time and got exactly
one step.

*Somebody has heated water and carried it down here.*

*That is work. Somebody has done work.*

And that was the step, and there was not a second one, because the second
one would have had to be about why a person does work, and he had nothing
whatever to put in that.

It happened again nine days later.

---

The second was the blanket.

There was one and then there were two, and the second one arrived on the
floor by the door while he was asleep, which he knew because it was not
there and then it was.

It was better than his. It was thicker and it was not damp at the corner
and it did not have the smell.

He looked at it for a while from where he was.

Then he got up and put it on the floor under the first one, because the
floor was the cold part and always had been, and lay down on the two of
them, and it worked, and he was warmer that night than he had been since
the ninth month.

*That is better.*

He did not have a thought after that one either.

He noticed, some days later, that he had not once considered putting it
over himself, and that if he had it would have been about the same, and
that he had put it underneath because the floor was the problem, and that
he had solved the problem correctly and instantly and without thinking
about it, and he was pleased about that in the small way he was pleased
about things.

---

The third was the gap, and the third is the one that cost him something,
and he did not find out what for two days.

The gap was high on the west wall and it was about four thumbs by eleven
and he could not reach it and had never been able to.

It had failed as a clock in the ninth month. He had gone at it for eleven
days — the light coming up the wall, where the edge of it got to, how long
it took to cross a course of the stone — and it had failed for three
reasons which he had written in his head where things went, and the third
of the three was the one that killed it, which was that on a grey day
there was no edge at all and a grey day looks exactly like the middle of
the night from the far side of a room.

So it was a failed instrument and he had stopped using it in the ninth
month of the year before.

He came back from the water on the second time and it was boarded.

---

He could not reach it, so he did it the way he had done the ceiling, which
was to throw pieces of the floor at it and listen.

It took two days and about sixty goes and it was the best work he had done
since the clock.

*Board does not sound like stone. Board is a hollow and stone is a nothing
and I can hear the difference from here every single time.*

He worked the edges. Left first, going up: hollow, hollow, hollow, then
nothing, and he threw at the same place four more times to be sure, and it
was nothing four more times. Then the right, going up, and the same. Then
across the top, which took the longest because he was throwing high and
tired and had to stop twice.

By the end of the second day he had it, and he did not want it.

*Three boards, across, on the outside.*

*And they stop where the stone starts. Both ends. Top and bottom.*

*That gap is four thumbs by eleven, and there is no board sticking out
past it anywhere I can find, and I have been round the whole of it.*

*If you cut a board long and put it up, you have a board sticking out.
These do not stick out.*

*So they were cut to it. And to cut a thing to fit, you have to have the
measurement before you cut, and to have the measurement you have to have
measured.*

He sat down on the floor.

*And they are on the outside, so they went on from a passage, so there is
a passage on the other side of that wall and it is high enough for a man
to stand up in and work above his head.*

He put that in with the things about the building, which was a short list
and had not had anything added to it since the eleventh month.

*Somebody has measured this room.*

---

He did not sleep that night and he was not frightened, and it is important
that he was not, because he did not have it.

What he had was a thing that would not sit down.

*I know this room.*

*It is eleven paces by seven and the pace is mine and I have done it four
times and got the same answer four times. The floor goes down about a
thumb across the width of it toward the north corner and the drain is
there. The door is one and a half paces from the east corner and it opens
inward and the hinge is on the side away from the drain. The ceiling is a
foot above my hand and I got that by throwing.*

*And somebody else knows it too.*

He turned that over and could not get it to be anything.

*I thought I was the only one who knew it. I have never once thought that
before, because I have never once had a reason to think about who else
knows anything.*

*And it is not that they came in and measured it. They did not. I would
have had that.*

*It is that a person who has never been in here has got a number off
this room and I do not know which number or when or what for.*

He got up and stood in the middle of the floor, which he did not do, and
which he had now done twice in about two months, and both times it had
been the same thing underneath and he did not connect them.

---

Then there was the doorway, and it is the fourth thing and he did not
count it as one, and it is the only one of the four that was about him.

Somebody stood in it.

It was open, which happened, and there was a man in it, which happened,
and the man did not come in and did not put anything down and did not say
anything and did not do anything at all.

Kimimaro counted it, because counting was what he had.

*Forty. Forty-one. Forty-two.*

He got to about forty seconds and then the man went and the door shut.

It happened again two days later and it was about the same and he counted
that one too and got thirty-eight, and he was pleased with the two numbers
and did not know what to do with them, and put them in with the rest.

---

He did the whole of it once more before he stopped, in order, the way you
do a series.

*Water that somebody heated. Twice, nine days apart.*

*A blanket that is better than the one I have.*

*Boards cut to a gap by a person who has the measurement.*

*And a man in the doorway for forty seconds, twice, who did not want
anything.*

He sat against the wall with his hand on the back of his other hand, and
the sixth piece was under it, at seventy-and-something out of about two
hundred, and he could feel the place where it was going to come through
and it was not sore yet.

*Something is going to happen.*

*I had that in the twelfth month off the bowl and I was right.*

*It has taken this long.*

And that is where it stopped, and it stopped there for the same reason it
had stopped every time for two years, which is that the next question is
*what*, and *what* is a question about people, and he had never once in
his life been given anything to answer a question about people with.

So he had four facts and a conclusion and no fifth step, and it was
correct as far as it went, and it went four fifths of the way, and the
last fifth was the only part that would have been any use to him.

He was thirteen.

He went to sleep warm, on two blankets, under a boarded wall, in a room
that somebody upstairs had the dimensions of.

*Act 2, year 1, month 3, the fifth to the ninth. Ōtoma and the ground
north of it. Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,700 words.*

---

It started on the fifth and it started while he was doing something else,
which is how all of them start and is why he had stopped trusting the ones
that arrived while he was looking.

He had the sheet out because he was doing the road again — the second
half, the six days across, which he thought was a day and a half generous
and wanted to see whether it was two — and he read down past the top of it
to get to the part he wanted.

And he stopped.

He was not aware of having stopped. What he was aware of, about three
seconds later, was that his eyes were at the top of the sheet again and he
had not decided to put them there.

---

He read it through properly.

> *Ōtoma to Sarugaya, thence to the crossing below Hane.*
>
> *1 case. Ab. 90 kan gross. Sealed at origin.*
>
> *11 days. 4,000, of which 1,000 on acceptance.*
>
> *Consignor seals. No weighing required at transfer. Hand over against
> the counterpart, which will be produced.*

Nothing.

He read it again.

Nothing again, and then, at the bottom, the thing.

*That rings.*

He sat back on his heels with the sheet on his knee and put it plainly to
himself, which was the first thing he did with anything.

*I have read that before.*

---

He went at it for four days and this is what four days of it looked like,
and it is worth writing down because it is the only time in his life he
ever failed at this and knew he was failing while he did it.

**The fifth.** He covered it with his hand, a line at a time, and read the
lines on their own, and none of them rang. Then he took the hand off and
read the whole thing and it rang.

*So it is not a line.*

**The sixth.** He read it out loud, which he did not do, standing on the
north ground with nobody in about a mile, and it rang worse.

*So it is not the look of it either.*

He was pleased with that for about a minute, because two negatives is
progress, and then he stopped being pleased because he had got the same
result twice from two directions and both of them were exclusions and he
was not any closer to a thing.

**The seventh.** He copied it out. Whole, in his own hand, on the back of
a page, exact, with the punctuation.

He read the copy.

It did not ring at all.

He sat there with the two of them side by side for about twenty minutes,
the sheet and the copy of the sheet, saying the same words in two hands,
one of which did something to him and one of which did nothing.

*That is a fact about me and not about the sheet.*

He wrote that down because it was true. He did not know what to do with it
and it stayed exactly that useful for three years.

**The eighth.** He did the thing he should have done first and did not do
first because it was the largest, and got nothing out of it either, which
is the part that mattered.

*Where have I read it.*

*I have read about sixty freight dockets and about forty of the other
kind and I could tell you what was in most of them, and I have never once
had to try, because I do not go and get things out. They are there or they
are not.*

*This one is there and I cannot get at it.*

He sat with that on the north ground for about two hours.

*I do not have a place where things go that I cannot get at. That is not a
room I have got.*

---

There is a thing in the middle of those four days that belongs to her and
he has almost none of it.

She asked on the sixth, in the evening: "Is it the sixth?"

"It's the sixth."

"You said the sixth."

He was on the ground with the sheet on his knee and he did not look up,
and he said, "Tomorrow," and she said, "Right," and went away.

She asked again on the eighth, differently, which he registered and did
not do anything with.

"Are you stuck on something?"

"No."

"You've read that four times since I sat down."

"It's a road sheet."

"Right," she said.

That was the whole of it. He put nothing about it in the book, because
nothing had happened, and what he had of it afterwards was that she had
asked him twice about the frame and he had said *tomorrow* once and had
not said anything the second time, and that she had stopped asking.

*She has stopped asking.*

He had that on the ninth, in about a second and a half, and then he lost
it, because he was on the sheet.

What she had of it was rather more, and he did not have any of that for a
year and a half.

She had watched him be slow at things about nine times and every one of
them had come out, and this one had gone four days and had not, and she
had asked him a direct question about it and been told no. She put a stick
on the fire and looked at the top of his head across it and did the
arithmetic she did on everything, and what she came away with went into
the place where she kept the things there was no paper for.

**It was the second one.**

The first was on a page in his hand that said *nine seconds* and
*eleven*, and she had been carrying it since the fifth of the second
month, and she never once put the two of them on the same line, because
they did not look like the same kind of thing and would not for a long
time.

---

**The ninth.** He gave it up, and gave it up properly, which was harder
than any of the four days.

---

The giving up took an evening and it went like this, and she was forty
feet off doing something with the mule and did not come near him, which he
did not notice and was the reason it went as well as it did.

*Set it out.*

*One. The sheet rings.*

*Two. It is not a line, not the look, and it is not the words, because the
words in my hand do not do it.*

*Three. Therefore it is the whole thing, or the hand, or the day I am
having.*

He stopped there and made himself go back, because *the day I am having*
had gone in as a possibility and he had not tested it and had put it third
on the list where it could not do any damage, and that is the sort of
thing he did not permit.

*Test it.*

He got two other dockets out — the Kanaya one and one from the eleventh
month — and read all three in a row, four times, in different orders.

The two did nothing. The one did it every time.

*So it is not the day.*

*Good. That is a real exclusion and it is the only one of the four that
cost me anything.*

---

And then he got to the part that stopped him, and he got to it honestly.

*What is the question.*

He put the pencil down.

*Because I have not got one.*

*If I had a question I could go and answer it. **Which docket. Which
month. Which clerk.** Any of those is work and I can do work.*

*I have not got one of those. I have got: it rings.*

*And **it rings** is not a question and it is not a finding. It is a
feeling, and a feeling is a thing I have about a wording, and I have never
once in my life written down a thing I felt about a wording, because there
is nowhere to put it and nothing to do with it and no way to be wrong
about it, and a thing you cannot be wrong about is not information.*

He sat with the book on his knee for a long time.

*I have got two columns. Operational and ordinary. Operational is what
happened and ordinary is what it cost.*

*This is neither of those. This is what I think about something.*

---

He put it in anyway, and the putting it in is the entry.

He turned to the operational column, because operational was where things
about work went and this was about work, and he ruled the line and dated
it.

Then he sat with the pencil above the line for about a minute and a half,
which for him is an enormous amount of time to spend on four words.

He wrote:

> *9/3. The Sarugaya sheet rings. Read before. Cannot place it.*

And then, because he was not going to have a sentence in that book that
looked like a finding and was not one, he put the mark beside it — the
small one, the one he had been putting beside made numbers since the sixth
month of the year before last, the one that said *this is a number I have
made and not counted.*

He looked at it after he had done it.

*That mark is for numbers.*

*And that is not a number, and I have just used it anyway, and the reason
I used it is that it is the only thing I have got that means **do not
build on this**.*

He left it.

---

There is one more thing in those four days and it is nine words in his own
head and he never wrote it down.

He shut the book on the ninth and sat looking at the fire and had the
thought that people have when a thing has beaten them, except that he had
never had it before and it arrived in his voice and not anybody else's.

*There is no question under it.*

*So there is nothing to do.*

And he was right, given what he had, and what he had was everything except
the one piece of it, and the one piece of it was three feet away under the
boards in a frame he did not open for six more days, in a pile he did not
open at all.

*Act 2, year 1, month 3, the tenth. Ōtoma. Nozaki Uzuki, age eleven. POV:
Uzuki. ~2,000 words.*

---

He got the frame out on the tenth and it took him forty minutes and she
watched every one of them.

He did not say anything about the four days. He came in in the morning and
moved the tin and lifted two of the boards and got down and brought it up
and put it on the floor and said, "Right," and undid it, and the whole of
the apology was that he did it before he ate.

She noticed that and did not say anything about it either.

---

There were more than she had expected.

That is the first thing and it is worth putting down properly, because she
had a picture of it and the picture was wrong, and she had built the
picture off four dockets she had seen and a boy who talked about work the
way other people talk about weather.

She had thought: a dozen.

What came out of the frame was a stack about two fingers thick, tied in
four bundles with the tie going round the short way, and every one of them
flat, and none of them creased at the corner.

"How many is that?"

"Sixty-one freight," he said. "Nine of the other. Two I didn't take."

"You keep the ones you didn't take?"

He looked at her as if she had asked why he kept his boots.

"They're the same information."

---

She sat down on the floor with the stack in front of her and he went and
sat six feet off with the Sarugaya sheet, and that was the arrangement for
the whole day and neither of them proposed it.

She started at the bottom, which was the oldest, and she went up.

And she did it wrong on purpose, or what he would have called wrong, and

*I am not going to read the job. I have got sixty-one jobs and I have not

*And what he had got was eleven years of names stopping. He had got two

*Act 2, year 1, month 3, the eleventh to the fourteenth. Ōtoma. Tadayoshi
Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~2,000 words.*

---

He found it on the eleventh and he was not looking for it.

That has to be said first, because he had given the other thing up on the
ninth and he had given it up properly and he did not go back to it, and if
he had gone back to it he would not have found this, and he never did work
out that the two were the same object.

What he was doing on the eleventh was the ordinary thing.

---

The ordinary thing was this, and he had done it before every job he had
ever taken.

*Reduce it.*

*A sheet is paper and paper gets wet and paper gets taken off you. So
before you go you take the sheet and you get it down to what you would
have to have if there were no sheet, and you carry that, and then the
sheet is a convenience and not a requirement.*

He had done it forty times. It took about two minutes. You read the thing
and you strike out everything that is furniture and what is left is the
job.

He sat down with the Sarugaya sheet on the twelfth morning and struck it
out.

> *Ōtoma to Sarugaya, thence to the crossing below Hane.*
>
> *1 case. Ab. 90 kan gross. Sealed at origin.*
>
> *11 days. 4,000, of which 1,000 on acceptance.*
>
> *Consignor seals. No weighing required at transfer. Hand over against
> the counterpart, which will be produced.*

It came down to nine words.

*Sarugaya, then the crossing below Hane. One case. Eleven days.*

He looked at the nine words.

*That is the job.*

*That is the whole job and I have not lost one thing off it. I could do
that with the sheet in a river.*

---

He was pleased for about four seconds and then he was not, and the reason
he was not is the only reason that ever stopped him being pleased, which
is that a thing had come out too clean.

*That is never nine words.*

He put the sheet down.

*The Kanaya one was four crates and a roll and eighteen days and it came
down to about twenty, and the twenty had **Ōtsuka's counterpart at the
Kanaya end and not the yard's** in it, and if I had lost that I would
have had to walk back.*

*The Hazaki one had a name in it.*

*Every single one of them has had one thing in it that had to be there,
and it is not always the same thing and that is the point of doing it.*

He got the frame back out.

---

He did nine of them on the twelfth afternoon and it took him about fifty
minutes and he did them the same way every time: read it, strike it,
look at what was left, and then take one more thing out and see whether
the job broke.

It broke nine times out of nine.

> *Anazawa, 26/9. Take out **the west lane** and there are four shutter-
> makers in Anazawa.*
>
> *Kurihama, 9/1. Take out **before the tide** and the crossing is not
> there.*
>
> *The eleventh-month one. Take out **the second man** and I go in

>
> *Sakabe, 11/1. Take out **upstairs** and it is a different building.*

He sat on the boards with nine dockets round him in an arc.

*Every one of them has got a load-bearing item. One. Sometimes two. Take
it out and you have to go back and ask, and going back and asking is the
thing the whole document exists to stop.*

*That is not a style. That is what a job sheet is.*

Then he picked up the Sarugaya one again and took things out of it until
there was nothing left, and the job did not break at any point.

---

He took *thence to the crossing below Hane* out.

*I would have to ask where it goes.*

Except that he would not, because *hand over against the counterpart,
which will be produced* meant somebody would produce a counterpart and a
counterpart says where, and the sheet said so on its own face.

He took *one case* out.

*I would not know what I was carrying.*

Except that it was ninety kan gross and sealed at origin, so it was one
thing or it was crated as one thing, and either way it went on a frame and
he would find out by looking at it.

He took *eleven days* out.

And that one nearly worked, and he sat with it for four minutes, and then
it did not work either, because four thousand of which a thousand on
acceptance is a fee with a shape and the shape says how long, and any
carrier in the country would read it the same.

He went round it four times and could not make it break.

---

He sat back.

*Every part of this refers to another part.*

*There is nothing in it that is only in it once.*

He said it out loud, on the boards, in an empty room, which he did about
twice a year:

"There's nothing in it you'd have to remember."

---

Then he did the thing that took the day and a half, and it is the best
work he did in the whole of the third month and it went nowhere at all.

*Test it the other way.*

*If it is on purpose, I ought not to be able to do it.*

He got a blank page and he wrote the Kanaya job out as a docket, from
memory, as if he were the clerk, and he tried to write it with nothing in
it you would remember.

It took him forty minutes.

He read it back.

> *Ōtoma to the Kanaya. 4 crates + 1 roll. 18 days. 1,400. Counterpart at
> the receiving end.*

*Ōtsuka.*

He had not written the name. He had written *the receiving end*, and it
was still Ōtsuka, because the Kanaya has one yard and everybody knows
whose it is, and a person reading that sheet in a room in another country
would get to Ōtsuka in about a day.

He tried again and took the Kanaya out and put *a river yard, eleven days
east*, and read it back, and there are four river yards eleven days east
and one of them takes crates.

He tried a third time and produced something that had nothing in it at
all, and read it back, and it was not a docket. You could not do the job
off it. He had not made it forgettable, he had made it useless, and those
are different and he could see exactly where the line was and could not
walk along it.

He put the pencil down at about eight in the evening on the thirteenth.

*I cannot do it.*

*I can write a sheet that tells you everything and I can write a sheet
that tells you nothing and I cannot write the one in the middle, and the
one in the middle is the only one that is any good, and somebody has done
it and it is sitting on the floor.*

---

He sat with that for a long time and what he arrived at is four sentences
and he wrote all four of them down.

> *14/3. The Sarugaya sheet.*
>
> *It is complete. I can do the whole job off it and I cannot take one
> thing out of it.*
>
> *And there is no item in it that is in it once. Nothing to carry.
> Nothing you would remember.*
>
> *A badly written one leaves the thing out by accident and you go back
> and ask. I do not have to go back and ask. So it is not badly written.*

And then the fifth, which was not part of the finding and which he wrote
anyway because it was true:

> *That is good work.*

He looked at it on the page.

*Somebody sat down and did that on purpose and it took them a while,
because I have just spent a day and a half failing at it and I am the best
person I have ever met at a piece of paper.*

*That is a skill. Somebody has that skill and uses it on freight
paperwork.*

He was not uneasy. It is important that he was not, and the reason he was
not is that he was pleased, and he was pleased in the specific way he was
pleased about three times a year, which was the way you are pleased when
you meet the work of somebody who is properly good at the thing you are
good at.

---

There is one more line and it is the one that sends him to the frame on
the fifteenth, and it is entirely correct and it is the beginning of five
days of looking in the wrong place.

*A person does not learn to do that on one sheet.*

*If somebody can write like that, they have written like that before, and
they have written like that more than once, and the ones before this are
in yards.*

*And I have got seventy-two of them under the boards.*

He shut the book.

---

He had now done this three times.

He had watched a man route a child through four hands in the twelfth month
and thought *he is doing what I do.* He had stood in a yard on the sixth
of the second month and watched a man describe an animal for money and
thought *this is a man who knows a thing and is describing it accurately.*
And he had sat on the boards on the fourteenth of the third month and
written *that is good work* under a sheet of freight paperwork.

Three men, three skills, three correct assessments.

And not once, in any of the three, the other half of it — which is that a
skill is for something, and that the thing it is for is a fact about the
person and not about the skill, and that he had been standing in front of
the answer to that question all three times and had never once asked it,
because his eye went where it went and it had been going there since he
was eight years old in a workshop being told his own work was two
thousandths out.

Nobody said it to him.

There was nobody in a position to.

*Act 2. A room eleven paces by seven. A boy, age thirteen. POV: the boy.

They brought a stool in, which had not happened.

Two of them brought it and one of them put it down about four feet from
the wall he sat against and a bit to the left, and then they both went out
and the door stayed open, which also had not happened.

He looked at the stool.

*That is for somebody to sit on.*

*Nobody sits down in here.*

---

The man came in about a minute later and he had a book.

He was not one of the two and Kimimaro had not seen him before. He was
somewhere past forty and he was clean and he had a book under his arm with

Then he said, "Stand up," and Kimimaro stood up.

---

What happened after that took eleven minutes and he can give you the whole

He opened his mouth.

The man got up off the stool and came over with the book shut on his

Kimimaro had that at the time, at the moment it happened, and put it away
whole.

He put both hands out.

He put it away again.

*Not sore yet.*

enough.

*Act 2, year 1, month 3, the fifteenth to the eighteenth. Ōtoma.
Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,600 words.*

---

He got the frame out again on the fifteenth and this time he knew exactly
what he was looking for, which is the part that makes it worse.

He had it as a sentence before he lifted the boards.

*Somebody who writes like that has written like that before.*

*So: find one.*

*And if I find one, then I have got two, and two is a line, and a line is
a thing you can follow.*

---

He set it up properly, because he set everything up properly.

He cleared the boards and put the four bundles out in front of him and
untied them and made two piles, and he made the two piles before he read
anything, because sorting is the first thing and he had known that since
he was nine.

On the left: the nine, and the two he had turned down.

On the right: the sixty-one.

And then he sat back on his heels and looked at the two piles and did the
thing that decided the next three years, and it took him about ninety
seconds, and he did it out loud in his head in order like everything else.

---

*Which pile.*

*Start with what I have got. I have got a sheet that is written to be
forgettable. Somebody sat down and took the memorable parts out of it on
purpose, and taking the memorable parts out of a document is work, and you
do not do work for nothing.*

*So: why would you.*

*Because there is something in it you do not want remembered.*

He was pleased with that and he was right about it, and it was the last
correct step he took for five days.

*And a thing you do not want remembered is a thing that matters.*

*So it is a job that matters.*

He looked at the left-hand pile.

*Which is that one.*

---

He did the freight objection because he did every objection, and this is
the part that is creditable and it is why it took five days and not one.

*Unless it is in the freight.*

He sat with it.

*Sixty-one of them. And I have read all sixty-one and I have never once
found one strange.*

*So either there is not one in there, or there is one in there and it did
not look strange to me.*

He went at the second half of that, and he got most of the way, and where
he stopped is exactly here:

*Freight is the most standard writing there is. Four clerks in this town
write the whole of it and they all write it the same and they have all
been doing it for years, and it is dates and weights and yards and it is
the same eleven phrases in the same order sixty-one times.*

*So a strange one would stand out in freight more than anywhere. A strange
one in a pile of identical ones is the easiest thing in the world to spot,

He had that whole and it took about two seconds and it went nowhere,
because the counterpart *would* be produced, and a counterpart is a name
and a place and a hand, and the sheet had said so on its face, and the
sheet had been right about everything else for twenty-five days.

"It'll be on the counterpart," he said.

"Right," she said.

She put a stick on the fire.

They left at half past three in the morning on the twenty-eighth to be at
Ochiai for first light on the twenty-ninth, and the mule went well and the
road was dry and the weather held, and it was, by every measure he had and
every measure she had, an ordinary start to an ordinary job.
