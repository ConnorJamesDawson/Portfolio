# Chapter 8, Scene 7 — Slowly, And Worse

*Act 1. A room. A boy, age twelve. POV: the boy. ~1,390 words.*

---

The one on the right hand had been out since the night he started.

He had never taken it back in. That was the whole of the point of it: it
was the one that had not been broken, and if he put it away he would have
nothing to hold the other one against.

It was about an inch and a half and it came up between the two middle
knuckles and it caught on everything.

It caught on the blanket while there was a blanket. It caught on the mat.
It caught on the inside of the bowl about twice a week and he had learned
to eat with the hand turned. It had gone through the skin over the top of
it three times and grown a hard shine on the end where he had knocked it
on the wall.

He kept it.

*It is not for anything except being the other one.*

---

The left one came back at a hundred and seventy-eight.

He did not know that on the day, because a thing that is growing does not
tell you the day it stops. What he had was that at about a hundred and
sixty it was still getting longer and at about a hundred and ninety it was
not, and so he took the middle of that and put it down as a hundred and
seventy-eight, and marked in his head that it was a made number and not a
counted one.

*A hundred and seventy-eight bowls.*

*Three bowls is a day, when they give three.*

*They have not given three since — they have given two some days. I do
not know how many days were twos. So it is a hundred and seventy-eight
and it is more days than a hundred and seventy-eight divided by three,
and I cannot say how many more.*

He put his hands together.

---

He had thought about doing it for about nine days and had put it off, and
he knew he was putting it off, and had not been able to work out why a
person puts a thing off.

So he did it in the dark, sitting up, with his back on the wall, and he
put the backs of his hands together with the two of them side by side and
felt along both at once with his thumbs, the way you would feel two sticks
to see which was the longer.

*Shorter.*

He did it again.

*The left one is shorter. It is about a fingernail shorter and it is
shorter every time I do it and I have done it four times.*

Then he did the other thing, which he had not planned to do and which he
knew the answer to before he had finished doing it.

*And it is not straight.*

*The right one goes out of the hand and it goes where the hand is
pointing. The left one goes out and it goes out to the side, about that
much, and it has a turn in it near the top.*

*The right one is smooth all the way along and the left one has got a
place on it where it changes.*

He sat with his hands together in the dark for a long time.

---

*So it came back.*

*It came back and it took a hundred and seventy-eight and it is shorter
and it is bent.*

*That is the answer.*

He went round it three times looking for a way for it to be a different
answer, and there was not one, and the reason there was not one was the
right hand, which had never been broken, and which was straight and long
and exactly what it had been on the night he put it out.

*The one that has not been broken is the good one.*

*So it is the breaking. It is not the hand and it is not the place and it
is not that I did it wrong. It is that a thing that is broken comes back
smaller and it comes back bent, and if I break it again it will come back
smaller and more bent, and I know that because I have got the one that
was not broken and it is right there and it is fine.*

He put his hands in his lap.

---

*He wanted to know how long it takes to come back.*

*And the answer is: a hundred and seventy-eight bowls, and worse.*

*That is what I have got for him. That is the whole of what I got in a
hundred and seventy-eight bowls, and it is the thing he stopped writing
for, and it is bad.*

He had never in his life been in a position to be disappointing to
anybody, because nobody had ever wanted anything off him except that he
hold still, and holding still is a thing you can do.

This was new and he had no shape for it at all.

*He came a long way. He came from somewhere and his shoes were quiet and
he did not have the passage on him and he was in the room for four hundred
and he asked eleven and he wrote after nine.*

*And the one he did not write after was that one.*

*And I have got it now and it is: slowly, and worse.*

*That is not a good thing to be.*

---

He sat against the wall with the bowl-count going in the back of his head
the way it always went, and beside it, since the six-door, the other one,
which had no name and which he could not stop.

*Eleven days from the twice.*

*I have had thirty-eight and then all of this and I do not know what the
number is any more but it is bigger than thirty-eight and it is not
eleven.*

*So I have had longer than him.*

*And I do not know how much longer there is.*

---

He did not decide to lie.

The thought was not available to him. He had heard perhaps two hundred
sentences in two years and none of them had ever been a false one that he
could identify, and the machinery for making one was not in him and would
not have been usable if it had been. What he had instead was arithmetic
and a wall and two hands, and he used them.

*The answer is bad.*

*The answer is bad because the number is big and the thing that comes back
is worse.*

*So make the number small and make the thing that comes back better.*

He looked at his left hand in the dark, which he could not see.

*It might get better. I do not know that it gets worse every time. I know
it got worse once. Once is one.*

*And if I do it again and it comes back at a hundred and sixty, then it
gets better, and if it comes back at two hundred then it does not, and
either way I will know, and either way when he asks me I will have a
number instead of I don't know.*

*And if it gets better I will do it again and again until the number is a
good one.*

**Nobody had told him that a thing gets better if you do it a great many
times. He had no word for practising and had never watched anybody do
it.**

*He worked it out from a hand.*

---

He put one out of the back of the left hand, in the same place, between
the two middle knuckles, where the skin was thicker now than it had been
and where there was a ridge under it that had not been there in the sixth
month.

It hurt more than the first time and more than the second and he had
expected that and it was not information.

He put his right hand over it.

*Do not make a noise.*

He broke it off.

---

He put the piece on the mat beside the first one, so that there would be
two of them, and so that when there was a third he could put all three in
a line and look at them together.

Then he lay down and waited for the bowl.

*One.*
