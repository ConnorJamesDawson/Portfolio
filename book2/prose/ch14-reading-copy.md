# Chapter 14 — The Speech

*Book two. Act two. Year 1, month 2.*
*11 scenes, 17,880 words. Tadayoshi ×5 · Uzuki ×4 · Kimimaro ×2.*



*Act 2, year 1, month 2, the third to the fifth. Ōtoma. Tadayoshi Ruri,
age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,700 words.*

---

The docket came on the third of the second month and it was the first
thing on the boards in eighteen days, and it was freight.

> *Ōtoma to the Kanaya. 4 crates + 1 roll.*
>
> *18 days. 1,400.*

He read it in the yard with Ōtsuka four feet away and did not say anything
about it, and went up the ladder and sat down and did the thing he had
been trying not to do for a month, which was to put it in the line.

*Fourteen hundred.*

*Nine hundred and eleven hundred were the two contracts in the first
month. So this is more than either of them.*

He sat with that for a while.

*A courier run is more than a contract.*

*And I have got eleven words for that and none of them are the true one,
and the true one is a sentence I am not going to write down.*

He did not write it down.

---

Then he did the other sum, and the other sum did not close, and it took
him about four minutes to find out how badly.

*Four crates and a roll. Call it two hundred and thirty pounds, and I will
know on the day.*

*Eighteen days, nine each way, in the second month.*

*Me at fifty-one for nine days uphill in the twelfth was fine and I have
got the numbers for it.*

*Two hundred and thirty is not fine. Two hundred and thirty is three
loads, and three loads is twenty-seven days of walking and the docket says
eighteen.*

He put the pencil down.

*And there are two of us.*

---

That was the part that had been coming for a fortnight and had not
arrived until it was on a piece of paper.

He went at it honestly.

*She can carry. She carried four ranks of wood under an eave in the tenth
month and she has told me about it twice and both times she was being
careful not to sound pleased.*

*Call her forty pounds over nine days, which is generous, and she will do
it and will not say anything and I will find out about it on the sixth day
when something goes.*

*Fifty-one and forty is ninety-one. Two thirty over ninety-one is two and
a half trips.*

*So it is still no.*

He sat on the boards with the docket in his hand.

*It has stopped being one person's job because there are two of us, and
two of us makes it harder and not easier, and that is not a thing I
expected and I should have.*

---

He got to the answer on the fourth in about ninety seconds, which was
fast, and then spent the rest of the day not liking it.

*Buy an animal.*

He went round it four ways.

*A horse does eleven miles an hour on a road and thirty in a day loaded
and I would have the whole of this run done in six days each way.*

*And a horse eats about a fifth of its own value in a year, and it wants a
road, and it is useless the moment I leave one, and I leave roads.*

*And there is one boy of eleven and one girl of eleven and a horse in this
country, and everybody who sees it will describe it, and they will
describe it accurately, because a horse is the easiest thing in the world
to describe.*

He crossed it out and did not put a case under it because the case was
obvious.

*A mule does not do any of that. A mule does about what I do and carries
eight times what I carry and eats grass and goes up a track a horse turns
round on.*

*And nobody has ever described a mule to anybody.*

---

He costed it that evening and did it properly, in the ordinary column,
with a rule under it.

> *Mule, sound, young — ask 6,000-7,000. Kuriya's man got a fourteen-year-
> old at Nakadō in the 6th mo. and that was a fourteen-year-old.*
>
> *Feed on the road: negligible in the 5th mo., call it 25 a day in the
> 2nd. 18 days = 450.*
>
> *Headcollar, rope, a bag. 300.*
>
> *Against: 1,400 on this one and it does not close without one.*

He looked at the bottom line for a while.

*It does not pay for itself on this run and it does not pay for itself on
the next four.*

*And I am not buying it for this run.*

That was true and it was the first entirely true thing he had thought
about the mule, and he did not follow it any further, because following it
would have meant asking what he was buying it for, and the answer to that
was standing about six feet away going through a pack for the third time.

---

She asked on the fourth in the evening and he said yes in about two
seconds.

"Am I coming?"

"Yes."

She looked at him.

"That's it?"

"Yes."

*Because the other thing is a room in an inn at Kurihama with a girl in it
who sat by the door in the dark for six hours with a knife on her knees,
and I have written that down and there is no solution under it, and
eighteen days is nine times two days.*

He did not say any of that either.

---

He did say one thing, on the morning of the fifth, going out, and he said
it because it was operational and he said everything that was operational.

"You'll carry about forty. That's the top of it. If it's forty-five you'll
do it and you won't say, and then on about the sixth day something goes
and I've lost four days."

"All right."

"So when it's too much, say."

She said, "All right," in the flat way, and he had been travelling with
her for fifty days by then and had learned exactly nothing about what her
*all right* meant, which was not a thing he had noticed yet.

---

They went out of Ōtoma on the fifth at about six in the morning and did
eleven miles, and the pack he had made up for her was thirty-eight pounds
because he had weighed it twice and taken two things out.

He did not tell her that.

At about four in the afternoon she was walking about a foot behind him on
the left, which she had done since the twentieth of the first month
without either of them arranging it, and she said, "How much is a mule?"

"Six. Maybe seven."

"Thousand?"

"Yes."

She did not say anything for about a quarter of a mile.

Then she said, "That's a lot," and he said, "It is," and that was the
whole of that conversation, and he thought no more about it at all, and
she thought about it for eleven miles and most of the next day.

*Act 2, year 1, month 2, the fifth and sixth. The Kanaya road. Nozaki
Uzuki, age eleven. POV: Uzuki. ~1,900 words.*

---

She had been going to ask since the seventeenth of the twelfth month and
she had rewritten it about forty times, and in the end she said it badly
and at the wrong moment, which is how she said most things that mattered.

They were sitting either side of a fire on the fifth and he had the book
out and she waited until he shut it, which she had learned in the first
week, and then said it.

"Will you teach me to fight?"

"No."

---

She had four things ready for *no* and she did not get to use any of them,
because he put the book inside his coat and looked at the fire for about
four seconds and then said something she had not prepared for at all.

"I haven't got a thing to teach."

"You —"

"No, listen, because it's not modesty and I'm not doing the thing where
you have to ask twice." He was not looking at her. He was doing the thing
he did when he was being accurate, which was to look at a middle distance
and talk slightly too fast. "A style is a set of things in an order that
somebody worked out and can hand you. I haven't got one. I've got about
forty pieces off about twenty people and I kept them because they worked
on the day and there's nothing under them. There isn't a first lesson.
There isn't a second one. If I try to give you it in an order I'll make
the order up and it'll be wrong and you'll build on it."

She sat with that.

*He has thought about this before.*

*He has thought about this before and got to the end of it and put it
down, and I have just asked him a question he already had the answer to,
and the answer is no, and it is not about me.*

"So that's no," she said.

"That's no to teaching."

And then, about four seconds later:

"Stand up."

---

He put her on the ground eleven times in about four minutes.

She counted the first four and then stopped counting because counting was
taking up room she needed for other things, and got the eleven off him
afterwards.

It was not what she had expected and it was not what anybody would expect,
and the part she had not expected was that it did not hurt very much at
the time. That came later. What it was at the time was *fast* — she would
be standing, and then there would be a thing about her balance that she
had not agreed to, and then the ground would be against her shoulder or her
hip or the side of her face, and the whole of it took about a second and
she never once saw how.

The fourth one knocked the wind out of her.

She lay on her side in the grass making the noise you make and could not
get a breath, and he crouched down about three feet away and did not touch
her and did not say anything, and waited.

When she got it back he said, "Again."

She got up.

---

He did not go easy.

She was clear about that at the time and she never once thought otherwise,
and she found out afterwards — much later, and not from him — that he had
been going at about a quarter of what he could do, and that a quarter of
what he could do was the lowest setting he had.

He was not gentle and he was not rough. He was *exact*. He did the same
thing eleven times and it worked eleven times and there was no more
violence in it than there needed to be, and about the eighth one she
understood that he was not trying to hurt her and was not trying not to,
and that both of those would have been decisions and there had not been
one.

At the end of it he said: "Nine seconds."

"What?"

"The last one. You had nine seconds on your feet. The first one was two."

She stood there with her hand on her ribs.

"Is nine good?"

"It's better than two."

And he went and sat down by the fire and got the book out, and she
understood that this was the whole of the encouragement available and that
he had given her everything he had.

---

She sat down on the other side and did the work, because there was nothing
else to do with an evening.

*Nine seconds.*

*He is counting how long I stay up.*

She turned it over.

*And that is a number and it will go up and it is a fair number and I
could get to twenty by the fourth month if I worked at it.*

*And it is the wrong number.*

She stopped there, because she had got somewhere and she did not know how,
and she made herself go back and find the road.

*Because it does not matter how long I stay up. If we are ever in a place
where a person like him is trying to put me down, the difference between
two seconds and twenty seconds is nothing at all. Both of those are dead.*

*So what is the right number.*

---

She got it at about the point where the fire went down and she had to
decide whether to put anything else on it, and when she got it she sat
very still.

*He did the same thing eleven times.*

She went back through them, all eleven, and she had them, because she had
been under each one and being under a thing is a very good way of learning
its shape.

*The same foot. The same hand on the same side. The same place on my arm.
Eleven times, and the last one was the first one and I still could not see
it coming.*

*So he has used one thing.*

*And he has got about forty.*

She put a stick on the fire.

*So the number is not nine seconds. The number is one.*

*And when it is two, that is a real thing, because it means the first one
has stopped working and he has had to go and get another one.*

---

She did not tell him.

She thought about telling him for about half an hour, and she worked out
why she was not going to, and the reason was not that she wanted a secret.

*Because he would take it seriously.*

*He would take it completely seriously and he would think about it for a
day and then he would start varying it on purpose so that the number went
up faster, and the number would stop being a measurement and start being a
thing he was doing for me.*

*And then I would have nothing.*

She lay down with her back to the fire.

*I have got one instrument and it works because he does not know it
exists.*

*Which is the same as the sacking. And the same as the bowl.*

---

She waited about two hours.

She waited until he was asleep — which she could tell now, off the
breathing, and had been able to since about the ninth day — and then she
got up and went about forty feet, behind a bank, out of the light, and sat
down and put her hands on herself.

The ribs first, which were the fourth one, and the hip, which was the
seventh and the ninth and was the worst of it.

*Little. Stop. Wait.*

She did about thirty and came off, because thirty is what you do for a
bruise and ninety is what you do for a thing that is going to matter, and
she had learned the difference off Tokuji's back in a store at the
Kadono-dera in the tenth month.

*And the stopping is still going off about one in seven.*

*I have done this four times since the eighth month of last year and I am
worse at it every single time and there is nobody to ask.*

She sat back on her heels in the dark and waited the forty seconds,
because you cannot tell during, and then let herself have an opinion.

*Better. Not fixed. Fine.*

---

She sat behind the bank for a bit longer than she needed to.

*He is going to do it again tomorrow.*

*And I am going to ask him to, because that was the arrangement and I made
it, and I am going to be doing this behind a bank every night for a
year.*

She looked at her hands.

*And he is never going to know, because if he knew he would stop, and if
he stopped I would not get any better, and I have got about fourteen
hundred days.*

*So the whole of it is: he does the thing that hurts me, and I fix it, and
neither of us says anything.*

She got up and went back and lay down where she had been.

*That is not a complaint.*

She turned that over once to be sure, because she was not going to let
herself get away with anything, and it held.

*It is not a complaint. I asked. He said no and then he did it anyway,
which is the only kind of yes he has got.*

*I would just like there to be one person alive who could tell me whether
I am doing the stopping right.*

She went to sleep with her hand flat on her hip, which she did not notice
doing and did every night for six years.

*Act 2. A room eleven paces by seven. A boy, age thirteen. POV: the boy.
~1,800 words.*

---

He had been without a clock for about a month and he knew it was about a
month only because he had counted the bowls, and the bowls came at no time
at all, so *about a month* was the most he had and he was not happy with
it.

He had tried the light and the noise and his own sleeping and all three
had failed and he had the reasons written in his head where things went.

So on a day he could not name he was lying on his side facing the wall
doing nothing, which was most of what he did now, and he heard it.

---

It was somebody screaming.

That is what it was and he identified it inside a second, because he was
not stupid and he had heard people make noises before, and the
identification did not go anywhere and did not connect to anything and he
did not return to it.

**What he noticed about it was that it was long.**

*That one went on.*

*I have heard six or seven since I came here and they were short and they
were at nothing in particular. That one was long and it went up and then
it stayed.*

He sat up.

---

He heard the next one some time after and he was ready for it and got
three things.

*It is the same one. It is the same voice, and I know that because of the
place it goes to at the top and where it stops.*

*It is north and above. Not directly. Through two walls at least, because
it comes in flat and there is no edge on it.*

*And it was long again.*

He put his hand on the floor.

*So there are two of them the same.*

*Two is not a line. Two is any line you like.*

He had said that to himself in the ninth month of the year before about a
piece of his own hand and it had been true then and it was true now, and
he had never been more pleased to have a rule ready.

---

He gave it eleven.

That took what he afterwards worked out was somewhere between three and
four days and he did not use one of them for anything, and he was careful
in a way he had not needed to be careful since the bowl.

He counted the gaps in the only unit he had, which was breathing, and he
knew breathing was bad and used it anyway because there was nothing else.

*One. Then about two hundred and ninety.*

*Two. Then about three hundred and ten.*

*Three. Then about two hundred and eighty.*

He sat with those three and did not like them and made himself go on.

*Four. Three hundred and forty. — That one is out.*

*Five. Three hundred.*

*Six. Two hundred and ninety.*

*Seven. Three hundred and five.*

He got to eleven and laid the ten gaps out in his head in the order they
had come, which was the way he laid out anything.

*Nine of them are between two eighty and three ten. One is three forty and
one is two forty.*

*And the two forty was the one where I lost count in the middle because
somebody walked past the door and I have already put that down as a bad
reading.*

*So: nine good, one out, one spoiled.*

---

He went at the one that was out, because you go at the one that is out.

*Three forty is sixty over. That is not noise. Noise is ten and twenty.*

He turned it over for a while and got it, and getting it was the largest
piece of work he had done since the sixth month.

*It is not sixty over. It is thirty short of two of them.*

*So that one is not a long gap. That is a missed one.*

He put his hand flat.

*The thing is happening at a regular interval and one of them did not
happen and the interval did not move.*

*A thing that goes on happening at the same interval whether or not it
happens is a thing somebody is doing on purpose, to a schedule, the way
the bowl was the same amount every time.*

He did not go any further than that and there was nowhere further for him
to go, because he had never been told one thing about one person in that
building or the last one, and a schedule is a fact about a building.

---

So he had a unit.

He tested it for another day before he would use it, which he did not
have to do and did anyway, and it held.

*Call it one. I have not got a word for it and I do not need one.*

*Nine bad readings in eleven would be no use. Nine good in eleven is a
clock.*

And then he did the thing he had been unable to do for a month, which was
to put something against it.

---

He got the third piece out.

Three and a quarter thumbs, straight the whole way, the one that had given
him the number, and he had carried it inside his shirt since the day they
took him out of the room and had not lost it.

Then he felt along the back of his left hand for the fifth.

*It is coming and I do not know how much of it is done, because I lost the
count when the bowls went and I have not had one since.*

*So the old number is gone. Nine hundred and three was in bowls and I have
not got bowls.*

He sat with that for a while and it did not upset him, and it did not
upset him because he had already had this exact thought about eleven times
in a month and had worn the edge off it.

*But I have not got to know how much is done. I have got to know how fast
it is going.*

*And I can have that from here.*

---

He measured it against his thumb on the day he was sure of the interval,
and he wrote the number in the place where numbers went, and then he did
nothing for four days except count.

He did not sleep well, because the counting had to go on through the
night, and he had never had a task that ran through the night before, and
he found — and this was the thing that surprised him — that he could do
it. He could half-sleep and hold it. He woke for every one of them.

At the end of the four days he measured again.

*It has come a thumb and a bit in about a hundred and ten of them.*

He did the arithmetic on his fingers against the floor, which took him
most of an afternoon and which he did three times because he had got it
wrong once and had found the place.

*And it wants about two more thumbs.*

*So it is about two hundred more.*

---

He put the piece back inside his shirt and lay down on his side facing the
wall and had it.

*I have got a rate.*

*And I have got a number, and the number is in a thing that is still
happening, and it will still be happening tomorrow.*

He went through the whole ladder from the beginning, because the ladder
was the thing he owned and he had not been able to look at it in a month,
and it came out clean.

*Four, three and a half, three and a quarter, and the fifth one will be
three when it is done, because it goes down by a half and I picked the
half and I am keeping it.*

*And after that there are six more.*

*And I know how long one of them is now. In this.*

---

He lay in the dark in a room eleven paces by seven with a drain in the
north corner and was more pleased than he had been since the sixth month
of the year before.

There is nothing else to say about it and it should not be dressed up. He
was thirteen, and he had lost the only instrument he had ever had, and he
had gone out and found another one and tested it eleven times and thrown
out the bad readings and built a rate on it in four days on his own with
nothing.

**It was good work and he knew it was good work and there was nobody to
tell.**

*Two hundred more.*

He put the four pieces in a row on the floor with the bases lined up, by
feel, the straight one at the end.

Then he waited, in the dark, with his hand on the back of his other hand,
until the next one came.

It came.

He counted it.

*Act 2, year 1, month 2, the sixth. A yard at Sakabe. Tadayoshi Ruri, age
eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,750 words.*

---

The man's name was Deguchi and he had four in the yard and three of them
were no good, and Tadayoshi knew that inside about two minutes and was
pleased with himself about it and should not have been.

*The grey one is too old and he knows it.*

*The little one is not a mule, it is a pony with a long face on it.*

*The near hind on the dark one has been done recently and has been done
because it needed doing and not because it was time.*

*And the fourth one is the fourth one.*

He went over to the fourth one.

---

Deguchi came out and did the speech and did not hurry it, and it took
about four minutes.

He did the age off the teeth and said seven and said it in a way that
invited you to look, which Tadayoshi did, and it looked like seven, and he
had no idea what seven looked like and knew he had no idea.

He did the feet, one at a time, picking each one up, and said which of
them had been done by a good man and which by a man in a hurry, and was
honest about the near fore.

He did the shoulder, and said the animal had been bred for more than it
had ever been asked to do, and stood back so you could see it.

He said what it would carry and for how many hours and on what.

He said what it would not do, which was back into anything, and said that
was not a fault in the animal, that was a fault in whoever had had it at
three.

And he said, at the end, what it was worth in the spring against what it
was worth now, and gave a reason for the difference, and the reason was
about grass.

---

Tadayoshi listened to the whole of it and thought it was very good.

That should be put down plainly and without any decoration, because it is
exactly what happened and it is the whole of the scene:

**He thought it was good work.**

*He has gone through it in an order. He has not left anything out. He has
told me two things that are against him and both of them are true, because
I checked the near fore before he said it.*

*This is a man who knows a thing and is describing it accurately to
somebody who does not, and he is not lying to me anywhere I can find.*

*I would take a report like that off anybody.*

He had never heard the speech before in his life. Nobody had ever done it
in front of him and nobody had ever done it *to* him, and there was no
reason on earth for him to hear anything in it except a competent man
being competent, and he did not.

---

Then he put his hand on the shoulder.

He did it without deciding to and he did it because it was the thing he
had been looking at for four minutes and because he wanted to know what it
felt like under the coat, and he was aware afterwards that if anybody had
asked him he could have justified it: *you feel a shoulder, that is how
you know what is under it.*

He put his hand flat on it and left it there for about two seconds.

And Deguchi's voice changed.

---

It changed by about a quarter of a tone and it changed on the fourth word
of the next sentence and the sentence was about hay.

Tadayoshi heard it exactly.

He had spent seven months learning to hear precisely that, off men in
kitchens and men on wharves and a man with a book in a hall above Ōtoma,
and he had it before Deguchi had finished the word, and he knew what it
meant, and he knew what was going to happen next, and he could not do one
single thing about any of it.

*He has got it.*

*He got it off my hand. He did not get it off my face and he did not get
it off anything I said, because I have not said anything. I put my hand on
a shoulder for two seconds and he has got the whole of it.*

*And now there is a man with a thing and a boy who has already said he
wants it, and there is no negotiation after that. There is a number and
then a smaller number and then the first number again, and I pay the first
number, and I have done this before with a boot.*

---

He took his hand off the shoulder and stepped back.

And then he did a thing he had never done in his life, which was to stop.

He did not say a number. He did not say *how much*. He stood in a yard at
Sakabe on the sixth of the second month with about eleven thousand ryō
about his person and a job that did not close without an animal, and he
shut his mouth and did not open it.

*If I say a number now it is the wrong number.*

*I have got no way of getting back to the front of this. There is no
version where I un-put my hand on it.*

He stood there for about four seconds and had nothing.

---

She was standing about four feet behind him and to the left, where she
had been all morning, with the hat on.

She said, "Can I?"

He said, "Yes."

That was the whole of the handover and it took under two seconds and there
were three words in it, and he thought about those three words on and off
for two years and never got them to come out anywhere useful.

He stepped back another pace and put his hands behind him.

---

What happened over the next eleven minutes he could not have described
accurately if his life had depended on it, and it very nearly did four
years later, and he still could not.

He got the shape of it.

*She has not gone near it.*

That was the first thing and it arrived at about the second minute and he
did not know what to do with it.

*She has been talking to him for two minutes and she has not been within
six feet of the animal, and she is looking at it the whole time.*

*I have been in this yard for twenty minutes and I have touched it and she
has not.*

The second thing came at about the fourth minute and he got it wrong.

*She has just contradicted him about the age.*

*She said something about the teeth and he stopped, and I do not know what
she said, because it was three words and it was about a groove.*

*And he has come back with a different number and it is a smaller number
and he did it without arguing.*

---

The third thing came at about the seventh minute and it was the only one
he got entirely right, and it was the one he could not use.

**She was doing the speech.**

Not answering it. *Doing* it — the same shape, the same order, the age and
the feet and the shoulder and what it would do and what it would not, in
the same rhythm, with the pauses in the same places, and she had turned it
round so that every item in it was a reason the animal was worth less than
Deguchi had said.

*She has got his own speech and she is giving it back to him.*

*And it is his speech. It is not the shape of his speech, it is the shape
of a speech, and he is using it too, and they have both got it from
somewhere.*

He looked at the side of her face under the hat.

*Where has she got that from.*

He did not ask. He never asked. It was on the list of things he had not
thought to ask, which by the second month of that year was a long list and
had four items on it that would have changed the whole of the next three
years, and he did not know he was keeping it.

---

Asked seven thousand five hundred.

Paid five thousand two hundred.

And then, when it was agreed and the money was out and Deguchi had put his
hand out, she said one more thing, and the headcollar and rope came with
it, and Tadayoshi did not hear what she said because he was counting.

---

He wrote it up that night and he wrote four things and he was pleased with
three of them.

> *1. She did not touch it. I did. That is the whole of the two thousand
> three hundred.*
>

Then he went to the front of the book and ruled a fresh page in the
ordinary column and put a heading on it.

The heading took him about four seconds.

> *WHAT IS LEFT.*

He put six thousand one hundred and forty under it and drew a line, and it
was the first page in that book that was going to go down.

---

He sat with the book open on his knee for a bit.

*I can head that one.*

He turned to the back, past the list, past the page with five lines on it,
to the page with a name at the top of it and one sentence under it and
nothing else, and looked at the white above the name where the heading
went.

Four minutes on the twenty-ninth of the twelfth month, and four more some
time in the first, and nothing.

*And I cannot head that one.*

*And it is the same book and the same hand and it took me four seconds to
call the other one what it is.*

He shut it.

---

Outside it was raining in the way it does at the end of the second month,
and the mule was under the lean-to at the end of the cooperage where he
had paid a man forty a night to have it, and she was asleep against the
wall by the boards with the hat over her face and the knife on the outside
of her belt.

He did the six minutes on the roll.

Then he sat for a while in the dark with his back against the wall and did
the thing he had done every night for eleven months, which was to work out
what the next thing was.

There was not one.

It was the twenty-seventh of the second month of the second year he had
been at this, and he had six thousand one hundred and forty ryō and a mule
and a girl and a page he could not head, and there was nothing on the
boards, and there had been nothing on the boards for thirty-six of the
last fifty-six days.

He went to sleep at about one and slept the whole night.
