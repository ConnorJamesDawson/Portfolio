# Chapter 4, Scene 5 — Nothing to Trade

*Act 1, month 4, the third — day eight. Kanmuri lane, Kayano. Nozaki
Uzuki, age ten. POV: Uzuki. ~2,050 words.*

---

He came down the lane at twenty past ten on the Friday morning.

She knew him from forty yards by the walk, which was the same walk she
had watched cross a yard six days ago, and she got up off the ground and
did not say anything about the step.

He did not go over it. He came down the lane and turned into the doorway
and put one foot down nine inches without looking, the way you do a stair
in your own house in the dark, and the door was open before he got to it
because Chiyo had heard him too.

He did not see her at all.

She was four feet to the left with her back against the neighbour's wall
and she was not hidden and he was not looking, and he went past her into
the house, and the door shut.

---

*Two hours.*

She sat down and put her arms round her knees.

*Chiyo said two hours and she said it like a thing everybody in the house
knows, so it is two hours, and I am going to sit here for two hours.*

She sat there for three and a half.

Nothing came out of that house. Once there was somebody in the front room
and once a window went up somewhere at the back and stayed up.

At about one o'clock a woman came down the lane with a child on her hip
and Uzuki said, "Mind the step," and the woman said, "Is he back?" and
Uzuki said, "Since twenty past ten," and the woman looked at the door for
a moment and then turned round and went back up the lane without
knocking.

*She wanted him and she isn't going to.*

*Because he came off a week and everybody in this lane knows it and they
are all standing back.*

*And I'm not.*

She put her chin on her knees.

*I'm going to be the one who doesn't.*

---

Chiyo opened the door at about two.

"You. In."

---

The kitchen was at the back and it had a table in it and Anzai was at the
table with a bowl he had got about a third of the way through.

He had washed and changed and he did not look any better for it. Up close
he was about sixty and there was a broken vein across one cheekbone and
his hands on the table were an old man's hands with a young man's nails,
cut right down, scrubbed to the quick.

He looked at her for about four seconds.

"Sit down."

She sat down.

"Chiyo says you've been in my door since Saturday."

"Four nights in it and two beside it."

"Why beside it?"

"Chiyo told me to be near the door and not in it," said Uzuki, "and I
worked out that was so you wouldn't have to step over somebody to get
into your own house."

Anzai put his spoon down.

"Right," he said. "Say it."

---

She had had eleven days of it and she had thrown almost all of it away on
the Thursday, and what was left she had gone over about four hundred
times in a doorway, and it came out flat and fast and much smaller than
she wanted it to be.

"My father's ill and I want you to teach me the palm work for one
afternoon so I can do his nights. He's four days from here. I can't pay
you and I've got nothing to give you and I'm asking anyway."

She stopped.

"That's it," she said. "That's the whole of it."

---

Anzai looked at her.

"Who told you it could be taught."

"A man called Tazu. He's not a medic, he's — he's got a garden and jars
and he sets wrists. He's been doing our village forty years."

"Is he any good?"

"He told me he knew about six things and my father's wasn't one of them
and he wasn't going to pretend."

"Then he's very good," said Anzai. "What did he tell you it'd get you?"

"The nights. The edge. He said the coughing that's got hold and won't let
go, half of that's the muscle panicking and you can talk muscle out of
things. He said I'd not get the thing itself, not in an afternoon and not
in ten years."

"That's right."

"He said a medic worth the name'd sit somebody down for an afternoon and
give them the bones of it, because it's quicker than doing it yourself.
He said you'd get a chest off your list permanently."

---

"Ah," said Anzai.

He picked the spoon back up and put it down again.

"Now that's the bit that's wrong," he said, "and I'm going to do it
properly because you've walked four days and you'll get it off somebody
eventually and it may as well be somebody who's not going to enjoy it.

"Your Tazu's talking about a man in this town. Somebody who's on my list.
An old fellow up the hill with a chest, three times a week, for years —
and if his granddaughter comes to me and says teach me and I'll do it,
then yes, that's a good afternoon's work for me, because I get four
hundred visits back over the next ten years and I'd be a fool to say no.

"Your father has never been on my list. He's four days away in another
country and I have never heard his name until eleven minutes ago and I
am never going to hear it again."

He put his hands flat on the table.

"So you're not taking anything off me, girl. There's nothing of mine to
take. You're asking me to give you four hours of a week I have not got,
and you've dressed it up as a trade because a man in a shed told you it
was one, and it is not one."

---

The kitchen was very quiet.

Chiyo, at the other end of it, was doing something to a pot and had
stopped doing anything to a pot.

*Say it.*

"I know," said Uzuki.

Anzai's eyebrows went up about a quarter of an inch.

"You know."

"I worked it out on Wednesday." She had her hands flat on her own knees
under the table. "It's not a trade. I've got nothing. I said that first
because it's true and I wasn't going to sit down in your kitchen and try
the other one on you when I already knew it was rubbish."

"Then why are you here?"

"Because I want it anyway," said Uzuki, "and I haven't got a better
reason than that, and I've been trying to find one for a day and a half
and there isn't one."

---

Anzai sat back in the chair.

"Wednesday," he said.

"Yes."

"What happened on Wednesday."

"I went down to the tannery."

"Chiyo," said Anzai, without turning round, "was there a girl in the
nettles on Wednesday."

"There was a girl in the nettles on Wednesday," said Chiyo, to the pot.

---

"Go on then," said Anzai.

"I wasn't going to ask you," said Uzuki. "I want that said, because I'd
already decided I wasn't and I'd said it out loud at the top of the lane
so I couldn't wriggle. I went to look. I sat on the wall for about six
hours and then I got in the nettles for two."

"What did you see?"

"You did the boy's leg for twenty-five minutes and he cried the whole
time and stopped forty seconds after you took your hands off."

Anzai said nothing.

"And then you did the man in the middle for about a quarter of an hour
and then the third one, and then you went out in the yard and stood with
your eyes shut, and then at half past three you sat down and did the boy
again."

"And?"

"Four and a half hours," said Uzuki. "Twenty-five minutes buys four and
a half hours and then you do it again. Three men, four times a day,
twenty minutes each. That's four hours of you every day in that shed and
you've been doing it since Tuesday and you sleep twice."

She looked at the table.

"So an afternoon's four hours. Which is that boy not getting his night.
That's what it actually is. It isn't a favour and it isn't a trade, it's
that, and I'm asking for it anyway, and I'd rather say what it is than
sit here and let you think I hadn't done the sum."

---

Anzai did not move for a while.

Then he got up, and went to the window, and stood with his back to the
room looking out at whatever was out there, and Uzuki sat at the table
with her hands under it and did not say one word, because there was
nothing left in her to say and because Ine had been right about waiting.

"Chiyo," he said. "What's Sunday look like."

"You've got the Ide baby and the woman at the top."

"Move the woman at the top."

"She's been moved twice."

"Move her again," said Anzai, "and tell her it's me and tell her she can
be as cross as she likes and she can be cross at me on Monday."

---

He turned round.

"Two o'clock," he said. "Not Sunday. Today. Two till it goes dark, which
is about half five, so it's three and a half hours and not four and
you'll take that and be grateful."

Uzuki got about half a sound out.

"Sit *down*," said Anzai. "I've not finished.

"Three conditions and they're not negotiable and I'll put you out of that
door if you argue about any of them.

"One. You'll do exactly what I say for three and a half hours and you'll
not ask me why until the end. You can ask me why at the end and I'll
answer it, and I'll answer it properly. Not during. During you'll do it.

"Two. You'll never tell anybody I taught you. Not your mother, not the
man with the garden, and particularly not anybody in this town. I've got
four hundred people on a list and if one of them finds out I gave four
hours to a child from another country I'll have a queue in that lane
until I die.

"Three." He came back to the table and sat down. "You'll eat something
first, because Chiyo says you've had one bowl of barley since Sunday and
you're no good to me shaking."

---

"Why," said Uzuki.

"I said not during."

"We haven't started."

Anzai looked at her for a long moment.

"You're going to be a nuisance," he said, "for about sixty years," and
pulled the bowl over and went back to it. "Chiyo. Feed her."

---

She never got an answer.

She asked at the end, at half past five, when he said she could — she
asked the *why* she had been holding since Wednesday, which was not why
the fingers went where they went or why you counted the breath, because
he had answered all of those.

And he said: "You did the sum."

And that was all of it, and she took it away with her and turned it over
on a road for three days and for a great many years afterwards, and it
was not the reason, and she never found out what the reason was.

*He had come off six days in a shed where a boy was crying, and he had
lost two of them, and it was Friday morning and he had not slept since
Tuesday, and what he needed more than sleep was for one thing that week
to have been worth doing.*

*And a child had been sitting outside his door for four nights.*

He did not tell her that.

He would not have known how to say it and would not have said it if he
had, and Uzuki Nozaki went to her grave believing she had been given
three and a half hours of the best medic in the Land of Hot Water because
she had gone and sat in a nettle bank and got the arithmetic right.
