# Chapter 14, Scene 4 — Can I

*Act 2, year 1, month 2, the sixth. A yard at Sakabe. Tadayoshi Ruri, age
eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,750 words.*

---

The man's name was Deguchi and he had four in the yard and three of them
were no good, and Tadayoshi knew that inside about two minutes and was
pleased with himself about it and should not have been.

*The grey one is too old and he knows it.*

*The little one is not a mule, it is a pony with a long face on it.*

*The near hind on the dark one has been done recently and has been done
because it needed doing and not because it was time.*

*And the fourth one is the fourth one.*

He went over to the fourth one.

---

Deguchi came out and did the speech and did not hurry it, and it took
about four minutes.

He did the age off the teeth and said seven and said it in a way that
invited you to look, which Tadayoshi did, and it looked like seven, and he
had no idea what seven looked like and knew he had no idea.

He did the feet, one at a time, picking each one up, and said which of
them had been done by a good man and which by a man in a hurry, and was
honest about the near fore.

He did the shoulder, and said the animal had been bred for more than it
had ever been asked to do, and stood back so you could see it.

He said what it would carry and for how many hours and on what.

He said what it would not do, which was back into anything, and said that
was not a fault in the animal, that was a fault in whoever had had it at
three.

And he said, at the end, what it was worth in the spring against what it
was worth now, and gave a reason for the difference, and the reason was
about grass.

---

Tadayoshi listened to the whole of it and thought it was very good.

That should be put down plainly and without any decoration, because it is
exactly what happened and it is the whole of the scene:

**He thought it was good work.**

*He has gone through it in an order. He has not left anything out. He has
told me two things that are against him and both of them are true, because
I checked the near fore before he said it.*

*This is a man who knows a thing and is describing it accurately to
somebody who does not, and he is not lying to me anywhere I can find.*

*I would take a report like that off anybody.*

He had never heard the speech before in his life. Nobody had ever done it
in front of him and nobody had ever done it *to* him, and there was no
reason on earth for him to hear anything in it except a competent man
being competent, and he did not.

---

Then he put his hand on the shoulder.

He did it without deciding to and he did it because it was the thing he
had been looking at for four minutes and because he wanted to know what it
felt like under the coat, and he was aware afterwards that if anybody had
asked him he could have justified it: *you feel a shoulder, that is how
you know what is under it.*

He put his hand flat on it and left it there for about two seconds.

And Deguchi's voice changed.

---

It changed by about a quarter of a tone and it changed on the fourth word
of the next sentence and the sentence was about hay.

Tadayoshi heard it exactly.

He had spent seven months learning to hear precisely that, off men in
kitchens and men on wharves and a man with a book in a hall above Ōtoma,
and he had it before Deguchi had finished the word, and he knew what it
meant, and he knew what was going to happen next, and he could not do one
single thing about any of it.

*He has got it.*

*He got it off my hand. He did not get it off my face and he did not get
it off anything I said, because I have not said anything. I put my hand on
a shoulder for two seconds and he has got the whole of it.*

*And now there is a man with a thing and a boy who has already said he
wants it, and there is no negotiation after that. There is a number and
then a smaller number and then the first number again, and I pay the first
number, and I have done this before with a boot.*

---

He took his hand off the shoulder and stepped back.

And then he did a thing he had never done in his life, which was to stop.

He did not say a number. He did not say *how much*. He stood in a yard at
Sakabe on the sixth of the second month with about eleven thousand ryō
about his person and a job that did not close without an animal, and he
shut his mouth and did not open it.

*If I say a number now it is the wrong number.*

*I have got no way of getting back to the front of this. There is no
version where I un-put my hand on it.*

He stood there for about four seconds and had nothing.

---

She was standing about four feet behind him and to the left, where she
had been all morning, with the hat on.

She said, "Can I?"

He said, "Yes."

That was the whole of the handover and it took under two seconds and there
were three words in it, and he thought about those three words on and off
for two years and never got them to come out anywhere useful.

He stepped back another pace and put his hands behind him.

---

What happened over the next eleven minutes he could not have described
accurately if his life had depended on it, and it very nearly did four
years later, and he still could not.

He got the shape of it.

*She has not gone near it.*

That was the first thing and it arrived at about the second minute and he
did not know what to do with it.

*She has been talking to him for two minutes and she has not been within
six feet of the animal, and she is looking at it the whole time.*

*I have been in this yard for twenty minutes and I have touched it and she
has not.*

The second thing came at about the fourth minute and he got it wrong.

*She has just contradicted him about the age.*

*She said something about the teeth and he stopped, and I do not know what
she said, because it was three words and it was about a groove.*

*And he has come back with a different number and it is a smaller number
and he did it without arguing.*

---

The third thing came at about the seventh minute and it was the only one
he got entirely right, and it was the one he could not use.

**She was doing the speech.**

Not answering it. *Doing* it — the same shape, the same order, the age and
the feet and the shoulder and what it would do and what it would not, in
the same rhythm, with the pauses in the same places, and she had turned it
round so that every item in it was a reason the animal was worth less than
Deguchi had said.

*She has got his own speech and she is giving it back to him.*

*And it is his speech. It is not the shape of his speech, it is the shape
of a speech, and he is using it too, and they have both got it from
somewhere.*

He looked at the side of her face under the hat.

*Where has she got that from.*

He did not ask. He never asked. It was on the list of things he had not
thought to ask, which by the second month of that year was a long list and
had four items on it that would have changed the whole of the next three
years, and he did not know he was keeping it.

---

Asked seven thousand five hundred.

Paid five thousand two hundred.

And then, when it was agreed and the money was out and Deguchi had put his
hand out, she said one more thing, and the headcollar and rope came with
it, and Tadayoshi did not hear what she said because he was counting.

---

He wrote it up that night and he wrote four things and he was pleased with
three of them.

> *1. She did not touch it. I did. That is the whole of the two thousand
> three hundred.*
>
> *2. She let him finish. He did four minutes at me and she let him do
> four minutes at her and did not say one word in the middle of it.*
>
> *3. She asked for the headcollar after the price was agreed and not
> before. That is a separate thing and I do not understand it yet and it
> worked.*

He looked at the three of them and they were all correct and all useful
and he had learned something from each one, and then he wrote the fourth
one, which was the one he had been building toward all evening, and it was
the flattest and most useless sentence he ever put in that book.

> *4. She is good with people.*

He read it back and was satisfied with it.

He had spent seven months building a method whose entire content was that
you do not describe a person, you describe what they did and in what
order, and he had just watched somebody take a man apart in eleven minutes
in front of him and had come away with a compliment.

He shut the book.

Outside, in the dark, the mule was standing where they had tied it and had
not been given a name and was not going to be.
