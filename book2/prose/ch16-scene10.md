# Chapter 16, Scene 10 — Ours Now

*Act 2, year 1, month 4, the ninth. The far bank, and a fire nine miles
north of it. Tadayoshi Ruri, age eleven. POV: Tadayoshi. ~1,900 words.*

---

He came up the drove track at about the count of four hundred with the
mule and it was over.

Eleven people had been doing something to each other on that bank and now
six of them were standing and two of those were doing something to a third,
and the rest of it was on the ground and was going to stay there.

Nobody stopped him. Nobody asked him anything. He came up a track into the
middle of an operation he had not been briefed on, leading an animal, and
about four people looked at him and every one of them looked away again,
and that told him more about the arrangement than anything anybody said to
him all day.

*They know who I am.*

*Nobody here has met me and they all know what I am for.*

---

She was standing about ten feet from the cart with her hands at her sides
and there were four men round the tailboard.

He did not go to her. He went to the cart, because the cart was where the
thing was, and he has thought about that since.

The man who was clearly the one was about thirty-five and had a cut down
the back of his own left hand that he had not looked at yet, and he did not
introduce himself and did not ask Tadayoshi's name.

He said: "You're the carrier."

"Yes."

"Cargo's in the crate. Take it and go north and don't stop."

---

Tadayoshi asked one question, because he asked one question.

"Whose is it?"

The man looked at him for about a second.

"Ours now."

---

He has kept that.

He has taken it out more times than he has taken out anything anybody has
ever said to him, and every time he has taken it out he has found the same
thing in it, which is that it was true.

It was true, and it was complete, and it answered the question he had
asked. He had asked whose, and he had been told whose, and the man had not
lied to him by one word and had not left anything out that Tadayoshi had
asked for.

*Ours now.*

*So it was somebody's, and now it is ours, and now is this morning, and the
this morning is the reason there are people on the ground.*

**That is the last entirely honest sentence anybody says to him about that
boy for three years, and it is two words long, and it is said by a man who
does not know his name and never learns it.**

---

They got the crate down off the cart and it took four of them and it took
longer than it should have.

The reason it took longer is the only thing that happened on that bank that
Tadayoshi wrote down.

One of them would not come near it.

He was one of the six standing and he was fine, and he had been doing
something to the man with the leg a minute before, and when the four went
to the tailboard he went the other way — not fast, not obviously, he simply
turned out to have something to do at the other end of the cart — and then
he came up the track and past Tadayoshi and said, "I'll hold him," and took
the mule's headcollar and stood there holding an animal that was tied and
did not need holding, for the whole of it, about eight feet back.

He did not say anything about it. Nobody said anything to him about it.

Tadayoshi got the whole of it in about four seconds — *he has done a thing
he did not want to be near, and he is not ashamed of it and nobody is going
to make him say so, and the other five have decided not to notice* — and
had no idea at all what it was about, and put it in the place where things
about people went.

*Ten items now.*

*And that one has got two facts in it, which is the first time.*

---

They took the lashings off and got the lid up and he was in there.

He is not going to write down what he thought when he saw him, because he
did not think anything for about two seconds, and afterwards what he had
was a list.

*Thirteen, fourteen. Thin the way somebody is thin who has been fed
regularly and not enough, which is different from starved and I know both.*

*Marks on his face. Two, under the eyes. They are not paint and they are
not a wound and they have been there a long time.*

*And he is looking at me.*

That was the part he had not expected, and he had not known he was
expecting anything else until it did not happen.

*He is not doing anything. He is not curled up and he is not shielding his
face and he is not saying anything. He is on his side with his hands in
front of his chest and he has turned his head and he is looking at me, and
he has been looking at me since the lid came off.*

*And he is counting.*

Tadayoshi did not know how he knew that and it was correct.

---

He said the only thing he had, which was operational, because operational
was the only kind he had.

"How long have you been in there?"

---

And what he got back was the longest answer anybody gave him that year and
it came out flat and in order and without a single hesitation in it, and
Tadayoshi stood on a riverbank with his hand on the lid of a box and
listened to the whole of it.

He did not interrupt.

At the end of it he said, "Right," and got his book out, standing, on the
bank, in front of six men, and wrote it down.

He wrote it down in the operational column because it was a measurement and
measurements went in the operational column.

He did not put the mark beside it.

*That is not a number I have made.*

*That is a number somebody counted.*

---

The brief ran out at about ten o'clock and he found the edge of it standing
on the track with the crate empty at his feet.

"North," he said. "How far north."

The man with the cut hand was already going. "North."

"For how long."

"Till somebody comes."

"Who."

And the man said, not unkindly and not evasively and with the whole of his
attention on his own left hand for the first time that morning:

"I don't know. That's not mine."

---

They went north at about half past ten and they did nine miles and the boy
walked all of it.

Tadayoshi did not offer him the mule and did not think of it. Uzuki
thought of it at about the third mile and did not say it, and worked out
why she had not said it at about the sixth, and the reason was that the boy
was walking perfectly well and offering would have been about her.

Nobody said anything for nine miles.

---

He did the book that night with the fire between the three of them and it
took him about forty minutes, and everything in it was ordinary except the
last thing.

He did the money. He did the mule — *9/4. 40 kan + frame, 9 mi, late
start* — and drew the line. He did the operational column and put four
lines in it about a ford and did not put anything in it about a spear.

And then he turned to the front of the ordinary column and ruled a page
and sat with the pencil over it for a very long time.

---

He was trying to write down a thing he had no account of.

*There is a thing that happens.*

*I have got about nine of them and four of them I could not tell you the
middle of, and this morning there were two seconds in a river.*

*I do not know what starts it. I have looked. I have got nine and there is
nothing in common between them that I can find and I have been at it since
I was nine years old.*

*So I cannot write down what it is and I cannot write down when it
happens.*

He turned the pencil over.

*What can I write down.*

He got there at about the fourth attempt and the fourth attempt was the
one, and it took him four minutes and it is nine words long.

> ***If I don't call your name, don't fight beside me.***

---

He looked at it for a while.

*There is nothing under it.*

*That is a rule with no reason under it and I have written one of those
before and it was in the eleventh month of last year and I knew it was
backwards when I wrote it and I wrote that down too.*

*This one I am not going to write that down about, because I have not got
the version where it is backwards. I have got: this is the only thing I can
say that is a thing a person can do.*

He put the mark beside it.

*That is the sixth time in six weeks and four of them are not numbers.*

*And this one is not even a measurement. This one is an instruction.*

He left it.

He did not date it, which he did with everything, and he never did work out
why he had not.

---

She read it.

He knew she would. He had left the book on the ground with the page open
and gone to see to the mule, which is a thing he had never once done in
fifteen months, and he has never decided whether that was an accident and
neither has anybody else.

She read it in about two seconds and then sat looking at it for rather
longer.

And what she said, when he came back, was not about the rule.

"You've not put a name on it."

"No."

"It says *your name*."

"It does."

She put a stick on the fire.

"There's two of us," she said.

And Tadayoshi, who had written it generically because he wrote everything
generically, and who had not once in four minutes considered that a rule
about names would be read by the only two people it could possibly be
about, said:

"There's three."

---

The boy on the other side of the fire, who had been given nothing to do and
had therefore done nothing for six hours, and who had been listening to all
of it because listening was free, turned his head.

Nobody explained it to him and nobody was going to.

**And what he took away from it was correct, and it did not occur to
either of them for four months: that there was now a rule with a name in
it, and that he had not been given a name.**
