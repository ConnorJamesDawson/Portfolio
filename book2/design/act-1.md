# ACT ONE — age 10, year 0

> **Thesis: becoming the monster everyone already believes he is.**
> Twelve months. Two threads. They meet on the last page.

**Month-by-month source of truth: `timeline.md` §2.** This file is the
chapter structure, the POV budget, and the information plan.

---

## 1. Parameters

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chapters** | **12** — *eleven until the creed moved into the act* |
| **Length** | 14k–20k each, 8–11 scenes. ~185k for the act. |
| **POVs** | **Tadayoshi. Uzuki. Kimimaro. Mikage — one scene, once.** |
| **Convergence** | Tadayoshi and Uzuki, ch12. **Kimimaro is still in the cell when the act ends.** |

**Implied total: ~750k across four acts**, which is book one's scale. **If a
shorter book is wanted, the lever is fewer chapters, not shorter ones** —
short chapters are where summary gets in, and summary-where-a-scene-belongs
is the failure mode that matters most. A ~400k book is act one in **seven**
chapters with Uzuki's thread compressed to three.

### Iwao gets no POV in act one — and that is the point

He is seen only from outside: liked, warm, funny, and then gone.

**You like people you see from outside.** His interiority is withheld from
the reader exactly as it is withheld from his brother, and his first POV —
act two at the earliest — recontextualises everything the reader felt about
him in chapters 1 to 3.

### Mikage gets exactly one, and it is the tribunal

**Recommended, and it is a real cost either way.**

One scene, ~2,000 words, in ch3. **She sits in a room and hears her son
testify that she is not his mother, and she does not contradict him, because
contradicting him kills him.**

**The reader gets it. Tadayoshi does not**, and carries the not-knowing for
the rest of the book while the reader carries the knowing. It also makes her
a person on the page rather than a memory, **which is what three years of
reading a dead woman's tone will need in order to land.**

*(The alternative — no Mikage POV, the whole warrant reconstructed — is
cleaner and loses the single best scene available in act one. I would spend
it.)*

## 2. Page-time budget

| POV | Share | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| **Tadayoshi** | **~50%** | months 1–6 and month 12 |
| **Uzuki** | **~35%** | months 7–11 |
| **Kimimaro** | **~12%** | nothing. That is the point. |
| **Mikage** | **~3%** | one scene, ch3 |

**The weight tells the reader which thread is in crisis.** Month 4 is his.
Month 9 is hers. **Do not average it out** — a quiet month for one thread is
a month the other thread owns, and the unevenness is the structure working.

## 4. The information plan — what lands where

### Established early and plainly, because these are premises, not twists

**Ch1:**

- **He fixed the tone two years ago and was told to hide it.** Stated, not
  revealed. The tension is consequence, not disclosure — **and it lets the
  reader connect *improved yield* to *collection order* one chapter before
  Tadayoshi ever can.**
- **The courses**, through work rather than exposition. Enough that a reader
  can hear a piece by ch5.
- **Hand seals, cast time, the hands.** Cheap in ch1, paid in full in ch5.
- **The father's argument** — *hiding is cowardice* — at a table, unresolved,
  with a boy of ten listening. His entire adult politics, and he will think
  he derived them from the purge.
- **Uzuki's dye**, and that nobody explains it. **Her father knows a little
  and will not say.**
- **She is the fast one**, established before it matters, so that
  volunteering in ch2 is character rather than plot.

**Ch1–2, the five who die** — each needs one thing to do, not a portrait:

| | Doing |
|---|---|
| **Meno** | **the register, open, on a table.** The reader sees a page. It has a *left* column. |
| **Akagane** | making a frame beautifully — **and Tadayoshi knowing it is unnecessary and saying nothing.** |
| **Hisui** | losing the argument to leave, again, for the ninth time |
| **Koishi** | following him. Asking him things. Six. |
| **Tadaharu** | coming back from the road with the money and an opinion |

### Withheld, and planted unremarked

- **The first job docket** is on the page in act one, in Fuda's hand, and
  **nothing is made of it.** It is the sheet he will go back to at twelve.
- **The register's *left* column exists** and no name in it is legible to
  the reader. Guren is on that page.
- **A piece in Meno's keeping has a fault in it that nobody can read.**
  Kongō's mark, fifty years old, in the house, unremarked, and it burns.
- **The Kaguya are a thing adults mention** — an assault two years ago, a
  clan gone. **No name.** The reader has the boy; the *adults* do not
  connect him, and neither does Tadayoshi, who was eight and saw a cage go
  past.
- **The cage in his own memory is never linked to the cell.** Tadayoshi saw
  a boy carried past at eight. The reader is in that boy's cell. **Neither
  of them ever puts it together in act one**, and the book does not help.

### Not in act one, at all

**The creed. Orochimaru under any name. The boilerplate as a pattern. Gatō.
Guren. The word for the threshold. The word *Kaguya*, attached to the boy in
the cell.**

The threshold **happens** in ch5 and again in ch12 and **is never named**.
It has no name until somebody gives it one.

## 5. The five hard problems, and how each is beaten

### 1. Five deaths must be grief, not arithmetic

**Two chapters of *before*, and the compound is mostly empty.** Forty
people's buildings held by eight; most of it shut; **and he knows what every
empty room used to be, because Meno told him.**

**Each death removes a named capability**, and he can list them. **The
catalogue only ever gets shorter.**

### 2. The warrant happens off-page, and that is deliberate

**He is not there.** Ch3 is **reconstruction** — what he is told, what he
works out, what he cannot determine.

Which does two jobs: it is the truest version, **and it teaches the reader
the book's method on the largest possible event two chapters before the
threshold uses the same grammar.** By ch5 the reader already knows how this
book handles a thing its POV did not witness.

**The one exception is the tribunal**, and it is an exception because the
reader needs a fact the protagonist will never have.

### 3. Months 7–8 are the montage trap

*Contracts* is the word that kills chapters.

> **Do not write the contracts. Write one contract, whole**, at full length,
> start to finish, and let the reader extrapolate the rest.

What months 7–8 are **actually** about is **Kajiya, Fuda, and a boy becoming
expensive to handle** — which is people, not jobs.

**And there are not many contracts to montage, because he is not a courier
for three years.** `combat.md` §10b: **carrying is the probation** —
months 6–7, one job, and the parcel is the test. **Month 8 is where the
investment starts**, because a rebellion that is losing has worked out that
every team hunting him is a team not hunting a cell, **and an untrained
lightning rod dies in eight months.**

> **The one contract written whole is therefore not a sample of three
> years. It is the last of its kind**, and the reader should only find
> that out afterwards.

### 4. The hunter curve must start as attrition, not as fights

**First contact, month 7: a lodging given up at two in the morning.** No
enemy on the page. A room, a decision, and a thing left behind because
carrying it was slower.

**Month 9: Sagara Nagi**, who survives, and goes into the book as **the
left-hander** because he has no other system for naming people.

**The curve is felt as shrinking options.** By ch12 there are three towns he
cannot use and he can name them.

### 5. Kimimaro's thread is a cell, and a cell is a waiting room

**The rule it has to beat** (`structure.md` §1.1): *every thread owns an
antagonist and a clock; a thread without its own opposition becomes a
waiting room.* A boy held in a room for twelve months of story time is the
purest possible failure of that rule.

**The antagonist is the man who values him.**

He is not a prisoner. **He is stock**, held pending a buyer, and somebody
has to periodically confirm he is still worth what the invoice says. That
gives the thread a person, a recurrence, and a clock:

> **He is being assessed, at intervals. And the illness has started.**

**Which is the same machine that will kill Mikage**, running two years
early, on a child, in another building. **The reader watches the mechanism
before it takes the mother** — a held bloodline asset whose survival depends
on continuing to be worth the upkeep, declining, and being measured.

**And he is not passive in that room.** This is the part that makes him a
person rather than a condition:

> **He works, every day, at being valuable enough not to be discarded.**
> Nobody taught him to. He worked it out.

**The one initiative of his life was spent on making himself better
property** — and that is the whole origin of the boy who asks permission to
eat and later asks to be used.

**In ch8, the other held child fails an inspection**, and Kimimaro sees what
happens to stock that stops being worth keeping. **And then he works
harder.** He never learns that child's name. He counts them instead.

### 5b. And the weeks he grows on are weeks Uzuki loses

**⚠ RULING.** `structure.md`, act one: **Uzuki is priced on 10/28 against a
buyer's date of *not before the eleventh*, so she is held eight or nine
weeks.** Nothing happens to her in them. **That is the point of her** — a
child in a root cellar with a bowl and a bell has no events, and writing her
events would be writing a different girl.

**So the page she stops needing goes to him**, and that is the only place in
act one his thread can grow without the structure doing him a favour:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ch10, scene 10 → 11** | **The joint.** She goes into a cellar with a date in her head; he has been in one for two years and has just worked out that his bowl is a decision. **The act's designed cut, and it is a handover.** |
| **Ch10, K weight** | **15%, two scenes** — level with ch6 and ch8, and the highest in the act. It is not a rise in his page count. **It is a rise in what the page is doing**, because he is the only one of the three whose thread is moving. |
| **Ch11** | **Holds at ~10%.** The chapter carries two Tadayoshi events and cannot take more. *His real compounding is act two.* |

> **The reader should not notice the transfer.** Two children in boxes, in
> two countries, for the same customer — **and one of them stops being the
> subject and starts being the frame.**

### 5a. His interior grammar: counted, not dated

The third voice has to be distinct from the other two in the first
paragraph, and it is, structurally:

| | Grammar |
|---|---|
| **Tadayoshi** | **dated.** Findings, filed, under a date, in a book. |
| **Uzuki** | **immediate.** No format at all. The thing, said. |
| **Kimimaro** | **counted, and anchored to nothing.** |

**He counts** — meals, footsteps, the number of times a door opens, days
since the last inspection. **And he does not know what month it is**, so
every number he holds is relative and none of them fix to anything.

> **One boy numbers everything and knows exactly when. The other counts
> everything and knows nothing.**

**Which sets up the first thing Tadayoshi is ever able to give him: a date.**

### 5c. Two formal rules for his scenes

1. **They are always the shortest in the chapter.** Consistently, without
   exception, and the reader will feel it before they notice it. **A life
   with less in it gets less room.**
2. **They never open with a time or a place.** He cannot supply either.

### 5d. And the snake's first appearance, without a name

**A high-value consignment gets inspected by the buyer's agent.**

So somewhere in act one — ch6 or ch8 — **a man from somewhere else looks at
a child and takes notes**, and is polite, and is more interested than the
keepers are, and asks a question the keepers would not think to ask.

**That is Orochimaru's first appearance in this book and he is not in it.**
It seeds the pipeline on the Kimimaro side, which is better than resting the
whole plant on Fuda's docket.

### 6. Uzuki must be a person, not a sequence of harms

Her thread is five bad things in nine months. The counter is
`character.md`'s rule, applied on every page she is on:

> **She is not passive. She is unlucky.**

She volunteers. She gets the technique. She keeps the knife. She decides to
run. **In every single chapter she takes an action** — and the world is
faster. That is heartbreak with a person in it.

**And she is warm first.** Ch1 has to be worth losing.

## 6. Where act one ends, and what it hands to act two

**Ch12 closes on the monastery**, and the act's last page is a boy asking
a girl her name and writing it at the top of a page with no heading —
**which is the one thing he did not do in the eleventh month, and neither
of them knows that.**

**Delivered to act two:**

0. **A third child, still in a box, with a sale scheduled** — and act two
   opens with him being moved. *Counting toward a number that is wrong.*
1. **A household of two**, formed by accident, **on a debt she offered and
   he accepted.**
2. **A dead client, no fee, and a failure to report.**
3. **The tone** — established as an instrument, trusted completely.
4. ~~**A vow**, sworn at ten, about a boy of thirteen.~~ **✅ RULED,
   AUTHOR: it moves to act two.** *It was never on the page in twelve
   chapters, and act one has no room and no cause for it.* See
   `act-2.md` §4.6.
5. **A curve**, three towns wide and rising.
6. **⚠ AND A CREED THAT HAS BEEN TESTED ONCE AND WIDENED ONCE, IN THE SAME
   WEEK.** *It held at the monastery, in the four minutes he cannot
   account for, and nobody in the building saw it work — **and that same
   morning he had already refused to apply it, awake, on a technicality.***
   **He ends act one governed by a rule that only works when he is not
   there**, which is the correct place for act two to start.
