# ACT ONE — age 10, year 0

> **Thesis: becoming the monster everyone already believes he is.**
> Twelve months. Two threads. They meet on the last page.

**Month-by-month source of truth: `timeline.md` §2.** This file is the
chapter structure, the POV budget, and the information plan.

---

## 1. Parameters

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chapters** | **11** |
| **Length** | 14k–20k each, 8–11 scenes. ~185k for the act. |
| **POVs** | **Tadayoshi. Uzuki. Mikage — one scene, once.** |
| **Convergence** | ch11. Not before. They do not glimpse each other. |

**Implied total: ~750k across four acts**, which is book one's scale. **If a
shorter book is wanted, the lever is fewer chapters, not shorter ones** —
short chapters are where summary gets in, and summary-where-a-scene-belongs
is the failure mode that matters most. A ~400k book is act one in **seven**
chapters with Uzuki's thread compressed to three.

### Iwao gets no POV in act one — and that is the point

He is seen only from outside: liked, warm, funny, and then gone.

**You like people you see from outside.** His interiority is withheld from
the reader exactly as it is withheld from his brother, and his first POV —
act two at the earliest — recontextualises everything the reader felt about
him in chapters 1 to 3.

### Mikage gets exactly one, and it is the tribunal

**Recommended, and it is a real cost either way.**

One scene, ~2,000 words, in ch3. **She sits in a room and hears her son
testify that she is not his mother, and she does not contradict him, because
contradicting him kills him.**

**The reader gets it. Tadayoshi does not**, and carries the not-knowing for
the rest of the book while the reader carries the knowing. It also makes her
a person on the page rather than a memory, **which is what three years of
reading a dead woman's tone will need in order to land.**

*(The alternative — no Mikage POV, the whole warrant reconstructed — is
cleaner and loses the single best scene available in act one. I would spend
it.)*

## 2. Page-time budget

| POV | Share | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| **Tadayoshi** | **~55%** | months 1–6 and month 12 |
| **Uzuki** | **~42%** | months 7–11 |
| **Mikage** | **~3%** | one scene, ch3 |

**The weight tells the reader which thread is in crisis.** Month 4 is his.
Month 9 is hers. **Do not average it out** — a quiet month for one thread is
a month the other thread owns, and the unevenness is the structure working.

## 4. The information plan — what lands where

### Established early and plainly, because these are premises, not twists

**Ch1:**

- **He fixed the tone two years ago and was told to hide it.** Stated, not
  revealed. The tension is consequence, not disclosure — **and it lets the
  reader connect *improved yield* to *collection order* one chapter before
  Tadayoshi ever can.**
- **The courses**, through work rather than exposition. Enough that a reader
  can hear a piece by ch5.
- **Hand seals, cast time, the hands.** Cheap in ch1, paid in full in ch5.
- **The father's argument** — *hiding is cowardice* — at a table, unresolved,
  with a boy of ten listening. His entire adult politics, and he will think
  he derived them from the purge.
- **Uzuki's dye**, and that nobody explains it. **Her father knows a little
  and will not say.**
- **She is the fast one**, established before it matters, so that
  volunteering in ch2 is character rather than plot.

**Ch1–2, the five who die** — each needs one thing to do, not a portrait:

| | Doing |
|---|---|
| **Meno** | **the register, open, on a table.** The reader sees a page. It has a *left* column. |
| **Akagane** | making a frame beautifully — **and Tadayoshi knowing it is unnecessary and saying nothing.** |
| **Hisui** | losing the argument to leave, again, for the ninth time |
| **Koishi** | following him. Asking him things. Six. |
| **Tadaharu** | coming back from the road with the money and an opinion |

### Withheld, and planted unremarked

- **The first job docket** is on the page in act one, in Fuda's hand, and
  **nothing is made of it.** It is the sheet he will go back to at twelve.
- **The register's *left* column exists** and no name in it is legible to
  the reader. Guren is on that page.
- **A piece in Meno's keeping has a fault in it that nobody can read.**
  Kongō's mark, fifty years old, in the house, unremarked, and it burns.
- **The Kaguya are a thing adults mention** — an assault two years ago, a
  clan gone. **No name. No boy.**

### Not in act one, at all

**The creed. Kimimaro. Orochimaru under any name. The boilerplate as a
pattern. Gatō. Guren. The word for the threshold.**

The threshold **happens** in ch5 and again in ch11 and **is never named**.
It has no name until somebody gives it one.

## 5. The five hard problems, and how each is beaten

### 1. Five deaths must be grief, not arithmetic

**Two chapters of *before*, and the compound is mostly empty.** Forty
people's buildings held by eight; most of it shut; **and he knows what every
empty room used to be, because Meno told him.**

**Each death removes a named capability**, and he can list them. **The
catalogue only ever gets shorter.**

### 2. The warrant happens off-page, and that is deliberate

**He is not there.** Ch3 is **reconstruction** — what he is told, what he
works out, what he cannot determine.

Which does two jobs: it is the truest version, **and it teaches the reader
the book's method on the largest possible event two chapters before the
threshold uses the same grammar.** By ch5 the reader already knows how this
book handles a thing its POV did not witness.

**The one exception is the tribunal**, and it is an exception because the
reader needs a fact the protagonist will never have.

### 3. Months 7–8 are the montage trap

*Contracts* is the word that kills chapters.

> **Do not write the contracts. Write one contract, whole**, at full length,
> start to finish, and let the reader extrapolate the rest.

What months 7–8 are **actually** about is **Kajiya, Fuda, and a boy becoming
expensive to handle** — which is people, not jobs.

### 4. The hunter curve must start as attrition, not as fights

**First contact, month 7: a lodging given up at two in the morning.** No
enemy on the page. A room, a decision, and a thing left behind because
carrying it was slower.

**Month 9: Sagara Nagi**, who survives, and goes into the book as **the
left-hander** because he has no other system for naming people.

**The curve is felt as shrinking options.** By ch11 there are three towns he
cannot use and he can name them.

### 5. Uzuki must be a person, not a sequence of harms

Her thread is five bad things in nine months. The counter is
`character.md`'s rule, applied on every page she is on:

> **She is not passive. She is unlucky.**

She volunteers. She gets the technique. She keeps the knife. She decides to
run. **In every single chapter she takes an action** — and the world is
faster. That is heartbreak with a person in it.

**And she is warm first.** Ch1 has to be worth losing.

## 6. Where act one ends, and what it hands to act two

**Ch11 closes on the monastery**, and the act's last page is a girl saying
one word and a boy who cannot account for the next four minutes.

**Delivered to act two:**

1. **A household of two**, formed by accident, on a debt he is carrying.
2. **A dead client, no fee, and a failure to report.**
3. **The tone** — established as an instrument, trusted completely.
4. **A vow**, sworn at ten, about a boy of thirteen.
5. **A curve**, three towns wide and rising.
6. **And no creed.** He ends act one with nothing governing him at all,
   which is the correct place for act two to start.

## 7. Open before drafting ch1

1. **Does the reader see the collection order being signed** (ch2), or only
   its consequences? Seeing it is dramatic irony; not seeing it keeps ch2 in
   two POVs instead of borrowing a third.
2. **How much Academy is left?** He is warranted at ten and the transfer was
   at nine. Is he still attending in months 1–3, or has the fear already
   pushed him out?
3. **Uzuki's village and country.** Never fixed. It needs to be far enough
   from Kiri that they cannot meet before month 12, and close enough that a
   cargo run reaches it.
4. **The chapter that establishes the mother.** She has one POV scene and
   perhaps four appearances before it. **Four is not many for a woman who
   has to carry three years of grief through a shop window.**
