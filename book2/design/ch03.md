# Chapter 3 — "Twenty Minutes"

**Act one, month 4, days 1–4. Year 0, ~3 pre-P1.**
**Tadayoshi 10 · Uzuki 10 · Kimimaro 12 · Iwao 13 · Mikage 34**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Scenes** | 9 — **WRITTEN, 16,870 words** |
| **Target** | ~17,000 words |
| **POV split** | T 5 / **Mikage 1** / U 2 / K 1 |
| **Weight** | T ~55% · M ~15% · U ~20% · K ~10% |

**The chapter's job:** destroy the compound without putting the reader in
it, hand the reader a fact the protagonist will never have, and give a
boy of ten the exact wound his mother has carried for fifty years.

---

## 1. The three rulings this chapter executes

**1. The raid is never on the page.** Not from anybody. There is no POV in
that yard. `act-1.md`: the truest version, and it teaches the reader this
book's grammar on the largest possible event **two chapters before the
threshold needs the same grammar.**

**2. Mikage's one POV scene is the tribunal**, and the reader gets a fact
Tadayoshi never will.

**3. Iwao gets no POV.** The reader has liked him for two chapters from
outside. **He stays outside.**

---

## 2. THE MECHANISM — how a boy of ten survives a collection

The plan has to answer this or the whole act is a contrivance. It answers
it with the smallest possible act by the least likely person.

### Aoyagi sends him on an errand

The party goes to Shioiri and to the Academy **at the same time**, because
a competent operation collects the asset and the household together.

And at about eleven, a schoolteacher who has been in that village a long
time, and who knows what a certain kind of visitor in a yard means,
**sends a ten-year-old out of the building with a verbal message for a man
at the ferry.**

- **He writes nothing down.** There is no note.
- **The message is nothing.** When Tadayoshi delivers it the man says
  *what?* — and Tadayoshi stands there and cannot make it mean anything
  and goes on home, an hour early, on the coast road.
- **He works out what it was about four months later**, and by then
  Aoyagi is a person he cannot go back to.

> **Nothing happens to Aoyagi.** He is a schoolteacher who sent a boy on
> an errand. **There is nothing to charge.**
>
> **The only effective resistance in the entire chapter is an act so small
> it cannot be punished** — which is the standing answer to Tadaharu's
> *hiding is cowardice*, and neither man will ever know they were arguing.

---

## 3. THE INHERITANCE — and this is what the chapter is actually for

He comes over the top above the inlet and **the hill is wrong.** Nothing
is burning. There are people on the paths who should not be on the paths,
and a boat at the inlet that is not a Shioiri boat.

**He stands at the top and does not go down.**

> **His mother has forty people on a hill who watched a man walk down it
> and did nothing, and she has had it at three in the morning for fifty
> years, and it is the whole of what she has instead of an argument.**
>
> **He becomes one person on a hill who watched and did nothing.**

**He gets her wound in one afternoon, at ten, and she is in a cart by
then and they never discuss it.** That is the inheritance, and it is
delivered by an image and not by a line, and **nobody in the book ever
names it.**

---

## 4. THE RECONSTRUCTION — and what it comes out as

He goes down on the second night and reads the compound the way he reads
an article.

**There are no bodies.** No blood, no fire in the houses, nothing knocked
over that mattered. Doors open. A meal on a table. **Eight people gone and
nothing to find** — because this was an arrest, not a massacre, and the
disposal happens somewhere else, and `threads.md` C1 already rules **he
never learns what happened to them.**

So what he can read is process:

- how many came, and from where
- where they entered, and that they entered at the top and the bottom at
  once
- that **nobody ran**
- **how long it took**

And it comes out at **about twenty minutes, and there was no shouting.**

> **Which are the exact words his mother used about Kongō, three weeks
> ago, at that table.**
>
> **And he has no way of knowing whether that is because these things are
> all the same, or because he is reading it through her account** — and
> the not-knowing is permanent, and it is the first time the book shows
> him unable to trust his own instrument.

---

## 5. THE FIRE, AND THE OBJECT

**They did not burn the houses. They burned the papers.** Procedure for a
household under order: a small fire in the yard, on the flags, gone out by
the time he gets there.

In it: **Meno's register.** A hundred and some years, four ruled columns,
nineteen *went*, and the names she would not say. **Ash.**

And Meno's box, and what was in it.

> **Crystal does not burn.**
>
> **He finds Kongō's drop in the ashes, intact — the plain one, bigger
> than the ones they make now, with the note a third of the way up, that
> he can tell is wrong and cannot tell is deliberate.**

**He takes it.** He will carry it for years. **He will be holding it when
he finally stands in front of the man who made it.**

**That is the chapter's closing image and the last line of the act's first
movement.**

## 7. THE TRIBUNAL — scene 5, and it is the act's largest scene

### Her grammar: she reads by hand and the room runs on paper

Twenty-two years of judging things by feel. **She can tell you the wood of
that table and not what the form says.** She notices weight, grain,
surfaces, and the fact that she still has grit under one nail from
Tuesday. **The arrest is in her hands, not in narration.**

**All seven of them are in that room.** Tadaharu, Meno, Akagane, Hisui,
Koishi, Iwao, her. **This is the last time the reader sees any of them
alive**, and the scene must know that and must not say it.

### What Iwao does — and he does not plan it

The register is read out. *Ruri Iwao, son of Ruri Mikage and Ruri
Tadaharu, assessed negative in his eighth year.* Somebody asks whether
the record is correct, because that is what you ask.

> **"That one's wrong."**

**Four words, and then he has to keep going**, and he does, and he builds
the rest of it in about ninety seconds standing up, out of nothing, in
front of his family.

**He is frightened and it is visible.** That is essential: **the reader
must be able to see a thirteen-year-old being terrified while doing it
beautifully**, or he becomes a villain and the book loses him.

**And being assessed negative does not save anybody.** Hisui was negative.
Akagane was negative. The purge takes the blood, not the ability. **The
only door in that room is not being blood at all**, and Iwao is the only
person who thinks to try it.

### The name

Asked who his father was, then — under pressure, in a pause, reaching —
**he says Kenji.**

**A man at a counter in Ōura who makes a face at beads.**

> **And Mikage knows exactly where the name came from, because she was at
> the table when he did the impression, and everybody laughed.**
>
> **She is the only person alive who will ever know that.**

A clerk writes it down. *(And a real man at a real counter now has a
problem. Available; not required.)*

### The silence

She is asked directly. Something narrow and procedural — *is that
correct*, or *do you dispute it*.

**And she takes too long.**

Inside the pause she does the arithmetic, and it takes about four seconds,
and it comes out: **contradicting him kills him and saves nobody.**

> **So she lets a silence do it.**
>
> **And a clerk records the silence as assent, and it is on the record for
> ever, and it is the same mechanism as twenty minutes on a hill with
> nobody shouting: nothing was said, and it became true.**

**Those four seconds are the rest of her life.**

### The determination

Read out flatly, in the language of the form:

- **Mikage — retained. Productive.**
- **The others — no requirement.**
- **Iwao — released. Struck from the household.**

**Nobody in the room says the word for what *no requirement* means**, and
the scene must not translate it. **Koishi is six and is in that room.**

### The close

**She is put to work.**

Handed a tray of grit, in a place with a bench in it, within a day.

> **And the reader knows — because `threads.md` and `combat.md` both know
> — that for the next two years her son will be reading those pieces in
> shop windows.**
>
> **The tone series begins here, in the last scene the reader ever gets
> from inside her.**

## 8. The other threads

### Uzuki — two scenes, and she has no idea

**Scene 2: the wood.** She keeps the promise, waits for a cart, and it
costs her half a day — **and her mother turns out to have been right**,
which the book owes her, and which must be shown and not explained.
Something on that road that a child alone would not have got past and a
child on a cart does.

**Scene 7: Kayano.** Bigger than anything she has seen. Asked, misdirected,
asked again. **She finds the house at dusk and it is shut**, and she sleeps
against the wall of it, and ch4 opens with the door opening.

**Craft rule for both:** *she is not passive, she is unlucky.* She keeps
the promise, she gets the parcels delivered, she finds the door. **The
world is simply faster.**

### Kimimaro — one scene, and it is hope

**The food gets better.** More of it, and warm, and then a blanket, and
then somebody comes and cleans him up.

**Improvement is terrifying, because improvement means a purpose.**

He does what he does: **works out what he is being made worth keeping
for.** And gets it wrong. And is **hopeful.**

> **Hope is the worst thing that happens to him in this chapter**, and the
> scene should be almost unbearably small — a boy with a blanket, counting,
> pleased.

**Not in this scene:** any explanation. The reader gets the same weather he
does.

## 9. What must not be in chapter 3

**The raid, from anybody.** **Any body, anywhere.** **The word *Kaguya*.**
**Any Ruri learning what happened to the others** — not in this chapter and
not in this act.

**And Tadayoshi must not decide anything yet.** No vow, no plan, no
direction. He ends the chapter with an object in his hand and nowhere to
be. **The genin team is next chapter's business and the vow comes after
it.**
