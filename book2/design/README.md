## The three binding rules

These exist because book one failed at each of them, and the failures are
documented rather than assumed.

### 1. No falsification without a replacement constraint

Book one's `falsified.md` killed twelve rules. **Every single replacement
made the protagonist stronger. Not one ever added a constraint.** That is
where the suspense went.

> **No rule may be falsified without a replacement constraint named in the
> same entry.** If an entry cannot name the new constraint, the old rule
> stands.

`falsified.md` has two columns from line one.

### 2. The age ledger is an anchor, not an output

Book one's protagonist drifted from a boy into a man across seventy
chapters, one small consistent addition at a time, because there was no
stated constraint to check against.

> **Every fixed event has an age written down in `timeline.md`, and no
> scene may move it. If a scene needs more time than the ledger has, the
> scene is wrong, not the ledger.**

**Three years on the road. Ten at the warrant, thirteen at the door.**

### 3. The threshold is never written from inside

> **Write up to it. Break. Resume after, and let the reconstruction be the
> scene.** A man who cannot account for what he did is more frightening
> than one who narrates it, and the reader never gets handed the power
> fantasy.

If it is ever broken, that is an event, and it goes in `falsified.md`.
