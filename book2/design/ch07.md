# Chapter 7 — "What It Cost"

**Act one. Month 6, 2nd – month 7, end.**
**Tadayoshi 10 · Uzuki 10**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Scenes** | **10 — WRITTEN, 18,070 words.** |
| **Target** | ~18,100 words. **Came in at 18,070**, which is the first chapter to hit its estimate. |
| **POV split** | **T 5 / U 5** |
| **Weight** | **T ~50% · U ~50%** — `act-1.md`'s split, exactly |
| **Kimimaro** | **not in this chapter.** His count runs off the page for a chapter and the reader feels the gap. |

**The chapter's job:** write one contract at full length so the next
three years can be assumed; open the hunter file with an entry that has
no enemy in it; and put a girl in a brick house that is better than the
one she left.

---

## 1. THE ARGUMENT — two children start keeping the same kind of record

Ch6 was three valuations by other people. **Ch7 is the two of them
learning to keep accounts on themselves**, and the chapter is built to
land that in the last two scenes, back to back:

> **Scene 9.** He opens a second book — the hunter file — and invents a
> column called *what it cost*, and the first entry has no name in it and
> one item in the column: **a coat.**
>
> **Scene 10.** She opens a fourth column on the flour bag, **and does
> not write a heading on it**, and puts a mark in it.

**Two children, four hundred miles apart, on the same night of the book,
start a tally neither will ever show anybody.** That is the act's second
best structural move and it costs nothing — the objects already exist.

### The cut that carries the title

- **Scene 7 → 8.** He finds out what a coat costs. **She counts to
  ninety.** The chapter's title lands across that white space and is
  never said on the page.
- **Scene 2 → 3.** A village watches a decent man be kind to his new
  wife's daughter. **A room in Fukae reorients around a boy who cannot
  say one plausible sentence.** Two rooms; two children being read by
  everyone in them; neither can do anything about it.
- **Scene 6 → 7.** She overrules her own count. **He overpays for a
  coat.** Both are ten and have just made the only kind of mistake their
  character permits.

## 2. TADAYOSHI — one contract, whole

> ## ⚠ IT IS A PROBATION, NOT A JOB
>
> `combat.md` §10b. **He is not a courier for three years — carrying is
> the test**, and the question being tested is *can he take an instruction,
> complete it, and not open the parcel.* **The parcel is the exam.**
>
> **Which makes the one contract written whole the LAST of its kind**, and
> the reader should only work that out in ch8, when the carrying stops.
> **Nothing in this chapter says so.**
>
> And it recolours ch6 in retrospect: *being owed something isn't a skill*
> was not Kajiya being cold. **It was a procurement policy.** The boy is a
> heat sink, and every team hunting him is a team not hunting a cell.

**`act-1.md` §5.3, and it is the discipline the whole of months 6–8
depends on:**

> **Do not write the contracts. Write ONE contract, whole, at full
> length, start to finish, and let the reader extrapolate the rest.**

**Whole means whole**, including the parts with nothing in them: the
route, the lodging, what he eats, the four days, the eleven minutes, the
fee, and what he does with the money. **The boring parts are the
argument.** A reader who has had one of these at full length will accept
"and he did that for three years" in a half-sentence, for ever.

### 2.1 The docket, and the road (scene 1, month 6, 2nd–11th)

**Four lines, ink, ruled off at the left, Fuda's hand** (planted, ch6
scene 9). A parcel from a yard at Sakabe to a man in the front room of an
inn at **Fukae** — four days inland, arrive on the twelfth.

**Three things get established and one gets planted:**

1. **How a boy of ten actually moves.** Off the road but parallel; towns
   entered at the two hours when a child is unremarkable; where he sleeps
   and what that costs him; the rule about not buying food in the same
   town twice. **He is superb at this and the reader should feel the
   competence before the chapter takes it away from him in a front room.**
2. **He does not open the parcel**, and builds the reason himself in
   about a mile, and writes it down. **It is the first moral position of
   his life that nobody gave him** — and it is arrived at by arithmetic,
   which is the only way he can arrive at one.
3. **He is carrying money for the first time** — nothing yet, but the
   fee is coming, and he does not know what a fee is worth.
4. **THE PLANT.** At the foot of the docket, three words: ***No return
   required.*** He asks about it later; it is standard; **he files it as
   house style and is wrong.** `structure.md` §3 — Orochimaru's
   boilerplate is camouflaged by the rebellion's own featureless
   documents. **Nothing whatever is made of it.**

### 2.2 Eleven minutes in a front room (scene 3, the twelfth)

**The first light of the missing organ, spent** (`character.md`: cannot
be subtle / cannot talk to her / cannot teach / cannot price — the fourth
was spent in ch6).

Four days of walking is the part he can do. **The job is eleven minutes
in a room with adults in it**, and it requires him to be a plausible
child, and he cannot:

- **He cannot lie** (standing rule, C5, held through ch6).
- **He cannot produce a small false sentence** — ch6 taught him
  *withholding*, and withholding is silence, and **silence in a front
  room with four people in it is the loudest available option.**
- The innkeeper's wife asks him a perfectly ordinary question about whose
  he is, and the room begins to attend, **and he is about eight seconds
  from a disaster he can see coming and cannot avert.**

**AND THEN A FAT MAN AT THE NEXT TABLE STARTS COMPLAINING ABOUT HIS
NEPHEW** — loudly, at length, tediously — and hauls Tadayoshi into the
complaint *as* the nephew, and the room's whole attention goes somewhere
else, and the parcel changes hands inside the noise.

> **Tadayoshi does not perceive that anything has happened for about
> forty seconds.**
>
> And then he does, and it is the single most frightening thing in the
> chapter, because **a man has just done something to a room that he
> could not do in a hundred years and could not have seen being done.**

**That is Fuda, introduced.** `portraits.md`: *the one adult who is
decent to Tadayoshi without needing anything from him.*

### 2.3 The back room, the fee, and the hand (scene 5)

- **He identifies Fuda by his handwriting before anybody tells him
  anything.** There is a man at a table writing dockets, and **the
  character for *the twelfth* has a foot that comes back under itself**,
  and the whole of it slopes a degree and a half right and does not vary.
  > **The book turns on him recognising a hand. He does it here, at ten,
  > on the man whose sheet will crack it, and it is a pleasantry.**
- **Fuda makes nothing of the front room.** Does not explain it, does not
  claim it, does not teach it. Asked directly, says something that is not
  an answer and moves on. **He likes the boy and it is not a scheme.**
- **THE FEE.** Ten days moving at a quarter. **And he cannot tell whether
  that is a lot.**
- **And Fuda gives him a cup of something and an accurate warning about
  the next client**, unpaid, unprompted, in about nine words.

### 2.4 The money (scene 7, late month 6)

**He is robbed by ordinary retail and nobody commits a crime.**

- **A coat, boots, a blanket, oil, and grit.** All of it needed; all of it
  entered in the kit with a date (`combat.md` §3).
- **He pays nearly double for the coat**, because he has never in his life
  had to decide whether a thing was worth what was being asked
  (ch6: *I cannot price anything. Not one thing.*).
- **Fuda tells him afterwards, casually, without malice, and does not
  offer to fix it.**
- **AND GIVES HIM THE RULE:** ***"Ask what three people paid before you
  pay anybody."***
- **THE LEDGER'S FIRST ENTRY THAT IS NOT ABOUT HIS MOTHER.**
  > The book was going to be handed to somebody. **It has just started
  > being a book about how to live**, and he does not notice the change,
  > and it never changes back.

### 2.5 Two o'clock (scene 9, month 7 — FIRST HUNTER CONTACT)

**`combat.md` §10 discipline 1, executed to specification: a lodging
given up at two in the morning. No enemy on the page. A room, a decision,
and a thing left behind because carrying it was slower.**

**The signal is administrative, and it is the mechanism that caught him
once already:**

> Through a floor, at two in the morning, a man asks the lodging keeper
> **how many are in the house tonight, and their ages.**

**Not a shout. Not a search. A staffing question** — which is exactly what
went up from Sunaba on the eleventh of the fourth month with his own name
on it. **He recognises the shape of it through a floorboard and is out of
the window before the answer is finished.**

**What he leaves: the coat.** Four days old, the first thing he ever
bought himself, wet, hanging on the back of a door — **and a wet coat is
nine minutes slower over a wall.**

- **The arithmetic is correct and complete and he does it in about four
  seconds and it is right.**
- **And he minds far more than he can account for**, and writes that
  down too, in the same flat hand, as a fact about himself.

**AND IT IS ALSO WHAT DECIDES THE INVESTMENT.**

> **He is being hunted in month 7** — seven weeks after a riverbed, before
> he has finished one posting. **That is fast**, and Kajiya will have the
> same date on the same sheet, **and it settles whether a ten-year-old is
> worth teaching**: the alternative is an untrained lightning rod who dies
> in eight months and hands the state's attention straight back.
>
> **Ch7 does not know this. Ch8 acts on it.**

**AND HE OPENS THE SECOND BOOK.**

> Not the ledger. **A separate series** (`combat.md` §10: *names where he
> has them, ranks, equipment, what they tried, how long it took, what it
> cost*).
>
> **Entry one has no name in it.** No rank, no number, no description,
> nothing tried. **A question asked through a floor. Two o'clock. A
> window.**
>
> **And he invents the last column on the spot**, because he gets to the
> end of the line and finds he needs somewhere to put the coat.
>
> ***What it cost.***

**The curve's first datum records a cost and contains no enemy at all.**
That is the whole doctrine of `combat.md` §10 discipline 1 stated as an
object rather than as a fight.

## 3. UZUKI — the brick house

### 3.0 THE HARD RULE governs every scene she is in

`portraits.md`, and it is not negotiable:

> **The narration never describes it. Not once.** No POV calls her
> beautiful, dwells on her face, or catalogues her. **It is conveyed
> entirely through other people's behaviour and through what it costs** —
> how a room reorients, how a price changes, how long a man takes to
> answer, what an innkeeper does with a key.

**And the corollary, which is the chapter's real engine:**

> **She counts who has looked at her too long. She has always counted
> them. There is a list and it has been running for years.**

### 3.1 The wedding (scene 2, month 6, the 29th)

Small. A shrine, a house, sake, about nine people. **Ten weeks a widow**
and everybody knows the arithmetic and nobody says it.

- **Her action:** she asks Shimoda a practical question, out loud, in
  front of everybody, exactly as she asked about the fortieth in his yard.
  **And he answers it well**, and the whole village watches a decent man
  be kind to his new wife's daughter.
  > **The trap closes in public, with witnesses, and every witness comes
  > away with the same true impression.**
- **She counts the room.** Who looked, and for how long, and she has the
  number before the sake is poured. **The prose gives the count and never
  the cause.**
- **And she watches her mother be, for about an hour, not frightened**,
  and cannot decide what to do with that.

### 3.2 The new house (scene 4, month 7, the 2nd)

**Brick. A door that shuts. A shed with the top of the door left open,
and the mule's head over it at six.**

**Two problems, and both of them are the plot:**

1. **There is no stone by the step.** The first thing she has to solve in
   a house that is not hers is **where to hide a piece of flour bag with
   three columns on it** — and she solves it, and the solution is a scene,
   and it will matter in month 9.
2. **She has her own room.** For the first time in her life. In the old
   house they slept in one room.
   > **It is a generous thing. Everybody says so. Her mother is pleased
   > about it and says so twice.**
   >
   > **And it is the mechanism**, and the book does not say that, and
   > will not need to.

### 3.3 The good weeks — and this scene must be genuinely good (scene 6)

**If the reader does not get these weeks, nothing after them lands.**

- **Food. A dry roof. Her mother laughing at something**, which Uzuki has
  not heard since the second month and has to leave the room about.
- **She is not made to earn.** The stones stop. Kuriya's twice-a-week
  stops being the thing between them and the autumn. **The pressure comes
  off, and the relief is enormous, and she lets herself have it.**
- **Her action: she starts to like him.** Notices herself doing it. Argues
  it out in steps, the way she argues everything. **Decides she is
  allowed to.**

**AND THE ONE WRONG NOTE — and it is the most important beat in the
chapter:**

> **He goes on the list.** Once. Early. Small — a look held past the
> length a look is held for, and she clocks it inside a second, because
> she always does.
>
> **And she takes him off it.**
>
> She reasons it out and the reasoning is good: he has been decent for
> eleven weeks; he told the truth about a mule and about a fortieth; her
> mother is laughing; the roof does not leak; and a girl who puts her
> mother's husband on that list is a girl who has decided to be unhappy in
> a house that is finally all right.
>
> **She has never in her life taken anybody off the list.**

**That is what she will not forgive herself for**, and it is entirely her
own act, and it is completely reasonable, **and it is the origin of the
girl at fifteen who has re-chosen it several hundred times and keeps a
record** (`portraits.md`). **She learns in month 7 never to overrule her
own count.**

### 3.4 The first time (scene 8, month 7, the 20th)

> ## ⚠ IT IS NOT HAPPENING TO HER. It is happening to her mother, and
> ## Uzuki is in the next room, and that is the whole of the scene.
>
> **Months 7 and 8 are Sae. Month 9 is Uzuki** — *he comes for her, and
> her mother watches.* That reversal is the act's cruellest object and it
> only works if the reader has spent two chapters watching the daughter do
> the listening first.

**And this is what her own room is for.**

The generous thing. The thing everybody was pleased about and her mother
mentioned twice (scene 4). **In the old house they slept in one room and
there was nothing to hear because nothing happened. Brick carries sound
and she is alone behind a wall.**

> **The kindness is the mechanism, and the mechanism is that she can hear
> it and cannot see it.** The book does not say either of those things.

### What she actually has, and the limit is the wall, not the prose

**She is not spared anything by my hand.** She is spared it by a brick
wall, which gives her exactly this and nothing else:

- **Voices, and then not voices.** The change is the information.
- **A chair going over, once**, and then somebody standing it back up,
  which is worse than the chair.
- **Two sounds that are not shouting**, and she does not have words for
  either of them and does not manufacture any.
- **And then it stopping.** *An argument does not stop. An argument runs
  down.* **This stopped.**

**No screaming, no dialogue through the wall, no scene staged for her to
overhear.** A house where somebody is being hurt quietly is quiet, and
that is the horror and it is free.

### THE INSTRUMENT — and it is not a coping mechanism

> **She does the ninety.**

**Not escape. Not dissociation. Not a metaphor for going away.** She does
not go away; she is entirely present, and she knows exactly what she is
listening to.

> **Little. Stop. Wait. Ninety times.**
>
> **And every single *wait* is a gap in which she could get up and go
> through that door, and does not.**

**The count is the physical form of a decision being remade ninety times
in twenty-five minutes.** That is what the page is: a column of numbers,
a wall, and a girl of ten choosing ninety separate times not to move.

**Which is the character, arriving:** `portraits.md` — *she has
re-chosen it several hundred times, and she keeps a record.* **She learns
to re-choose here, and the first thing she ever re-chooses is not going
through a door.**

**And it retro-loads scene 6 exactly.** She took him off the list eleven
days ago, on good reasoning, and the first thing that happens afterwards
is that she has to sit in a room and not act on a list she has already
emptied.

### THE COST — a series that dies on a date, in silence

**Not "she can never do the palm again."** She does it on Kuriya twice a
week and on Kimimaro for years. What stops is smaller and shows on the
page:

> **The empty ninety stops.**
>
> The thing she has done every night since month 4 on the corner of a
> table, on nobody, for its own sake — **20, 22, 19, 24, 24, 26 … 31
> good … 28 …** — **ends in month 7 and does not resume.**
>
> **The column just stops.** Three chapters of a rising number and then
> nothing. **Nobody remarks on it, in this chapter or in any other.**

**From month 7 she can only do it on a person.** Which is why, years
later, her hands on Kimimaro are not a kindness she is performing —
**they are the only form the exercise is still available to her in**, and
the reader has been watching that since a Tuesday in a brick house.

### 3.5 The morning (scene 10, month 7, the 21st)

**Her mother is doing the fire and is fine.**

That is the scene's opening fact and it does most of the work. **There is
nothing to see.** Nothing about the kitchen is different. The chair is
standing up. **And Uzuki, who has spent twenty-five minutes counting, now
has a morning in front of her with no evidence in it at all.**

> **It is the second time in eleven days she has been asked to overrule
> her own count** — and the first time she did it herself, on good
> reasoning, and was wrong.

**Her action, and it is the only one available: she raises it.** Directly,
badly, in her own register, because she cannot not — *"I heard the chair."*

**AND HER MOTHER LIES TO HER.**

> One sentence. Small. Domestic. About a chair.
>
> **Sae has not told her daughter a nice thing instead of a true thing
> once in this book.** Not on the road (*ask me tomorrow* — and then the
> honest answer). Not about the money. Not about the marriage — *I'm not
> doing it for you, if I tell you it's for you then you owe me.*
>
> **Her one virtue breaks in month 7, in a kitchen, over a chair.**

**And Uzuki knows it is a lie, instantly, because she has eleven years of
calibration on that voice and it is the one instrument she owns.**

> **She has just lost the ability to read her mother**, which is the
> thing she was going to use for the next two months, **and she loses it
> in the first four seconds of the first morning.**

**THE FOUR REASONS SHE SAYS NOTHING TO ANYBODY ELSE** — reasoned in steps,
airtight, and wrong:

1. **There is nobody.** The village watched a decent man be kind to her
   at his own wedding, with witnesses, and every witness came away with
   the same true impression (scene 2).
2. **The hundred is spent**, and it went into the left column on the
   twenty-seventh, and she watched it go.
3. **The rent is his now.** Kuriya has eleven widows and does not move
   things, and said so at a gate.
4. **AND THE ONE THAT CLOSES IT — and ch6 loaded it deliberately:**
   > *"I'm not doing it for you. If I tell you it's for you, then you owe
   > me."*
   >
   > **So there is no debt to spend, and no standing to spend it with.**
   > **Sae's finest quality — her refusal to make her daughter indebted —
   > is precisely and exactly what leaves the girl with no lever**, and
   > **her mother has now used up the other one by lying.**

> **The mother is not a villain in ch6 and is not a villain here. She is
> a woman doing the only two things she has ever done — a clean account,
> and then, once, not.**

**THE LAST BEAT:**

> She gets the flour bag out.
>
> **The ninety column, and she does not add to it, and does not decide
> not to.**
>
> The money column. The third column — **1,703** — and she takes one off,
> the same as the twenty-seventh. **On the twenty-seventh it was a plan.
> It is now a sentence.**
>
> **Then she turns it and starts a fourth column.**
>
> **She does not write a heading on it.**
>
> **She puts one mark in it.**

## 7. THE FIVE HARD PROBLEMS

**1. Writing the first time.** The POV is in the next room, so **the
limit is a brick wall, not authorial squeamishness** — and inside that
limit the scene is concrete and does not flinch: voices then not voices, a
chair going over, somebody standing it back up, two sounds she has no
words for, and then it stopping. **A house where somebody is being hurt
quietly is quiet.** No screaming through the wall, no dialogue staged for
her to overhear, no fade. *The test: the scene should be unbearable
without containing one sentence that could be lifted out as a depiction.*

**1b. And the ninety is not a coping mechanism.** If it reads as *she
goes away in her head*, it is wrong. **She is entirely present and knows
exactly what she is listening to.** The count is the physical form of a
decision remade ninety times — **every *wait* is a gap in which she could
get up and does not** — and that is why it produces the girl at fifteen
who has re-chosen several hundred times and keeps a record.

**2. The good weeks must be genuinely good.** Not ironised, not
foreshadowed, not written with a hand on the reader's shoulder. **The
relief is real and the reader takes it too.** If the chapter withholds the
good weeks it becomes a machine for punishing a child, which is not a
story.

**3. Shimoda must not become a monster on the page, and Sae must not
become a victim on it.** Nothing before scene 8 is written to be re-read
as a clue except the one look — which Uzuki *herself* rules inadmissible,
on reasoning the reader will agree with. **The reader must make the same
mistake she does, in the same scene, for the same reasons.** And Sae is
not written as broken: she is doing the fire in the morning and she is
fine, and the only thing wrong with her is one sentence about a chair.

**4. One contract, whole, must not be dull.** Beaten by putting the
difficulty in the wrong place: **the walking is the part he is good at
and the room is the part that nearly kills the job.** Competence
established, then removed, in the same chapter, by a woman asking whose
he is.

**5. The hunter contact must not be a fight and must still frighten.**
Beaten by making the signal the same administrative object that caught
him in ch5 — **a staffing question through a floor** — so the reader
recognises it a beat before he does and the whole scene is dread with no
antagonist in it.

## 7b. REVISED ON THE PAGE

**1. Fuda IS Ōtsuka, and the password is buried inside the mill.** The
plan had a third party at the next table. Written: **the man Tadayoshi
was sent to ask for collects him first**, and *"what was the weather?"*
goes off in the middle of a rant about a tail race and nobody hears the
answer. Cleaner, and it means Tadayoshi never gets to execute his own
plan at all.

**2. The boilerplate is defused innocently and completely.** Not planned.
Asked what *no return required* means, Fuda gives a true, dull, complete
answer — *it's on all of them, every one I've written for eleven years* —
**and then explains the register: "Because I was a clerk. Fourteen years,
and you never get it out of your hands."** Tadayoshi files it as house
style **and he is right**, and it is still the thing. *A plant with a
satisfying wrong answer attached is stronger than a plant with none.*

**3. The grit goes perfectly, which is the joke.** Not planned. He prices
the third grade to a hair — *a fortieth a tin since before I was born* —
and is a complete mark on everything else. **He can price exactly one
category of object in the world, and it is the one his family bought for
four hundred years.**

**4. And the mittens.** The woman stands with the money in her hand,
opens her mouth, does not say it, and gives him a pair of mittens with a
hole in the thumb out of guilt. **He enters them as *Free. She gave me
these for nothing.*** Not planned, and it is the cruellest thing in his
half of the chapter.

**5. Hanase is scene 9, not a third posting.** The plan dated the hunter
contact 7/23 on an unshown job. **Written on 7/2–3, at Hanase, which
makes it the night of the bed he was told to refuse** — so the warning
that saved him from one thing put him in the other, **and he will never
know whether the bed would have been worse.**

**6. He tried to lie, and the failure is physical.** Not planned as a
beat. In the front room he makes the attempt for the first time in his
life: *"he got as far as an opening sound… and then there was nothing
behind it at all. Not a refusal. Not a scruple. Nothing came. There is
not a mechanism."*

**7. Uzuki's wedding question is a test she builds and loses by winning.**
The plan had her ask something practical. Written: **she asks about her
father's grave, in public, on purpose, having costed both outcomes four
days earlier** — and he answers better than well, in front of eight
witnesses. *You cannot put a thing on the record and then only keep the
half you wanted.*

**8. The counting hands.** Not planned. Sae has done a thing with her
hands at the box every night for ten weeks, and **at the wedding she does
not do it for about an hour**, and Uzuki has been watching it without
knowing she was watching. **It is the evidence she convicts him with in
scene 6 and the evidence she acquits him with in the same scene.**

**9. The sword goes up the lane in Shimoda's arms.** Not planned here at
all — it arrives from `combat.md` §7b, which was written after this plan.
**He carries it through the village in daylight like a rolled mat and
hangs it above the new back-room door on two nails he puts in himself**,
and **she says nothing, which she never does**, and works out why an hour
later: *asking about it is saying it's mine.*

**10. And the morning turns on hands, not on the lie.** The plan had Sae's
one lie as the scene's centre. **Written: the lie is the tell, and the
proof is physical.** Uzuki crosses the kitchen and takes her mother's
hands exactly as she did on the 27th of month 6 — **and Sae turns, puts
the kindling down on the shelf, and uses the turn to take them out of
it.** *She has never once in my life moved her hands away from me.*
**Nobody can be quoted on it.**
