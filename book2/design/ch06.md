**The chapter's job:** put a price on three children in the same
chapter, from three different directions, by people who are not being
cruel — and let two of the three be bought.

---

## 1. THE ARGUMENT — three valuations, and the cut is the argument

`act-1.md`: **three people being valued in one chapter.** That is not a
theme to be gestured at. It is the cutting order.

| | Valued by | Instrument | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kimimaro** | a buyer's agent | eleven questions and a book | **not bought** |
| **Tadayoshi** | a handler | a riverbed, read off the ground | **bought** |
| **Uzuki** | the arithmetic | a rent, a day rate, a hundred | **bought, and not by her** |

**Nobody in this chapter is cruel.** The victualler is aggrieved, the
handler is honest, the landlord is scrupulous, the neighbour is solvent,
the agent says *thank you*. **Every single valuation is fair.** That is
the horror and it is the only register the chapter is allowed.

> **Ch5 was what a boy is worth to the state. Ch6 is what a child is
> worth to the people who are being reasonable about it.**

### The rhyme cuts, in order

- **Scene 2 → 3.** A mule is priced in a yard; a boy is priced in a
  room. **The same grammar, and the mule's valuer is kinder.**
- **Scene 4 → 5.** Eleven days of water for nine people; a day rate for
  a child. **Two people converted into units by books that were not
  written about them.**
- **Scene 8 → 9.** One boy fails to sell and gets a colder bowl. **The
  next boy sells, and is given a word to be called by.** Adjacent, and
  the chapter does not comment.
- **And the chapter ends on the only sale that closes silently** — a
  woman taking a line off a number in a household book.

## 2. TADAYOSHI — Nezu, and the advance that does not solve anything

### 2.1 The town already has the story (scene 1)

He comes into Nezu on the 12th, four days from the riverbed, and **the
market is full of it.** Four Kiri genin dead in a bed a day south.

`combat.md` §10: *every hunter he kills is a specification handed to the
state.* **This is that specification being drafted, out loud, by people
who were not there**, and he stands in it and buys bread.

Three things have to happen in the noise:

1. **He hears his own work described.** Some of it is right. The fences
   are in it — *he put up a fence, like a fence, of glass* — and that is
   the part he can bear.
2. **Some of it is wrong in ways that matter.** The count is wrong (five,
   or six). The direction is wrong. **And it is a man in the story, a
   grown man, and that is protective and he knows it and he cannot make
   himself glad of it.**
3. **One detail in it could only have come off the ground.** *Ninety
   yards up the road.* Nobody survived to say that. Which means
   **somebody has been down and read it**, and is back, and has talked.

> **Finding: a fight that nobody survives still files a report. It just
> takes four days and goes through a market.**

**And he cannot correct any of it**, which is the first time in his life
that being the only person who knows something has been worth nothing at
all.

### 2.2 The victualler, and what a book is for (scene 4)

**Ch5's lesson, paid.** *Find out what the book was FOR before you look
in it.* The dues book was for dues, so the boat was **Ex.** and blank.

> **A boat that nobody may go near still has to be fed. And a
> provisioning account is a book about DISTANCE.**

**Kihara**, sixty-ish, chandler and victualler, north quay. And the way
in is not cunning, it is **grievance**:

> **A man who is owed money will explain the whole transaction to
> anybody who stands still.** The boat was paid on a **warrant**, not in
> coin, and warrants take four months to clear, and Kihara has been
> saying so to everybody on that quay since the third.

**Tadayoshi does not ask a question. He lets a man complain**, which he
has never done deliberately before and which works better than anything
he has tried in five months.

**What comes out:**

- **Water and dry stores for nine, eleven days.** No fresh food, no
  charcoal for a galley worth mentioning.
- **In on the third, out the same night. Back on the fifteenth.**
  > **So eleven days is out AND back.** He does the sum wrong first —
  > eleven days of coast — and then corrects it to **five out**, and
  > **the correction is worse, because five days is close.**
- **The warrant itself, on a spike behind the counter.** Kihara shows it
  because he wants somebody to look at how long it is taking.

**The stamp — and this is the chapter's material advance:**

> **STORES — SECOND OFFICE.** A warrant number. And a smaller
> countersign, a mark he does not know, which is **not the harbour and
> not the navy.**
>
> Kihara: *"Twice in nine years I've had that stamp. Both times four
> months."* **And the other time was three years ago, on this coast.**

**He copies the whole thing into the ledger, character for character,
including the parts he cannot read.**

### 2.3 And it does not solve anything — three constraints, named

1. **Nine is a crew number.** Provisioning counts the people who eat at
   the table. **It may not count cargo at all.** So the nine could be
   eight men and a cook, and she is a thing in the hold that got what
   was left.
   > **The book he finally found does not count her either. No book in
   > this country is for her.**
2. **Five days' sail is an arc, not a place.** Bounded is better than
   unbounded and it is still most of a coastline.
3. **A warrant office is an accounting authority, not an address** — and
   a boy of ten with no name and no standing cannot walk into one.
   **He has an address he will not be able to use for years.**

> **That is the shape of the whole search from here: he can always find
> the money. He can never find the room.**

## 3. TADAYOSHI — the recruitment, and its three costs

### 3.1 Kajiya is not looking for him (scene 6)

**The recruitment must not be a lucky break.** It isn't:

> **The rebellion sends somebody to look at every dead Kiri patrol it
> hears about.** Kajiya went down and read the bed, the same as
> Tadayoshi did, and got a different set of numbers. Then he went to the
> nearest town and asked who was new — **and a boy who has come forty-one
> miles in twelve days and is asking about a boat in a victualler's yard
> is the most conspicuous thing in Nezu.**

**The same conspicuousness that will get him killed is what gets him
hired.** Scenes 1 and 4 lay the trail in front of the reader; scene 6 is
the reader finding out somebody has been walking it.

**Found on the 20th, above Nezu, on the day he wrote entry 43** — the
riverbed, nine lines, two hours, and he did not get up for the rest of
that day. **A boy sitting still in the open for six hours is findable.**

### 3.2 Kajiya's opening move is accuracy, not threat or kindness

> *"You went down from the north end and got into the channel because
> the footing's bad and it was the only cover. That's the right answer
> and I don't think anybody taught you it."*

And then, unprompted:

> ***"Four of them, and you're ten. I'm not going to ask you about the
> last hundred yards."***

**That line does three jobs at once.** It proves he read the ground; it
demonstrates leverage; and **it is a mercy, offered to a child, by a man
who will spend him.** `portraits.md`: *the closest thing to a father in
act one, and a man spending a child.*

**And Tadayoshi wants to be asked.** He has carried an unanswerable
question for twelve days and the one man alive who might resolve it has
just told him it is not a question. So he asks, and Kajiya answers:

> *"I've walked four fields that read like that one. Two of them were
> men who'd had a fright. I couldn't tell you which yours is and nor
> could you, and the difference is worth nothing to anybody but you."*

**Finding, filed:** *the question I cannot stop asking has no market
value.* **The first time the world prices his interior life at zero, and
he writes it down, which is worse than being wounded by it.**

*(Discipline: this does not resolve ch5's unanswerable. `combat.md` §9 —
the threshold is never shown from inside and its accounting is never
closed by an outsider. Kajiya declines the question; he does not answer
it.)*

### 3.3 The terms (scene 9)

**Offered plainly, because Kajiya does not dress it:**

- **Work. Carrying, mostly, to start. Then other things.**
- **Man's pay.** *"You'll get what a man gets, because you do what a man
  does. If I paid you what a boy gets you'd work it out, and then I'd
  have a problem."* **Self-interest and honesty in one sentence** — which
  is why Tadayoshi trusts him further than he has ever trusted anybody
  kind.
- **A route, a place to sleep at each end, and papers.**
- **And one condition: no paper. Nothing written. Not a name, not a
  place, not a date.**
  > *"Everybody taken in the last four years was taken off a piece of
  > paper. Not one of them off a mouth."*
  >
  > **Which is Tadayoshi's own Sunaba finding, arrived at independently
  > by a professional — and that, not the money, is what convinces him
  > the man is real.**

### 3.4 THE THREE COSTS — and the anti-widening rule, honoured

**He gains reach, money, food, papers and a handler. Name what it
costs, in the same entry.**

**1. Heading.** He has been going toward her in a straight line —
Shioiri, Ōura, Kanaya, Sunaba, Nezu. **The moment he takes a posting he
goes where the postings go**, and the postings go south this year.

> He does the arithmetic and it comes out **for** taking it: alone he
> covers forty-one miles in twelve days and arrives with nine ryō and no
> standing; employed he covers four hundred in a month, eats, and **gets
> into rooms with books in them.** *He is not wrong.*
>
> **Nezu is the last place he chooses for himself for three years, and
> the chapter does not say so.**

**2. His name.** Kajiya asks what he does. Tadayoshi answers technically
and at length — courses, points, the tin, placement — because he cannot
be brief about work. **Kajiya lets him finish**, and writes one word.

> **杭 — KUI. The stake.**

**He described the fences and not the other thing, and he did not decide
to.** The Akagane move from C1 — *the truest available sentence that was
not the sentence* — recurring, and this time it is an omission he
benefits from.

> *"I did not lie. I answered the question that was asked. And a man now
> has a word for me that is made out of the six minutes and not out of
> the ninety yards, and I did not choose it, and I am not going to
> correct it."*

**The fall's first step is one word long.**

**3. Obedience — spent on the first day.** He agrees to keep no paper,
and **keeps the ledger anyway.** He tells himself the two are separable:
the ledger is not a record of postings, it is a record of *her*. He
knows that is untrue in about a second and a half and does it regardless.

> **The first dishonest thing he has ever done, and he does it to the
> first man who has ever told him the truth.**
>
> **And he did not write down that he had decided to go on writing
> things down.**

*(Plant, load-bearing: this disobedience is the mechanism by which, at
twelve, he still has his own file to go back through. Without it there
is no needle in the haystack. `structure.md` §3.)*

### 3.5 The docket, and the plant that must not be remarked on

Kajiya hands him a sheet. **It is not in Kajiya's writing.**

Tadayoshi notices the hand, because he reads hands for a living, and
asks whose it is. *"That's the ticket's."* **No explanation, and none
asked for.**

> **The sheet that cracks the case is put in front of him at ten, in
> Fuda's hand, and he files the handwriting as a curiosity.**
> `act-1.md` §4: on the page, and nothing made of it.

## 4. UZUKI — the arithmetic, attempted, in four moves

**The rule (`act-1.md` §6): she is not passive, she is unlucky. She acts
in every scene and the world is faster.** Her four scenes are an
escalating attempt to become an income, and each one fails for a
different and correct reason.

### The household numbers, fixed here and load-bearing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Recurring income** | **1½ a month** (Iwasa's). *The mill's 4 in the box was arrears settled once, not income.* |
| **Rent to Kuriya** | **3 a month** |
| **Everything else out** | **~2** |
| **THE HOLE** | **about three and a half a month** |
| **The mule** | **6** — *under two months* |
| **The hundred** | **100** — *over two years* |

**Six is two months. A hundred is two years.** A ten-year-old can do
both sums and the reader can do them at the same speed she does.

### 4.1 The mule (scene 2, month 5, the 14th)

Shimoda comes to pay and take it. **Uzuki holds the halter.**

- **Six ryō, and it is not a cheat — it is the price.** Shimoda explains
  the valuation rather than haggle, **because explaining is cheaper than
  haggling**, and a decent man would rather be seen to be fair.
- **She gets the household's arithmetic done to her face by a neighbour
  who is not trying to be unkind**, and comes out of the yard **with a
  target number for the first time.**
- **And the mule going is the last of her father in the yard.** Nobody
  says so.

### 4.2 The honest way (scene 5, month 5, the 22nd — ONE day, whole)

**Discipline: write one day of it, at length, and let the reader
extrapolate the other eight.** No montage. `act-1.md` §5.3.

**Morning: she goes to Kuriya and asks for the autumn to be moved.** She
has it prepared. He says no, and he says it well — not because he is
hard, but because *"if I move it for you I've moved it, and then I've
got a thing I do for widows, and I've eleven of them."*

**All day: Iwasa's top field, stone-picking and thinning.** Written whole
— the hands, the row, the sun, the woman who won't talk to her, the
older boy who is faster and knows it.

**Evening: the day rate for a child.** About **a fortieth of a ryō.**

> **To close a hole of three and a half a month she would have to work a
> hundred and forty days inside thirty.**

**And she writes it on the flour bag, in a second column beside the
ninety.** Two columns now: **the ninety is going up and the money is
not.**

### 4.3 The forbidden way (scene 7, month 6, the 4th)

**She uses the palm on Kuriya's knee.** `combat.md` §11b, exactly as
fixed: hand on, take something out first, a thimble and stop, **little,
stop, wait, ninety times**, twenty-five minutes, **and you cannot tell
during.**

- **It works.** Forty seconds after her hands come off, it works, and
  she watches a man of sixty stand up differently.
- **He pays her. A quarter of a ryō, in coin, correctly.** And he says
  why he will not take it off the rent:
  > *"If I take it off the rent it's not work, it's a favour, and then
  > I'm a man who takes favours off a child. I'd rather pay you."*
  >
  > **Scrupulous. Kind. Catastrophic.** She needed the rent moved and
  > she has been paid the going rate instead.
- **And then the sentence that closes the market:**
  > *"I'll not tell anybody. Not for your sake. If it got about that I'd
  > had a girl of ten put hands on me for my knee, I'd not hear the end
  > of it, and nor would your mother."*

**THE FINDING, and it is the real one:**

> **The ceiling is not her hands. It is that she is ten.**
>
> She has one treatment a day in her and thirty a month is seven and a
> half ryō, which would fix everything. **And there are three people in
> Nakadō with money and a pain, and one of them is her landlord, and the
> other two will not let a girl of ten touch them.**
>
> **She has a saleable skill and no market, and none of the reasons
> change inside the time available.**

**And Anzai's rule (`threads.md` C4) is not broken so much as proved:**
*nobody may ever be told* is also **the rule that guarantees she cannot
earn from it.** She broke it on purpose and the secrecy reformed around
her anyway.

### 4.4 H (scene 10, month 6, the 27th — and it closes the chapter)

**She asks what H is.** And her mother tells her the truth, because Sae
tells the true thing rather than the nice thing and explains why she is
telling it (established, C5).

> **"It isn't a letter. It's where I'd put a name if I'd decided."**

**Ten weeks of not writing a name in a book, because writing it makes it
a thing.** Then the name: **Shimoda.**

**Which retro-loads C5 with a thump.** The man who is going to do this
to her is the decent neighbour with two fields and a brick shed who came
round on a Tuesday about a mule. **He is not a monster. He is the
arithmetic.**

**Sae's account — and it must be defensible, or ch9 does not work:**

- *"There isn't anything else. I've looked at it every night for ten
  weeks and there isn't."*
- **She refuses to make her daughter indebted, and says why:** *"I'm not
  doing it for you. If I say it's for you then you owe me, and you'd
  carry it, and in about four years you'd work out what I'd charged you
  for it."* **The exact rhyme with the *ask me tomorrow* speech.**
- *"He's asked twice and I said ask me after. He's not pushed it."*
  **His patience reads as decency now.**

**Uzuki puts her numbers on the table** — the ninety, the quarter ryō,
the nine days, the flour bag. **And Sae credits them accurately, which
is annihilating:**

> **"That's very good. It's four ryō a year."**

**THE LAST BEAT — a valuation, silent, in a household book:**

> **Sae crosses out the H, writes the name, takes the line off the
> hundred, and puts it in the in-column.** The sale closes in one stroke
> of a pen and Uzuki watches it happen.

**And Uzuki starts her clock.** She works out the days until she is
fifteen — *because I'll be fifteen, that's the whole of it* (C5) — and
writes it in a third column on the flour bag. **About seventeen
hundred.** It runs from here to the end of the act.

## 5. KIMIMARO — the viewing, and what he does about it

**Rules (`act-1.md` §5c), both held:** his scenes are **the two shortest
in the chapter**, and **neither opens with a time or a place**, because
he cannot supply either.

### 5.1 The viewing (scene 3, ~1,500 — shortest but one)

**This is `act-1.md` §5d: the buyer's agent. Orochimaru's first
appearance in the book, and he is not in it.** No name, no
identification, no description the reader can convict on.

**And it pays off the fattening in ch3** — warm food, more in the bowl,
a blanket, being washed. **The reader will understand *viewing* in one
line. Kimimaro never will.**

**He is described only in what Kimimaro can supply, which is counted:**

- **Eleven questions. He wrote after nine of them.**
- **In the room for a count of about four hundred.**
- **Quiet shoes.**
- **He was the first person to come in who did not bring the smell of
  the passage in with him.**

**THE THREE QUESTIONS THE KEEPERS WOULD NOT THINK TO ASK:**

1. ***"Can you put one out somewhere else?"*** The keepers have only
   ever used the one bone in the right forearm with a marked strip of
   wood (C1). **Kimimaro does not know whether he may, and asks, and is
   told he may — in words.** The first permission of his life granted in
   a sentence. **The back of the hand.**
2. ***"Does it hurt?"*** Nobody has ever asked him a question about
   himself. **He answers yes, because a direct question was asked**, and
   it is the first true thing he has said out loud in two years.
3. ***"How long does it take to come back?"*** **He does not know.**
   Nobody has ever taken one.
   > **And then: *"Would you break that one off for me?"* — *Would you.*
   > A request.** He does it. It is worse than anything that has ever
   > happened to him. **He makes no noise, because noise makes visits
   > longer** (C1, his own finding, weaponised against him).

**The man watches his face while it happens and writes.**

**And then: *"Thank you."*** — and Kimimaro has nowhere to put it.

> *"That is the first time. I have not got a number for that, because it
> is one."*

**The keepers stand in the room the whole time and say nothing and are
uncomfortable** — because the man is talking to the stock, and that is
worse than handling it.

> **The keepers measure the stock. The buyer measures the yield.** One
> is asking what it is worth; the other is asking **how often he can
> take some.** `act-1.md` §5: *the same machine that will kill Mikage,
> running two years early, on a child, in another building.*

**He is taken back. The verdict is not said in front of him. His own
conclusion: it went well.** Eleven questions answered, the thing done,
and a man said thank you. **He is going to be used.**

### 5.2 After (scene 8, ~900 — the shortest in the chapter)

**Nothing happens, and that is the scene.**

- **Four days. Then nine.**
- **The blanket goes.** Not lost — the keeper comes in, picks it up,
  goes out. **Folding was a thing you did to a thing that was going to
  be somewhere** (ch3, scene 4). It is not going to be somewhere.
- **The food comes cold on the eleventh day after. And there is less in
  the bowl.**
- **He goes back through the eleven questions in order and finds the one
  he got wrong.**

**And the answer he arrives at is *"I don't know."*** That was the
failure. So:

> **He takes one off himself, in the dark, on the mat, on purpose — and
> starts a count.**
>
> **So that next time he will have the answer.**

**Nobody taught him to. He worked it out** (`act-1.md` §5). **The one
initiative of his life, spent on making himself better property**, and
it is 900 words and it is the worst thing in the chapter.

**Last line: the number he is counting toward, which has no end he knows
of, and which nobody is going to ask him for.**

*(And it loads ch8 — the other held child fails an inspection — like a
hammer.)*

## 9. THE FOUR HARD PROBLEMS

**1. The recruitment must not be luck.** Beaten by making Kajiya's route
to him the trail the reader has already watched him lay — scenes 1 and 4
are the trail, scene 6 is the man on it. *The thing that will get him
killed is the thing that gets him hired.*

**2. The Nezu finding must advance and must not solve.** Beaten with
three named constraints (§2.3): nine is a crew number; five days is an
arc; an office is not an address. **He can always find the money and
never the room** — which is the search's shape for the rest of the book.

**3. Sae must stay defensible.** Ch9 requires a mother who watches. That
only lands if the reader has watched her make a sound decision here with
no information that the man is dangerous. **She is not selling her
daughter. She is a woman with no income and a solvent neighbour.** Her
refusal to claim it is for Uzuki is the strongest evidence of her
decency and the exact thing that leaves Uzuki with nobody to tell.

**4. Kimimaro's scene must be diagnostic, not a reveal.** No name, no
face, no confirmable identification. **Everything about the man is
counted, because Kimimaro counts.** The reader may suspect and may not
convict. **And the scene's engine is courtesy, not cruelty** — a child
experiencing the first politeness of his life from a man pricing him.

## 9b. REVISED ON THE PAGE

**1. The Nezu radius halved, and then was marked *unchecked*.** The plan
had five days' sail; the arithmetic done on the page off Kanaya's own run
(six days Sunaba–Shioiri return) gives **four down, four back, four in
the middle — two days' sail from the inlet.** Closer is worse. And the
whole of it rests on one number a cross old man gave him about a bill, so
he wrote it with the working shown and **marked the first step
*unchecked*.** *A number you can check is worth having wrong.*

**2. The crew number replaced the cargo-doesn't-eat argument.** Planned:
*nine may not count her at all.* Written: **the crew is six, so three
more were expected to eat than there are men who sail her** — which is a
harder object, and it carries its own horror. *If she is one of the
three, then two other people were taken that week and I am never going to
know their names.*

**3. Kajiya's free lesson turned out to be the specification, explained.**
Not planned. *They're not measuring the dead ones — a dead one is a page,
your work is a book.* **And Tadayoshi cannot comply**, because the four
