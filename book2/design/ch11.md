## 0. ⚠ AUTHOR CORRECTION — and it replaces the whole spine of the plan

> **AUTHOR:** *"You're going a bit far with the boy angle. He should move
> on from the boy, and they get admonished by the client for leaving a
> survivor — that's what puts him on courier duty. He was told during his
> probation that he has to follow orders. Now he didn't."*

**The previous draft of this file had him spend six scenes trying to fix
the boy — money, the aunt, the institutions, the merchant's step, a woman
at the mill, verification at four hundred yards. All of it is dead.**

### What that changes, and it is nearly everything

| Was | Is |
|---|---|
| He agonises and tries to solve the boy | **He looks at him and leaves, and does not go back** |
| The fault is moral, and the reader shares it | **The fault is commercial, and the client names it: *a survivor*** |
| He kills the merchant / loses the merchant | **The merchant stays.** *The organisation keeps the client and spends the boy* |
| The demotion is caused by an incident | ⚠ **The demotion is caused by insubordination** |
| The creed is a lesson learned | ⚠ **The creed is a retrofit written over a gap** |

### ⚠ And the killed version was worse in a way worth naming

**I had the reader and the organisation agreeing about what went wrong.**
*That is a chapter with one opinion in it.* **The corrected version has
three, and none of them match:**

> **The reader thinks the fault was walking away from a child.**
> **The client thinks the fault was leaving him breathing.**
> **They are the same act.**
>
> **And Tadayoshi has no opinion at all, because he never decided
> anything** — *which is the hole the creed gets written over.*

## 1. THE ARGUMENT — the creed is written backwards

### 1.1 He does not spare the boy

**PAGE, ch10 sc10: the docket says *Cargo: the ground*.** *He read that
correctly in ch10 and said so out loud: **the ground is the cargo because
he wants the ground and Terasaka is what is on it.***

> **The boy is on the ground.**
>
> **So *the boy is not in the count* is not arithmetic. It is a reading he
> made, at forty feet, in eleven minutes, and then told himself was
> arithmetic** — *and the client's complaint is that it was wrong, and the
> client is right.*

**He does not spare the boy out of mercy and the prose must never suggest
he does.** *He looks at him for eleven minutes, produces nothing at all,
and walks. There is no decision anywhere in the scene, and the absence is
the point.*

### 1.2 Then he moves on, genuinely, for three weeks

**⚠ THIS IS THE PART THAT MAKES IT WORK AND IT WILL BE TEMPTING TO CUT.**

**He is not haunted.** He does two more contracts in the back half of the
tenth month and the front of the eleventh, and he is **faster than he has
ever been**, and the ledger says so, and he sleeps.

> **The organisation's clock is slower than his.** *The complaint takes
> three weeks to come down a chain, and by the time it arrives he has
> genuinely stopped thinking about it.* **That gap is the chapter's best
> structural asset: the summons lands on a boy who had moved on.**

### 1.3 The admonishment is commercial, and it is correct

**Hōki delivers it. Ōtsuka is in the room and says nothing** — *and that
silence costs, because Ōtsuka is the one who told him in ch10 that there
was nobody else in the building who would.*

- **Hazama's man walked the eleven acres and there is a boy in that valley
  who is Terasaka's**, and everybody local knows whose he is, **and in ten
  years he has a claim.**
- ***He did not buy four bodies. He bought a field with nothing on it.***
- **The order: go back and finish it.**
- **"No."**
- ***"Give me a reason I can take upstairs."*** **And there is not one.**

### 1.4 And the probation is what gets cited — AUTHOR

**The probation's entire content was doing a thing you were told without
being given the reason** *(`combat.md` §10b; PAGE in ch7 and ch8)*:

| Where | What he was told | What he did |
|---|---|---|
| **ch7** | ***You will be offered a bed. Don't take it.*** | **Obeyed it. Never found out why.** *(C7 open loop 7)* |
| **ch8** | **Four parcels, one of them rags** | *Worked out in eleven seconds that the parcel was not the product, the four days were* — **and was not upset** |
| **ch8** | **The Hanase road** | **Refused — and was right, and it did not remove the job**, and a boy of sixteen went instead |

> **So the file already contains a refusal.** *The difference, and Hōki
> puts it to him in one sentence: **that one had a reason and you gave it
> to us on a sheet.*** **This one has nothing after the *no*.**

### 1.5 Which is why the creed is a retrofit, and it must read as one

**He goes looking for the reason for four days and builds the case for
going back instead, and the case is airtight.** *The boy is a liability.
The client is right. The organisation is right. He would say the same
about anybody else's job and has.*

**And he still is not going.**

> **What he cannot tolerate is not the killing and not the boy. It is an
> outcome with no decision under it** — *and he has met that shape once
> already, in ch10 sc9:* ***things do not get decided; they get decided
> once, by accident, and then they are what there is.***

**So he makes it a rule.** *Afterwards. To cover something he already
did.* **And he knows exactly what he is doing while he does it, and does
it anyway, and writes that down too**, which is the only reason the scene
is not a cheat.

> ### ⚠ AND THAT IS WHY IT HOLDS AT THE MONASTERY AND HE CANNOT SAY WHY.
> **There was never a why.** *There is a rule written over a gap, and the
> rule is load-bearing and hollow at the same time* — **which is exactly
> what makes widening it possible for the next three books.**

## 2. TADAYOSHI — six scenes

### 2.1 He says yes, and asks a new question (scene 1, 10/13–16)

**The Thursday.** *ch10 already spent the agonising; he does not repeat
it.* He says yes, and then asks the question contractors do not ask —
**who is in the house** — and Ōtsuka's face does something.

- **Four in the count**: *Terasaka, about sixty; his son; the son's wife;
  the brother who farms the top field and sleeps in the house.* **Which is
  what fourteen thousand buys.**
- **And a boy of seven, who is not in the count** — *and that phrase is
  Tadayoshi's, not the docket's, and nobody corrects it, and nobody
  confirms it either.*
- **He goes to Ōgi and asks the aunt himself.** *A woman at a door, a boy
  audible inside, and eleven words that are true when she says them.*
- **Thread — the tone.** *Kajiya has not been seen since 2/8*, the reading
  is overdue, and nobody in Ōtoma will tell him anything about either.

### 2.2 The job (scene 3, 10/19–20) — ⚠ the largest on the page so far

**Four in one house, written whole, at `method-delta.md` §2i's register.**
*Named anatomy. Dying takes time. The POV leaves before it finishes.
Indifference staged, never stated.*

- **The book must feel the difference** between one shutter-maker in
  eleven minutes and a household.
- **The son is the fault** — *not a fight he loses; a man who does not do
  the expected thing, and it costs ninety seconds and a decision.* **It
  goes in the ledger as a correction, like everything else.**
- **The ground, at the end.** *He walks eleven acres in the dark because
  the cargo line said the ground and he wants to know what it was for.*
  **It is a field. There is nothing on it.**
- **Last line: a boy on the road.**

### 2.3 ⚠ ELEVEN MINUTES (scene 4, 10/20) — and nothing happens

**Forty feet. A boy of about seven on a step. Nothing said** *(PAGE,
`timeline.md` §2 month 11).*

**The scene is one image and no action, and the point is that he never
produces one.** *`method-delta.md` §5 — he acts instead of saying — breaks
against him here for the first time in the book.*

- **He runs the three questions** and all three answer instantly and none
  of them is about a boy.
- **He counts the minutes**, because counting is what he has, and the
  count is the scene's clock.
- **⚠ AND THEN HE LEAVES.** *No money. No arrangement. No decision. He
  turns round and goes and the boy is still on the step.*
- **The last line is not about the boy.** *It is the time, or the road, or
  the weather* — **because that is what is actually in his head, and the
  reader will supply the rest and will not forgive him, and neither of
  those is the prose's business.**

### 2.4 He moves on (scene 6, 10/21 – 11/7) — *contracts, plural*

**AUTHOR, `act-1.md` ch11: *the trade — months of learning what he does.
Contracts, plural, with bodies in them.*** **This is that scene, and it is
also the alibi the chapter needs.**

- **Two more, ledgered.** *One at length, one in four lines.* **He is
  faster than he has ever been and the series proves it.**
- **He sleeps.** *Say it plainly and once.*
- **Thread — the hunter sheets.** *On the first of the eleventh month he
  checks them, because ch10 sc9 left him that instruction.* **There is
  something to add, from the twenty-first, and he does not add it, and he
  notices himself not adding it.** *(Closes ch10 open loop 3, badly.)*

### 2.5 ⚠ THE ADMONISHMENT (scene 8, ~11/9) — the chapter's hinge

**See §1.3 and §1.4.** *Short, flat, no shouting, and nobody is unfair to
him at any point — which is the same note Uzuki's cellar is written in,
two scenes later, and the book does not point at that either.*

- **The consequence is not framed as punishment. It is framed as
  pricing.** ***You're expensive now. Expensive things get put where they
  can't cost anything.***
- **Nobody is sent after the boy.** *A second operative sent to kill a
  seven-year-old for a client who is already unhappy costs more than it
  saves.* **They eat it, and what they spend instead is him.**
- **Hazama goes on PEOPLE WHO WILL TELL SOMEBODY** — *third entry, and the
  first one he puts there out of something other than observation.*

### 2.6 The creed (scene 11, 11/10–26)

**See §1.5.** Four days of building the case for going back, and the case
is airtight, and he is not going.

**Then the rule, in his own grammar** — *short imperative, reason under
it, because he always writes the reason:*

> *Not the ones who did not choose.*
>
> *No exceptions. If there is an exception I will find it, because I am
> good at finding them.*
>
> *I am writing this after. I know I am writing it after.*

**PROPOSED:** *stay your hand from the blood of the innocent* — `spec.md`
§2g's wording — **is a later compression and is not his.** *He never says
it. It is what the sentence turns into in somebody else's mouth, or in
his own at fifteen, and the flattening is itself the first widening.*
**Ruling wanted.**

- **Last beat: a docket on the boards with a cargo line on it.** *Nine
  crates. A mountain temple.* **He reads the cargo line for a long time,
  because he spent three weeks with dockets that did not have one and
  knows exactly what it means that it is back.**

## 6. THE FIVE HARD PROBLEMS

**1. Eleven minutes of nothing must not become a feeling.** *The risk is
interior monologue with a boy in it.* **The clock is the structure**: he
counts, each interval he fails to produce an action is a beat, and **the
scene ends with him walking, not with a realisation.** *If the prose tells
us what he felt, the admonishment three scenes later has nothing to land
on.*

**2. Scene 6 must be genuinely untroubled.** *Every instinct will push a
crack into it — a bad night, a hesitation, a thought about the step.*
**Resist all of them.** He is fine, the work is good, he sleeps.
**The horror is that it cost him nothing until an invoice arrived.**

**3. The creed must not be remorse.** *He is not sorry anywhere in this
chapter.* **He is intolerant of an act with no decision under it**, which
is a completely different engine, and the reader supplies the rest. **One
line of grief for that family and the scene is wrong.**

**4. Four dead in one house cannot become montage.** *`method.md`'s
compression rule was bought in ch10 by writing Anazawa whole.* **Scene 3
is the same purchase at a larger size** — and if it compresses, the book
has taught the reader that a household is cheaper than a shutter-maker.

**5. Nobody in this chapter is cruel, and the cut order does the work.**
*The valuer, Hōki, Tokuji, Enkai — all courteous, all fair, none of them
raising a voice.* **Scenes 7, 8 and 9 must sit in that order** and no
character may notice the rhyme.
