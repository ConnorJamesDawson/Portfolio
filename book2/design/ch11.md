## 0. ⚠ TWO THINGS I AM CHANGING, AND WHY — READ FIRST

### 0.1 He does not kill the merchant. PROPOSED, and it needs a ruling.

`structure.md` act two says the boy gets **"money, a placement, killing
the merchant. All doing. None of it the thing the boy needed."** *That
line is unmarked and I believe it is mine, carried from the four-act
skeleton.* **It no longer works, and the reason is ordering.**

> **The merchant is a subscription client at 4,000 a quarter (PAGE, ch10
> sc10).** Killing him is not a strike — it is the single most expensive
> thing anybody in that organisation could do, and it lands **in month
> 11**, *before* the monastery.
>
> **AUTHOR ruled the monastery is strike one.** *"Someone from the
> resistance has murdered a couple of monks, which isn't good for contract
> confidence, so he can't stay."* **A boy who has already killed the
> subscription does not get sent on a cargo run. He gets buried.**

**Replacement, and it costs him more than the killing did** — per
`falsified.md` §1a, *a price, not a wall*:

> **He takes the boy to the merchant's house and tries to give him to
> him.** *The ground was the cargo; the boy came off the ground; therefore
> the boy is the merchant's.* **It is a ledger operation and it is
> completely coherent and it is insane.**
>
> **And the merchant does not die. He stops paying.** *No body, no
> accusation, no scene about blame.* **Sixteen thousand a year, gone,
> because the contractor came to the client's house afterwards** — which
> is the exact standard ch10 spent 14,000 words installing: **what a
> client buys is that the thing gets done and nothing else happens.**

**Which gives ch12 its cargo docket a cause that is not temperament.**
*`timeline.md` §2 asserted the punishment posting and justified it with
"he argues and does not report cleanly."* **Now there is an invoice.**

*And killing the merchant stays available — years later, and worth more
then, because by then it is not a solution to anything.*

### 0.2 The two children are the other way round now, and it improves

`spec.md` YEAR 1 has the pair in this order: **Uzuki is what happens when
he takes the child; the boy is what happens when he pays the child** —
*and "Uzuki is in the house for the second one."*

**AUTHOR moved the creed before Uzuki, so the order is inverted.** *The
boy is month 11. Uzuki is month 12.* **She cannot be in that house and
her jealous, ungenerous, correct opinion about him cannot happen in
scene.**

> **Take the inversion, because it is better.** *In the old order he
> succeeds first and then fails and learns nothing.* **In this one he
> fails first, writes a procedure against the failure, and then the
> success arrives one month later and is not his doing at all** — she
> follows him because she will not be handed off, which is the one
> variable his procedure has no column for.
>
> **`spec.md`'s line survives intact and only changes tense:** *the
> difference is not his intention, which was identical, but which of them
> latched.*

**Her opinion of the boy still happens** — *in act two, retrospectively,
when she finds out.* **And it is worth more there, because by then she is
the one who latched and she knows it.**

## 1. THE ARGUMENT — the method fails while working perfectly

**The chapter's job is to produce a creed out of arithmetic, not out of
remorse.** *If it reads as a moral awakening the book stops being about
this boy.*

### The failure has a precise shape and it is not carelessness

| He did | Outcome |
|---|---|
| asked who was in the house — **which nobody has heard him ask before** | five names, and one is a boy of seven |
| asked where the boy would be | at his aunt's, at Ōgi |
| **went and checked it himself**, because he checks everything | **it was true** |
| did the job on the strength of it | **and the boy came home on the wrong day** |

> **The verification did not fail. A person changed their mind.**
> *The aunt had had enough and put him on the road, and she told
> Tadayoshi the truth on the day he asked her and it stopped being true
> afterwards.*

**So no amount of better checking fixes this**, and he is the kind of
person who will look for the checking fix first and will not find one.
**The only rule that survives a failure of information is one that does
not depend on information: a class you do not touch, whatever you know.**

> **Which is why the creed is absolute and why widening it is the whole
> rest of the series.** *He has seen an exception arrive with the
> paperwork correct.*

### And he does not spare the boy out of mercy

**⚠ THIS IS THE LINE THE CHAPTER LIVES OR DIES ON.**

> **The boy is not on the docket.** *The cargo is the ground. The boy is
> not the ground and is not in the count.* **He does not kill people who
> are not in the count, because that is sloppiness, and he is not
> sloppy.**

**A method reason, not a moral one** — *which is precisely why the creed,
when he writes it, is written as method.* **And it takes him eleven
minutes standing forty feet away to work out that *not on the paperwork*
and *all right* are not the same fact.**

## 2. TADAYOSHI — six scenes

### 2.1 He says yes, and asks a new question (scene 1, 10/13–16)

**The Thursday.** *He does not agonise; ch10 already spent the agonising.*
He says yes and then asks the question that is new — **who is in the
house** — and Ōtsuka's face does something, because contractors do not ask
that.

- **Five names.** *Terasaka, about sixty; his son; the son's wife; the
  brother who farms the top field and sleeps in the house.* **Four adults
  in the count, which is what fourteen thousand buys.**
- **And a boy of seven**, who is **not** in the count, and Ōtsuka has been
  told to find out how old and has.
- **He goes to Ōgi and stands where he can see the aunt's door**, and then
  does the thing that will cost him everything: **he asks her.** *Not a
  neighbour. Her.* A woman at a door, and a boy he can hear inside, and
  eleven words that are true when she says them.
- **Thread — the tone.** *Kajiya has not been seen since 2/8*, and the
  reading is due, and nobody in Ōtoma will tell him anything about either.

### 2.2 The job (scene 3, 10/19–20) — ⚠ the largest on the page so far

**Four in one house, written whole, at the register `method-delta.md` §2i
set.** *Named anatomy. Dying takes time. The POV leaves before it
finishes. Indifference staged, never stated.*

- **This is the count going up**, and the book should feel the difference
  between one shutter-maker in eleven minutes and a household.
- **The son is the one that goes wrong** — *not badly, not a fight he
  loses; a man who does not do the expected thing and costs him ninety
  seconds and a decision.* **Enter it as a fault afterwards.**
- **The ground, at the end.** *He walks eleven acres in the dark because
  the cargo line said the ground and he wants to know what it was for.*
  **It is a field. There is nothing on it.**
- **Last line: a boy on the road.**

### 2.3 ⚠ ELEVEN MINUTES (scene 4, 10/20) — the chapter's centre

**Forty feet. A boy of about seven on a step. Nothing said** *(PAGE —
`timeline.md` §2 month 11)*.

**The whole scene is one image and no action, and the point is that he
cannot generate one.** *`method-delta.md` §5 says he acts instead of
saying. Here there is no act available, and the rule breaks against him
for the first time in the book.*

- **He runs the procedure.** *Is it operational. Does it matter. Can I do
  it.* **All three answer instantly and none of them is about a boy.**
- **He counts the minutes**, because that is what he has, and the count is
  the scene's clock and its indictment.
- **The only thing he can do is a transfer**, so he does one: **he puts
  the fee in the boy's hands.** *Fourteen thousand ryō in the hands of a
  seven-year-old.* **And the boy holds it, because a seven-year-old holds
  what you put in his hands.**
- **And he walks away from that**, and it is the worst thing he has done
  in eleven chapters, **and the prose does not say so.**

### 2.4 The three transfers (scene 6, 10/21–27)

**Every one of them is him handing the boy to somebody else. He never once
considers staying, because staying is not an operation.**

| | What he tries | What it meets |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **He goes back on the 21st** because the arithmetic did not close | **The boy is on the step and the money is still in his hands and he has not moved** |
| **2** | **The aunt** | *She will not take him.* **She is not a monster and she is not sorry and she has four of her own** |
| **3** | **The institutions** | ⚠ **There is nothing.** *No place, no waiting list, no rate* |

> **⚠ AND THIS IS THE CHAPTER'S BEST RHYME AND IT MUST NOT BE POINTED
> AT.** *Uzuki spent ch10 scene 2 discovering there is nothing for a child
> in Ōgi. He spends this scene discovering the same fact from the other
> end — **she had nothing and found there was nothing; he has fourteen
> thousand and finds there is nothing** — and neither of them will ever
> know the other did it in the same month.*

- **Then the merchant.** *Hazama, in Ōgi, courteous and frightened.* **He
  offers money, which is the one thing Tadayoshi has too much of**, and he
  will not take the boy, and Tadayoshi **leaves him on the step and
  walks**, and Hazama does not keep him.
- **Thread — the hunter sheets.** *On the first of the eleventh month he
  checks them, because ch10 sc9 left him that instruction.* **There is
  something to add, from the twenty-first, and he does not add it, and he
  notices himself not adding it.** *(Closes ch10 open loop 3 in the worst
  available way.)*

### 2.5 He solves it (scene 8, 10/29 – 11/4)

**And this is the scene that makes the boy an antagonist rather than a
casualty.**

- **He finds a woman at the mill** — *a hand's widow, forty, three of her
  own, a roof* — **and pays her the whole fourteen thousand to keep him.**
- **She takes it and she means it.** *She is not a villain and nothing bad
  is going to happen to that boy.*
- **He verifies twice, at eleven days and at nineteen, from four hundred
  yards, and it holds both times.** *Fed. Shod. Working the yard.*
- **⚠ AND HE WALKS AWAY FROM A SOLVED PROBLEM**, and the boy is on the
  step watching him go, **and he has never once asked his name.**

> **The ledger entry has a blank in the first column**, exactly like the
> first line of the hunter sheets, **and for exactly the same reason: he
> has no name to put in it and it did not occur to him to get one.**

**PROPOSED, and it is the antagonist's whole engine:** *the injury is not
that the boy was hurt afterwards, because he was not.* **It is that the
person who killed his family solved him like a consignment and never asked
what he was called** — *and the day he comes back, the payment is that he
says it.*

### 2.6 The creed (scene 11, 11/8–26)

**Hazama stops paying, and Ōtsuka tells him flatly, without accusation.**
*Nobody says it was his fault and nobody has to.*

**Then he is alone with it, and goes at it the only way he owns.**

- **He looks for the checking fix for four days and does not find one**,
  and writes down why not, and the why-not is the derivation.
- **Then he writes the rule**, in his own grammar — *short imperative, and
  the reason under it, because he always writes the reason:*

> *Not the ones who did not choose.*
>
> *Because I checked, and I was right, and it did not help.*

- **And then the sentence that makes it a creed instead of a preference**,
  which is the one that matters and the one the next three books are
  about:

> *No exceptions. If there is an exception I will find it, because I am
> good at finding them, and it will arrive with the paperwork correct.*

**PROPOSED:** *stay your hand from the blood of the innocent* — `spec.md`
§2g's wording — **is the compressed form, and it is not his.** *He never
says it. It is what the sentence turns into later, in somebody else's
mouth or his own at fifteen, and the flattening is itself a widening.*
**Ruling wanted.**

- **Last beat: a docket on the boards with a cargo line on it.** *Nine
  crates. A mountain temple.* **He reads the cargo line for a long time,
  because he has spent three weeks with dockets that had one and knows
  exactly what it means that this one is back.**

## 6. THE FIVE HARD PROBLEMS

**1. The creed must not read as remorse.** *He is not sorry in this
chapter and must not be shown being sorry.* **He is annoyed, at length,
with precision, at a method that produced a wrong answer from correct
inputs** — and the reader supplies everything else. **If a single line has
him grieving that family, the scene is wrong.**

**2. The boy must be fine.** *Every instinct will push toward hurting him
afterwards to justify the guilt.* **Resist it completely.** The woman is
good, the boy is fed, the placement holds — **and the antagonist is built
out of that, not out of neglect.** *A boy who was solved.*

**3. Eleven minutes of nothing has to hold a scene.** *The risk is that it
becomes interior monologue with a boy in it.* **The clock is the
structure**: he counts, and the counting is the only thing happening, and
each interval he fails to produce an action is a beat. **No epiphany at
the end. The scene ends with a transfer, which is the failure.**

**4. Four dead in one house cannot become montage.** *`method.md`'s
compression rule bought ch10 its ledger scenes by writing Anazawa whole.*
**Scene 3 is the same purchase at a larger size** — and if it compresses,
the chapter has taught the reader that a household is cheaper than a
shutter-maker.

**5. The thesis must never be said.** *Him arranging a child and her being
arranged, in alternating scenes, in the same month.* **No character
notices. No narration draws it. The cut order does the whole job** — and
scenes 6, 7 and 8 must sit in that order for it to work.
